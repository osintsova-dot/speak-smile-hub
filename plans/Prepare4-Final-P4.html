🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Sit the official Cambridge End-of-year test (tasks 1–10 and 12, plus the task-11 email in the last ten minutes) after 45 minutes of express revision built from the paper's own shapes — and close the year on what the group can now do.
What is genuinely hard
Stamina and self-management: 79 quick decisions plus an 80-word email inside 45 minutes, with the listening at a fixed moment. The language is all familiar; the clock is the only opponent left.
Typical error to expect
The Olympics held in Brazil — the was/were dropped on the way to the passive; and the mirror error, he was died [high priority, A2/B1]
A blank on a multiple-choice paper is the only unforgivable answer: a guess scores sometimes, a blank scores never [exam craft, said calmly]
Why the lesson is built this way
The revision half is built ONLY from the paper's task shapes — two stations, words then grammar, nothing decorative — and the briefing names the two traps the volleys exposed yesterday. The paper is sat whole except task 13: the paired discussion is not run under test conditions because the group produced a better speaking sample in lessons 69 and 71 — its idea list becomes the year's closing conversation instead. The email is sat by signal in the last ten minutes so nobody burns the writing time on task 3. The year ends on a can-do round and one September sentence, not on a number.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One paper between you and the summer — and you have already met every line of it. Forty-five minutes of warm-up, forty-five of paper, and then we close the year the right way: with what you can DO now.”

WHAT WE DO: the can-do round and the September sentence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Papers in, chairs turned — the last circle of the year.
  2. The can-do round, one each, present tense: I can tell a story in two past tenses. · I can order food in English. · I can disagree politely.
  3. The September sentence, assigned with a straight face: over the summer, one English thing a week — and bring me ONE sentence about the best of it in September. That is the whole homework.
  4. If minutes remain: the paper's own task-13 idea list as a free-choice chat — how SHOULD a school year end? Agree in pairs.
  5. The last line of the year, said once: You came in speaking Prepare 3. You leave speaking Prepare 4. See you in Prepare 5.
📺 On screen: a circle of chairs — no backup required
👦 Students do: declare a can-do, take the summer sentence, leave as a group. Expect the can-do round to run long — let it; this is what the number never shows

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The listening moment catches students mid-taskBrief it and signal it: two-minute warning, pens down, task 9 together, then back to wherever each student was. The paper is designed to survive the interruption.
The email squeezed into the last ninety secondsThe signal owns it: at minute ten from the end, everyone moves to task 11 regardless. An unfinished task 6 costs one mark; an unwritten email costs the writing.
A student freezes on task 7 (transformations)The briefing's craft line: name the SYSTEM first — reported? passive? unless? — then write. The volleys drilled exactly this order of operations.
The year ends flat after papers are collectedIt must not: the can-do round and the September sentence are IN the plan with protected minutes. The paper is the second-to-last thing that happens, never the last.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the OGE — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: The grade scale idea — the OGE written paper is 53 marks plus 15 speaking; 29 passes, 55 opens the specialist stream.

Today's paper is a Cambridge end-of-year test on the language of this course, not that scale — and must not be presented as it. The reason to know the Russian numbers is the opposite one: to be able to say, honestly, that the distance from a solid Prepare 4 finish to an OGE pass is one more year of language, not a mountain.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
