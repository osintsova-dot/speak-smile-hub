🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The 14 compound nouns come in two baskets, with gestures and pictures — the whole list at once would drown them. The real difficulty is not the meaning (the objects are recognisable) but the writing, «one word or two», so the baskets are physical and stay on the desks all lesson. For new students listening comes in three steps: first we say the pictures out loud (that is the unit subskill), then the order, then the details — and three plays if they are needed.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“You're going to Yosemite. One week in the mountains, one backpack — and everything in it has a name in English. Some of these names are ONE word: backpack, toothbrush. Some are TWO: sleeping bag, water bottle. We'll sort them with our hands. Then we listen to Brett, a real park guide, and find out why he talks about toothpaste and a bear. And at the end: your own backpack — six things, and why.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We are going to Yosemite: one week, one backpack, and every thing in it has an English name. Some are written as one word, some as two — we will sort them with our hands. Then we listen to Brett, a real guide (there is a bear and some toothpaste in it) and pack our own backpack of six things.

WHAT WE DO: two baskets with pictures and gestures: ONE WORD / TWO WORDS; recognition → production → ex.4 sorting → ex.5 labels A–K

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Basket by basket: picture → chorus → gesture. I show the gesture first and we repeat it together: toothbrush — brush your teeth! · sleeping bag — sleep! · water bottle — drink! · sun cream — rub your arms!
  2. Once the gestures are learnt — Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols, now without the picture as support: I show the gesture — you shout the whole word AND say 'one' or 'two'. Everyone answers in chorus, so getting it wrong is not scary.
  3. ex.4 with their hands: we lay the cards out into the two baskets. TB key: One word — hairbrush, guidebook, penknife, suitcase, toothbrush, toothpaste (+ the example backpack) · Two words — phrase book, sleeping bag, sun cream, wash bag, water bottle, waterproof jacket (+ the example beach towel).
  4. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the object cards: you show the picture — your partner says the word and says ONE or TWO — then you trade cards.
  5. ex.5, labelling the photos A–K (TB key: A backpack · B penknife · C guidebook · D suitcase · E sleeping bag · F waterproof jacket · G wash bag, toothbrush, toothpaste · H water bottle · I phrase book · J sun cream, beach towel · K hairbrush).
📺 On screen: SB p.53 ex.4–5 · the basket cards · word bank on screen (Backup: the word bank written on the board and uncovered before the lesson)
👦 Students do: they sort with their hands and name the items. Expected: the baskets hold the spelling better than a rule does

WHAT WE DO: track 27 step by step: we name the pictures → play 1, the order (ex.7) → play 2, the details (ex.8) → play 3 if it is needed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1 (subskill): Look at the seven pictures. Name everything you see. What words will you HEAR? Two things are NOT allowed — which? We do this together (TB technique).
  2. Step 2 — play 1, the order of the pictures only (ex.7). TB key: a 3 · b 5 · c 7 · d 1 · e 4 · f 6 · g 2.
  3. Step 3 — play 2, the details (ex.8). TB key: 1 times · 2 photos, plants or flowers · 3 animals · 4 backpack · 5 box · 6 hat, (full) water bottle. TB technique: put your hand up as soon as you hear your picture.
  4. A third play — if the class asks for it, with no discussion.

WB Unit 4 · the ONE WORD / TWO WORDS baskets stay in the classroom
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name and write 14 compound nouns correctly (one word / two words), catch 4 details from a guide's briefing, and present their own 6-item backpack with a frame.
  • Subsidiary. The Outward Bound leaflet (ex.1–2) as context; ex.3, four things in the photo; ex.4, sorting with their hands; ex.5, labels A–K; the subskill using pictures to help you understand; ex.7–8 (track 27) step by step; ex.9, pros and cons.
  • Personal. Audio is scarier than a text — I play it three times if I have to; before the sound we always say ALL the pictures out loud. I never ask for a fact after the first play (TB rule).
By the end, students can…
  • name 14 things to take on a trip
  • say and write which are one word and which are two
  • use pictures to predict what a recording is about
  • present my backpack with a frame

Form
some compounds are written as ONE word: backpack, guidebook, hairbrush, penknife, suitcase, toothbrush, toothpaste · others as TWO: beach towel, phrase book, sleeping bag, sun cream, wash bag, water bottle, waterproof jacket · there is no 'by ear' rule — this has to be memorised and checked against the list in Quick review p.57 (track 29)

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«back pack» as two words — is that allowed? → no: backpack is one word.
«sleepingbag» joined up — is that allowed? → no: sleeping bag is two words.
What is the first step before any listening with pictures? → name everything you can see and guess the words.

Assumptions: SB p.53, KTP pair 33 (the KTP says p.54 — a shift of +1, the real page is 53). The names of the objects are mostly recognisable; the difficulty is in the writing and in the speed.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
14 compounds at once do not fitTwo physical baskets on the desks, 7 cards each; recognition first (put it in the basket), production second.
Panic during listeningBefore the sound we say all seven pictures out loud; three plays are allowed; the first question is only about the order of the pictures.
Spelling of long wordsThe spelling station covers only the 6 frequent ones (backpack, sleeping bag, water bottle, sun cream, toothbrush, waterproof jacket); the rest are recognition only.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
