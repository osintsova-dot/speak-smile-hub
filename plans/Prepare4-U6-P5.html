🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that the vocabulary and grammar of two units work without the book: fourteen body parts and the illness words, the nine illness verbs, fourteen animals, will/be going to by meaning, must/might/can't by evidence, the adverbs of probability in their slots — and correct spelling.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the gap between oral and written: out loud the class knows almost everything, on paper cough loses its gh, stomach ache scrambles, mosquito drops a letter, and mustn't sneaks back in for can't under time pressure.
Typical error to expect
stomach ache (not stomachache with the letters scrambled) · cough (the gh says f!) · toothache and earache are ONE word · mosquito · a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
It mustn't be a fox for «этого не может быть» — Russian «не может быть» maps onto mustn't, but English impossibility is CAN'T; mustn't is prohibition, not deduction [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built as retrieval practice, not re-teaching: two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it — and shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which form still breaks. The official Cambridge game sits between the stations and the briefing on purpose: it hides revision inside a sentence-building race, and a class that has just competed walks into a test calmer. The grid is shared with December's game, so today the Unit 11–12 circles are the legal core and the old money/food circles are bonus-only spaced retrieval. Nothing new is introduced anywhere today, and the five-mistake lists that leave this room are the entry ticket to the Review 3 lesson in two days.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. Everything on these two papers you have already done in this room — I can name the lesson for every line. First forty-five minutes we play it back. Then forty-five minutes you show it. Your score is not the point: the point is your own list of five mistakes — because in two days that list is your ticket into the review lesson.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast card round of the block's words, and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — hot round. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the block's word cards (body parts + illnesses + animals + the verbs), two trades each: Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest: Everything you just said is in today's test.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, call definitions and pairs answer chorally
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety dropping. Typical stumbles: cough and mosquito — both spelling, both in the stations

Lesson frame

Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: put the test sections up in advance — transparency takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the six sections of the two papers on the board, one line each: health words · illness verbs · will/going to ×2 · animals · adverbs of probability · modals ×2.
  2. Look at the board. Have we done all of this in class? Yes or no? The class answers chorally — the answer is always yes; if anyone doubts, name the lesson for that line.
  3. The rule of the day: Your score goes in the register. Your five mistakes go in your notebook — and that list is your TICKET into the review lesson. Bring it.
📺 On screen: the six section lines in chalk — the board is the medium and it stays up for the whole first half, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the sections and answer chorally. Expect visible relief. Typical problem — they don't believe «we have done all of it»: point line by line at the lesson it came from

Review station 1 · words

Why: Retrieval practice rather than re-teaching: they pull the block's vocabulary out of memory in short bursts, which is what makes it stay.

WHAT WE DO: the block's vocabulary: 40+ words, against the clock and in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, oral: definitions at speed — the part between your foot and your leg? (ankle) · you get it from someone and it comes with a fever? (flu) · the insect that kills the most humans? (mosquito) · went and came back — been or gone? (been). Twelve items.
  2. Round 2, frames: you name the noun, teams fire every verb that fits — a cold! (catch/get/have) · your ankle! (break/cut/hurt/injure) · better! (feel/get). Six items.
  3. Round 3, written: Kagan Showdown — you say the meaning, teams WRITE the word. Load the traps deliberately: cough, stomach ache, toothache, mosquito, forehead.
  4. Every misspelling goes into a «watch out» column on the board — it stays there right up to the test.
📺 On screen: definitions read aloud, answers on mini-whiteboards — voice and boards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: say, fire, then write the words. Expect almost everything orally and a visible drop in the written round — which is exactly why the column exists. Typical error: coff
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Block6_Units11-12_Match_2up_A4 + Prepare4_Block6_Units11-12_Translate_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to be joined to their English pairs A–L (aches and illnesses, will against going to, health verbs, wild animals, must/might/can't for deduction, probability adverbs, describing a photo), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 written out in English from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the block's exit line.

20–32′
Review station 2 · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar — and it shows you, twenty minutes before the test, exactly which form still breaks.

WHAT WE DO: the block's grammar: ten mixed sentences with the reason every time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ten mixed sentences, one at a time: three on will/going to (with the deciding question), four on the modals (including one mustn't plant — catch it!), two on the illness verbs, one on adverb slots.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the REASON, not the answer — Say WHY, not just the answer.
  3. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Sentence six — number fours, boards up!
  4. Boil every explanation down to the step: decided before → going to; evidence → must or can't (never mustn't); possible → might; perhaps first, probably inside.
📺 On screen: the ten sentences written on the board before the lesson and uncovered one by one with a sheet of paper — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with an explanation, then report by number. Expect the mustn't plant to be caught with joy — that is the point of planting it. Typical error: will for the booked plan — the deciding question, one more time

32–40′
Review game
teams of 2 vs 2
Why: The official Cambridge game hides revision inside a reason to listen to each other; a class that has just competed walks into a test calmer.

WHAT WE DO: «Four in a row!» — the official Cambridge review game, our block's circles as the core

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Two teams per group, one cut sheet each (word grid + Topics ticklist). On each turn a team picks a circle from the grid and a topic from the ticklist and makes one sentence that uses the word ON that topic — Body and health → aches → «My back aches after training.» If the other team accepts the sentence, the circle is coloured in the team's colour and the topic is ticked off. The sheet's own aim is four circles in a row.
  2. ⚠️ The grid covers Units 9–12, and the Unit 9–10 circles were played in December — so today only the Unit 11–12 circles are legal: ankle · bee · sore · cough · might · hurts · deer · definitely · ant · fever · finger · injure · could · fox · probably · catch · worm · aches · throat · perhaps · cheek · must (about twenty-two of the forty-two). The money and food circles are spaced retrieval — a team MAY claim one for a bonus point, but the game is won on the block's own circles. With half the grid soft-locked a four-in-a-row may be impossible — play for MOST circles won in eight minutes instead, and say so up front.
  3. Sharper groups: the «you give me the topic» version from the teacher's notes — team B picks the topic team A must build on.
  4. Set the bar: The other team judges your sentence — so listen like judges: does it show the meaning, yes or no?
📺 On screen: one cut sheet pair per group, run off in advance — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed; with no printer, write the twenty-two legal words on the board and teams claim them by sentence, ticking topics off a shared list
👦 Students do: pick a circle and a topic, build the sentence, judge each other, score. Expect the judging to be as useful as the building. Typical stumble: a sentence that USES the word without SHOWING it (I like might) — quote it, laugh, rebuild it

Test briefing
class
Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to the «watch out» column: the five words that were misspelt. Look at these five words for thirty seconds. Then I close them.
  2. Instructions: two official papers, Unit 11 then Unit 12, about 35 minutes total; phones in the bag; finished early — five deductions on the back. One honest heads-up: Three quiz animals visit the animal section — tiger, kangaroo, snake. You know them.
  3. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  4. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect silence to settle in 20 seconds if the flat hand goes up before you start talking

47–85′
Block test
solo, silent mode
Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats, official papers. Everything in them has been done in class, so the test measures learning, not surprise.

WHAT WE DO: the official Unit 11 and Unit 12 tests, back to back — 64 items in about 35 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. Unit 11 first. You have thirty-five minutes for both papers. Start.
  2. Walk the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  3. Write the time left in chalk: 20 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  4. Anyone who finishes early writes five deductions about animals on the back and sits quietly.
  5. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling — cough, stomach ache, mosquito.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; nothing at all is put up in the room while the test is running
👦 Students do: work in silence for 35 minutes. Expect the vocabulary sections to be closed fast, the U11 future-form building and the U12 rewrites to slow them down. Typical error: mustn't for can't in the rewrite section — it was mentioned in the briefing, and it will still happen

85–90′
Test wrap-up
circle
Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the review briefing.
  3. Homework: once the papers come back — rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly; that list is the ENTRY TICKET to the Review 3 lesson, collected at the door.
  4. Teaser: In two days: four units in one lesson — a crossword, an auction, and the only cloze in the book that mixes soup with flu.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is full, read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to name the rewrites and will/going to most often — that is the plan for the review briefing

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Nerves before the testSay it out loud and early: nothing will come out of thin air, every format has been done. Put the section list on the board before you start.
Someone finishes both papers in 25 minutesThe rule: hand them in, turn the sheet over and write five deductions about animals — must/might/can't with evidence. It is read, not marked.
After the test they want to compare answers out loudCollect the papers in silence; the analysis is next lesson. Today, once a paper is in, the only thing left is personal reflection.
The game's four-in-a-row is impossible on the split gridNamed up front, not discovered mid-game: the U9–10 circles are bonus-only, so we play for MOST circles in eight minutes. The Cambridge rules otherwise stand.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
