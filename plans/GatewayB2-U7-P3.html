🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Listen to a podcast for detail and judge eight true/false statements against it; then use a range of reporting verbs (admit, agree, announce, claim, complain, explain, promise, remind, warn, suggest, apologise for, accuse somebody of, criticise somebody for) with the structure each one demands.
What is genuinely hard
Two different difficulties in one lesson. The listening problem is that the true/false statements never repeat the words of the recording, so a student who listens for matching words gets them all wrong. The grammar problem is that every reporting verb carries its own pattern and there is nothing to reason from: you SUGGEST doing, APOLOGISE FOR doing, ACCUSE somebody OF doing, ASK somebody TO do. It is a memory task disguised as a grammar task.
Typical error to expect
«He apologised to be late», «She admitted to steal it», «He suggested me to go» — every reporting verb carries its own pattern: apologise FOR + -ing · admit + -ing · suggest + -ing or that-clause · accuse somebody OF + -ing · warn somebody AGAINST + -ing. Learned as chunks or lost.
Listening for words instead of meaning. Statement 1 says romance is the most popular genre; the podcast says exactly that — and it is marked FALSE, because the statement says «fan fiction». One word decides five marks.
Why the lesson is built this way
The listening comes first and whole, because it is the source of the language: the podcast is two people talking about story-sharing websites where teenagers publish their own writing, seventy million users, and one girl whose story became a bestseller and then a Netflix film. The eight statements are answered twice — once for the answer, once for the PROOF, because «why is it false» is where the learning is. The grammar then comes straight out of the same podcast: the Student's Book prints the actual sentences from it. The patterns are built as a five-column grid the class fills, and the lesson finishes with sentences about their own lives that cannot be produced without them.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Beth Reekles was fifteen. She published her story on a website for free, a real publisher rang her, it became a bestseller, and then Netflix filmed it. Today: a podcast about those websites — and the verbs English uses to report what people claim, admit, promise and complain about.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: a real argument, with reasons and examples compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3, four minutes in pairs: Are story-sharing websites a good idea? Could they have any drawbacks? — pairs first, then the floor
  2. Give the frame on the board before they start, because at B2 the frame is the difference between an opinion and an argument: I think … because … For instance, … On the other hand, some people would say …
  3. Take four opinions to the floor. Make each speaker react to the one before: Do you agree with what he just said?
  4. Push once for the drawback side if nobody raises it: quality control, no editor, copying, time lost. The podcast itself says bad stories are easy to avoid — is that true?
  5. This stage feeds the Speaking lesson (P4) directly: the argument-plus-example habit is exactly what is assessed there.

Grammar · with an object, or without
class → pairs
Why: Grammar · with an object, or without

WHAT WE DO: Grammar in context 2 ex.1a–1c and 2a–2b: the verbs from the podcast, sorted

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed warmer, one minute. Write: Jack said that he liked thrillers. · Jack told me that he liked thrillers. Why said in one and told in the other? Because told needs a person.
  2. ex.1a, three minutes: which verbs in bold need an object pronoun (like told) and which do not (like said)? Answers: with an object — warn, remind · without — explain, add, claim. Pre-teach claimed (says it but has not proved it).
  3. ex.1b: dictate the six Teacher's Book sentences and let the class sort the new verbs from them — James admitted that he was wrong. · Clara announced that she had won a prize. · Max complained that the exam was too hard. · Maria promised me that she wouldn't forget my birthday. Answers: without an object — admit, agree, announce, complain · with or without — promise.
  4. ex.1c, two minutes in pairs: define the five verbs in English, not in Russian. Admit — agree something is true when you are sorry or surprised. Announce — public and official. Complain — say you are not satisfied.
  5. ex.2a, four minutes individually: the «Supernatural success story» text about the teenage author Lee Kim — choose the best verb nine times. Answers: a announced · b claimed · c agreed · d admitted · e added · f – · g complained · h warned · i promised.
  6. ex.2b, two minutes in pairs: put five of those sentences back into direct speech. It is the reverse gear again, and it proves whether the meaning landed.
📺 On screen: the two Jack sentences and the sorted verb lists on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: sort eleven verbs by whether they take a person, define five in English, then choose nine in a real text

Structure

Form
verb + infinitive: agree, claim, offer, promise, refuse · verb + object + infinitive: advise, ask, instruct, tell, order, invite, persuade, remind, warn · verb + gerund: admit, advise, deny, recommend, regret, suggest · verb + preposition + gerund: apologise for, insist on, object to, confess to · verb + object + preposition + gerund: accuse somebody of, congratulate somebody on, criticise somebody for, warn somebody against.
Pron. watch
compound NOUNS take the stress on the first word (BLOCKbuster, CLIFFhanger, SOUNDtrack, BOX office) while compound ADJECTIVES take it on the second (best-SELLing, eco-FRIENDly) — that is exactly what track 38 tests · clichéd /ˈkliːʃeɪd/ keeps the French vowel and the class will say «kli-CHED» · genre /ˈʒɒnrə/ · lyrics /ˈlɪrɪks/, not «LYE-rics» · and in the emphatic I really DO think that… the stress lands on do, which is the whole point of the structure
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«He admitted taking the money.» Is he happy about it? → no — admit carries regret or surprise. «He announced it»? → public, official, planned. The meaning decides the verb, not the grammar.
«She apologised … not having time.» What is missing? → FOR. Which verbs take a preposition? → point at the grid, column four.
ICQ before the listening: Do you write the answer on the first listening? — No. What do you do? — Get the general picture.

All eight true/false answers wrong because they listened for wordsDo not re-play immediately. Put statement 1 on the board next to what the podcast actually said and let the class see the difference (fan fiction vs romance). Then re-play. One demonstration is worth three replays.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 3–9 (the seven-item interview — the same format, the same length, heard twice) and tasks 19–25 for the reporting verbs.

The Russian listening section is built on exactly this: a studio interview with a writer or an actor, seven questions, three options each. The skill that transfers is not «understanding English» but reading the options BEFORE the recording and listening for the MEANING rather than for the matching word. The reporting verbs are worth a separate mark: admit, claim, warn, remind, apologise for, accuse of — the pattern after the verb is what the exam tests.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
