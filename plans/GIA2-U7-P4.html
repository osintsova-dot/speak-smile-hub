🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): Adjectives for describing people are not introduced as a list but as a judge's tool: the crew gives the prize to one of the two teenagers from the podcast and has to justify the choice with three precise words, not “he is good”. The unit subskill (listening for numbers) works literally: without the numbers the justification is empty — $80,000 against $600, thirteen years old, £1,000, a year ago. Did…? questions turn up in the podcast, but they are NOT announced as the topic of the lesson — that is the work of P5.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Judges, one prize, two candidates. Easton: fourteen years old, built a robot hand out of Lego and parts of model planes. He met a girl whose hand cost eighty thousand dollars — and he got angry, so he made one for six hundred and put the whole thing online for free. Amineh: thirteen, from Syria, started speaking English one year ago, and won a thousand pounds for a poem. One prize. Your crew writes the citation: sixty seconds, three adjectives, three numbers. No number, no prize.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: one prize, two candidates from the podcast — Easton and Amineh. The crew writes the citation: 60 seconds, three precise adjectives and three numbers from the recording. With no numbers the justification does not count.

WHAT WE DO: the level for our students: the opposites factory — the prefixes and the suffix that are not in the task

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Weak citation: 'He isn't bad.' Useless. English has machines for opposites — take them.
  2. The factory (TB note p. 102): un- · im- · il- · ir- · non- · dis- and the ending -less. im- goes before p: polite → impolite. il- before l: legal → illegal. ir- before r: regular → irregular.
  3. Testing the factory: possible · patient · kind · usual · care · hope · friendly — build the opposite in five seconds each.
  4. One machine-built word in your citation = richness points.
📺 On screen: a slide with the prefix factory (Backup: write the four prefixes and -less on the board and leave them up to the end of the lesson)
👦 Students do: students build opposites against the clock. Expect: “unpolite” → corrected to impolite. Typical problem: they swap “unkind” and “impolite” — sort it by the first letter of the root

PRODUCT · the award citation
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second citation: who gets the prize · three adjectives · three numbers · one reason why not the other one

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes of preparation: Keywords only. Everyone owns one block: the name · the adjectives · the numbers · why not the other one.
  2. The rule of the ceremony: 'Good' is banned. Three precise adjectives, three numbers from the recording.
  3. A support frame on the board: The prize goes to … . He/She is … , … and … . When he/she was … , … . It cost … . That's why … .
  4. Sixty seconds per crew. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness — cards up!
  5. A challenge from the floor (TB ex.8): Can we describe them as heroes? Why/Why not?
📺 On screen: 60-second timer · judges' cards · the rubric (Backup: write the citation frame in chalk on the board)
👦 Students do: students perform in their roles, the judges work off the rubric. Expect: the bar holds the format. Typical problem: “He's very talent” — the judges take accuracy off

WB Unit 7 · points onto the leaderboard next lesson
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can describe a person with 13 adjectives and their opposites and deliver a 60-second award citation built on three adjectives and three numbers from a podcast.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 three dictionary definitions; ex.2 five definitions (all positive); ex.3 pairs of opposites; ex.4 six people; ex.5 describing the photos; ex.6–7 the podcast (track 46) for numbers; ex.8 discussion; the “minus” prefixes un- / im- / il- / ir- / -less as the bonus.
  • Personal. Do NOT announce Did…? questions as the topic: for now they are simply “how presenters ask”. Log “What did he did” silently — that is the material for the clinic in P5.
By the end, students can…
  • describe a person with a precise adjective, not 'good' or 'bad'
  • give the opposite of an adjective (careful → careless)
  • catch numbers in a recording and say what each one means
  • deliver a 60-second award citation with evidence

Structure

Form
the adjective goes after be (She IS generous) or before the noun (a generous boy); intensifiers very / really / not … at all; the “minus” prefixes un- · im- · il- · ir- and the suffix -less.
Pron. watch
from the Wordlist: generous /ˈdʒenərəs/ · talented /ˈtæləntɪd/ · polite /pəˈlaɪt/ · careless /ˈkeə(r)ləs/ · unfriendly /ʌnˈfren(d)li/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He is very talent” — what is wrong? → you need the adjective: TALENTED.
“unpolite” — do people say that? → no: IMPOLITE (im- before p) or simply rude.
“£1,000” — how does it sound? → one thousand pounds: the currency is written before but said after.

Assumptions: SB p.89, syllabus pair 57. Adjectives of appearance are familiar from the Starter; character adjectives are new. Students hear big numbers and currencies badly — the unit subskill is about exactly that.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“Good” and “bad” for everythingThe judging rule: the word “good” scores zero. Three precise adjectives or the citation does not count.
The numbers slip past the earTB routine: say all eight numbers ALOUD before listening, including £1,000 = one thousand pounds. The ear only catches what the mouth has already said.
They argue about the “real hero” in RussianThe argument is allowed, but inside a frame: two adjectives + one number. No frame and the turn goes to another crew.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
