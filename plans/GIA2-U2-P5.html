🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The Wh- questions are hidden inside a press conference: the star answers only a grammatically clean question, a crooked one burns without an answer. The three tables in the book (be / have got / do-does) are assembled not as «a rule» but as three channels for reaching the person answering. The sports quiz ex.4–5 works as a warm-up and closes the form at the same time. The intonation and the linked do you /djʊ/ are «the professional journalist's accent».
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Press pass, agents. In twenty minutes a sports star sits in that chair. You get questions — but the star answers ONLY perfect ones. 'Why she likes it?' — silence. 'How many cousins have you?' — silence. And one more thing: real journalists don't sound like robots. Wh-questions go DOWN at the end, and 'do you' sounds like one word. Six clean questions per crew. Prepare!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: A press conference: the star answers only faultless questions, a crooked one burns without an answer. Plus the pro's accent: a Wh- question falls, do you is linked.

📓 Interactive notebook — Get_Involved_A2_Unit2_Grammar2_Wh_Questions_Visual_Takeaway_A5
A5 grammar card — BUILD A WH-QUESTION: six question words (who · what · where · when · why · how / how many) and three helper branches: BE (Who is the team captain?), HAVE GOT (What have you got in your bag?) and other verbs through do/does (Why does he like hockey?), with the rule “choose the helper first”
  • Glue the card in; label each branch with its helper.
  • Build one question on each branch — out loud.
  • The trap: after does — base verb; mark it on the card.
  • The card stays open through the question machine.
When: ~5 min at the start of the “Sprint: the question machine” stagePrep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Scissors, glue sticks, pencils.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 complete the quiz questions → the crew answers with one head → ex.5 (track 16) the check → a duel of crews on their own questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4: Which word is missing — do, does, is, are or have? Two minutes. (TB key: 1 do · 2 have · 3 is · 4 does · 5 are).
  2. We answer the quiz through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: the crew puts their heads together and agrees on one answer — Number twos, stand and answer! A random number answers, so everyone has to know it.
  3. ex.5 (track 16): we listen and check the quiz answers. TB key: 1 b · 2 c · 3 b · 4 b · 5 a.
  4. The duel: each crew writes 2 sports questions of their own with options and «fires» them at their neighbours; a crooked question burns.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can build clean Wh- questions with be, have got and do/does at speed, ask them with falling intonation, and run a 6-question interview and report it.
  • Subsidiary. The three tables in ex.1 are completed from the examples; ex.2 building questions; the sports quiz ex.4–5 (track 16); ex.6–7 their own questions and the interview; Grammar round-up (bike polo) as the final check; the pronunciation of do you /djʊ/ (SB p.116).
  • Personal. I watch for the FALLING intonation (that is what makes it different from the Yes/No questions in Unit 1) — I drill it chorally. The questions that burn I write down for the clinic in this same pair.
By the end, students can…
  • build Wh- questions with be, have got and do/does
  • ask with falling intonation and linked 'do you' /djʊ/
  • run a 6-question interview without a broken question
  • report an interview in writing

Structure

Form
question word + be/have-has/do-does + subject (+ got / + bare verb, no -s)
Pron. watch
the intonation of a Wh- question FALLS (unlike Yes/No): Why is hockey so important to you? ↘ · SB p.116: an unstressed do you in a question sounds like one word /djʊ/ — What sport do you like? · Where do you play?
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Why she likes it?» — why doesn't that work? → you need does: Why does she like it? — and the verb is bare.
«How many cousins have you?» — what is missing? → got: How many cousins have you got?
«What you do in your free time?» → What DO you do in your free time?

Assumptions: SB p.30 + Pronunciation p.116, KTP pair 17. The Wh- words are familiar from A1+; what is new is the three different channels (be / have got / do-does) and staying clean under the pressure of the game.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The questions degenerate into «Do you like…?»The star's role cards are built for Wh-: age, team, timetable, reasons — a Yes/No question gets you nothing.
Why she likes it? — the classicThe rule of the conference: a crooked question burns WITHOUT an answer. The burnt ones we go over in the clinic in this same pair.
The intonation rises, as in a Yes/No questionA drill with the hand: on a Wh- question the hand GOES DOWN. Three choral runs before the conference.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
