🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the fourteen animal words and build deductions with must / might / could / can't — conclusions from evidence, said with the evidence attached (It's got six legs, so it must be an insect).
What is genuinely hard
The grammar concept is genuinely new: Russian «должно быть» and «не может быть» both look like must-family words, so it mustn't be a fox writes itself — but English deduction-impossible is CAN'T. One wrong mapping, drilled early, saves the whole unit.
Typical error to expect
It mustn't be a fox for «этого не может быть» — Russian «не может быть» maps onto mustn't, but English impossibility is CAN'T; mustn't is prohibition, not deduction [high priority, A2/B1]
An opinion with no reason attached — I think zoos are bad full stop — scores as no opinion at all in the speaking [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The animals arrive against photos with the recording confirming, then the book's own CREATURES quiz makes every word a stake in a team game — vocabulary with adrenaline. The weird-animals reading is the unit's gift: its missing-first-sentences task forces reading for the point of a paragraph, and its creatures (blobfish, lamprey, pufferfish) hand the grammar stage perfect material — every deduction about them is real. The modals are discovered from the stick-insect dialogue, drilled through eight mystery sounds (deduction by ear), and produced over four macro photos with a class vote. The mustn't/can't trap gets its own CCQ, its own board card and its own minute — it is the one error that survives politeness.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“There's a fish that looks like a sad old man, a fish that drinks blood, and a fish that can kill you after it's dead. Today you meet all three — and you learn to think out loud in English: it CAN'T be a plant, it's got eyes… so it MUST be an insect. Detective grammar. By minute seventy you'll be deducing from photographs like Sherlock.”

Reading · weird animals
solo → pairs
Why: Missing first sentences force reading each paragraph for its point, not its facts — the hardest reading skill on any exam, met here on the friendliest possible text.

WHAT WE DO: six creatures, three paragraphs, missing first sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 — photos first, books just opened, pairs, one minute: Which of these six would you touch? Which never?
  2. ex.2 gist race, 3 minutes: match the six bold names to photos 1–6. Key: bold names to photos: 1 pufferfish · 2 golden poison dart frog · 3 star-nosed mole · 4 slow loris · 5 lamprey · 6 blobfish.
  3. ex.3 — the unit's real skill: three first sentences removed, put each back. Solo first, then Kagan RallyCoach with the reason: What is paragraph B ABOUT — one word? Key: first sentences to paragraphs: 1 C (What animals do you think are the most dangerous…?) · 2 B (There are some very funny-looking animals out there — funny here = strange!) · 3 A (Scientists discover hundreds of new and unusual animals every year).
  4. ex.4, pairs, 3 minutes — the six highlighted words. Key: highlighted words: 1 harmless (not dangerous) · 2 poison (a substance that can kill you) · 3 blind (unable to see) · 4 pretty (quite, but not completely) · 5 the ground (the surface of the earth) · 6 shape (the physical form).
  5. ex.5 triaged — items 1, 4 and 6 in class (pressure, loris, pufferfish), the rest for fast finishers. Key: detail: 1 at 1,000 m the water pressure pushes the blobfish into more of a fish shape · 2 the lamprey drinks the blood of another fish · 3 the mole's tentacles feel for worms · 4 the loris bites — poison in its mouth · 5 the frog carries poison on its back · 6 people eat pufferfish because it tastes delicious.
  6. Close with one Talking point: Why have we discovered only about 20% of the world's animals and plants? · What other weird-looking or dangerous animals can you think of? — TB extra: class votes for the most dangerous and the weirdest-looking
📺 On screen: track 58 is the recording of the text — optional (Backup: skip the audio entirely; the stage is built on silent reading)
👦 Students do: match, place the sentences with reasons, mine the words, argue the detail. Expect paragraph B's sentence («funny-looking») to be resisted — the funny=strange note wins the argument; expect the pufferfish facts to be retold in Russian from excitement — ask for the English version and get it. Typical problem: reading every word of every creature — the paragraph question is the shield

Grammar · deduction
class → pairs
Why: The stick-insect dialogue IS the grammar: possible, certain, impossible — three degrees extracted from a photo argument. The Russian «не может быть» trap (mustn't for can't) gets its own CCQ because it will otherwise survive the lesson.

WHAT WE DO: the stick insect: possible, certain, impossible

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB warmer, one minute: TB grammar warmer: a wrapped box with a name tag on the desk; on the board It can't be… ✗ / It could·might be… ?? / It must be… ✓ — the class deduces what is inside and who it is for.
  2. ex.1 — the photo dialogue: read it in two voices with a student, then pairs match the modals to rules a–c. Key: rules: a might/could (possible) · b must (certain) · c can't (impossible). The photo is a stick insect — reveal it with ceremony.
  3. Concept checks from the plan header — six legs, «не может быть», and the fifty-percent might, cold-called by name.
  4. The trap gets its minute: Mustn't is a RULE — you mustn't run in the corridor. Impossible is CAN'T. In this unit, mustn't is banned. Write «mustn't ≠ deduction» under the card.
📺 On screen: the three-line card is already on the board from minute 5 — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read, match, answer the CCQs, absorb the ban. Expect «why banned?» — because Russian «не может быть» will push it forever; name the interference honestly, they respect it. Typical error: it mustn't be — the card answers silently
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit12_Grammar_Probability_Modals_A5
A5 grammar card — HOW SURE ARE YOU?: a 100 % → 0 % scale with MUST (I'm almost sure it is true: It must be a wolf.) · MIGHT (it is possible: It might be a fox.) · CAN'T (I'm almost sure it is impossible: It can't be a shark — we're in a forest!), the formula must / might / can't + BASE VERB (most often be), the EVIDENCE CLUES strip (paw prints · sound · habitat) and the CRITICAL NOTE that this must means deduction, not obligation
  • Glue the card in and mark the three points on the certainty scale.
  • Highlight the critical note — this must is not the must of rules.
  • Play deduction in pairs: one gives a clue, the other guesses with all three modals.
  • Every guess needs its evidence: It must be…, because…
When: ~5 min at the start of the “Grammar · deduction” stagePrep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Scissors, glue sticks, pencils.

Grammar · controlled choice
pairs
Why: Choose-and-justify practice: the deciding question is said out loud every time (planned? evidence? right now?). The justification is what transfers to new sentences.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2: eight choices with the evidence read aloud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2 solo, 4 minutes — choose the modal; the sentence's own evidence decides (flew onto the roof · hasn't moved all day). Key: choose: 1 can't · 2 could · 3 might · 4 could · 5 might · 6 must · 7 can't · 8 must.
  2. Pair check as Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the evidence, not the answer — What did you SEE in the sentence?
  3. Kagan Showdown, three calls: you give evidence (It's got fur and it says miaow. · It's 40 metres long. · There's a shadow under the water.), teams write a full deduction and show on three.
  4. Every mustn't sighting goes on your silent log — and gets the card point, silently.
📺 On screen: calls by voice, answers on mini-whiteboards — voice and boards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: choose by evidence, justify, commit in writing. Expect items 4 and 5 to split pairs (could vs might — both fine; say so, the overlap is real). Typical error: it must to be — bare infinitive, one choral fix

Sound deduction
groups
Why: Eight mystery sounds turn the modals into reflexes: nobody can deduce from a sound without might/must/can't. It is also the stage that proves the grammar is for something.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 (track 59): eight mystery sounds, deduced out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Groups of three-four. Eight sounds. After each one: one can't, one might, one must — with because.
  2. Play track 59, pausing after each sound; groups confer 20 seconds, one group reports per item. Key: track 59 — eight sounds, groups deduce: 1 a tiger · 2 birds · 3 a basketball game · 4 an egg frying · 5 heavy rain · 6 a motorboat · 7 a plane taking off · 8 an audience clapping and cheering. Model: It can't be a bee — a bee is too small. It must be a tiger, because it sounds like a big cat
  3. Model the chain once, on sound 1: It can't be a bee — a bee is too small. It must be a tiger, because it sounds like a big cat.
  4. Score nothing; applaud the best BECAUSE of each round.
📺 On screen: track 59 in the whiteboard app (Backup: the sounds cannot be read aloud — skip ex.3 and run ex.4's macro photos for the extra six minutes instead; the same deduction chains over pictures instead of sounds)
👦 Students do: listen, confer, deduce in chains. Expect the frying egg and the motorboat to produce wild guesses — wild is fine if the frame is right. Typical error: evidence dropped in the excitement — the because-applause keeps it alive
SB p.70 Grammar ex.3 · track 59
68–80′
🎯 Speaking out · the deduction panel
teams → class
Why: The productive outcome: four macro photographs, teams building chains of evidence out loud (it can't be… so it must be…), and a class vote on the most convincing chain — deduction with an audience.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: four macro photos, chains of evidence, a class vote

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four macro photos (ex.4), one per team, 3 minutes: build a chain of at least three links — It can't be…, because… · It might be…, because… · So it must be…!
  2. Kagan Numbered Heads Together: every member must be able to deliver the chain; you call the number.
  3. Called members deliver; the class votes for the most convincing CHAIN (not the right answer). Then reveal: macro photos A–D: A part of a butterfly's wing · B the eye of a donkey · C a sheep · D parrot feathers — class votes for the most convincing deduction
  4. Point at the winning chain's because: That's the whole grammar. Evidence, then the verdict.
📺 On screen: the four macro photos are on SB p.70 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: build chains, rehearse every member, deliver, vote. Expect the butterfly wing to fool everyone — perfect: a wrong answer with a great chain WINS the vote, and that is the lesson. Typical error: chains collapsing to one link under nerves — the team rehearsal minute exists for it

Structure

Form
must / might / could / can't + bare infinitive; deduction ≠ prohibition: impossible = can't (never mustn't); evidence attached with so / because.
Pron. watch
mosquito /mɒˈskiːtəʊ/ · wolf /wʊlf/ (plural wolves!) · worm /wɜːm/ · eagle /ˈiːɡl/ · the four insects of the set: ant, bee, fly, mosquito
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«It must be an insect — it's got six legs» — am I guessing or concluding? → concluding from evidence: six legs.
«Этого не может быть» — mustn't or can't? → CAN'T. Mustn't is a rule (don't do it!), never a deduction.
«It might be a fox» — how sure am I? → maybe 50%: possible, not certain.

«it mustn't be…» for impossibilityFront-load the trap: the CCQ, the board card (✗ can't · ?? might/could · ✓ must) and a choral fix the FIRST time it appears. Every later slip gets a silent point at the card.
Animal words guessed loudly in Russian during the quizThe quiz rule: an answer only counts written in English on the team sheet. The letters A–D make it fair for weaker spellers — the animal word is a bonus, not the gate.
The reading's six creatures overload weaker readersThe paragraph-first task is the shield: nobody needs every word, only the point of A, B and C. The detail questions are triaged — three in class, three for fast finishers.
Deductions arrive without evidence («It must be a cat.»)The frame is compulsory and visible: … , so it must be… / It can't be…, because… . An unfinished frame is an unfinished answer — hand it back with a smile.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
