🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The unit project is a digital presentation about a hero from the past, and the Super skill here is not “creativity in general” but a specific skill: asking for feedback, hearing it and reworking your own piece. That is why the lesson has a compulsory “slide surgery” round BEFORE the presentation: the crew gets three concrete questions from their neighbours and has to make at least one change. Quick review p.93 is not a separate block but the gate: an audit of your own hero against the unit checklist.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Last mission of the unit. Four slides about a hero from the past — and one of them has to end with their own words. Katherine Johnson worked at NASA when there were no computers; people called her 'the human computer'. Her last slide is one sentence: 'Girls are capable of doing everything men are capable of doing.' That's the standard. Build it, show it to another crew, take their questions — and change at least one thing before you present. Feedback isn't an insult. It's a free upgrade.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the final mission of the unit: four slides about a hero from the past, the last one with their own quote (as with Katherine Johnson). Before presenting there is a compulsory feedback round, and at least one change has to be made.

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: an audit of your own hero against Quick review p.93 — what I already have and what is missing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

🔥 Challenge · slide surgery

WHAT WE DO: ex.7–8 Super skills: three written questions about your own work → the feedback round → at least one edit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. In writing first (TB routine): Write THREE questions about your own work: 'Do you think I have enough about her early life?' 'Is it clear why he's a hero?' 'Is there too much on this slide?'
  2. The Super skills Key phrases onto the board: I really like the part about … · How about including … ? · Why don't you … ? · This part is really clear. · What do you think about … ? · I didn't really understand …
  3. The rule of the round (TB requirement): You may say something negative — but politely, and always with a suggestion.
  4. Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: two minutes per crew, then move round.
  5. The compulsory action: Change at least ONE thing. Write down what you changed — you'll say it out loud.
📺 On screen: Super skills phrase cards · 2-minute timer (Backup: write the six phrases large on the board and leave them up to the end of the lesson)
👦 Students do: students ask questions and edit their work. Expect: the edits are small but real — usually they cut text and add a date

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can build and deliver a four-slide presentation about a hero from the past (who they were · early life · work · why a hero) and give and take focused feedback with the Super skills phrases.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the Katherine Johnson video; ex.2–3 taking the Model project apart; ex.4–5 the list of heroes and their own choice; ex.6 building it with the How to… tips p.121; ex.7 Super skills; ex.8 rehearsal with edits; ex.9–10 presentation and peer review; Quick review p.93; Final reflection.
  • Personal. Watch that it stays constructive: criticism is allowed, but politely and always with a suggestion for the fix (the TB requirement). Send any slide with long sentences back to be redone.
By the end, students can…
  • choose a hero from the past and say why they matter
  • put information on four slides in note form, not sentences
  • ask a focused question about someone else's work
  • change my own work after feedback and say what I changed

Structure
She was a mathematician and a space scientist. · She started work at NASA in 1953. · I really like the part about … · How about including … ? · 🚀 bonus: Why don't you add a date here?
Form
the language of a slide is notes, not sentences: “1918 – born in West Virginia”, “went to university – studied maths and French”. The spoken presentation runs in full past simple sentences.
Pron. watch
the quote on the final slide is read more slowly and more quietly than everything else — that is the trick that holds a room. Rehearse exactly that.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Long sentences on a slide — why not? → they are hard to read while somebody is talking (the TB question).
Feedback like “I didn't like it” — what is wrong with it? → there is no suggestion for the fix.
“Katherine Johnson was born in 1918” — slide or speech? → on the slide “1918 — born”, in the speech a full sentence.

Assumptions: SB pp.93–95, syllabus pair 60. The biography drafts from P6 are in hand — they can be turned into the presentation. The technology (laptop/phone) can be let down by the internet — so a paper version of the slides counts equally.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
