🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The topic is named openly again: questions in the past simple, and ago. The discovery goes through the book's tables, which we fill in together, and through the podcast from the last two lessons — the ex.2 questions are built on it, so the content is already familiar and the brain is busy with the form only. Everything is written before it is spoken: first we build the questions on paper, then we go and interview.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today's topic is on the board: questions about the past. You already know how to SAY what happened. Now you learn to ASK. There's one rule and it saves you every time: the past sits in DID, so the verb stays clean. 'What did you eat?' — not 'ate'. And one small word for time: ago. Ten minutes ago. A year ago. At the end you interview your partner about their real yesterday — with your questions written down first.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the topic is announced openly: questions in the past simple and the word ago. One rule: the past sits in did, so the verb stays clean. First we write the questions on paper, then we interview a partner.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: written first: five questions of their own, then the interview and reporting one answer to the class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can build Yes/No and Wh- questions in the past simple with short answers and use ago correctly in questions and answers.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the two tables; ex.2 questions on the P4 podcast; ex.3 rewriting five sentences; ex.4 pair interview; ex.5 time words; ex.6 the ago circles; ex.7 Grammar round-up with support.
  • Personal. The switch gesture from P3 works here too: one hand on did — “the past is here”, the other opens — “the verb is clean”. The question-formula poster goes up next to the verb poster.
By the end, students can…
  • build a Yes/No question with Did and answer it short
  • build a Wh- question with a question word + did
  • keep the verb clean after did
  • use ago after a period of time (two days ago)

Target language
Lexis
Did … ?What did … ?When did … ?How did … ?How much did … ?Who did … ?agosecondminutehourdayweekmonthyear
Structure
Did it cost a lot? — Yes, it did. · Did she speak English? — No, she didn't. · What did Easton design? · When did they move? — A year ago.
Form
(question word) + did + who + the infinitive of the verb. The past sits in did. ago always comes AFTER a period of time and has no plural.
Pron. watch
did in a question is unstressed and runs together: “What-d-you say?”; in a short answer it is the opposite, stressed: “Yes, I DID.” We drill both chorally.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“What did you ate?” — where is the mistake? → did is already past: What did you EAT?
“When did you born?” → this one takes be: When WERE you born?
“ago ten minutes” — is that right? → no: ten minutes AGO.

Assumptions: SB p.90, syllabus pair 58. The irregular forms were set in P3 (poster on the wall), the P4 podcast is fresh — the ex.2 questions are built on it.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“Did you saw?” — a double pastThe switch gesture from P3 + the question-formula poster on the wall. I correct with the gesture, not with a word.
Word order falls apartThe formula on the poster is broken into four cubes: [question word] [did] [who] [verb]. Students build the question by physically moving the cube cards.
ago is stuck anywhereSupport on the board: [a period of time] + ago. I check with my finger: ago is always on the right. And no “agos”.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
