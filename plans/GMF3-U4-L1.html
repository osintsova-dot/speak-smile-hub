Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name 12 food items / pizza toppings and say what is on a pizza: “Do you like (onions) on your pizza? — Yes, I do. It's delicious. / No, I don't.” and “I've got (olives) and (cheese) on my pizza.”
  • Subsidiary. Sing «Let's make a pizza» (CD2 19) & the vocabulary chant (CD2 18); brainstorm toppings (act. 1); preview the L2 request “I'd like some (cheese)”; early literacy — read the food words and find them in a word-search (AB p.38).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; stirrer→settler rhythm — topping-mime is high-energy, plan the freezes. Notice countable/uncountable gently (some cheese · some onions) — full quantifier work is L2.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name the 12 food items / pizza toppings;
  • say “Do you like … on your pizza? — Yes, I do / No, I don't”;
  • read the food words and match word ↔ picture.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Do you like tuna on your pizza — am I asking about your FAVOURITE or the toppings on a pizza?” → toppings you like on a pizza.
“olives — one olive or lots on a pizza?” → lots! (many little ones).

Assumptions: Pupils know food words from GMF2 and fruit / vegetables from Unit 3 (healthy eating). The 12 toppings are largely NEW; Do you like…? is known — production of the pizza-topping phrase is today's stretch. Readers — word cards work alongside pictures.

10–20′
Presentation · 12 toppings
carpet → board

Present with flashcards + chant (act. 2, CD2 18). Each card: What's this? It's… cheese! Choral ×3 + one gesture (cheese = triangle hands, mushrooms = umbrella, chillies = fan your hot mouth). Contrast pair held together: ONIONS — OLIVES!

Do you like … on your pizza? (act. 4). Board frame: Do you like onions on your pizza? — Yes, I do. It's delicious! / No, I don't. Hold each card — thumbs up/down round the circle, then the full answer.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: Stand up if you like this topping… pineapple! — flash cards fast, everyone moves every time.

💪 Support: answer with the gesture + thumb. ⭐ Extension: full answer — Yes, I do. It's delicious!

28–36′
Word ↔ picture · Memory
carpet, pairs

▶ Memory · I've got … — in pairs. Each pair: 6 picture cards + 6 WORD cards (readers!) face-down in two rows. Turn one of each; a match is claimed only with a sentence: I've got cheese on my pizza! Wrong/no sentence → cards go back. Most pairs wins.

ICQ: Can you keep the cards if you only say the word? (→ no — a whole sentence: I've got cheese!)

✔ Assess: stand behind one pair at a time — are they reading the word card, not just guessing?

 6+6 cards per pair
36–44′
Topping mime & guess
carpet, 2 teams

▶ Slow-motion topping mime. One player mimes eating/adding a topping (fan mouth = chillies, peel = pineapple); their team guesses together: It's chillies! Mimer confirms. Point per correct guess; rotate the mimer every round — everyone mimes once.

Whole team must guess together — no solo shouting: One voice, one team!

desks

Speak. ▶ RallyRobin: Take turns: On my pizza I've got… — three toppings each.

Write (notebooks / AB p.38 format). Board frame: I've got ___, ___ and ___ on my pizza. Each child draws a quick pizza and writes 3–4 toppings. Fast finishers add Do you like…?

💪 Support: copy the frame + choose from 3 word cards. ⭐ Extension: add an opinion: It's delicious!
