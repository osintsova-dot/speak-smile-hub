🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that the vocabulary and grammar of two units work without the book: thirty-nine words, the reflexive pronouns and the first conditional.
What is genuinely hard
The first conditional. Everything else in this block is knowledge that either is or is not there; the conditional is a habit fighting a Russian one, and it breaks under time pressure even when it is solid in an exercise.
Typical error to expect
If I will go, I will play with my friends — will in the if clause. English puts the present simple after if and will only in the other half (SB p.103 ex.5) [high priority, A2/B1]
I hurt me / She saw her in the mirror instead of myself, herself — the object pronoun where the subject and the object are the same person [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built as retrieval practice, not re-teaching: the two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it and what shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which form still breaks. The book's Review 5 covers Units 17–20 and half of it needs the passive, which is block 10 — so the stations take ONLY the items that are ours and the page itself is left for lesson 68, where it belongs. The game is chosen for what it does to the room rather than for what it revises: it has no right answers in it, which is the safest possible thing to put in front of a nervous class twenty minutes before a test. The specification goes on the board before the paper is handed out, because a test you can see the shape of is a test and a test you cannot is a threat.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two units, ninety minutes, and nothing new. First forty-five minutes you play it back — words, pronouns, conditionals, and a game where there are no wrong answers at all. Then forty-five minutes you show it. The whole test is on the board before you start.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast round of the block's words, and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — block rewind. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade as a hot round: cards for all thirty-nine words of the block, two trades each. Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest with them: Everything you just said is in today's test.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show. And the whole test goes on the board before you start.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, read the words out from a list and students write the translation in pairs
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety dropping. Typical problem: they stumble on embarrassed and on chapter

WHAT WE DO: the Units 17–18 items of Review 5 Grammar (SB p.118) — reflexives and the conditional

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Grammar ex.1, items 1 and 2 only: choose the correct option. 1 yourself · 2 is · 5 I bought a blue shirt for myself which is very nice · 6 If you like, we'll go by car · 7 You'll like the class if you come · 8 You need to bring a photo of yourself with your name on the back. Items 3 and 4 are the passive, which is block 10
  2. Then items 5, 6, 7 and 8 of the same exercise: correct the mistakes. Items 3 and 4 are the passive — skip them and say why.
  3. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the reason, not the answer — Same person or different person? for the reflexives, Which half has the future in it? for the conditionals.
  4. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Sentence seven — number twos, boards up!
  5. Boil every explanation down to one line and leave the four lines on the board: same person → ‑self; alone → by + ‑self; future in one half only; comma only when the sentence starts with if.
📺 On screen: the Review 5 grammar exercise on the screen, one item at a time, with items 3 and 4 skipped (Backup: it is printed on SB p.118 — uncover the items on the page with a ruler and keep the four one-line summaries in chalk on the board)
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with an explanation, then report by number. Expect the reflexives to be cleaner than the conditionals, because the conditional is two lessons newer and fighting a translation. Typical error: item 6 (we go by car) left as it is — they read it as a present habit

Review game
groups of 4
Why: A game that hides revision inside a reason to write; nerves before a test drop when the last thing they did was laugh at their own sentence.

WHAT WE DO: the game «Crazy Sentences! (Review game Units 17–20, Cambridge, PHOTOCOPIABLE)» — three blanks per card, folded and passed on, and nobody sees what anybody else wrote

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Fourteen cards, each with three gapped lines. Groups of three or more. One player takes a card and reads out the first line — «Please bring back my ___,» — and everyone secretly writes a word at the top of their sheet, folds the paper over so it cannot be seen, and passes it to the left. The second line is read, everyone writes, folds and passes again; then the third. When all three blanks are filled the sheets are unfolded and read out, and the group decides which version is the funniest.
  2. ⚠️ Important: ⚠️ The pack covers four units and half the cards need Units 19–20 language that has not been taught. Ours are eight cards: 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12 and 14 — «Please bring back my ___» (phrasal verbs) · «The author wrote a book about ___ with lots of drawings of ___» (book words) · «If you come to the ___ tonight, I'll help you make your ___» (first conditional) · «The lonely old ___ wanted to find out about ___» (emotions + find out) · «Are you enjoying yourself? asked the angry ___» (reflexives + emotions) · «If you don't give back my ___, I'll ___» (first conditional + phrasal verb) · «When the ___ man found a ___ in her stomach, he took it out» (body parts + take out) · «You will enjoy yourself if you ___ every day» (reflexives + conditional + confident). Pull cards 3, 4, 6, 10, 11 and 13 out before you photocopy — they need the passive, cooking verbs, ingredients and get married, which are block 10.
  3. Why this game today, said out loud if anyone asks: The book's own rule: there is no right answer, only a word that fits the blank grammatically. That is what makes it safe for the weakest student in the room the lesson before a test.
  4. Scoring, if you want it competitive: There is no scoring in the book's version. If you want it competitive, each group reads out one sentence at the end and you award points for originality, imagination, humour, good vocabulary, effort, spelling and grammar — your judgement, said out loud so it does not look arbitrary.
  5. Follow-up if a group finishes early: they take the funniest of their sentences and make it grammatical, which is a quiet grammar exercise wearing a joke.
📺 On screen: one printed set of sentence cards per group, run off in advance and cut along the dotted line, plus a blank sheet of A4 per student — paper is the medium here, so no backup is needed; with no printer, read the card lines out yourself and let the whole class fold and pass
👦 Students do: write, fold, pass and then read out. Expect the loudest three minutes of the block and a noticeable drop in test nerves. Typical error: a word that does not fit the blank grammatically — that is the only thing the group is allowed to reject

Test briefing

Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions for the test

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go back to the «watch out» column on the board: the five or six words that were misspelt. Look at these for thirty seconds. Then I close them.
  2. Instructions: 30 minutes, six parts, 52 marks; phones in the bag; finished early — three conditional sentences on the back. Part 6 is done TOGETHER at the start: it is the pronunciation part and it runs once for everybody.
  3. Say what part 6 is before the paper goes out, because there is no recording for it: I read eight words. You circle A for the sound in «food» or B for the sound in «good». Twice each. That is the only part with sound in it.
  4. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  5. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; everything else in the room is switched off so there is nothing to look at
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect the pronunciation instruction to need repeating once — it is the only part that is not on paper

Block test

Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats. Everything on the paper has been done in this room, and the specification was on the board before they started.

WHAT WE DO: the block 9 test: six parts, 52 marks, 30 minutes, plus eight minutes of settling, the pronunciation part and collection

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. You have thirty minutes. Start.
  2. Part 6 (pronunciation) comes first and you read it: eight words, twice each, clearly, in order — blue, put, school, could, two, wood, room, woman. 3 minutes, after which students work on their own.
  3. Walk round the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  4. Write the time left in chalk: 15 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  5. Anyone who finishes early writes three first-conditional sentences on the back and sits quietly.
  6. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling — and check every conditional has the future in one half only.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board and nothing else — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed; the pronunciation part is read aloud by you, which is why this paper needs no technology at all
👦 Students do: work in silence for 30 minutes. Expect the vocabulary parts to close fast and part 5 (the conditional) to take a third of the time. Typical error: they repair a conditional by adding will to both halves, which is the original mistake again

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework, and the bridge into block 10

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip of paper, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the feedback lesson.
  3. Homework: rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly once the papers come back; for now — the Vocabulary or Grammar worksheet Unit 17–18 (Standard or Plus, teacher's call) if anything is still open.
  4. Say the next step plainly: Next lesson is the last exam lesson of the year — talking about pictures, with a partner and then without one. Bring your voice.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is already full, simply read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to be about the conditional most often — that is the plan for the next block's warmers

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
