🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): With newcomers I name the grammar openly — they need clarity, not intrigue. The topic is announced, the table is built together, and every rule gets its own colour and its own place on the poster. Three mini-tests with self-check along the way: the error is found privately and repaired straight away. The product is six lines about your own fridge — safe, and about yourself.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today we learn one thing that English does and Russian doesn't. Look: one egg, two eggs, three eggs — you can count them. Now: milk. One milk? Two milks? No. In English milk doesn't count, and that changes the words around it. We'll build one table together, put it on the wall, and by the end each of you writes six lines about your own fridge: what's there and what isn't. Step by step, with the table in front of you.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: today the topic is named straight out: some words can be counted (one egg, two eggs), others cannot (milk), and that changes the words around them. We build one table, put it up on the wall, and at the end everyone writes six lines about their own fridge.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 the table is built together in colours → mini-test 1 with self-check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  4. Circle what you missed. Look at the poster — which colour did you forget?

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 (track 32) with pauses → ex.4–5 their own answers to the same questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Then Kagan RallyCoach: in the pair the first one picks the word out loud and says WHY (“it's a negative, so any”), the second looks at the three-colour poster, confirms or repairs it, and praises. WHAT WE DO: the ladder: a drawing of the fridge → notes → 6 lines from the frame → reading in the crew

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1: Draw your fridge — six things inside. Just words.
  2. Step 2 — the frame: In my fridge there's a … . There are two … . There's some … . There isn't any … . There aren't any … .
  3. Step 3: they run the checklist themselves (a number → are? a negative → any? uncountable → no a?).
  4. Step 4 — Kagan RoundRobin: round the crew clockwise, everyone reads their six lines off the sheet, nobody interrupts. The crew notes who matches their own fridge and where.
  5. Volunteers to the board with the sheet: Three lines each. Class: whose fridge is most like yours?
📺 On screen: the frame · the poster · the checklist · timer
👦 Students do: they write up the ladder with supports. Expect: everyone has 6 lines; the drawing helps a lot

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can tell countable from uncountable nouns, use a/an, some, any and there is/isn't, there are/aren't to write 6 sentences about their own fridge.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 sorting with a prompt sheet; ex.2 the table built together; ex.3 the Jonah interview (track 32) with pauses; ex.6 the there is/are table; ex.7 the lunchbox; ex.8 Bento box; ex.9 Brain teaser as a bonus.
  • Personal. I name the grammar as the topic. We build the poster together and hang it up — it stays on the wall until the end of the unit. Self-checked mini-tests after each block.
By the end, students can…
  • say if a noun is countable or uncountable
  • use a/an, some and any correctly
  • use there is/isn't and there are/aren't
  • write 6 sentences about what is and isn't in my fridge

Structure
Singular countable: a small cake / — · Uncountable: some beef — not any milk · (no article: You eat it with rice.) · Plural: some chips — not any apples · two bananas · There is a wonderful smell. — There isn't a better snack. · There is some fantastic food. — There isn't any milk. · There are some great food stalls. — There aren't any chips.
Form
singular countable — a/an: Can I have a banana, please? · uncountable — some (+), any (−/?) or no article at all: I usually have some milk. · We haven't got any butter. · We often have pasta for dinner. · plural — some/any + noun, or a number: some chips · any apples · two bananas · there is/isn't — before singular and uncountable; there are/aren't — before plural
Pron. watch
there's — run together /ðeəz/ · isn't /ˈɪz(ə)nt/ and aren't /ɑːnt/ — the final t is audible
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“There is three bottles of water” — what's wrong? → it's plural: There ARE three bottles.
“There aren't some strawberries” — correct it. → any in the negative: There aren't any strawberries.
“There isn't milk” — what's missing? → any: There isn't any milk.

Assumptions: SB p.64, KTP pair 40 (grammar in both halves). there is/are may have come up before, but it has never been dealt with systematically; countable/uncountable is new.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“I don't get why milk can't be counted”I show it physically: two eggs on the table — you can see two. Milk in a glass — we count the glasses, not the milk. TB technique: “do you need a bottle of / a bowl of? then it doesn't count on its own”.
They confuse some and anyThree colours on the poster: green + = some · red − = any · yellow ? = any. The poster stays up until the end of the unit.
Russian logic: “bread is one thing”The TB list goes on the poster: advice, bread, furniture, information, luggage, news, traffic, weather, work — “in English these are never counted”.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
