🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The past simple of regular verbs is hidden inside a chronicle: the crew keeps a record of the month — what it did and what it did NOT do. The form becomes the price of an entry: a wrong ending or a wrong sound (/d/ /t/ /ɪd/) and the entry is rejected. The spelling rules from p.81 are drilled as 'the chronicler's handwriting', and the Grammar round-up about 'Crocodiles of the World' as the final accreditation. The topic of the lesson is the chronicle, not '-ed'.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Every serious organisation keeps a chronicle. What we did. What we didn't do. With dates. Your crew writes the chronicle of the last month — five entries, at least two of them negative, all with real dates: last weekend, in March, yesterday. Two rules from the archive: the ending must be right on paper, and it must SOUND right — played is /d/, watched is /t/, visited is /ɪd/. Wrong ending, wrong sound — the entry doesn't go in. Chronicles open!”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the crew keeps a chronicle of the month: five entries — what they did and what they did not do, with dates. An entry is accepted only if the ending is right AND sounds right (/d/, /t/, /ɪd/).

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 p.78 (they work out the two rules themselves) → the spelling rules p.81 → ex.2 eight verbs → a team spelling game, Showdown on mini-whiteboards (TB technique)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: Look at the four examples. How do we make the affirmative? And the negative? (TB key: 1 -ed · 2 didn't). They formulate the rule.
  2. TB technique on the board: write She didn't visited her grandparents and cross the -ed out in chalk. The -ed turns back into an infinitive.
  3. Take the spelling rules on p.81 as four gates: + -ed · + -d · y → -ied · doubling the consonant.
  4. ex.2 as a race (TB key: finished, liked, organised, played, studied, travelled, visited, watched).
  5. The TB game through Kagan Showdown: show a card with the infinitive — everybody writes the form silently, each on their own mini-whiteboard, and on the command Showdown! they raise them together. If the crew matches — a point; an error and the turn and the point go to the next crew.

👦 Students do: build the rules and compete on spelling. Expect: travel → travelled needs a separate note (the British spelling)

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 six sentences in the affirmative/negative through RallyCoach + ex.4 their own true sentences on an 8-second Showdown conveyor

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3 through Kagan RallyCoach, 4 minutes: in a pair inside the crew the first student solves the sentence out loud and says WHY (affirmative or didn't), the second is the coach: checks it against the rule, catches -ed after didn't and praises; on the next sentence they swap roles. TB key: 1 didn't finish · 2 watched · 3 didn't play · 4 didn't visit · 5 didn't like / liked · 6 organised.
  2. The Time expressions box is covered right here: yesterday · last night / last weekend · in August / in 2017. They can stand at the beginning OR at the end.
  3. ex.4 on an 8-second conveyor — Kagan Showdown, six rounds: you give a prompt, everybody writes their own TRUE sentence and raises the boards together: watch TV / last night → I watched TV last night. OR I didn't watch TV last night. Boards up!
  4. The constraint: say -ed after didn't and the crew's turn burns.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can form and use the past simple of regular verbs (affirmative with correct spelling, negative with didn't + infinitive), sort -ed endings into /d/ /t/ /ɪd/, and write and deliver a five-entry chronicle with past time expressions.
  • Subsidiary. The rules in ex.1 are drawn out of the examples; the spelling in ex.2 follows the rules on p.81; ex.3 affirmative/negative against the clock; ex.4 their own true sentences; the Irregular verbs box (went, saw, had, met); Grammar round-up ex.5; Pronunciation p.117 (tracks 72–75).
  • Personal. The grammar is never announced as the topic of the lesson. Watch especially for the fact that there is NO ending after didn't — TB technique: write 'She didn't visited' on the board and cross the -ed out in chalk. Log the errors for the clinic in the next lesson.
By the end, students can…
  • spell the past simple of 8 regular verbs correctly
  • use didn't + infinitive without -ed
  • sort -ed endings into /d/, /t/ and /ɪd/
  • write five chronicle entries with past time expressions

Structure
She started to sell the drink. · She visited the White House. · She didn't like bees. · She didn't stop there. · yesterday · last night, last Thursday, last weekend · in August, in 2017
Form
affirmative form: verb + -ed (TB key ex.1: -ed) · negative form: didn't + infinitive WITHOUT -ed (TB key ex.1: didn't) · most verbs + -ed: visit ➞ visited · verbs in -e ➞ + -d: receive ➞ received · consonant + -y ➞ y + -ied: carry ➞ carried · consonant-vowel-consonant ➞ double the last consonant + -ed: stop ➞ stopped, travel ➞ travelled · irregular: go ➞ went · see ➞ saw · have ➞ had · meet ➞ met (Irregular verbs box, SB p.78)
Pron. watch
SB p.117: three ways to say -ed — /d/ (played, listened, received, travelled) · /t/ (watched, finished, helped, stopped) · /ɪd/ (visited, decided, needed, started). TB key for ex.3: /ɪd/ comes after the letters t and d.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«She didn't visited the park» — what is one word too many? → -ed: didn't VISIT (didn't already shows the past).
«He stoped» — how many p? → stopped: consonant-vowel-consonant → we double it.
«I studyed» — correct it. → studied: consonant + y → -ied.

Assumptions: SB p.78 + Pronunciation p.117, syllabus pair 50. Present simple and the rule 'auxiliary + bare verb' are familiar from A1+; what is new is the -ed spelling, the three ways of reading the ending, and the speed.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
