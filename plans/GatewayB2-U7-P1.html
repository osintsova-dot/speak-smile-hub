🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the vocabulary of film, fiction and music (genres · plot, screenplay, soundtrack, sequel, box office, audience, critics · lyrics, gig, track, chart, release, tour) and thirteen adjectives that carry an opinion (gripping, clichéd, predictable, stunning, well-produced) to describe and judge something you have watched, read or listened to.
What is genuinely hard
The words are not hard to understand — they are hard to RETRIEVE. A B2 student who knows «plot» passively still says «the story of the film» in speech, and a student with no adjectives says «interesting» four times in one minute. The second difficulty is the reading task: page 85 demands answers in the student's OWN words, and the instinct of every teenager is to copy the sentence from the text.
Typical error to expect
«I think it is good. It is interesting.» — an opinion with no example under it is a two-mark answer in both the oral and the essay. Every claim in this unit is followed by For instance… / Just to give you an idea… / You only have to think of…
Copying the text. The Teacher's Book says it explicitly: an answer that substantially repeats the words of the text is not accepted. That is also how the Russian and the Cambridge examiners mark it.
Why the lesson is built this way
The vocabulary arrives inside two real texts rather than as a list: an Irish punk band who went to number five on vinyl, and the actor Cillian Murphy, who plays the villain in a horror sequel and refuses to say a word about it. Both are Culture exchange texts, so the words are learnt with a fact attached — which is what makes them come back later. The reading is a newspaper article about vending machines that print free short stories in one, three or five minutes, and it does two jobs at once: it is genuinely worth arguing about, and it forces the paraphrase discipline the exam pays for. The productive stage is a sixty-second spoken review, so every word learnt today leaves the room in a sentence of the student's own.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“In a station in London there is a machine. You press a button — one minute, three minutes or five minutes — and it prints you a story to read on the train. Free. Today: the English you need to talk about films, books and music like an adult — and then you review something in sixty seconds.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: the thirteen review adjectives sorted with the body, not the pen

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two ends of the room: POSITIVE and NEGATIVE. No middle allowed today.
  2. You read one adjective from ex.4 and everybody walks. Then one student from the winning end uses it in a sentence about a real film. Thirteen adjectives, thirteen walks, thirteen sentences — it takes four minutes at pace.
  3. Answers: positive — amusing, convincing, fast-moving, gripping, hilarious, inspiring, intriguing, moving, realistic, stunning, well-produced · negative — clichéd, predictable. Track 36 gives the pronunciation if you want it.
  4. Finish standing: Choose the five adjectives that would make YOU watch a new film. Tell your neighbour why. Thirty seconds, then sit down.
  5. The strip on the wall gets filled as they sit.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: detailed comprehension where copying the text scores nothing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the rule before they start, in these words: Eight questions. Every answer in YOUR words. If your answer is a sentence copied from the text, it is wrong. Then the ICQ: Do we copy? — No.
  2. Put the three paraphrase tools on the board and leave them there: synonym · opposite + negative · change the word class (he refused → his refusal).
  3. ex.3 in pairs, seven minutes — the Teacher's Book recommends pairs precisely because the difficulty is phrasing, not understanding, and two students phrase better than one.
  4. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: teams of four, everyone must be able to answer, you call a number. For each question take the answer AND the line of the text it came from — that is the proof habit the exam rewards.
  5. Key points to accept: the machine lets you choose length and genre · the Canary Wharf Group installed them after research on lack of reading time · Paresh has no time for long novels and wants something different · Babita Bismal worries about her children's phones, Sam Rankin likes paper · Horowitz wanted to see whether he could write a good very short mystery · fiction helps us feel less stressed · short fiction is easier when time is short.
  6. Refuse copied answers on the spot, politely and consistently: Same idea, different words. Try again.
📺 On screen: the three paraphrase tools written on the board and left there — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: work in pairs on eight detailed questions, then answer under the Numbered Heads rule with the proof line
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 12–18 (multiple choice on a long text). The Russian paper never repeats the wording of the text in the correct option — it paraphrases it. A student who has just spent fourteen minutes forbidden to copy is doing the exam skill directly.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: after the Numbered Heads check, take three of the eight questions and turn them into four-option items on the board, with one option copied word for word from the text (and wrong). Five minutes. Take the time from the words-from-context stage, which runs on four words instead of seven.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4–5: six unknown words guessed from the text, then the Critical thinkers question

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 in pairs, three minutes: guess the underlined words from context, then check in a dictionary — all set (ready and prepared) · give up on (stop hoping and stop doing) · on the lookout for (searching for) · tangible (something you can touch) · whodunnit (a story where you don't know who did it until the end) · notion (an idea, often unusual) · chunk (a large piece).
  2. Fast pairs get the Teacher's Book follow-up: write a gapped sentence for two of the words and swap it with another pair.
  3. ex.5, Critical thinkers, four minutes: In your opinion, should we read more and use our smartphones less? What makes you say that? — pairs first, then the floor Insist on the second half of the routine — What makes you say that? — every single time. That question is the whole thinking skill.
  4. Take three opinions to the floor, and make the third student react to the second rather than start again: Do you agree with what she just said? Why not?

Why: 🎯 Speaking out · the sixty-second review

WHAT WE DO: a personal task that cannot be done without today's vocabulary

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the frame on the board: WHAT it is · WHO is in it / who wrote it · WHAT happens (two sentences, no spoilers) · TWO adjectives from the wall · WOULD you recommend it, and to whom?
  2. Four minutes, silent, individual: make notes for a sixty-second spoken review of the last film, series, book or album you finished. Rule on the board: at least five words from the wall, at least two opinion adjectives, and the word «interesting» is banned.
  3. 🎯 Kagan Timed Pair Share, sixty seconds each way, then swap partners and do it again — the second telling is always twice as fluent, and that is the point of doing it twice.
  4. Kagan RallyCoach in the second round: the listener's only job is to count the wall words on their fingers and report the number afterwards. It makes listening auditable.
  5. Then four reviews to the whole class, chosen by you, not by volunteers. After each one the class votes with thumbs: Would you watch it?
  6. Close the loop with the three titles from the opening: We started the lesson with three films. Would anybody like to change their recommendation now that you have the words?
  7. Correct only vocabulary today — the wall words and the adjectives. Nothing else.
📺 On screen: the five-point frame and the banned word on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone speaks for a full minute twice, and every listener has a counting job
🇬🇧 Cambridge B2 First for Schools: this stage is Speaking Part 1 material (personal questions about films, music and free time). The sixty-second review is longer than Part 1 demands, which is exactly why it works: a student who can talk for a minute gives a comfortable twenty-second answer.

Structure
It's a crime thriller with a fast-moving plot and a stunning soundtrack. · The ending was completely predictable, so I wouldn't recommend it. · The band released their first album on vinyl and it went straight into the charts.
Form
genre + noun (a crime novel, a rom-com film, a rap track) · adjective + noun for judgement (a gripping plot, a clichéd villain) · release / record / download / star are verbs AND nouns — the form changes, the word does not.
Pron. watch
compound NOUNS take the stress on the first word (BLOCKbuster, CLIFFhanger, SOUNDtrack, BOX office) while compound ADJECTIVES take it on the second (best-SELLing, eco-FRIENDly) — that is exactly what track 38 tests · clichéd /ˈkliːʃeɪd/ keeps the French vowel and the class will say «kli-CHED» · genre /ˈʒɒnrə/ · lyrics /ˈlɪrɪks/, not «LYE-rics» · and in the emphatic I really DO think that… the stress lands on do, which is the whole point of the structure
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«The film was predictable.» Did the speaker enjoy it? → no. Is that word positive or negative? → negative. Every adjective on the wall gets that question.
«Sequel» — is it the first film or the second? → the second. And «screenplay»? → the written text of the film, not the story itself.
ICQ before the reading: Do you copy the sentence from the text? — No. Do you use your own words? — Yes. Ask it, do not assume it.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«Interesting» four times in one minuteBan the word out loud, on the board, from minute thirty: The word «interesting» is now illegal in this room. Use the wall. It is the fastest vocabulary intervention there is and students find it funny.
Answers copied straight out of the reading textDo not mark them. Hand the sentence back and say Same idea, different words, and put three tools on the board first: synonym, antonym + negative, change the word class.
Passive knowledge that never becomes speechQuiz-Quiz-Trade with word cards is the answer, not more gap-fill: each student holds one word and has to explain it to three people who then explain theirs. Everyone speaks, nobody waits.
Fast finishers in the vocabulary stageThe Teacher's Book extra activity: write one example for each category on the board (action film, dance music, crime novel) and let them fill it. Or SB p.139 Reach higher, Unit 7, exercise 1 — the odd word out with a written reason.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 10–18 — the whole reading section. Task 10 is the headings task this lesson rehearses on three parallel texts; tasks 12–18 are multiple choice on one long article, which is exactly what page 85 is.

Culture, cinema and books are a standing topic of the Russian paper: the reading section prints an article about them almost every year, and the oral card «your favourite film / book» is permanent. The lexical set that arrives today — plot, screenplay, soundtrack, sequel, box office, audience, critics, lyrics, gig, track, chart — is the exact vocabulary a candidate needs to say anything precise about either. Without it they say «interesting» four times and lose the range mark.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
