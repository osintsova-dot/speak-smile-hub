🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Choose between a / an, the and the zero article using eight rules taken from a text the class has already read — first mention against second mention, only-one-of-it, general plurals, and the geography rules — and apply them in an open cloze (Reading Part 6).
What is genuinely hard
There is nothing to transfer. Russian has no articles at all, so this is not a rule that is applied wrongly, it is a decision that is not made — and it has to be made about once every ten words for the rest of their lives. The two rules that go on breaking after the lesson are the generic plural (people, not the people) and geography, where Australia takes nothing and the Blue Mountains take the.
Typical error to expect
«I went to cinema», «she is doctor», «in city where I live» — Russian has no articles at all, so they are not forgotten, they were never there. Every English noun needs a decision: a, the or nothing — and the decision is made about a hundred times in a hundred-word email.
«The people should take more care of the environment», «I like the films» — the opposite error, and the more advanced one: plural and uncountable nouns used in a general sense take NO article. The exam test has this item twice.
«the Australia», «Volga» without the, «the Mount Everest» — countries, cities, continents, single lakes and single mountains take no article, but groups of mountains, oceans, seas, rivers and plural country names take THE.
Why the lesson is built this way
Every example in exercise 1 comes out of yesterday's reading, so the class is not learning what the sentences mean, only why the article is what it is — and one of those sentences was noticed out loud at the end of the last lesson, deliberately. The rules are matched, not explained. Then the practice moves from choosing to producing in three steps: choose between two, write into a gap with the rule named, and finally an exam cloze where nothing is offered at all. The productive stage is a country fact-file, because geography is the half of the article system that is arbitrary and therefore has to be met, not reasoned.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“The smallest words in English. Your language manages without them completely — and every hundred words you write, you will make about ten decisions about them. Today: eight rules, and by the end you will fill an exam gap with nothing offered to choose from.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, Workbook p.60–61 self-marked, and one student sentence kept on the board for the warmer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what is the best thing about the street you live on? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — Workbook p.60–61 checked from the board, self-marked in pencil. Take questions on TWO items only.
  3. 1 minute — the rewind, and it hands the lesson its first example. Kagan Think-Pair-Share: One sentence: the best thing and the worst thing about the place where you live — use a word from the wall.
  4. Take two aloud and write ONE on the board exactly as said, articles and all. Do not correct it yet. It is the warmer's raw material.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): point at the sentence on the board: Three of the words in this sentence are the smallest words in English. Two of them are missing.

WHAT WE DO: four gapped sentences that build the concept before any rule

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed. On the board, with numbered gaps: There's 1___ new mall in town. She works in 2___ office near here. I saw her in 3___ high street. Have you been to 4___ bank?
  2. Ask what kind of word is missing (articles), then take the four answers WITH reasons: 1 a — first mention, and new starts with a consonant · 2 an — first mention, vowel · 3 the — there is only one · 4 the — one particular bank, and we both know which.
  3. Write the line that stays up all lesson, in a colour: General = NO article.
  4. Say the shape in 20 seconds: eight rules out of yesterday's text, then three kinds of practice, ending with an exam gap where nothing is offered.
  5. Add the pronunciation footnote nobody teaches, ten seconds: the is /ðə/ before a consonant and /ðiː/ before a vowel — /ðə/ city, /ðiː/ open spaces.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the examples matched to the rules, and every example is one they have read

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Point at the source before starting: all seven example sentences come from the Australia texts on p.87. They have read every one of them.
  2. Mixed-ability pairs, four minutes: cover the rules, look at the words in bold, and say WHY each article is what it is. Then do the matching.
  3. Check by reading each sentence aloud and taking the letter: the examples matched to the rules: 1 h · 2 a and c · 3 e · 4 f · 5 d · 6 b · 7 g — and rule h covers the names of beaches too, which is why it is Bondi Beach and not the Bondi Beach
  4. Sentence 2 gets its own minute because it contains both: I didn't go to A school until I was 12. THE nearest school was a six-hour drive away. First mention, then a particular one. This is the pair that explains the system.
  5. Copy the geography rules into notebooks — not read, COPIED: no article for towns, countries, continents, single lakes and single mountains and beaches; the for groups of mountains, oceans and plural country names.
  6. Grammar reference is SB p.161 for anyone who wants it in writing; the answer key is TB p.267.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2: seven items, each one a decision with a nameable rule

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2 individually, three minutes: seven choose-the-option items.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach, three minutes: partner A says the answer AND the rule, partner B checks against the rule list, then they swap. The rule has to be said aloud — that is the whole coaching.
  3. Check: 1 the · 2 the · 3 the · 4 a · 5 the · 6 a · 7 a. For each item take the rule, not just the word.
  4. Item 5 is the one to slow down on — my grandparents have always lived in THE countryside — because Russian speakers expect no article there and it takes one. Add it to the board as an exception worth memorising, along with the cinema, the theatre, the seaside.

Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer where the whole room answers at once

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three corners of the room: A/AN · THE · NOTHING.
  2. You say a phrase with the article missing and everybody walks: ___ Volga · ___ United States · ___ pollution is a problem · she is ___ doctor · ___ Mount Elbrus · I love ___ animals · ___ Pacific Ocean · ___ capital of Russia.
  3. After each walk, one student from the winning corner names the rule in four words.
  4. Eight phrases, eight walks. Then freeze and sit: Exercise three. Australia — and every single gap is geography.

👦 Students do: every student commits with their body eight times and eight students name a rule

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: six sentences about Australia where the article has to be produced

  6. Then the transition question, thirty seconds: If nothing were offered — no options, no words — could you still fill a gap?

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: the Tasmania text, six gaps, nothing offered

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the format and the honest caveat the Teacher's Book gives: In this exam part you write ONE word in each gap and nothing is offered. Today every gap happens to be an article — in the real exam it can be a preposition, a pronoun, an auxiliary or a modal.
  2. One minute before they write: what kinds of word can fill an open cloze? Build the five-category list on the board — it is the strategy for the whole exam part.
  3. ex.4 individually, four minutes, clock visible. Read the whole text first, gaps ignored — that instruction is worth saying twice.
  4. Weaker students get the Teacher's Book support: the six answers written on the board in a random order, so the task becomes matching rather than production.
  5. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Reading Part 6, the Tasmania cloze: 1 the · 2 The / Its · 3 the · 4 a · 5 an · 6 The / Its. For each gap take the rule AND the alternative — gaps 2 and 6 accept Its as well as The, which is a useful thing to notice about open clozes.
  6. Exam information for this part is on SB p.126 if anyone wants it in writing; the teaching notes and key are TB p.253.
📺 On screen: the five-category list of possible words, kept on the board for the rest of the block. Backup: the cloze is on the page and needs nothing else
👦 Students do: read a whole text first, produce six words alone, then justify each with a rule
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE task 11 (inserting missing parts) and tasks 19–25. An open cloze where nothing is offered is the closest thing in the Cambridge paper to the Russian grammar-in-a-text section: the candidate has to decide the CATEGORY of the missing word before anything else.

Why: 🎯 Writing out · the country fact-file

WHAT WE DO: a task where geography forces every article decision

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it in one line: Write a six-sentence fact-file about a country. Every sentence needs at least one article decision, and two of them must be geography.
  2. Model on the board with the class, one sentence: Norway is a country in the north of Europe. It is surrounded by the Norwegian Sea.
  3. Pairs, seven minutes, on half sheets in big letters — one fact-file per pair, country of their choosing, and no two pairs take the same one.
  4. Gallery walk, four minutes, pencil in hand: everyone reads every sheet and puts a small tick over every article they think is right and a question mark over every one they doubt.
  5. Five minutes at the board: take the three most-questioned sentences and repair them as a class, naming the rule each time. This is where the lesson is actually learned.
  6. Keep the sheets — they go back up on the wall for the review lesson.
📺 On screen: the fact-files on the walls with ticks and question marks — paper and pencil only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write six sentences in pairs, audit the whole class's articles with a pencil, then repair three together

Workbook p.62
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can choose a, an, the or no article in a connected text and can name the rule behind the choice — first mention, second mention, only one, general, or geography.
  • Subsidiary. Recycling yesterday's place vocabulary inside the new decision; reading an open cloze for what KIND of word is missing; the two pronunciations of the, /ðə/ and /ðiː/.
  • Personal. One board line stays up the whole lesson and it is the one that costs the most marks: General = NO article. People, films, teenagers, pollution. Every time somebody says the people, point at it and say nothing.
By the end, students can…
  • say I went to A school, then THE school was far away — and know why
  • leave the article out of general plurals and uncountables
  • get the geography right: Australia, the Blue Mountains, the Pacific
  • fill six gaps in an exam cloze with nothing offered

Structure

Form
a / an — first mention, and a person's job · the — second mention, a particular one, the only one, groups of mountains and oceans, plural country names · zero — general plurals and uncountables, towns, countries, continents, single lakes and single mountains, and the names of beaches.
Pron. watch
the compound phrases carry ONE main stress and the class will give them two: AIR conditioning · STREET lights · HEALTH centre · MODern architecture · hisTORic buildings · Open spaces · faCILities /fəˈsɪlətiz/, which is also the hardest of the twelve to say · and the article the has two pronunciations that nobody teaches: /ðə/ before a consonant, /ðiː/ before a vowel — the city, but /ðiː/ open spaces
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I saw a dog. The dog was huge.» Why a, then the? → first mention, then second mention — we both know which dog now.
«People should take care of the environment» — the people or people? → people. Are we talking about particular people? → no, people in general.
«I'm going to Australia and then to the Philippines.» Why does one take the? → plural country name. Same rule as the Blue Mountains and the Pacific Ocean.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The rules are understood in class and gone by FridayThat is normal and it is why the lesson ends with a fact-file and not with a rule box. What survives is the ONE board line — general means no article — plus the geography list, which is copied into notebooks, not just read.
Everything gets theThe generic plural is the fix and it is worth being blunt about: if you can put ALL in front of it, take the article out. All people, all films, all teenagers.
The cloze feels impossible because nothing is offeredBefore they start, one minute on what KIND of word can go in an open cloze — article, preposition, pronoun, auxiliary, modal. Then the gap is a decision between five categories instead of a blank.
Fast finishers finish everything in half the timeSend them to the Grammar reference practice on p.161 and then to the Grammar worksheet Unit 15. Do not let them coach until the RallyCoach stage — it is timetabled.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text) and, more quietly, tasks 37–38, where articles are marked as accuracy.

Articles are not a separate exam item — which is exactly why they are expensive. Russian has none, so an English text written by a Russian candidate loses accuracy marks a few words at a time, in both written tasks, and the loss is invisible to the writer. Today's eight rules are the cheapest accuracy insurance in the course, and the Reading Part 6 gap on this page is where the exam does test them directly.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
