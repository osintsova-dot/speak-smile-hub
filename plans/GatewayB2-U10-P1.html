🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Name twenty-one sections of a news site, decode twenty-two words that only exist in headlines (axe, plea, probe, blaze, wed, PM), state the five grammar rules of headline English, and decide True or False on a text by naming the line that proves it.
What is genuinely hard
Two separate difficulties. The headline words are short, frequent and completely opaque: nothing in Russian tells a student that axe means cut, that a probe is an investigation or that back is a verb meaning support. And headline grammar deletes exactly the words a learner leans on — articles and auxiliaries — then strings three nouns together (World economy collapse fear), so word-by-word reading produces nonsense. The True/False task looks easy and is not: the statements paraphrase, they never repeat.
Typical error to expect
Reading a headline as a normal sentence — «Humans to reach Mars by 2035» is a future, «President shot during speech» is a passive with the auxiliary deleted, «World economy collapse fear» is three nouns in a row. Students translate word by word and produce nonsense; the fix is naming the five rules on p.122 and using them.
Why the lesson is built this way
The vocabulary arrives through the object itself. Three real headlines go on the board before any book is open (ex. warmer), the sections are met as a question — where would you look to read THIS? — and the headline words are matched to their everyday twins rather than translated. Then the five rules are discovered from the five example headlines on the page, and immediately used: every student writes three headlines of their own and the room guesses the story behind them. The reading half is two genuinely astonishing stories — tardigrades on the Moon and a university evacuated because of a fruit — and the exam discipline is built in by the book itself: every True/False answer must come with the line number.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Water bears are the toughest animals on Earth. They survive in a vacuum, they survive being frozen, and in 2019 a few thousand of them crashed onto the Moon. Nobody knows if they are still alive up there. Today: the language of the news — and how to read a headline that has had half its words deleted.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 discovered from the five examples, then ex.6a–6b: everybody writes and the room guesses

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5 with the whole class, six minutes, one example at a time. For each one ask the Teacher's Book question rather than reading the rule: «President shot during speech» — what word is missing? (is) · «Humans to reach Mars by 2035» — has it happened? (no, it is the future) · «World economy collapse fear» — how many nouns in a row? What does it mean? (fear that the world economy will fail).
  2. Write the five rules on the board as five short lines. They stay there for the rest of the lesson.
  3. ex.6a in pairs, four minutes: invent THREE headlines about imaginary news, using the words from ex.3 and ex.4 and the grammar from ex.5, and write a one-sentence explanation of each underneath, hidden.
  4. ex.6b, two minutes: swap with another pair, read only the headlines, and try to guess the story. The model on the page is worth reading aloud first — «The director of a phone company is going to marry a famous tennis player» becomes «Phone boss to wed tennis star».
  5. Take the three funniest to the floor and vote with a show of hands. Sixty seconds, no more.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: predict from photographs and headlines, then read the whole page once for gist

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1, ninety seconds in pairs: three photographs and two headlines. What will each story be about? Take predictions without confirming them.
  2. Ask the question the Teacher's Book asks, because it is the best thirty seconds of the page: «Un-news-ual» — what kind of word is that? It is invented: unusual with news inside it, and it sounds the same if you say it fast. Journalists do this constantly.
  3. Say the rule for the first read: Four minutes, silent, both stories, one question only. You will not know every word and you do not need to.
  4. ex.2 checked by nomination: story 1 is a mission that was not a complete success — to take water bears to the Moon; story 2 is a university library closed because the smell of a durian was mistaken for a gas leak.
  5. Thirty seconds in pairs: which of the two is more astonishing, and would you have believed either of them from the headline alone?

True, False — and the line that proves it
individual → pairs → class
Why: True, False — and the line that proves it

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: six statements decided on named lines, which is what the exercise actually asks for

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the format first: Six statements. True or False AND the line number. An answer without a line number is not an answer.
  2. Give the Teacher's Book warning before they start, because it is the whole skill: Do not look for the same words. The statement says the idea in different words on purpose.
  3. ex.3 individually, six minutes, clock visible. Underline in the text, write the line numbers in the margin.
  4. Pairs compare — line numbers first, letters second. Two minutes. Where the lines differ, they re-read together before they argue.
  5. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: 1 False (5–11) · 2 True (13–14) · 3 False (26–27) · 4 False (37–38) · 5 True (47–50) · 6 True (55–57). The nominated student reads the LINE aloud, then says the letter.
  6. Close the stage with the four-step strategy from the Exam tip, said in the students' own words: read fast for the gist, read the statements, find the place, read that place slowly. And never leave a blank.
📺 On screen: the six answers with their line numbers written up as they are confirmed — chalk only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: decide six statements alone with the lines marked, compare evidence in pairs, then read the proof aloud instead of the answer
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 10–18 (the reading section). The Russian paper never asks for the line, but every one of its answers has one, and the candidates who lose marks are the ones who choose by memory of the text instead of by evidence in it. Requiring the line number today builds the habit that transfers.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: after the class check, take two of the six statements and ask what would have to change in the TEXT for the answer to flip. Four minutes, taken from the words-from-context stage, which survives with five words instead of seven.

pairs → individual → class

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 worked out from the text, then ex.5: an opinion defended with an example

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 in pairs, five minutes: samples, cargo, endure, vacuum, chain, breadcrumbs, divisive. Rule: the definition has to come from the sentence around the word, and the dictionary comes second.
  2. Check quickly with the compact versions: samples — a small amount that shows what all of it is like · cargo — goods sent by ship or plane · endure — put up with something difficult for a long time · vacuum — a space with no air · chain — shops owned by one company · breadcrumbs — tiny pieces of bread used in cooking · divisive — producing opposite opinions.
  3. 🎯 ex.5, three minutes silent: write ONE sentence answering the Critical thinkers question, then ONE example. Of the two stories on this page, which one is real news and which one is entertainment? Can a story be both? What makes you say that?
  4. Kagan Timed Pair Share, ninety seconds each way: read your sentence, the listener asks one follow-up question. Then take four answers to the floor and let them disagree.
  5. Offer the Teacher's Book position as a counter-argument if nobody does: these stories are entertainment more than science, and very often the headline does not match what the article actually says. That last point is the seed of the next five lessons.
  6. Correct only two things today: headline word order, and «news» used as a plural. Everything else waits.

👦 Students do: infer seven meanings from context in pairs, write a justified opinion, then defend it against a partner and against the room

Structure
You'd find that in the … section · It's the kind of story that … · The headline means that … · True — line 13 says … · False — lines 26 to 27 say the opposite …
Form
headline grammar: the auxiliary and the articles are deleted (President [is] shot during speech) · the present simple carries recent events (Tortoise smashes through wall) · to + infinitive carries the future (Humans to reach Mars) · nouns are stacked as adjectives (World economy collapse fear) · direct speech loses its inverted commas.
Pron. watch
the headline words are short and stressed, and the group will swallow them: axe /æks/ with a clean final cluster · plea /pliː/ not «plee-a» · probe /prəʊb/ with a diphthong · blaze /bleɪz/ against blast /blɑːst/ · wed /wed/ · PM said as two letters, pee-EM, stress on the second · the collocations take a compound stress on the FIRST element (FRONT-page news · NEWS bulletin · PRESS conference · CHIEF editor) · and the question tag carries the whole meaning in its intonation: falling = I am sure, rising = I am asking. Drill the two contours physically, with a hand
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Obituaries» — is it about people who are famous, or people who have died? → both: famous people who have just died. Which section would a story about a footballer's wedding go in? → showbiz or gossip.
«PM pledges extra aid to unemployed» — how many words are missing? → none are missing here, but three words are doing unusual jobs: PM, pledges, aid. Say it in normal English.
«The introduction of tardigrades onto the Moon was completely accidental» — where in the text does it say so? → nowhere: lines 5–11 say it was an ambitious PLAN. That is why it is False.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Students translate the headline words instead of using themThe rule is stated before ex.3 starts and enforced twice: No Russian. Give me the everyday English word. The wall has two columns for exactly this — axe next to cut, probe next to investigate — and nothing goes up until the twin is there.
Headlines read word by word as normal sentencesDo the demonstration on the board before the book: write «Humans to reach Mars by 2035», ask Has it happened? and let the class discover that to + infinitive is the future. Then the other four rules take ninety seconds each.
True/False decided by feeling, with no line namedNo line number, no answer accepted — say it once and then hold to it. When you check, the nominated student reads the LINE first and the letter second. The two statements the group will get wrong are 1 and 3; budget an extra minute for them.
Fast finishers in the readingThe Teacher's Book gives four questions to project or write up while the others work (what happened to the tardigrades · what do they need to wake up · what does «provided the students could face the smell» mean · why were the plane passengers unhappy), answered in their own words.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 10–18 (the reading section — media, information and «fake news» are standing topics of the paper) and tasks 32–38 (lexical choice).

The True/False work the class does today on the tardigrade and durian stories is the Russian task 3–9 machine in Cambridge clothes: the statement paraphrases the text, never repeats it, and the decision is made on a named line, not on a feeling. Gateway even forces the proof — students write the line numbers. That habit alone is worth several marks, because the standard Russian error is choosing True for anything that sounds plausible. The headline vocabulary is a second gift: axe, ban, boost, probe, quit, spark are exactly the short high-frequency verbs the lexical section loves.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
