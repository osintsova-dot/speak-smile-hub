Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /tʃ/ sound (witch chops chicken kitchen — “tch says ch”), talk about life on a Scottish island, and read & plan the end of a fable — three mini-blocks, one “helping & home life” thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that ch and tch spell the same /tʃ/ sound (recognise → say → sort); culture — life on a Scottish island (Sarah · Olivia · James) + Channel 21 video, 21C: helping others; literacy — text type a fable (Anansi the spider), the message of a story, adjectives before nouns, plan & write a fable ending.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (chant/drum bridges); the witch tongue-twister is play; cap each block hard so the fable plan actually gets written.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the witch /tʃ/ twister and sort ch / tch words;
  • name 2–3 things Scottish-island children do & compare with our life;
  • find the message of the fable and plan an ending with a partner.

Structure
The (witch) (chops) (chicken) in the (kitchen).  ·  I travel to school by (ferry). / I feed the (sheep) every day.  ·  fable: Anansi is (greedy) because he doesn't help.
Form
/tʃ/ = ch (chicken, chops, chair) AND tch after a short vowel (witch, kitchen, watch, catch). Fable: put the adjective before the noun — a greedy spider · a clever idea · dirty hands.
Pron. watch
/tʃ/ clean in chicken/witch/kitchen; contrast /tʃ/ (chair) vs /ʃ/ (shair×); ferry /ˈferi/; island /ˈaɪlənd/ (silent s).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“witch and chicken — same first SOUND? Same spelling?” → same sound /tʃ/, different spelling (tch / ch)!
“A fable — does it just tell a story, or does it teach a lesson?” → it teaches a lesson / message.

Assumptions: Chores, free-time & frequency are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from earlier phonics is enough to enjoy the ch / tch noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short island texts & the fable with support.

Block 1 · Phonics /tʃ/
carpet → desks

Listen, read and say (act. 1, CD1 39). PB p.23: “tch” says “ch”. The witch chops chicken in the kitchen! Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort ch / tch. Two hoops/columns: ch (chair, chicken, chops, beach) · tch (witch, kitchen, watch, catch, pitch). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card.

Notice the rule. Look — witch, kitchen, watch… tch comes after a short vowel! (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more /tʃ/ word to a hoop (e.g. match, chin).

39–52′
Block 3 · Literacy · a fable
carpet → desks, pairs

Before you read (act. 1–3, CD1 41). PB p.24 «Why Anansi has got eight long, thin legs»: Who is Anansi? The title tells you about the story! Read/listen to find out (a greedy, lazy spider who tricks his friends).

▶ Fable scavenger hunt. Race in pairs to find in the fable: Why is Anansi greedy? Why is he lazy? What happens at the river? First hands up read the answer aloud — everyone scans.

Investigate a text (PB p.25, CD1 42). «Anansi and Turtle»: choose the best title; then the Writing tip — Adjectives go BEFORE the noun: a greedy spider, a clever idea, dirty hands! Underline 2 adjectives together.

▶ Plan a fable ending (Cooperative learning, AB p.22 format). As a class/pairs, plan the end of the Anansi-and-Turtle fable: What does Turtle say? · What can't Anansi do? · What happens? · How does Anansi feel? (cold/hungry/tired/wet) Use an adjective before a noun. 2 pairs share one line aloud.

💪 Support: fill the plan template with word support. ⭐ Extension: write the full ending with two adjectives.

📓 Interactive notebook — GMF3_Unit2_Lesson5_Finish_The_Fable_A5
A5 glue-in writing template — “Anansi and Turtle”: sentence 1 given, sentences 2–4 to finish, with WORD HELP (swim under water · take off · floats to the top · hungry · wet), a WRITING TIP (adjective + noun) and a CHECK YOUR FABLE list
  • Glue the template in; read sentence 1 aloud in Turtle's voice — You can't wear a coat when you eat lunch…
  • Pairs plan sentences 2–4 orally first (30 sec), then write on the template lines.
  • Point at the WRITING TIP: every pupil must get at least one adjective + noun into their fable.
  • Read the CHECK YOUR FABLE list aloud one line at a time; pupils tick their own boxes.
  • Two or three pupils read their ending aloud — What lesson does Anansi learn?
