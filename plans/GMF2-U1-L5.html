A1 young learners (~8–9 y.o., early readers) · 60 min · Phonics (recognise → blend → segment) + Culture + Literacy product · carpet + desks
Week 28.09–04.10 · Lesson 8🔤 Phonics ai / ay~12 pupils · 3 teams of 4📖 Poem “Ball!” · Culture: Sports Day
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & write words with the alternative vowel spellings ai / ay (recognise → blend → segment) and to read a poem and write their own short “Ball!” poem about sports.
  • Subsidiary. Culture — read about Sports Day in Britain (reading for gist / detail); review sports + He/She plays / goes … on (Monday) (3rd person, from L1&L3); spelling & fine motor (colour-coding, accordion notebook).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; give an ICQ before every task; run a stirrer→settler rhythm; model writing with I Do → We Do → You Do before pupils write alone.
By the end, pupils can…
  • read, blend & sort words with ai and ay;
  • name the Sports Day races & say a sport they play at school;
  • read the poem “Ball!” and write & perform their own short poem.
Target language
Phonics (main)
ai / ay → /eɪ/ (same sound, two spellings): rainsnailSpaintrainrainbow · playdayMaysayTuesday — twister: “ai–ay–ai–ay. Snails play in the rain all day.”
Lexis
big/small balltennishockeyfootballbasketballvolleyball · catchbouncekickthrow · Sports Day: egg and spoon racesack racewheelbarrow raceballoon toss
Structure
Poem: Catch me! Bounce me! Kick me! Throw me! · Review: He/She plays tennis / goes swimming on (Monday).
Form / rule
ay usually at the end of a word (play, day); ai in the middle (rain, snail). Both = one sound /eɪ/.
Pron. watch
/eɪ/ not /aɪ/ — contrast rain vs *rine; rhythm of the poem (chunk by ball, clap the beat).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Do rain and day have the same sound?” → yes → /eɪ/.
“Where do we usually see ay — start, middle or end?” → end.
“Is ball a big or small word in the poem?” → the loud one → BALL!

Assumptions: sports vocab & Do you play/go…? known from L1–L2; He/She plays/goes from L3; single-letter sounds & CVC blending known; pupils are early readers → support text with pictures, audio & colour. Teams: max 4 (12=3×4 · 10=3+3+4 · 9=3×3 · 8=2×4 · 7=3+4).

10–24′
Phonics · ai / ay
board → desks

① Recognise (PB p.13 №1). Show 🦝 Toby's tongue twister: ai–ay–ai–ay. Snails play in the rain all day. Read it slowly, then faster ×2. Dual coding: underline ai in RED (snail, rain) and ay in BLUE (play, day). Same sound — /eɪ/! Two ways to write it.

CCQ. Where is ‘ay’ — start, middle or end? (end) · And ‘ai’? (middle). Air-draw both.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: say a word — /eɪ/ word (train, May, rain, play, snail, Spain) → stand up tall; a trick non-/eɪ/ word (dog, sit, red) → crouch small. Fast, every child moves every time.

② Blend + ③ Segment. Finger-blend on the board: s–n–ai–l → snail. p–l–ay → play. Then reverse: I say ‘rain’ — tap the sounds… which spelling, red ai or blue ay? (ai). Do 3–4 words.

▶ Flyswatter (Мухобойки) — ai/ay: 8–10 word-cards on the board. Everyone plays at once: class holds up a RED (ai) or BLUE (ay) card to vote the spelling, THEN a rotating pair (one per team) races to swat the word you blend aloud. Correct swat + correct class vote = team point. Rotate the pair each round. Freeze! to reset.

Write — AB p.10 №2 (desks, 2 min). Colour the ‘ai’ words red and the ‘ay’ words blue. Say each word. Model one, then go.
