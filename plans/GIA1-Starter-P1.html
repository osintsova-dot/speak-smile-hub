WHAT WE DO: a grown-up meeting at the door (handshake/fist bump — their choice); summer rewind: 3 questions with their GMF4 toolkit; bridge

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door, to each one: Welcome back! Handshake or fist bump? — the choice of gesture sets the tone.
  2. Summer rewind in pairs, 90 sec: Three questions from your GMF4 brain: Where were you in summer? What was the best day? Did you speak any English? (they do have past simple!).
  3. 2–3 answers out loud; I praise the past simple: You just used past simple after three months off. Respect.
  4. Bridge: You finished the kids' course. Today you enter the TEEN course. New book, new rules, new league.
📺 On screen: board: 3 summer questions · the new Get Involved! A1+ coursebook on display
👦 Students do: they choose a gesture, recall the summer in past simple in pairs. Expected: the first-minute stiffness melts; someone says “I was in Sochi”. Typical error: “I go to Moscow in July” — recast with no analysis: “went — nice!”

WHAT WE DO: p.6 ex.1–3 as a sprint: the Max/Lara dialogue + formality (Hi vs Hello, Thanks vs Thank you); a drill with rotation

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1, 90 sec: fit 3 phrases into the dialogue (key: I'm fine · I don't know · Hi Max); audio 1 — check.
  2. Pairs read by role, SWAP, again — faster pace: Second run — no reading pace, ACTING pace!
  3. Ex.3 blitz-match (key 1c 2e 3d 4a 5b) + a 30-sec mini-lecture on “formality”: Hi + Thanks + Bye = friends. Hello + Thank you + Goodbye = teachers, adults, interviews. Teens know the difference.
  4. Drill: I throw out a situation (job interview! best friend!) — pairs produce the right register.
📺 On screen: SB p.6 · audio 1 (Backup: no track — I read it myself by role)
👦 Students do: they fill in the phrases, act out the dialogue at increasing speed, switch registers. Expected: fast and clean — this is the base. Typical error: Goodbye to a friend — “too formal! Bye!”

Challenge: pronoun accuracy in writing
🔥 Challenge · pronoun accuracy race
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: p.6 ex.4–5: the pronouns/possessives table at speed + a choose-race; “catch the mistake” in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4 trio race, 3 min: fill in the table (key: my · your · he · her · it · we · their). Anchor on the board: pronoun + VERB · possessive + NOUN.
  2. Ex.5 individually, 2 min, check in the trio (key: our · They · My, I · your, it's · She, our).
  3. TB reinforcement trick: before you choose, ask yourself — “is it a verb or a noun after the gap?”
  4. “Catch the mistake” on mini-boards: I write “Her is my friend” · “This is he bag” · “Its my pen” — trios fix them in writing, boards up (+1 for a clean fix).
📺 On screen: SB p.6 ex.4–5 · mini-boards · anchor pronoun+VERB / possessive+NOUN
👦 Students do: they race through the table, choose forms, fix mistakes on the boards at the same time. Expected: easy orally, they get caught on its/it's in writing. Typical error: Its my pen — a 20-sec apostrophe breakdown.

Memory duel · Inside-Outside Circle
two circles

WHAT WE DO: Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: p.7 ex.8 as a memory duel — what's in your partner's bag? two circles facing each other, the outer one rotates; questions with no peeking

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model: What's in your bag? — A mobile phone, my keys and a book. — What colour's the book? — I think it's black.
  2. Launch Inside-Outside Circle: Two circles! Inside circle — face out. Outside — face your partner. Round 1: bag interviews, 60 sec, we answer from memory (no peeking!), then check against the real bag — were you right?
  3. Rotation: Outside circle — rotate two steps! A new partner — now you recall the PREVIOUS one's bag: What's in Misha's bag? Two black books… — a test of memory and plurals; 2 more rotations.
  4. +1 for every clean plural and a/an in the answers; the Checker in the trio counts.
📺 On screen: real bags · timer
👦 Students do: they interview, check their memory against the real bag, retell about a third person. Expected: laughter at the checks (“I've got NO money!”). Typical error: two book — the Checker catches it (+1 to them).

Preparing the 60-sec intro dialogue
Prepare the product
desks · solo→trios

WHAT WE DO: we prepare a 60-sec intro dialogue without prompts; a run-through via Kagan Timed Pair Share (60 sec of speech · the listener has a job · swap)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The task: Your product: a 60-second introduction dialogue. Greeting (right register!), three questions, FULL answers, finish politely. Keywords only on paper.
  2. ICQ: Can you write the whole dialogue? — No! Five keywords max.
  3. The judges' rubric on the screen; a run-through via Timed Pair Share: A speaks sixty seconds — B listens with a JOB: count full sentences. No interrupting. Swap! The Timer in the trio keeps the time; rotation ×3 — everyone speaks twice.
  4. I walk around, fixing only register and full sentences.
📺 On screen: rubric slide · timer (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they build a prompt out of keywords, run the dialogue through twice. Expected: 60 sec already holds. Typical error: they write out the whole script — I take it away, leave 5 words.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — forms are clean (1–3) · Richness — details, colours, numbers, connectors (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		prep slips · stopwatch

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can hold a 60-sec introduction dialogue WITHOUT prompts (greetings + questions + bag/colours) and pass the accuracy check on pronouns, possessives, a/an + plurals.
  • Subsidiary. New course rituals are launched: gate from minute 8, the leaderboard, roles in trios; a fluency diagnosis after the summer; spelling gaps are logged (pencil case! their!); the “grown-up” tone of the course is set.
  • Personal. The first meeting after the summer: not “we're revising kids' stuff” but “you've moved up to the big league”. Respect the GMF4 base out loud, but show the bar right away: full answers, written accuracy, a serious format.
By the end, students can…
  • greet, ask and answer without prompts for 60 sec
  • use subject pronouns + possessives accurately in writing
  • apply a/an + plural rules (-s/-es/-ies) at speed
  • describe a partner's bag from memory (colours + objects)

Structure

Form
subject pronoun BEFORE the verb, possessive BEFORE the noun (my/your/his/her/its/our/their); a + consonant SOUND, an + vowel SOUND; plurals: +s / -ch-sh-s-ss-x→+es / consonant+y→-ies.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“a apple or an apple? Why?” → an — a vowel SOUND (but a university — /j/!).
“city → citys?” → cities: consonant + y → -ies (but keys: vowel + y → +s).
“His or He's?” → his + noun (possessive), he's = he is.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: greetings, colours, objects are active orally. The real gaps after summer: written spelling (their/there, pencil case), fluency without prompts, plurals -ies. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling! numbers by ear! alphabet at speed!), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The “we learned this in year 3” whineDon't argue — gate: spell 6 words in writing. Usually 3/6. “Knowing it orally ≠ owning it in writing. This is a teen course — here you write”.
Summer fluency slumpThe first 20 min — only success-guaranteed formats (pairs, choral); don't drag anyone up to the board individually.
New rituals get read as “games”Frame it as sport: the leaderboard = a season, the gate = qualification, judges = like in football.
The strong ones dominate in pairsRoles in trios: Speaker/Checker/Timer, rotating every 2 min — everyone speaks.
a/an by letter, not by soundAnchor on the board: an hour · a university — “listen to the SOUND, don't look at the letter”.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
