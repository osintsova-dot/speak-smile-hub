🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Build and use the past simple passive — was/were + past participle, with by for the doer — and decide correctly when the passive is the right tool and when a verb must stay active.
What is genuinely hard
Two opposite errors live side by side: was/were glued onto verbs that must stay active (he was died, it was tasted terrible) AND the auxiliary dropped where the passive is needed (the film called Now You See It). The decision — does the subject DO or RECEIVE? — is the invisible step under every form.
Typical error to expect
He was died in London · The medicine was tasted terrible — was/were glued onto verbs that must stay active; and the mirror error, the film called Now You See It with the was dropped [high priority, A2/B1]
This house it's built in the 16th century · we were allow to eat — the participle or the auxiliary lost on the way to the passive [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
Discovery starts from the two sentences parked on the board last lesson — the grammar grows out of a text the class has already read, the block's signature move. The do-or-receive decision is physicalised in the standing sort before any written practice. Practice climbs from choose (street painting) through complete (the canal hole) to the Corpus error hunt — the last one is the exam item seen from the wrong side. The productive stage is the £5-painting story from the Grammar reference, retold as a real story, then students build their own two-line passive facts and quiz each other — the passive earning its keep on true content.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“A class of schoolkids paid £5 for an old picture in a second-hand shop. It was painted five hundred years ago. It was sold to a London gallery for a MILLION pounds. Every sentence of that story needs today's grammar — by the end you'll tell it whole.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a three-chunk repair clinic, and the bridge into the parked passive sentences

📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit20_Grammar_Past_Simple_Passive_A5
A5 grammar card — PAST SIMPLE PASSIVE, built as a school-news route (the poster was designed → was printed → articles were written → the notice was put up): the formula WAS / WERE + past participle with the active→passive pair (People printed the poster yesterday → The poster was printed yesterday), the plural (The stickers were designed by students.), the negative (The notice wasn't removed.) and the question (Were the articles published online?), plus the CRITICAL REMINDERS — was singular / were plural · the main verb is a past participle · keep the past time marker · use by only when the doer matters
  • Glue the card in and follow the news route with your finger, naming each step.
  • Check the number in every sentence: was or were, and why.
  • Turn four sentences about your school's news into the passive.
  • The crossed-out «was print» is the mistake to watch for — mark it.

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: the do-or-receive standing sort

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up. I say a sentence — subject DOES: hands push forward. Subject RECEIVES: arms cross like a shield. Fire eight: The school was built… · The dog ate… · The song was recorded… · He died… (trap!) · etc.
  2. Debrief the trap in one line: died — nobody did it TO him. Active. On the veto list it goes.
📺 On screen: instructions by voice, nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: push or shield, catch the trap. The shield gesture is the receive-test made visible — it returns silently all lesson

WHAT WE DO: retell the GR ex.4 story, then build a passive facts quiz

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Tell the story once yourself, gap-free and dramatic: Grammar reference ex.4 is a ready-made story for the production stage: a class of schoolchildren was given an old picture, bought for £5 at a second-hand shop — it was almost hidden behind others, it wasn't signed, it was probably painted about 500 years ago… and it was later discovered to be valuable and was sold to a London gallery for a million pounds. Sixteen gaps, active and passive mixed (started · were left · noticed · paid £5 · took · was discovered · was sold…).
  2. Pairs run GR ex.4 (16 gaps, active and passive mixed) as a retelling relay: A tells the first half from the gaps, B the second — the machine and the veto list are on the board, use them.
  3. Now their own: each pair writes TWO true passive facts they know (Our school was built in… · This song was recorded by… · Vkontakte was created by…) — one may be a lie.
  4. Team quiz, Kagan Timed Pair Share energy: pairs read their facts to another pair, who call TRUE or LIE! — and must repeat the sentence correctly either way.
  5. You walk and log: dropped auxiliaries and veto-verb slips — they open lesson 6.
📺 On screen: the story gaps are in every book (GR p.157) — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: retell, invent, quiz. Expect the lie-hunting to produce loud repetition of passives — which is the point. Typical error: was borned — the participle list on p.158 settles it

Structure
The picture was used in a car advertisement. · This dress wasn't just designed to look good. · Were your shoes made in Spain? · 150 km of canals were built between 1790 and 1820.
Form
was/were + (not) + past participle; by + doer only when the doer matters; state verbs (die, taste, look, happen) never take the passive.
Pron. watch
was /wəz/ weak in the middle of the sentence · built /bɪlt/ — silent u · designed /dɪˈzaɪnd/ — the g is silent
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Sunflowers was painted by Van Gogh» — who did the action? → Van Gogh; why is the painting the subject? → because the painting is what we are talking about.
«The internet wasn't used until the 1990s» — do we know exactly who used it? → no, and it doesn't matter: that is why the passive.
«The medicine was tasted terrible» — what is broken? → tasted is a state here, not an action done TO the medicine: the medicine TASTED terrible.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«he was died», «it was tasted terrible»The do-or-receive test made physical in the standing sort, then policed by pointing at the boarded rule: states stay active. The Corpus hunt drills exactly these two.
The auxiliary dropped («the film called…»)Mirror trap, named out loud: passive with no was/were is just a naked participle. Who called the film? Nobody? — then WAS called.
by in every sentenceThe default is doer-less: use by only when the doer is the news. In the practice, cross out every by that adds nothing — theatrically.
Weak pairs freeze on irregular participlesThe participle list lives on SB p.158 — put the page number in the board corner and let anyone check without asking.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
