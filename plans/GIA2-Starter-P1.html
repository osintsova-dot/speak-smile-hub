🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): This is the FIRST pair of the year, and it has two jobs at once. The first is to set the class up: show them how the year works (missions, points, crews, roles), so that later you never have to explain it on the fly. The second is to kill the «we know all this» groan on the doorstep: countries, nationalities and be really are familiar to them, so instead of revision we give them a gate and the agent-registration frame. The grammar of be is never announced out loud: we are not «revising the verb to be», we are going through passport control, where a broken question does not get through. The diagnostic snapshot (how many countries in 90 seconds, what kind of questions they build) I collect silently — it will feed the personal gap list in pair 4.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Season Zero. The missions of this year start next week — but nobody flies without a passport. Today you register as agents: name, age, country, nationality, languages. Two checks: the Passport Office — can you fill the file fast and clean? And Border Control — can you survive an interview where a broken question is REFUSED? You've spoken English for years. Ninety seconds says how much of it is ready. Passports out.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: First lesson of the year: we register as agents of the season — passport (name, age, country, nationality, languages), then a border interview where a broken question does not get through. Along the way we sort out how the year works: crews, points, missions.

🎯 GATE · passport speed-write
solo · mini-boards

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: as many country–nationality pairs in writing as possible; then 3 questions to a neighbour in 60 seconds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ninety seconds. As many country–nationality pairs as you can. Spelling counts: 'Spanish' with one n is a fake passport. GO!
  2. They write their own score in the margin: Your number. Nobody else sees it.
  3. Second run, 60 seconds: Now write THREE questions you'd ask a stranger at the border. Any question words.
  4. I log it and close it with no feedback: Keep both numbers. Rematch at the end of the lesson.

The rules of the year: crews, points, missions
Season rules · setting the class up
board → crews

WHAT WE DO: how our year works: what a mission is, how points are counted, how crews and roles work

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four rules onto the board, one at a time, each with an example — not as a list at the end:
  2. 1. The mission. Every lesson has ONE mission. Not a topic — a job. Today: register as agents. The topic of the lesson is never announced — only the mission.
  3. 2. Points. Points go to the CREW, never to one person. You get them for the product, for clean English and for gold runs — nine out of nine on the rubric. I show the empty unit leaderboard on the wall.
  4. 3. Crews. I split them myself, mixing them: Crews are mine to make, not yours. Every unit — new crews.
  5. 4. Roles. I hand out the cards: captain (holds the time and the decision) · writer (writes for everyone) · checker (catches errors before it is handed in) · speaker (talks on stage, changes every pair).
  6. Concept check in crews, 60 seconds: Crew check: who gets the points — you or your crew? What happens if only one person talks on stage?
📺 On screen: the «season rules» slide (backup — 4 points in marker + an empty leaderboard table on a big sheet of paper that stays on the wall all unit) · role cards
👦 Students do: they sit in their new crews, sort out the roles, answer the checking questions. Expected: an argument «can I be with Roma?» — the crews are mine, that was announced as a rule. Mistake: they think the points are personal — the concept question catches that straight away

🔥 Challenge · border interrogation
pairs → crews

WHAT WE DO: a fast interrogation at the border: ex.4–5 they complete the be table themselves → ex.6–7 → a duel of questions, 5 seconds per turn, a broken question is REFUSED

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4–5 with no rule explained: The table has gaps. The examples have answers. Three minutes — close it. We check the key chorally, and they formulate the rule, not me.
  2. ex.6 they read on their own and choose the option; I only confirm: Verb first. No contraction in 'Yes, I am'. Contraction in 'No, I'm not'.
  3. ex.7 question↔answer matching as a race in pairs.
  4. The duel: Border Control. Five seconds per question. 'Are you Irish?' — 'No, I'm not.' Broken question or 'Yes, I'm' — REFUSED, your turn burns. Three rounds with a change of partner; I count the burnt turns.
📺 On screen: SB p.6 ex.4–5 + p.7 ex.6–7 (the «All around the world» block continues) · 5-sec timer · a REFUSED sign (Backup: on the board — just the word REFUSED in big letters, which I point at with my hand)
👦 Students do: they complete the table themselves, then fire questions. Expected: by the third round «Yes, I'm» disappears by itself — the price of the mistake works better than an explanation. Mistake: «You are Spanish?» with rising intonation — REFUSED, the turn burns

WHAT WE DO: a level for our students: ex.6–8 + audio 4 — the Wh- question machine and the upgrade «a question that pulls an answer»

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

PRODUCT · passport + border interview
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: the agent's passport (5 fields in writing) + a 60-second border interview in front of judges with the rubric

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The passport, 5 minutes in writing: Five fields: name · age · country · nationality · languages you speak. Plus ONE line: 'I'm not …, I'm …' — kill a wrong guess about you.
  2. A run-through in the crew: the checker goes down the checklist (country with from? nationality without from? short answers clean?).
  3. On stage: Sixty seconds per pair. One is the agent, one is Border Control. Minimum four questions — at least one Wh- and one 'pulling' question. Judges, cards up!
  4. After each pair — one challenge from the floor: Wait. Are you REALLY from there? Prove it — one fact.
  5. A gold run (9/9) I name out loud straight away and put +5 on the poster.
📺 On screen: the passport checklist on the slide · 60-sec timer · judges' cards (backup: I draw the passport fields on the board as a frame, and the judges hold up 1–2–3 fingers instead of cards)
👦 Students do: they write, check each other, come out in pairs. Expected: the bar and the judge role hold the format — nobody reads off the page, because the rubric takes marks off for fluency. Mistake: the interviewer asks four identical Yes/No questions — the judges take off richness

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, agents can register themselves in English (name · age · country · nationality · languages) and survive a 60-second border interview, asking and answering clean Yes/No and Wh- questions with be.
  • Subsidiary. The class is set up in the format of the year: crews, roles, points, what a mission is. The 14 country↔nationality pairs are covered by a race (SB p.6 ex.1–3) + stress from audio 1. The six classroom instructions (SB p.7 ex.4, audio 3) are introduced as «the station code» — all year they will be given in English.
  • Personal. In the first pair of the year I do NOT correct out loud: I collect the live errors (Yes, I'm · I'm from Spanish · How old you are?) in my notebook — they will become the clinic in P2 and P4. The words «the verb to be» are not said out loud once.
By the end, students can…
  • write 12+ country–nationality pairs in 90 seconds
  • ask and answer Yes/No questions with be, with correct short answers
  • build five Wh- questions and interview a stranger
  • follow six classroom instructions in English

Form
in a Yes/No question the verb comes FIRST (Are you…?), the subject after it · the short affirmative answer takes NO contraction: Yes, I am (not «Yes, I'm») · the short negative answer DOES contract: No, I'm not / No, they aren't · in a Wh- question the verb comes AFTER the question word: What's this? Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Yes, I'm» — can we say that? → no: no contractions in a short affirmative answer, Yes, I am.
«You are from Spain?» — is it a question? → by intonation yes, but the rule puts the verb first: Are you from Spain?
«I'm Spain» — what's wrong? → I'm from Spain (country) OR I'm Spanish (nationality).

Assumptions: SB pp.6–7, KTP pair 1 (lessons 1–2). Countries, nationalities and be are familiar from A1+ — what is new is the speed, the clean short answer and the format of the year itself. There is no Workbook section for Starter: the Workbook begins with Unit 1 (p.4).

First pair of the year: the class does not know each other / does not know everyoneI form the crews myself, mixing them; I hand out the roles straight away (captain · writer · timekeeper · speaker). Stand Up–Hand Up–Pair Up at the start is compulsory, so that they talk to more than «their own people».
«Yes, I'm» and «You are from Spain?» — the classics at this levelThe border rule: a broken question/answer is REFUSED, the turn burns with no explanation. The feedback comes in P2, as an anonymous clinic, not as a telling-off in the first lesson.
The rules of the year are explained but not taken inThe rules are given once in words and immediately used in action: I award the first points in this very pair, saying out loud what they are for.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
