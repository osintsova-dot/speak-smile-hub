🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Two things that are almost never about English: putting one grammar word into a gap and spelling it right, and making sure a short message answers all three questions it was asked.
What is genuinely hard
Both parts are lost for non-linguistic reasons. In the cloze, students write two words where one was asked for, or misspell a word they know. In the email, they write pleasant fluent English that replies to nothing — sample B on p.126 is exactly that, and it is the best English on the page.
Typical error to expect
One of the three questions in the email is simply never answered. In the Russian writing task that alone drops the first criterion, and a zero there cancels the whole piece of work [exam report]
becouse · tomorow · alot · wich · beatiful · intresting — the most frequent misspellings; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
Why the lesson is built this way
Each part is taught through one worked item rather than a list of rules. Part 5 goes first because its single move — decide what KIND of word is missing before you try to think of one — is what the book's own first exercise asks for, so the page does the teaching. The four sample answers on p.126 are then judged BEFORE anybody writes anything: discovering that the most fluent of the four is the worst answer teaches the criterion in one minute, and it is a discovery, not an instruction. Writing under time comes after that, with a partner check on one thing only — are all three questions answered — so the feedback stays usable. The OGE version comes last and is deliberately only started, not finished: the point is to see that the job is identical and only the size differs, and twelve minutes is enough for that.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Open page one hundred and twenty-five. Six gaps, one word in each — and two words in a gap scores zero, however clever they are. Then an email, and a warning: on the next page there is an answer with beautiful English that gets no marks at all. Your job is to work out why.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, the test handed back with one shared error repaired, and the bridge into the Prepare for the exam section

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last lesson rewind. Hand the test papers back and give exactly 2 minutes of silent reading. Look at your five mistakes. Do not compare with your neighbour yet.
  3. One error the whole block shares goes on the board and the class repairs it chorally: My parent's car is old. → My parents' car is old.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): You showed what you know. Now look at what it is FOR — open page one hundred and twenty-five.
📺 On screen: the sentence to be corrected goes on the board — the board is the medium here and the papers are on paper, so nothing has to be displayed and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: look through their own papers and repair the shared sentence. Expect a calm reaction if the test was honestly «from the classroom». Typical problem: they start comparing marks at once — hold the rule «your own mistakes first»

WHAT WE DO: set the frame of the day: the skill is one, but it can be asked for in different sizes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Draw two columns on the board, without any drama. On the left, in the coursebook: an email with three questions, answer in 25 words. On the right, in the OGE: an email with three questions, answer in 100–120 words.
  2. The key line of the lesson, and it gets repeated: Same job. Different size. Answer every question you were asked.
  3. Say the exam bit calmly and briefly: Some of you will take an English exam in Year 9, some of you will not. Today is not about the exam — it is about answering what you were actually asked.
  4. Set the bar: By the end you can look at somebody's answer and say in ten seconds whether it works.
📺 On screen: a slide with the two columns «in the coursebook / in the OGE» (Backup: the same two columns in chalk on the board — they stay up all lesson and are needed again at 68–82′)
👦 Students do: compare the two columns. Expect the word count on the right to produce a groan — that is fine, it is two years away and they are told so. Typical problem: they start worrying about the exam; take it off the table in one sentence

9–19′
Reading Part 5 · format
class → pairs
Why: The one decision that unlocks this task — what KIND of word is missing — is taught before any gap is filled, and the book's own first exercise asks exactly that.

WHAT WE DO: the format of Part 5 and the one decision that unlocks it (SB p.125 ex.1)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Warm the topic first, TB p.254: TB p.254, books closed, two minutes in pairs: describe your bedroom to each other — what you like most, what you dislike, what you would change. Then open the book. The text is about exactly that, so half the vocabulary is already in the air.
  2. Read the Exam information: A short text — usually an email, sometimes two — with six gaps. You write one word in each gap, and the missing words are grammar words: articles, prepositions, pronouns, auxiliaries, conjunctions.
  3. Put the five Exam tips on the board: 1) read the text once without thinking about the gaps, so you know what it is about; 2) think what KIND of word is missing — a preposition, a pronoun, an article; 3) read the whole sentence before you decide; 4) one word only in each space, or the mark is lost; 5) spell it perfectly, or the mark is lost.
  4. Read the two emails once, no gaps, and check comprehension: TB p.254, before the gaps: «Why is Shammi happy?» (he is going to change the colour of his bedroom walls) · «What colour are the walls now?» (pink) · «Do we know what colour he is going to paint them?» (no).
  5. Now ex.1, and it is the whole stage: The first move, and it is ex.1 on the page: what KIND of words are missing here — grammar or vocabulary? Answer: grammar words. Elicit the categories from them: determiners, prepositions, pronouns.
  6. Work the example gap through together: The example gap 0 is worked through together (TB p.254): «What kind of word often goes before a noun?» (a determiner or a preposition) — «Have we already got a preposition here?» (yes, in) — «So which word shows it is Shammi's bedroom?» (my).
  7. Kagan Think-Pair-Share: Look at gap two. Don't tell me the word — tell me what KIND of word it is.
📺 On screen: the two emails on the screen with the kinds of word written above the gaps as they are named (Backup: the two emails are printed in the book on p.125 — write the five Exam tips on the board before the lesson and have students pencil the kind of word above each gap in their own copy)
👦 Students do: read, then name the kind of word for each gap. Expect «a verb» for gap 2 before somebody says «an auxiliary». Typical error: they start guessing words immediately — hold the order, kind first

19–31′
Reading Part 5 · exam conditions
solo → pairs
Why: Timed, silent, no help: the strategy only counts if it survives real conditions. And two of the rules here are mechanical — one word, spelt right.

WHAT WE DO: the six gaps in exam conditions, then a paired check where the rule is named

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two rules on the board before the timer starts: 1 ONE word. 2 Spelt correctly. Both of them lose more marks than grammar does.
  2. Silent mode: Six minutes. Alone. Write the finish time on the board.
  3. After 6 minutes — Kagan RallyCoach, 4 minutes: Say the KIND, then the word. Why does it have to be that kind?
  4. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Gap four — number ones, boards up!
  5. Key (TB p.254): 1 because / as / since · 2 Do · 3 all · 4 how · 5 if · 6 what / which (TB p.254)
  6. Point out the one gap with two right answers — gap 1 takes because, as or since, and gap 6 takes what or which. Both are accepted.
📺 On screen: the finish time written on the board — the board is the medium here, no backup needed; while they work nothing is displayed at all and the task is open in the book on p.125
👦 Students do: work in silence, then justify in pairs. Expect 4–5 of 6. Typical error: gap 4 — they write that, missing the fixed phrase how much

Move break
standing
Why: A stirrer that also forces one English sentence. Teenagers who have been sitting for forty minutes need to stand before the grammar block, or the grammar block dies.

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds of movement between the two parts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up. I say a word — you show me the kind. Preposition: point at the door. Pronoun: point at yourself. Article: hands up. Eight words, fast: in, she, a, of, they, the, with, mine.
📺 On screen: instructions by voice, nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: sort eight words into three kinds with their bodies. Expect the categories to become automatic in ninety seconds. Typical problem: mine confuses them — it is a pronoun, and they met it two lessons ago

Writing Part 6 · four answers
pairs → class
Why: Judging four ready answers teaches more than writing one: they discover that the best English on the page is the worst answer, because it replies to nothing.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.126 ex.1: four sample answers, one of which is perfect — and it is not the prettiest

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed, the lead-in from TB p.255: small groups, two minutes, talk about your last birthday. Then open the book and read Danny's email: SB p.126: an email from Danny — «Tell me about your birthday presents! What's the best thing you got? Who gave it to you? Why do you like it?» Write an email to Danny and answer the questions.
  2. Before anything else, the move that decides the whole part: Underline the three questions. Not the whole email — the three questions.
  3. Two questions from TB p.255 that save half the mistakes: TB p.255 asks the question that saves half the mistakes before they happen: which tenses does this need? Past simple for the first two questions, present simple for the third. And: writing more than 25 words does NOT give a better score.
  4. The page prints four answers, A–D, and the whole stage is deciding which one is perfect and what is wrong with the other three. Pairs, 4 minutes: which one is perfect, and what is wrong with the other three?
  5. Kagan Showdown: every pair reveals its letter at once. Then the verdicts: A is perfect — three questions, three answers, and a friendly closing line. · B does not answer any of the questions in the email. It is pleasant, fluent and worth nothing. · C has bad punctuation, answers only one of the questions and is too short. · D has spelling mistakes, grammar and tense mistakes, and is too short.
  6. Repair D together, 2 minutes — it is the fastest error clinic on the page: TB p.255 has the class repair D, and it is two minutes well spent: My best present was an earring from my friend. It is beautiful. I love it and always wear it.
📺 On screen: the four sample answers on the screen, large enough to read the handwriting (Backup: all four are printed in the book on p.126 — work from the page and keep a four-line verdict table on the board)
👦 Students do: judge four answers and defend the choice. Expect B to be defended by somebody — let them, then ask where Danny's three questions are answered in it. Typical error: they choose C because it is short and «exam-like»
SB p.126 · mini-whiteboards
45–58′
Writing Part 6 · exam conditions
solo → pairs
Why: Twenty-five words under time, then a partner check on one thing only — are all three questions answered.

WHAT WE DO: their own answer to Danny, 25 words or more, then a partner check on one thing only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silent mode: Eight minutes. Alone. At least twenty-five words. Answer all three questions. Write the finish time on the board.
  2. One instruction that changes everything and costs nothing: When you finish, put a tick next to each of Danny's three questions.
  3. Then swap, 3 minutes. The partner checks ONE thing: are all three questions answered? Not spelling, not grammar. Three questions.
  4. Two students read theirs out loud, and the class does the ticking.
  5. Show the model answer only at the end: Model answer, TB p.255: Hi Danny — I had a great birthday. My favourite present was a new winter coat, which my uncle bought for me. I like it because it's my favourite colour and it's really warm. Sam
  6. Take the emails in at the end of the lesson, mark them, and hand them back for a second draft — TB p.255 asks for exactly that, and the second draft is where the learning is.
📺 On screen: the finish time on the board — the board is the medium here and no backup is needed, since nothing is displayed while they write and the task is open in the book on p.126
👦 Students do: write 25+ words, then check a partner's for the three answers. Expect most of them to reach 30–40 words. Typical error: the third question (why do you like it) is the one that goes missing — the ticking exposes it in five seconds

Why the answer fails
class
Why: Analysis of the four samples turns into a checklist the students wrote themselves, which is the only kind of checklist anybody uses.

WHAT WE DO: the checklist, written by them out of the four samples

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Build the checklist on the board from what they have just seen — four lines, dictated by the class: A is perfect — three questions, three answers, and a friendly closing line. · B does not answer any of the questions in the email. It is pleasant, fluent and worth nothing. · C has bad punctuation, answers only one of the questions and is too short. · D has spelling mistakes, grammar and tense mistakes, and is too short.
  2. Name the conclusion out loud, because it is counter-intuitive: Where marks go, and it is almost never grammar: one of the three questions is simply not answered. That is what makes B the most instructive of the four samples — it is the best English on the page and the worst answer.
  3. Then the same for the cloze, briefly: Where marks go: two words written where one was asked for, and a spelling slip in a word the student actually knew. Both are avoidable and neither has anything to do with English. And the six explanations, one line each: 1 — the second half of the sentence explains the first, so a conjunction is needed: because / as / since. · 2 — it is a question, so it needs an auxiliary: Do you want to come and help me? · 3 — all day means the whole day. · 4 — part of the fixed phrase how much: I know how much you hate your pink walls. · 5 — it is a request: Is it OK if I come at ten? · 6 — a determiner before colour: what colour / which colour.
  4. Finish with the question that makes it theirs: Which of these four mistakes is YOURS? Write it at the top of your email.
📺 On screen: the four verdicts and the six cloze explanations on the screen (Backup: build the same checklist in chalk from the class's dictation — it is better on the board anyway, because they watch it appear)
👦 Students do: dictate the checklist and name their own weak point. Expect «I didn't answer all three» to be the most common self-diagnosis. Typical error: they name spelling because it is easier to admit — push for the honest one

🎯 OGE version · the same three questions
teams → solo
Why: The same job in the Russian wrapper: three questions, an email, a signature. Only the size changes — and experiencing that is what stops the exam looking like a different subject.

WHAT WE DO: the same job in the Russian wrapper: task 35 in the school trainer, planned in class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go back to the two columns on the board and open the right-hand one: An email from an English-speaking friend with three questions in it. You answer all three, in 100–120 words, and you sign it. Four criteria: the task itself (3), the organisation (2), vocabulary and grammar (3), spelling and punctuation (2). Today's topic: «School» — the block topic: your favourite subject, how long homework takes you, what you do in your free time.
  2. What is the same: Exactly the same job as Writing Part 6 on SB p.126: read the questions, answer ALL of them, and check that nothing has been left out. Sample answer B on that page fails for one reason only — it answers none of the questions. What is different: Two differences, and only two. Size: 25 words there, 100–120 here — under 90 words the Russian task scores zero. And layout: the greeting, the closing phrase and the signature each go on their own line.
  3. Write the rule on the board before they start: The rule that decides most of the marks, and it is the same rule in both exams: underline the three questions before you write anything, and tick them off one by one when you have finished.
  4. Open the trainer link (the same one goes home as optional homework): https://speakandsmile.online/oge.html#/hw?writing:writing~55343D — it opens straight away, no password.
  5. In class they do only the first two moves — underline the three questions and write the opening plus the answer to question one. Everything else is homework, so the stage stays inside twelve minutes.
  6. Teams of three, one phone or laptop, 8 minutes: underline the three questions, then write the greeting and the answer to question one together. Three questions. Underline them first. Nothing else matters.
  7. Kagan Timed Pair Share to finish, 2 minutes: read your opening to somebody from another team and let them count the questions you have answered so far.
📺 On screen: the trainer task open on the screen or on the teams' phones (Backup with no internet: print the same task in advance through «Print worksheet» in the teacher mode of the app, one sheet per team — then nothing has to be displayed at all)
👦 Students do: underline three questions and draft the opening. Expect the size to be the shock and the job to feel familiar — that is exactly the intended reaction. Typical error: they start writing before underlining; the rule on the board is there for that

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
The gap needs a word you cannot think of — what do you decide FIRST? → what kind of word it is: a preposition, a pronoun, an article, a conjunction.
You have written two words and both are right — how many marks? → none. One word per gap.
The email is beautiful English but answers none of the three questions — what does it score? → almost nothing. That is sample B on the page.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They try to think of a word before deciding what kind of word it isMake the order compulsory and visible: first write the KIND above the gap — prep, pron, art, conj — and only then the word. Check the pencil notes when you collect.
Two words in a gap, or a misspelt word they actually knowBoth are worth a rule with no discussion: one word, spelt right. Put both on the board and leave them there for the timed run.
«Why is answer B bad? It's the best English here»That is the discovery of the lesson and it should be allowed to be an argument. Let them defend B for a minute, then ask them to find Danny's three questions in it.
«Why are we doing two exams at once?» — at the OGE stageAnswer honestly and briefly: there is one exam, it is two years away, and not everybody here will sit it. Today is about the fact that the same job appears in different sizes.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
