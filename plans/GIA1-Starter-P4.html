🎯 REMATCHES · beat your own gate

WHAT WE DO: rematches of the three gates: spelling 6 words → 6 numbers by ear → commands 2+2; comparison with P1–P3 on their own numbers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rematch 1 (P1): I dictate the same 6 words (keys · orange · notebook · purple · pencil case · their). Self-check.
  2. Rematch 2 (P3): 6 numbers with traps (thirteen · forty · fifteen · fifty · eighteen · eighty). Self-check.
  3. Rematch 3 (P2): Wednesday · Thursday in writing + carry out 2 commands.
  4. Everyone checks against THEIR OWN margins from P1–P3: Write your delta: gate 3/6 → today 5/6 = +2. THAT number goes on the leaderboard. Growth = points (everyone's delta goes into the team score).
📺 On screen: key slides · their own slips with the P1–P3 margins (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they go through the rematches solo, work out their personal delta. Expected: +1…+3 for most — proven growth. Typical error: someone with a delta of 0 — the frame “what's YOUR one thing to fix before Monday?”

16–38′
Starter Gate: the team quiz (5 rounds)
🏆 THE STARTER GATE · team quiz
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: the big team quiz over pp.6–11 in the Kagan Numbered Heads Together mechanic: 5 rounds × 4 questions; the NAMED number answers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rounds: 1 Words (object from a description, colour, international category) · 2 Grammar (a/an, plural -ies, his/he's, this/those) · 3 Ears (numbers and letters by ear) · 4 Commands (give/carry out, don't-forms) · 5 Book (section pages, unit by topic).
  2. The Numbered Heads Together mechanic: numbers 1–3 in the team; a question → Heads together — twenty seconds! → I call a number (Number two — your answer!) — THEY answer for the team → +2 clean / +1 with a self-correction. Nobody knows whose number will come up — the team prepares EVERYONE.
  3. All the questions are reworded from the exercises on pp.6–11 (TB keys on hand); written rounds — on mini-boards, everyone at once.
  4. The result as a table; the “gate passed” threshold — 60% for EVERY team (genuinely achievable). Gate — PASSED. Unit 1 — OPEN!
📺 On screen: round slides · mini-boards · score table · TB keys pp.12–17 (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they confer for 20 sec, answer by rotation, write volleys on the boards. Expected: dense excitement for 20+ minutes; everyone passes the threshold. Typical error: calling out of turn — the question burns (the rule is known in advance).

⚡ Speaking sprints · Timed Pair Share ladder
pairs rotate

WHAT WE DO: Kagan Timed Pair Share as a ladder: 30→60→90 sec on familiar topics, the listener has a job; partner rotation; the loop trick is allowed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Launch Timed Pair Share: A speaks, B listens with a JOB — count pauses over three seconds on your fingers. No interrupting. Swap on my signal! A familiar topic: My bag & my things / My ideal classroom / My week (days!) / Numbers in my life.
  2. Round 1: 30 sec. Rotation. Round 2: the same topic, BUT 60 sec — add details: colours, numbers, because! Rotation. Round 3: 90 sec.
  3. The loop trick, legally: Finished early? Loop: say it again in other words. Fluency is muscle, not magic.
  4. Finale: Hands up who survived 90 seconds! — almost everyone; I log it as an achievement of the course.
📺 On screen: timer on screen · 4 topics on the board (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they speak up the 30-60-90 ladder with new partners — everyone at once. Expected: 80%+ hold 90 sec, delight at “I did it”. Typical error: a freeze-pause — the partner throws in What about…? (they've been trained to).

Class contract · our rules

WHAT WE DO: class contract: trios propose rules in English → voting → signatures; the contract goes on the wall forever

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The task: Seniors write their own rules. Each crew: TWO rules for our English club. In English, imperative form — you learned it in Pair 2!
  2. Model: Speak English first. · Don't laugh at mistakes. · Help your crew.
  3. The trios write, read them out; voting with stickers for the top 5; the rules go on a flip-chart sheet, EVERYONE'S signature (and mine).
  4. I state it: This is OUR law now. When something breaks — we point at the contract, not at each other.
📺 On screen: flip-chart sheet · markers · stickers
👦 Students do: they formulate the rules in imperatives, vote, sign. Expected: “Don't laugh at mistakes” gets proposed by them — the most important clause. Typical error: a rule in Russian — we translate it together, the imperative structure is already there.

Product: the WDYT discussion + peer review
🏁 PRODUCT · WDYT mini-debate + peer review onboarding
trios

WHAT WE DO: the course's first WDYT ritual: opinions via Kagan Timed RoundRobin in trios + the first structured peer review with a checklist (2 stars + 1 wish)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The WDYT question of the day (a bridge to U1!): What makes a great English class? Opinions via Timed RoundRobin: Thirty seconds each, clockwise, opinion + because. No skipping, no interrupting! — the trio collects 3 opinion sentences.
  2. A trio's performance (rotating speaker) — 60 sec; the LISTENING trios work with a checklist: Voice · English-only · Because-argument.
  3. The peer review formula on the board: 2 stars + 1 wish: Star: loud clear voice. Star: three arguments. Wish: try eye contact.
  4. Every trio gets a review from the neighbouring one; the wish starts with Try…; points for the quality of the review (not only for the speech!).
📺 On screen: peer review checklists · opinion frames
👦 Students do: they discuss, perform, give structured feedback — every trio both speaks and judges. Expected: the first review is clumsy — that's fine, it's the onboarding of the ritual. Typical error: a wish without Try — I point at the checklist.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — forms are clean (1–3) · Richness — details, colours, numbers, connectors (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		checklists

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners pass the Starter Gate (team quiz over pp.6–11), hold 90-sec timed speaking sprints, sign the class contract and run their first structured peer review — “we're ready for Unit 1”.
  • Subsidiary. The gaps from all three pairs are targeted precisely (spelling! numbers by ear!); the gate rematches P1–P3 show growth; the WDYT discussion and checklist peer review are rehearsed as course rituals.
  • Personal. The climax of the Starter: not a test, but qualification into the season. Show EVERYONE the growth “gate P1 → today” on their own numbers. The contract is their document, not mine: they formulate the rules themselves.
By the end, students can…
  • answer mixed Starter questions at team-quiz speed
  • speak for 90 sec on a familiar topic without stopping
  • run peer review with the checklist (2 stars + 1 wish)
  • state the class contract rules in English

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is the gate a mark in the register?” → No: it's the team's qualification into the season; we count growth, not mistakes.
“Peer review = criticising?” → 2 stars first! Two strengths first, then one wish.
“Does the teacher write the contract?” → No — the rules are yours, the signatures are yours.

Assumptions: Three pairs are behind them: the material is familiar, the rituals (gate/leaderboard/roles) are absorbed. Today — consolidation + two new rituals: checklist peer review and the class contract. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling! numbers by ear! alphabet at speed!), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Likely problemWhat you do
Quiz stress for the weaker onesAnswers are ONLY team-based, after a 20-sec discussion; no individual “to the board” at all.
The strong one decides for the teamRotating spokesperson round the circle: whoever's turn it is answers, the team only advises.
90 sec of speech is scaryA ladder: 30 sec → 60 → 90 on the same topic; a “loop” is allowed: repeat what was said in other words.
Peer review slides into “fine”A checklist with criteria in hand; the wish must start with Try…: Try louder voice.
The contract gets signed formally and forgottenWe hang the contract on the wall FOREVER; we point to a clause at every discipline slip.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
