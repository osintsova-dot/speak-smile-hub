WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Timed RallyRobin: the Unit 9 rewind is woven into the new frame — an «I can…» ping-pong in pairs; a bridge into the finals season

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: High five — one thing you can do in English now that you couldn't in September!
  2. ▶ Kagan · Timed RallyRobin (60 sec): Pairs! Take turns — ‘I can…’, one sentence each, ping-pong, no pauses. Different units — different cans. GO! BOTH speak in turn, in every pair at once.
  3. 2–3 answers out loud (+1). Bridge: You CAN a lot. Who knows it? Me. You. Your notebooks. Who doesn't? Your PARENTS. Time to show them.
📺 On screen: board: the Now I can… frame · a clean season leaderboard next to the old one
👦 Students do: Students formulate «I can» about themselves — the year's review lands straight in the season's theme. Expected: they list grammar («I can use past simple!») — I nudge them towards something alive: «I can order food in English».

14–24′
Station race: the year's language bank
⚡ Year stations · language bank race
stations · crews

WHAT WE DO: 8 unit stations around the classroom: each one has a unit card (topic+grammar+Key phrase); crews collect 1 «live phrase» from each

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I lay out 8 unit cards around the classroom: U1 Our world · U2 Family · U3 Eat well · U4 My time · U5 Tech life · U6 The world is art · U7 Famous lives · U8 From A to B.
  2. Task: Rotate! At each station: read the card, write ONE alive phrase you can use in a project — not a rule, a PHRASE. Ninety seconds per station.
  3. Whistle every 90 sec — rotation; I go round rejecting «rules» in favour of phrases: not «past simple +ed», but Last year we visited…
  4. Finish: everyone has 8 phrases — the season's starter language bank.
📺 On screen: 8 «wall of the year» unit cards (asset) · timer · bank sheets
👦 Students do: Students move round the stations hunting for phrases — a review of every unit in 10 minutes, and all of it put to use. Expected: arguments over «which phrase is more alive» — that's exactly the point. Typical error: they copy out a rule — I swap it for a phrase on the spot.

34–44′
Teams, roles, contract
Teams & roles · contracts
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: teams of 3–4 (I put them together myself), roles director/writer/speaker/editor with checklists; a mini team contract

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I announce the line-ups (prepared in advance). Teams are final — like a film crew, you don't pick your colleagues, you make it work.
  2. Roles: Director (order+timing) · Writer (script/text) · Speaker (main voice) · Editor (editing/design). Roles rotate between events! Everyone gets a role card with a checklist.
  3. Team contract (3 lines, in English): Our team name… · We speak English when… · If someone is stuck, we… Signatures!
  4. I announce the gate: Topic approved by the end of today — that's the gate.
📺 On screen: role cards with checklists · contract forms
👦 Students do: Students receive their roles, write and sign the contract. Expected: the names brainstorm is fun; we keep the contract short. Typical error: an argument over who's speaker — roles rotate between events, everyone gets a turn.

Bank harvest · for your own topic
desks · solo → mix

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Mix-Pair-Share: everyone adds 8 phrases to the bank FOR their topic (Phrasebook p.122, Quick reviews) → we mix and swap the best ones

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Solo task (6 min): Eight phrases YOU will say in the talk. Steal from your year: Phrasebook p.122, Quick reviews, the station sheets.
  2. Rule: a phrase = I'll actually say it myself; a «rule copied from the table» doesn't count.
  3. ▶ Kagan · Mix-Pair-Share (3 min): MIX — walk! … PAIR — nearest person! SHARE your two best phrases — steal one from your partner if it fits your talk! Two rounds with new pairs — everyone speaks at once.
  4. The writer collects the team's best phrases into the header of the TED-script template.
📺 On screen: SB Phrasebook p.122 + Quick review pages · bank sheets · TED-script template (asset)
👦 Students do: Students dig through the book PURPOSEFULLY, then swap finds in the mix — the year's review works for the product, phrases circulate. Expected: they find Key phrases on their own. Typical error: off-topic phrases — the question «Where in YOUR talk?»

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can pitch their team's showcase plan in 30 seconds (topic + format + roles) and have a personal «language bank» of year phrases to build on.
  • Subsidiary. The season roadmap is accepted (TED Day → Video premiere → Project fair → Showcase Day with parents); teams and roles (director/writer/speaker/editor) are set; the year's language is collected station-by-station into a bank — review is woven into the prep, not separate.
  • Personal. A frame shift: «we're not learning any more — we're SHOWING». Let them feel the status of the graduation season: judging axes, a leaderboard, parents at the finale. Independence — but with gates and timers.
By the end, students can…
  • explain the season roadmap in English
  • name their team role and its job
  • collect year phrases into a personal language bank
  • pitch a project plan in 30 seconds

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Is Showcase an exam?» → No: it's a SHOW. We show our parents what we can do — and we're proud of it.
«Is the director role the boss?» → No: everyone has their own job; the director is responsible for order and timing, not for giving orders.
«Is the language bank about copying out the book?» → No: only the phrases YOU will actually say in your project.

Assumptions: The whole course is completed (U1–9), Unit 9 review closed on pairs 56–60. The Skills boost format (THINK-PREPARE-PRACTISE-PERFORM), peer review and the judging axes Layout/Content/Language/Visuals are familiar to the students from the year's projects — the season collects them into one big form.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«A project again?» scepticismSell the scale: this isn't a one-lesson poster — it's a season with premieres and parents in the room. Show the roadmap poster and the leaderboard right away.
Teams split «by friendship» unevenlyI form them myself: every team gets a strong speaker + a reliable editor; I take friendship into account, but the balance isn't up for discussion.
Choosing a topic eats half the lessonGate: the topic must be approved BEFORE the end of the pair; choose from the prepared topic menu, or pitch your own in 60 seconds for approval.
The phrase bank turns into copying outLimit: exactly 8 phrases per person, only the ones they'll actually say themselves; a 6-minute timer.
The strong ones carry everythingRoles with personal accountability: every role has its own checklist and its own line in the judging rubric.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
