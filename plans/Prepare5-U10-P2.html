🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Join two clauses with the right linking word by naming the RELATIONSHIP first: contrast (although, whereas, while), purpose (so that, in order to) or reason (because, as).
What is genuinely hard
Russian «чтобы» covers both purpose linkers, and «хотя» and «тогда как» blur into one contrast, so the choice feels stylistic when it is grammatical. Each linker has a fixed shape: so that takes a CLAUSE, in order to takes an INFINITIVE, and although and whereas do different jobs even though both are called contrast — although marks something surprising, whereas simply sets two things side by side.
Typical error to expect
«in order that I can», «for to get fit» — the two purpose linkers have fixed shapes: so that + a clause, in order to + an infinitive. Nothing else is available, and Russian «чтобы» happily translates into either, which is why the choice has to be made consciously.
«despite it was raining», «because of I was late» — despite and because of take a NOUN or an -ing form; although and because take a whole clause. Same rule as Unit 18, three weeks ago, and it is worth saying that aloud so the two lessons become one.
Why the lesson is built this way
The whole lesson is one question asked twenty times: is this contrast, purpose or reason? Nothing is taught as a word list, because a word list is what produces «in order that». The examples come out of the eight adverts they read yesterday, so the grammar is already in their heads before it is named — the fast finishers in lesson 62 were sent to underline exactly these words. A dictation warmer opens the lesson with three half-sentences they have to finish themselves, which is production before analysis. The Grammar reference on page 165 is used properly for once, as a middle stage rather than a footnote, because this unit gets three lessons and there is time to read a rule and test it.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Yesterday you underlined although, whereas and while inside eight job adverts. Today you find out why the writer chose each one — and why «in order that» does not exist.”

WHAT WE DO: the Teacher's Book warmer: production before any rule is named

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed. Dictate three half-sentences and give them time to write: You have to study hard so that… · Accountants work in offices, whereas… · As we didn't finish our homework, the teacher…
  2. Three minutes, individually, to finish all three with their own ideas. No help, no rule, no explanation. They can already do this and the point of the stage is that they find out.
  3. Two minutes: compare in pairs, then take three or four answers to the floor. Write the best one for each on the board.
  4. Now ask the question that starts the lesson, and ask it about the three sentences on the board rather than in the abstract: These three little words are doing three different jobs. What are the jobs?
  5. Take whatever they say, in whatever words. Do not supply «contrast, purpose, reason» yet — that comes off the page in two minutes and it lands better if they have reached for it first.
📺 On screen: the three student sentences written on the board, kept for the whole lesson — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: produce three complete sentences before any rule exists, then try to name what the linkers are doing

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: the rule off the page, then seven gaps

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 as a class. Write the first example on the board — You'll have to be strong so that you can lift the heavy boxes — and identify the two main verbs (have to be, can lift). Underline so that. Then two questions: does this part explain WHY you have to be strong? Is the relationship contrast, purpose or reason?
  2. Repeat for examples 2 and 3, faster each time. Answers: 1 b (purpose), 2 a (contrast), 3 c (reason).
  3. Read the rules aloud and add the extra examples the Teacher's Book supplies — Go to the gym every day so that you'll be fit for the competition. In Spain people finish work late, whereas in the UK they finish at around five. As he works in an office, he spends most of the day sitting down.
  4. Make the two distinctions that matter, and put both in the columns: although is like «despite the fact that» and marks something SURPRISING; whereas and while simply contrast two things with no surprise in them.
  5. ex.2 individually, five minutes: seven gaps, and warn them that several items accept more than one answer. Answers: 1 as / because, 2 Although, 3 so (that), 4 While / Although, 5 order (in order to), 6 whereas / although, 7 because.
  6. Mixed ability, straight from the Teacher's Book: stronger students cover the rules and do it from memory; weaker students keep the rules open.
  7. Check with Kagan Showdown — pens down, answers up together, one item at a time. Every confirmed word goes into a column with its shape beside it.
📺 On screen: the three columns filling up with linkers AND their shapes — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: derive three relationships from three sentences, then fill seven gaps and reveal them simultaneously

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that drills the only question of the lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three corners of the room: CONTRAST · PURPOSE · REASON.
  2. You read a whole sentence, linker included — from the adverts on p.109, from the dictation, or invented. Everybody walks to the corner.
  3. The first person to arrive says the relationship in their own words and names an alternative linker that would also work. Get it wrong and another corner corrects it.
  4. Eight sentences, eight walks. Then freeze and sit: Now you write your own, and they have to be true.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: four sentence halves matched AND joined, which is the test format

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Do item 1 with the class and model the order of operations out loud, because the test does this too: underline the key words in the first half (people, work, desk, unhealthy), find the topically related ending (c, they don't get any exercise), ask what the relationship is (reason), and only then choose the linker (because or as).
  2. ex.3 in mixed-ability pairs, six minutes, and stronger students have to explain the relationship aloud to their partner before writing. Answers: 1 c because / as · 2 d so that · 3 a whereas / while · 4 b although.
  3. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: the named student gives the match, the relationship AND the linker — three things, in that order.
  4. Then the two minutes that pay off next week: for item 3, ask whether although would also work, and why it would change the meaning. Answer: it would suggest the contrast is surprising, and it is not.
  5. Say the test warning here, six days early: on the block test this section asks for the match AND the linker, and a right match with a wrong linker scores nothing.
📺 On screen: the four answers with the relationship named beside each — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: match, name the relationship and choose the linker, in that order, then justify all three when their number is called

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: the linkers used on the only topic that cannot be faked

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four stems on the board, from the page: I hope to / I don't want to earn a lot of money… · I'm learning English… · I really like / dislike science… and one added for this group: I'm going to work in the summer / I'm not going to work in the summer…
  2. Six minutes, silent, individually: finish all four, each with a different linking word, each one TRUE. Rule on the board: four sentences, four different linkers, and each linker is underlined.
  3. Walk the room and check one thing only: that the two halves are logically connected. That is the Teacher's Book instruction and it is the thing that actually goes wrong.
  4. 🎯 The guessing round, ten minutes, and it is the best part of the lesson. Nominate a student to read only the FIRST half of one sentence plus the linker. Three other students guess how it ends. Then the writer reads the real ending.
  5. Run it for eight or nine students, then Kagan Stand-Up Hand-Up Pair-Up for three minutes so that everyone who has not read aloud reads to two partners.
  6. Delayed correction, three minutes: five sentences you heard, on the board, names removed. The class names the relationship in each and repairs the linker.
📺 On screen: the four stems and the three columns, both still on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write four true personal sentences with four different linkers, then have the endings guessed by classmates

Consolidation · the linking relay
teams of four
Why: Consolidation · the linking relay

WHAT WE DO: the Teacher's Book cooler turned into a twelve-minute team game

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Teams of four. Each team gets a strip of paper and a pen.
  2. Round one, three minutes: you call a relationship — PURPOSE — and every team writes a whole sentence about school or work using a linker of that type. First correct sentence read aloud takes the point; the sentence has to make sense as well as be grammatical, and you are the judge.
  3. Round two, three minutes: harder. You call the LINKER and the team has to produce a sentence in which it is the only word that works.
  4. Round three, four minutes and it is the one that teaches most: you read a sentence with the linker MISSING and the teams write all the linkers that would fit. One point per correct option — which means the teams that have understood the shapes score three where others score one.
  5. Two minutes: award the points, and for the last item name why each option worked. Then take the fast finishers' whereas sentences from earlier and let the class guess true or false.
📺 On screen: the running score and the three columns on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: every team writes every round, and the last round rewards knowing ALL the options rather than one

  • Subsidiary. Reading a grammar reference and testing it against examples; recycling the work verbs inside longer sentences; personalised production.
  • Personal. Three headed columns on the board all lesson — CONTRAST, PURPOSE, REASON — and every linker is written into one of them by a student, with its shape (+ clause or + infinitive) written beside it.

  • use so that with a clause and in order to with an infinitive
  • tell the difference between although and whereas

Structure
You'll have to be strong so that you can lift the heavy boxes. · Last year's students worked outside, whereas this time you'll be in the financial department. · As the school is in the north of the city, it takes about an hour from the centre.
Form
CONTRAST: although / whereas / while + clause · PURPOSE: so that + clause, in order to + infinitive · REASON: because / as + clause. All seven join two clauses; none of them takes a bare noun.
Pron. watch
calculate /ˈkælkjuleɪt/ and organise /ˈɔːɡənaɪz/ both take the stress on the FIRST syllable and the class will put it on the second · deal with runs together as /ˈdiːlwɪð/ in speech · although /ɔːlˈðəʊ/ has no /g/ sound in it at all, whatever the spelling promises · and in as and like the difference is heard in the stress: he works AS a teacher, he works LIKE a machine
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I studied hard so that I could pass» or «in order to pass»? → both, and they are not interchangeable in shape: so that + I could, in order to + pass.
«Although» and «whereas» — is one of them about surprise? → yes, although. Whereas just puts two things side by side.
«In order that I can go» — right? → no. In order TO go. The other one is so that.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«In order that», «for to get fit»The shape goes in the column next to the word, not in a rule: so that + CLAUSE, in order to + INFINITIVE. Then every correction is a point at the column.
They put the linker in and never check the relationshipEnforce the order of operations out loud: name the relationship FIRST, then choose. The Teacher's Book models this in exercise 3 and it is worth insisting on for the whole lesson.
The Grammar reference page gets skimmed and forgottenGive it a task, not a reading. Three minutes, in pairs, with one job: find ONE thing on page 165 that was not said in the lesson. Then take three findings to the floor.
Fast finishers everywhere in this lessonA standing task: write two sentences about a classmate using whereas — one true, one false. The class guesses which is which during the relay stage.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text) and tasks 32–38 — linkers are tested in both.

Contrast, purpose and reason are the three relationships the Russian paper asks a candidate to signal, and the six linking words of this lesson are the ones it swaps for each other: although against despite, because against because of, so that against in order to. The grammar underneath is the same one the class met three weeks ago with despite and unless — a clause or a noun — and saying so aloud is worth a minute, because it turns two isolated lessons into one rule.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
