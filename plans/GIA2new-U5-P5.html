🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The second grammar pair, and again the topic is named openly. The question form is built out of the affirmative they already know (the P3 poster is hanging right there), and the much/many rule gets its own colour on the same poster. Three mini-tests during the pair show growth inside a single lesson. The product is a town interview using ready-made questions — safe, and with the sheet in hand.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Yesterday you could SAY what's in a town. There's a skate park. There are three museums. Today you learn to ASK — and that's what you actually need when you arrive somewhere new. Is there a library near here? How many cinemas are there? How much traffic is there? Two little rules, one poster, and then you interview each other about the town you'd like to live in. Step by step — the questions are already on your sheet.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: yesterday you could say what a town has. Today we learn to ASK — that is what you actually need when you arrive somewhere new. Two rules, one poster, and then you interview each other using ready-made questions.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 the table is built together → ex.2–3 two passes → mini-test 1 with self-check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 line by line together. TB key: 1 Is · 2 isn't · 3 Are · 4 aren't. I add a third block to the poster: ? one / no count → IS THERE · ? many → ARE THERE.
  2. TB technique before ex.2: For each question — is the noun singular, plural or uncountable? Point at it first.
  3. ex.2 in pairs. TB key: 1 Is there · 2 Are there · 3 Are there · 4 Is there.
  4. ex.3 — short answers about themselves (TB key: Yes, there is./No, there isn't. and Yes, there are./No, there aren't.). Choral drill with the ↗ / ↘ intonation.
  5. MINI-TEST 1 (6 items, 2 minutes): Is there / Are there. The key is on the slide, they mark it themselves.

A5 grammar card — ASK ABOUT A TOWN: four question branches — Is there…? (one or uncountable) · Are there…? (plural) · How much…? (amount of an uncountable) · How many…? (number of countables), with short answers and the scale A lot. / Some. / Not much. / Not many.
  • Glue the card in; label each branch with when it is used.
  • Ask four questions about your own area — one per branch.
  • Answer along the scale, not with a single word.

crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 the rule with the poster as support → ex.6–7 practice → mini-test 2

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5: we look at the two examples in the table. TB key: 1 uncountable (How much) · 2 countable (How many). I add a fourth block to the poster, in colour.
  2. The TB strips technique: I hand out the words of the question on cards and the students physically move are there to the end: How many | cycle lanes | are there?
  3. ex.6 in pairs (TB key: 1 How many · 2 How much · 3 How much · 4 How many · 5 How many) — and we answer about our own town straight away. I talk the first item through out loud as the model (“traffic can't be counted → How much”), then Kagan RallyCoach: one pen per pair, the first explains their choice out loud and writes, the second looks at the poster and asks Can you count it?; at the next item they swap roles.
  4. ex.7 with the TB hint (you need an auxiliary verb!), we do the first two on the board. TB key: 1 How many brothers and sisters have you got? · 2 How much homework do you do at weekends? · 3 How much time do you spend watching TV? · 4 How many comics or magazines do you read? · 5 How much fruit do you eat every day?
  5. MINI-TEST 2 (6 items, 2 minutes): How much / How many. The key is on the slide.

PRODUCT · a town interview
pairs → stage

WHAT WE DO: the ladder: 8 ready-made questions on a sheet → we ask and note the answers → 3 lines of conclusion

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1: I hand out a sheet with eight questions (4 with Is/Are there + 2 with How many + 2 with How much) — the students add one of their own.
  2. Step 2 (ex.8): Interview your partner. Write short answers.
  3. Step 3 — the conclusion frame: In my partner's town there's … . There are … . There aren't any … . I think it's a good/quiet/busy place.
  4. Step 4: volunteers to the board with the sheet, three lines each. The audience: Thumbs up if your town is the same!
📺 On screen: the question sheet · the conclusion frame · the poster · timer
👦 Students do: they ask and answer with the sheet in hand. Expect: the interview runs briskly — the questions are ready, so they only have to think about the answer

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask Is there … ? / Are there … ? and give short answers, and choose How much or How many correctly, in a short interview about a town.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the question table; ex.2–4 three passes at the form; ex.5 the much/many rule with a prompt sheet; ex.6–8 their own questions and the interview; ex.9 Grammar round-up as a bonus for those who finish early.
  • Personal. The topic is named. The P3 poster hangs next to it — I build the new block onto it rather than starting from scratch. A mini-test after each block, self-checked against the key.
By the end, students can…
  • ask Is there … ? and Are there … ?
  • give short answers (Yes, there is. / No, there aren't.)
  • choose How much or How many by the noun
  • interview a partner about a town with 8 questions

Structure
Is there a sports centre? — Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. · Is there any street food? · Are there five football teams? — Yes, there are. / No, there aren't. · Are there any cycle lanes? · How much traffic is there? — A lot. / Some. / Not much. · How many cycle lanes are there? — A lot. / Some. / Not many.
Form
Is there … ? — with a singular countable and with an uncountable; Are there … ? — with a plural · in questions it is usually any, not some: Are there any juice bars? · How much — with uncountables (traffic, homework, time, fruit, public transport) · How many — with plural countables (libraries, skate parks, cinemas) · word order (the TB asks you to point with your finger): How many cycle lanes ARE THERE? — is there / are there go to the end of the question
Pron. watch
Is there /ɪz ðeə/ — run together · the question takes a RISING intonation: Is there a skate park? ↗ · with How much/many it falls ↘
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Are there a library?” — why doesn't it work? → one library: Is there a library?
“How much museums are there?” — correct it. → museums are counted: How MANY museums are there?
“How many homework do you do?” → homework is not counted: How MUCH homework do you do?

Assumptions: SB p.66, KTP pair 42 (grammar in both halves). The affirmative there is/are was closed off in P3 and is on the poster; what is new is the question, the short answer and much/many.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“Are there a library?”The technique: finger on the noun first (one or many?), then the first word of the question. We drill it on ten words in a row.
“How many homework”The P3 poster works here too: the uncountables are already in their column. I point at it: a word from there → How much.
Word order: “How many are there cinemas?”TB technique: I run my finger along the line — the noun goes straight after How many, and are there moves to the end. Strips of card with the words on them, which the students physically rearrange.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
