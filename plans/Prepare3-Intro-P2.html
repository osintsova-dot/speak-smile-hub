🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Produce, sort and use the everyday vocabulary of Prepare 2 across six topics — people and family · home and town · school · food · free time and sport · clothes and everyday things — in sentences rather than in lists.
What is genuinely hard
Recognising a word and producing it are two different abilities, and after ten weeks off only the first one survives. The group will understand almost everything today and produce about half of it — which feels like failure to them and is completely normal.
Typical error to expect
The word arrives in Russian first and then stops. Also the classic pair house / home, and lend / borrow. And in the placement slice: reading the question instead of the four options, which costs two or three marks every time.
Why the lesson is built this way
Nothing is given to them in the first half — not one word list. Everything is retrieval: they build the six word maps themselves, they write the Quiz-Quiz-Trade cards from their own maps, and they sort their own words. That is deliberate and it is the whole method: a word that has been pulled out of memory is remembered far better than a word that has been read off a list, and the gaps in what they produce are the data you came for. Then, in the middle of the lesson and not at the end, the measurement: thirty questions of the real Cambridge Placement Test, which is the only instrument in the building that can honestly answer «is this child in the right room» — including for the students who joined the school last week. It is placed in the middle so that the lesson does not END on a paper: the last stage is speaking, and they leave talking. ⚠️ There is no Prepare 2 coursebook in the school's materials, which is the other reason the first half is built out of the students rather than out of a book.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“You have not spoken English since June, and you think it has all gone. It has not — it is behind a door and the door is stiff. For forty minutes you are going to push it open yourselves: six topics, no lists from me, everything out of your own heads. Then thirty questions from Cambridge, which will tell you — not me, you — exactly where you are. No mark, nothing in the register. And then you will talk for six minutes straight.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, the first error feedback of the year, and the bridge into the six topics

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last lesson feedback, and it is the first feedback of the year. Four sentences on the board from your correction sheet, all of them said in this room on Tuesday, none of them with a name attached: Where you go in the summer? · Last summer I go to Kazan. · I stay there two weeks. · What you did there?
  3. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs, two minutes: one pen, one writes the repair and the other says why. Then take all four aloud. Name the single rule out of it: one past marker per sentence.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the lesson: All four of those are words you had. Today we go and get the rest of them back.
📺 On screen: the four sentences in chalk on the board — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: repair four sentences in pairs. Expect the missing did to be found fast and I stay there two weeks to be found slowly. Typical problem — they correct did you went back into did you go and then cannot say why; make them say it

Lesson frame
board
Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: name the language aim of the lesson and the standard an answer has to reach

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the six topics on the board as six headings and nothing else — no words under them yet.
  2. Explain the aim in 40 seconds: today the words come out of you, not out of me. Six topics, and by the end you will use twelve of them in true sentences.
  3. Name the measurement now rather than springing it later: In the middle of the lesson there are thirty questions from Cambridge. Fifteen minutes. No mark, nothing in the register — you mark it yourself and the number is yours.
  4. Set the bar: A word only scores if somebody in your team can put it in a sentence.
📺 On screen: the six topic headings in chalk — the board is the medium here and the headings stay up all lesson, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the six headings and start volunteering words before you have asked. Expect the announcement of the paper to lower the temperature for about ten seconds and then be forgotten — which is the reason for announcing it early

8–20′
Word maps · six topics in teams
teams of 3–4
Why: Retrieval, not re-teaching. Nothing is given to them: the words have to come out of their own heads, which is the only thing that actually strengthens the memory — and it also shows you, in eight minutes, which of the six topics is thin in this group.

WHAT WE DO: Kagan Jot Thoughts: six topics, everything out of their own heads, against the clock

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. One A3 sheet per team, folded into six. Kagan Jot Thoughts: everybody writes at the same time, one word per slip, no discussion, no repeats.
  2. Ninety seconds per topic, and call the change yourself. The six topics are the ones the syllabus names for this lesson:
    • People and family — aunt · cousin · neighbour · relative · friendly · funny · kind · lazy · polite · popular · quiet
    • Home and town — flat · balcony · ceiling · cupboard · stairs · post office · supermarket · library · museum · square
    • School — biology · chemistry · geography · history · ICT · maths · PE · physics · timetable · homework · exam
    • Food and meals — breakfast · butter · cream · fruit · vegetables · meal · snack · menu · delicious · order
    • Free time and sport — badminton · climbing · dancing · fishing · football · reading · skateboarding · swimming · a video game · join a club
    • Clothes and everyday things — cap · jacket · jumper · trainers · phone · charger · wallet · backpack · key · ticket
  3. ⚠️ The list above is for you, not for them: it is the minimum you should see coming back, drawn from what the placement questions actually test. Do not put it on the board.
  4. After the sixth topic: teams count their words and shout the number. Then the condition from the frame — Cross out every word nobody in your team can put in a sentence. The second number is the honest one.
  5. Walk round with your notebook and write down which topics came out thin. That is the real result of this stage and it is worth more to you than the paper.
📺 On screen: the six headings on the board and A3 paper on the desks — paper is deliberately the medium here so that the maps can be kept and reused in lesson 4, and nothing can fail, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write words simultaneously, then cut their own list down. Expect «school» and «free time» to be rich and «clothes and everyday things» to be thin. Typical problem: one student writes everything while the others watch — the Jot Thoughts rule is «everybody writes at once», so enforce it in the first thirty seconds

20–30′
Quiz-Quiz-Trade · on their own words
standing, whole class
Why: The cards are written by the class in the previous stage, so every card is a word somebody in the room could produce and somebody else could not. That is a far better set than any printed pack.

WHAT WE DO: the class's own words turned into cards and traded round the room

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Each team picks its ten best words and writes ten cards: the word on one side, a definition or an example sentence with a gap on the other. Four minutes.
  2. ⚠️ Enforce one thing while they write: The clue may not be a translation. A definition or a gapped sentence, nothing else. This is the part that does the learning.
  3. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade, five minutes: quiz your partner, get quizzed, swap cards, find somebody new. Six trades each.
  4. Freeze the room twice during the five minutes and take one card at random: Read your clue. Everybody — the word.
  5. Collect the cards at the end and keep them: they are the revision deck for lesson 3 and they cost you nothing to make.
📺 On screen: blank cards on the desks and the ten-card instruction on the board — cards and chalk are the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write ten cards, then mingle and trade. Expect the writing to be slower than the trading and the definitions to be poor at first — the two freezes fix that faster than any explanation. Typical error: clues that are translations; take one such card and rewrite it publicly
blank cards · marker pens · board
30–38′
Odd one out · and say why
pairs → class
Why: Sorting forces the meaning to be handled rather than the spelling. The «why» is the whole task: a student who can say why cap does not belong with ceiling, cupboard, balcony has the category, and the category is what holds the words.

WHAT WE DO: eight sets of four words, one of which does not belong — and the reason is the task

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Eight sets on the board, all built from words the class produced ten minutes ago. For example: ceiling · cupboard · balcony · cap · chemistry · physics · biology · timetable · aunt · cousin · neighbour · relative.
  2. Pairs, four minutes: cross out the odd one and write the reason in English. The answer is the reason, not the word.
  3. Kagan Numbered Heads Together to collect: agree in the team, then a number answers for the team.
  4. The aunt / cousin / neighbour / relative set is the one worth eight minutes on its own — relative is the category and the other three are members of it, except the neighbour. Let them find that.
  5. Fast finishers: build one more set of four for the class and put it on the board themselves.
📺 On screen: the eight sets written on the board — the board is the medium here and the sets come from the class's own paper, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with a written reason, then answer by number. Expect the sets to be solved fast and the reasons to be slow — the reason is the language. Typical error: the reason given in one word («different»); push for a full sentence every time

Move break
standing
Why: A stirrer that also forces one English sentence. Teenagers who have been sitting for forty minutes need to stand before the second half, or the second half dies.

WHAT WE DO: two minutes of movement and a switch from words to the paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up. I say a word — if it is food, hands up; if it is a school subject, sit down; if it is neither, stay still. Ten words, fast: snack · physics · trainers · butter · chemistry · wallet · breakfast · geography · charger · menu.
  2. Then everybody stands, chairs pushed in, and comes back to a clean desk — the desk has to be clear before the paper comes out, and doing it standing costs nothing.
📺 On screen: instructions by voice, nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: move on ten words and then clear their desks. Expect two mistakes on menu, which is worth one sentence afterwards. Typical problem: they stay standing and talking — the clean-desk instruction is what closes the stage

Measurement briefing
desks
Why: The four minutes that decide whether the next fifteen produce data or anxiety. The rules are said before the paper appears: no mark, no register, self-checked, and the second half is language nobody has taught you yet.

WHAT WE DO: the four minutes that decide whether the next fifteen produce data or anxiety

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Papers face down on the desks before you say anything. Then the rules, in this order and no other:
  2. Thirty questions. Fifteen minutes. Four options each — read all four before you choose. No mark. Nothing in the register. You will mark it yourself in ten minutes' time.
  3. Then the sentence that keeps the second half honest: ⚠️ Say this out loud in the staffroom, not in class: questions 11–20 are Prepare 3 language, which the group has NOT been taught yet. A score of two or three out of ten there is the normal, expected, correct result in the first week of September. It is not a bad mark, and it is never announced.
  4. One working rule: Thirty seconds per question. If you do not know it, guess and move on — a blank scores the same as a wrong answer, and the guess sometimes does not.
  5. Names on the sheets. Then Turn them over — go. and start the timer visibly.
📺 On screen: the four rules written on the board while you say them, and the visible timer — chalk and a timer are the medium here on purpose, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: listen with the paper face down. Expect one question about marks even though you have just answered it — answer it again, calmly, the same way. Typical problem: somebody starts early; that is what «face down» is for

⚡ Placement slice · questions 1–30
solo · silence
Why: A real Cambridge instrument used for exactly what it was built for: thirty questions, three levels, an official key and an official table. Fifteen minutes buys you a defensible answer to «is this child in the right room» for every student, including the ones who walked in from another school this week.

WHAT WE DO: the real Cambridge Placement Test, questions 1–30, fifteen minutes, silent mode

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silent mode, palm down. Walk the room slowly and answer nothing about content — only about procedure.
  2. Call the time at 7 minutes and at 12: Seven minutes gone. You should be near question fifteen. That single announcement saves three or four students from spending nine minutes on question six.
  3. ⚠️ Stop on the timer even if half the class is at question 24. An unfinished paper is honest data; an overrunning paper eats the speaking stage.
  4. Pens down, papers stay on the desk face up. Nobody moves and nobody compares.
  5. For your own reference while they work: course folder → tests → Prepare_2ed_3_Placement_test → «PRE - Placement Test final.pdf» (question sheet) · «PRE -Placement Test Student Answer Sheet.pdf» (answer sheet) · «PRE -Placement Test Teacher Answer Key.pdf» (key) · «PRE- Placement Test Teacher's Guide.pdf» (the rules below come from it word for word)
📺 On screen: the visible timer and nothing else on display — the room is deliberately quiet and free of anything projected for these fifteen minutes, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: work alone in silence for fifteen minutes. Expect questions 1–10 to go fast, 11–20 to slow down and 21–30 to be largely guessed — that is the designed shape of the instrument, not a failure. Typical problem: a student who stops at question 20 because «the rest is too hard»; tell them to guess and finish

Self-check + the personal gap list
solo → solo
Why: They mark their own paper against the printed key, and the number stays theirs. What leaves the room is not a score but a list of words they could not produce — which is a homework task that writes itself.

WHAT WE DO: marking your own paper against the official key, and turning it into a list

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the key, one per desk, and read it aloud at the same time in blocks of ten so nobody gets lost. Ticks and crosses only, in their own hand.
  2. Then the three numbers that matter, and they write all three on the sheet: out of 10 (questions 1–10) · out of 10 (11–20) · out of 30. Official placement table (out of 30 for our purposes): 0–9 → Prepare 1 · 10–18 → Prepare 2 · 19–27 → Prepare 3 · 28–30 → above Prepare 3.
  3. ⚠️ Do not read scores out and do not ask for them. Walk round with your notebook and write one line per name. That notebook is the only place any of this is recorded.
  4. The task that turns the paper into something useful: Look at your crosses in questions one to ten. Write the six topics down the side of a page and put a dot next to the topic each cross belongs to.
  5. Papers into the folder, key collected. That page is your revision plan for the year. Nobody else has yours.
📺 On screen: the printed key, one per desk, read aloud in blocks of ten (Backup: if the key has not been photocopied, read all thirty answers from the teacher's copy slowly, twice, and have them tick in pencil)
👦 Students do: mark their own thirty, write three numbers and build a topic list. Expect relief when they see that questions 11–20 were never theirs to know. Typical problem: two students comparing scores out loud — cut it immediately, and remind them of the rule that the number is private

🎯 Speaking out · one minute on each topic
pairs, standing
Why: The productive outcome: the words come back out in sentences, under a timer, to a partner. A word that has been said in a sentence today is a word that survives to lesson 4.

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share on all six topics — the words come back out in sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody up, new partner, not the one they sat with. Kagan Timed Pair Share: sixty seconds each way on one topic, then change partners.
  2. The six prompts, one per round: Somebody in my family… · My street and my town… · My timetable this year… · What I eat on a normal day… · What I do when there is nothing to do… · What is in my bag right now…
  3. The condition, and it is what makes this a language stage rather than a chat: Six words minimum from today's maps, and your partner counts them on their fingers.
  4. After round three, freeze and take two examples aloud — one good, one that stopped. Do not name the second one; rebuild it with the class instead.
  5. Log errors silently on the correction sheet; correct nothing aloud. The feedback slot is the first three minutes of the next lesson.
📺 On screen: the six prompts on the board, revealed one per round — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: speak for six minutes each across six rounds, counting each other's words. Expect the last two rounds to be the best English of the lesson — the words have been handled four times by then. Typical error: house for home and I have 13 years; both go on the sheet

Wrap-up + homework
circle
Why: Self-assessment against the aim they heard at the start, so the lesson closes the loop it opened. Today's homework is the September writing sample — it is set once and read in May.

WHAT WE DO: an “I can…” self-check, homework built from their own gap list, a teaser

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to the standard from the lesson frame: A word only counts if you can put it in a sentence. Six words in six seconds — go. Round the circle, one each, no repeats.
  2. Three lines on the board; students point at the ones that are true for them: I got fifteen words out in every topic. · I can say why a word does not belong in a group. · I know my own three weakest topics.
  3. Homework into the diary: finish your own gap list — six topics, five words each that you could not produce today, one true sentence each.
  4. Teaser: Next lesson: four tenses, an auction — and a hundred questions that are a map of everything we are going to do between now and May.
📺 On screen: an “I can…” slide with the three lines (Backup: the same three lines written on the board and students point at them)
👦 Students do: produce one word each, then rate themselves. Expect the third line to be ticked by everybody, which is the first honest self-knowledge of the year and worth saying so

Structure
My aunt is my mother's sister. · There's a post office next to the supermarket. · We have chemistry on Tuesdays and Fridays. · I usually have a snack after school. · I go climbing at the sports centre.
Form
Nothing new is taught today. The forms that carry the sentences are the present simple, there is / there are and have got — all three are Prepare 2 and all three come back properly in Unit 1.
Pron. watch
word stress in the longer words, because that is where a known word becomes unrecognisable: CHEM-is-try · TIME-ta-ble · su-per-MAR-ket · de-LI-cious · re-LA-tive.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«My aunt» — is that my mother's sister or my mother's mother? → her sister. The mother's mother is my grandmother.
house and home — same or different? → a house is a building, a home is where you live. A flat can be a home; it is not a house.
lend or borrow: «Can I ___ your pen?» → borrow. You borrow FROM somebody, you lend TO somebody.
ICQ before the placement slice: How many questions? (thirty) How long? (fifteen minutes) Do you write your name on it? (yes) Does it go in the register? (no)
«I don't know question 24 at all.» Is that a problem? → no. Questions 21–30 are Prepare 4 language. Not knowing them is the expected result.

Assumptions: Syllabus lesson 2. Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
