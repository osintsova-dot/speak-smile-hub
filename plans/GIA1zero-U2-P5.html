WHAT WE DO: “one good thing”; P4 blitz revision: a volley of questions with etiquette + Key phrases in chorus; bridge to Friday

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: One good thing — three seconds each!
  2. P4 revision rapid-fire: Have you got any cousins? Has your dad got a beard? — in chorus, FULL short answers; the etiquette buzzer is in the game.
  3. Key phrases echo: Who's — …this? That's a — …great photo!
  4. Bridge: Friday. Basketball practice. And your friend's train arrives at 6:35. Problem? An EMAIL solves it. A boy called Joshua shows how.
📺 On screen: mini-boards
👦 Students do: they answer politely, finish the phrases. Expected: clean. Typical error: “Yes, I've” — the class buzzes it themselves.

10–21′
Reading Joshua's email (model + skeleton)
Model · Joshua's email
desks

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: we read the letter: ex.1 (what it's about) → ex.2 paragraph order = a 5-move skeleton on the board → ex.3 fix the facts on mini-boards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 (90 sec): Who writes to whom? And WHY: plans, HELP or a new friend? (key: b — asks to help).
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everybody writes at the same time, boards face down, on the call No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up together — I see every answer, nobody can hide.
  3. Ex.2 → I build the skeleton on the board: ① hello → ② favour + problem → ③ describe Ben → ④ thanks → ⑤ sign (TB key: c·e·b·a·d).
  4. Ex.3 on mini-boards: all 5 sentences — false; we fix them with detail: Ben isn't from London — he's from MANCHESTER! The train is at 6:35!
  5. Move ② ritual: three fingers — WHAT exactly? WHEN? WHERE? — details decide it.
📺 On screen: SB p.32 · board: 5-move skeleton (stays up) · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they open up the model, fix the facts, memorise the skeleton. Expected: they repeat the skeleton in chorus. Typical error: mixing up moves ③/④ — the numbers on the board hold it.

21–30′
Discovery: the two jobs of the apostrophe
Discover · apostrophes (subskill)
board → desks

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: the two jobs of the apostrophe: the houses from P3 return; a hunt ex.4 (which one is the owner?) + ex.5 full forms

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I bring back the 2 houses: House 1: a missing letter (he's = he is · don't) · House 2: the owner (Ben's train).
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle under the timer, one move per person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. Hunt ex.4: Find EVERY apostrophe in the email. Which ONE shows the owner? (TB key: Ben's parents; trap: “Ben's 13” = Ben IS!).
  4. Ex.5 orally in a chain: contraction → full form (I hope you are well · I have got · Ben is 13 · He is not very tall · He has got…).
  5. CCQ: Ben's 13 — owner? — NO! Ben IS. House one!
📺 On screen: board: 2 houses (stay up) · SB p.32
👦 Students do: they sort each apostrophe into a house. Expected: the “Ben's 13” trap catches and teaches. Typical error: “Ben's parents = Ben is parents?!” — laughter + house 2.

30–38′
Contraction blitz on mini-boards
Drill-play · contraction blitz
desks · mini-boards

WHAT WE DO: blitz p.33 ex.6: 10 sentences → contractions on mini-boards in 10 sec each; streaks ×2

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 10-sec rounds: She is 14 → She's 14! · Do not talk → Don't talk! · I have got two cousins → I've got… — all 10 of ex.6.
  2. Boards UP! after each one; +1 for a clean one, a streak of 3 in a row = ×2.
  3. Rounds 6–10 — STANDING (a movement break before writing): a clean board = you sit down; the last one standing gets a choral rescue check.
  4. Apostrophe check in chorus on the tricky ones: aren't · hasn't · We're.
📺 On screen: SB p.33 ex.6 · mini-boards · timer
👦 Students do: everybody writes the contractions at speed at the same time. Expected: 8–10 clean for most. Typical error: a “did'nt-style” drift — freeze frame: the apostrophe goes where the letter disappeared.

WHAT WE DO: choosing an addressee and a problem from the bank; notes in keywords: problem + time/place + 3 details about the person

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK (ex.7): Pick from our bank: who to write to + why you're busy. Secret is fine!
  2. PREPARE: notes in WORDS (not sentences): problem · exact time+place · friend: age, hair (the hair rule!), 1 detail.
  3. ICQ: Sentences in notes? — No! Keywords! Ticks for THINK·PREPARE on the tracker.
📺 On screen: bank on the board · note sheets · step tracker
👦 Students do: they plan the letter in keywords. Expected: “granddad / feed cat / Friday 5 pm”. Typical error: they write the full text right away — “Notes first, email later!”

WHAT WE DO: portion 1 (moves ①–②, 6 min) → portion 2 (moves ③–⑤, 7 min); frames from the model on the board; support skeletons

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. WRITE portion 1 (6 min, timer): Moves one-two: Hi…! How are you? I want to ask you a favour… I'm busy on… EXACT time and place!
  2. WRITE portion 2 (7 min): Moves three-four-five: describe the person (he's got…, hair rule!), thanks, sign.
  3. I keep the frames from the model on the board; support skeletons with gaps — for the ones flagged at the blitz.
  4. A tick for WRITE on the tracker.
📺 On screen: board: frames (Hi… · I want to ask you a favour · He's got…) · timers · support skeletons
👦 Students do: they write step by step in two passes. Expected: 5–7 sentences. Typical error: they freeze on the first phrase — a finger on the frame “Hi…! How are you?” — take it and move on.
💪 Support: a skeleton with the first words of each move. ⭐ For the fast ones: +1 detail, +1 question to the addressee.		notebooks · timers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write a 5–7 sentence favour email by steps with correct apostrophes (both jobs) and have got — and peer-check it by 4 boxes.
  • Subsidiary. Joshua model → 5-move skeleton; subskill: apostrophes (possession vs contraction); contraction blitz; writing cycle THINK→PREPARE→WRITE→CHECK in timed portions; «market» vote; Quick review lightning = the map of the test.
  • Personal. Writing pair — never 20 minutes of silence: portions with timers, market with chips. The two jobs of the apostrophe get their own visual (2 houses from P3 return!). Step tracker ritual continues.
By the end, students can…
  • reproduce the 5-move email skeleton
  • tell apostrophe jobs apart (owner / verb)
  • write a favour email in portions
  • peer-check by 4 concrete boxes

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Ben's 13 — owner or missing letter?” → missing letter: Ben IS 13.
“Do we write the email in one go?” → No — two portions with a timer.
“In peer review do we say ‘bad’?” → No — check the 4 boxes and help fix.

Assumptions: have got (all forms) + possessive 's live after P3–P4; the favour situation is real for kids (meet someone, feed a cat, bring keys). Apostrophe's two jobs met separately — today unified.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
