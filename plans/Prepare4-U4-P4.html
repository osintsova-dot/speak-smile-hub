🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Own the eight travel phrasal verbs (set off, take off, check in, get back…), complete a notes card from a heard talk, and make, accept and reject suggestions with the Prepare-to-speak phrases in a real planning conversation.
What is genuinely hard
The phrasal verbs collide with Unit 7's set (get ON vs get IN vs get BACK — three different verbs to a Russian ear). The listening asks for 1–2 exact words against planted distractors (Cambodia, Peru — past destinations). And suggesting is a social skill: teenagers either command («Let's go!») or go silent — the soft machinery (Why don't we…? · I'm not sure…) is effectively new.
Typical error to expect
Our plane took off... I mean took? flew? — the particle dropped or swapped (get back/get on, take off/take away): phrasal verbs stored as verb + particle chunks or not at all [A2/B1]
A one-word reply («Yes.», «Canada.») does not count as an answer at all: an answer needs a verb in it [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The phrasal-verb work opens the lesson because it is light and winnable — energy for the listening half. The listening gets the full three-pass treatment with PREDICTION first (what KIND of word fits each gap?) because prediction is the sub-skill that separates listeners from guessers; the distractors are named to the teacher, never to the class. The speaking is built as two rounds with an error clinic in between — the improvement between rounds is audible within one lesson, which is the cheapest motivation there is. The suggestion phrases are compulsory: a plan made without them scores zero in this room.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“First prize: a trip to Canada. To win it you have to LISTEN — every detail counts. Then I teach you the four moves of the perfect suggestion: how to propose, how to push back politely, how to close the deal. By the end you'll plan a weekend in pairs like two people who actually like each other.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a three-sentence future-form repair, and the bridge into the travel verbs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — future-forms clinic. Three anonymous sentences from lesson 3's log on the board (a dropped BE, a will-for-arrangement, an airport-word misspelling). Pairs find and fix, hands up, repair together.
  3. Point at the journey line still on the side board: Today we fly it — set off, take off, get back.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the page: Page forty-eight, bottom half. Eight verbs that move — then a competition, and the art of the suggestion.
📺 On screen: three faulty sentences in chalk — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hunt and fix in pairs. Expect the dropped BE to be found first, the will-slip last. The point is closure on lesson 3, not re-teaching

WHAT WE DO: name the aim and hang the suggestion menu skeleton

Vocabulary · travel phrasal verbs
pairs → class
Why: Eight travel verbs with meanings chosen (not guessed), irregular pasts and the two opposite pairs (take off/land, check in/check out) — the full kit in one stage because the listening and speaking need it live.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: choose the meanings + pasts + the two opposite pairs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs: eight verbs, meaning a or b — 3 minutes, justify from the sentence. Key: meanings: 1b going away = visiting another place · 2b set off = started a journey · 3b getting back = returning · 4a take off = leave the ground · 5a check in · 6a getting in = arriving · 7a look around · 8b pick up = collect · irregular pasts: go–went, set–set, get–got, take–took · opposites: take off ↔ land, check in ↔ check out.
  2. Harvest onto the board: the pasts (set–set, take–took, go–went, get–got) and the OPPOSITES boxed: take off ↔ land, check in ↔ check out.
  3. The three-question anchor for the get-family: arriving? IN. friends? ON. returning? BACK.
  4. Call-and-response, sixty seconds: you say the meaning, the class shouts the verb — started the journey? — SET OFF! collect someone? — PICK UP!
📺 On screen: the eight verbs, pasts and opposites land on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: choose, justify, drill. Expect get in to collide with Unit 7's get on — the anchor exists for exactly that. Typical error: the plane took UP

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 complete + ex.3: the questions asked for real

Listening · the competition
pairs → solo
Why: Three passes, three depths: predict WHAT KIND of word fits each gap, then complete, then check — the same recording trains prediction, listening for specifics and self-correction.

WHAT WE DO: three passes: predict the word-types → complete → correct

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 predict, pairs, 1 minute: the photo — where is the skier? what is he doing? where is he going?
  2. ex.2 — the prediction pass, the heart of the stage: for each of the six gaps, decide WHAT KIND of word fits. Pairs, 3 minutes. Key: what kind of information fits each gap: 1 a place name · 2 a period of time · 3 something of interest when you travel · 4 a number · 5 a month · 6 personal information (a phone number, a photo, an address).
  3. Pass 1, track 37, ex.3: complete the notes — 1–2 words or a number per gap. Solo, in silence.
  4. Compare with a partner WITHOUT changing anything yet.
  5. Pass 2, track 37 again, ex.4: correct and confirm. Key: 1 Canada · 2 weeks · 3 (the) culture · 4 1,500 · 5 February · 6 email address.
  6. Check letter by letter on the board — an almost-right word is a wrong word: F-e-b-r-u-a-r-y.
  7. Mixed ability: TB mixed ability: pair weaker listeners with stronger ones for the note-taking pass.
📺 On screen: track 37 in the whiteboard app, played twice (Backup: read the competition talk from the audioscript, TB p.267 — steady pace, pause at the numbers; the task works identically)
👦 Students do: predict, listen, compare, correct. Expect Canada to be caught by everyone and «weeks»/«1,500» to need the second pass — the distractor countries will surface in weaker notes. Typical error: Februari — the letter-check catches it

Speaking model · making suggestions
solo → pairs
Why: A recorded model shows the bar before students have to reach it: the suggestion phrases are caught by ear (which one is NOT said?), sorted into the menu, then drilled with melody.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3 (track 38): catch the missing phrase, fill the menu, drill the melody

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 fast, pairs: why do the two questions use different tenses? Key: why the tenses differ in the ex.1 questions: present simple = regular weekend activities, present continuous = definite plans for THIS weekend — the lesson-3 columns, back in one line.
  2. Play track 38, ex.2: Two friends, one weekend — what do they decide? Key: they decide to go to the National Football Museum.
  3. ex.3 — the detective pass: One phrase in the Prepare to speak box is NEVER said. Which? Play track 38 again. Key: the phrase you do NOT hear on track 38: «… might be a better idea.».
  4. Fill the four menu boxes on the board from the box as the class dictates.
  5. Drill the menu with melody: Why don't we…? rises hopeful; I'm not sure… falls soft; Yes, let's do that! lands flat and final. Choral, then rows.
📺 On screen: track 38 in the whiteboard app, played twice (Backup: read the friends' dialogue from the audioscript, TB p.267, in two voices — the missing-phrase hunt works identically)
👦 Students do: listen for the decision, hunt the ghost phrase, drill the melody. Expect the ghost hunt to force real re-listening — that is its job. Typical error: flat robotic suggestions in the drill — replay the melody
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit8_Speaking_Suggestions_A5
A5 dialogue map — LET'S MAKE A PLAN: the suggestion bank (Let's… · Why don't we…? · How about + -ing…? · We could…) with two response banks — YES (That's a great idea. · Sounds good! · Yes, let's do that.) and CHANGE (I'm not sure. · I'd rather… · How about … instead?) — a four-turn model dialogue about a day trip and four idea cards (train · museum · picnic · shopping)

🎯 Speaking out · round 1
pairs
Why: The productive outcome: a real planning conversation with the phrases compulsory. Round 1 is the diagnosis — you log errors and phrase-dodging for the clinic.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4–5: three activities, one decision, phrases compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pairs pick THREE activities from the ex.4 box: ex.4 box: go walking in the mountains · hang out in a country park · visit a cool museum · go to the beach · go sightseeing
  2. One minute solo: one reason FOR each activity, one problem WITH one of them.
  3. Round 1, Kagan Timed Pair Share — 2 minutes each way: suggest, push back at least once, counter-suggest, DECIDE. Only the listener may close with Yes, let's do that. Details in going-to/continuous earn extra credit: We're visiting the museum on Saturday morning.
  4. You walk and log SILENTLY: commands instead of suggestions, missing decisions, particle slips.
  5. Close: Freeze. Round two is coming — after the clinic.
📺 On screen: the phrase menu and the activity box stay on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: suggest, push back, decide. Expect stiffness — normal for round 1; the clinic and the second round exist because of it. Typical error: the plan closes on the first idea — no pushback means no practice; remind the rule

Error clinic
class
Why: Delayed correction between the rounds: anonymous sentences from your sheet, repaired by the class — feedback lands while the conversation is warm and there is still a round 2 to use it in.

WHAT WE DO: delayed correction between the rounds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four sentences from your round-1 log on the board, anonymous: one command-instead-of-suggestion, one missing decision, one particle slip, one future-form slip.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs: fix all four, then hands up; repair together fast.
  3. One melody replay: the class pushes back on YOUR idea politely, chorally: I'm not sure — the problem with that is…
  4. Announce the round-2 delta: Same game, new partner, new activities — and I listen only for phrases and decisions.
📺 On screen: four faulty sentences in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: fix, drill the melody once, reset. Expect recognition laughs at the command sentence — anonymity keeps it safe

74–86′
🎯 Speaking out · round 2
new pairs
Why: Same task, new partner, higher bar: the improvement is audible within one lesson, which is the cheapest motivation there is.

WHAT WE DO: same task, new partner, audible improvement

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: stand, mix, new partner, sit.
  2. Round 2 — 2 minutes each way, three NEW activities from the box, phrases and a decision compulsory. You listen only for the delta.
  3. Two pairs perform their best 30 seconds to the class; the class reacts ONLY with menu phrases: That sounds great! · I'm not sure!
  4. Praise the delta by name: Round one you said «Museum. Let's go.» Round two you said «Why don't we visit the museum? We could go on Saturday morning.» — that's the lesson, done.
📺 On screen: the menu stays up; performing pairs stand where everyone sees them — the room is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: re-run the planning better, react with phrases. Expect audibly longer exchanges — say so out loud. Typical leftover: the decision dropped under speed; one reminder, not a correction

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«We set off at 6 am» — is that arriving or starting? → starting the journey. «We got in at midnight» → arriving.
«The plane took off» — up or down? → up; and the opposite? → landed.
«I'm not sure. The problem with that is…» — is the plan dead? → no: it invites a BETTER suggestion; that is why the phrase exists.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
