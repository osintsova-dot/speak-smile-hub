Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name and group 17 school subjects and talk about their timetable & preferences using “My favourite subject is (art) because…”.
  • Subsidiary — listening. Develop the sub-skill “Identifying the type of listening” (people · place · formality); listen for gist then detail to a British/Finnish teen conversation (Ansa) — SB p.17 ex.3, 5–7.
  • Subsidiary — pronunciation. Notice & produce silent letters in subject words (history, chemistry, geography, literature, design) — SB p.116.
  • Personal. New-starters line: go slow, always give picture + word-bank support, model the frame before every speaking turn, replay every listening at least twice.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name 17 school subjects and sort them into families (Science / Languages / Create / Digital / People & Places / PE);
  • give an opinion about subjects & days: “My favourite subject is… because…”;
  • identify the type of a listening text and answer gist + detail questions about it;
  • read silent letters correctly in subject words.

Structure
My favourite subject is (…) because (…) · I’d like to go to Ansa’s school because… · I prefer to study… · Which is your favourite day? — (Tuesday), because…
Form
opinion + reason: subject + because + clause. Light preview: Present-simple Yes/No — Do you have (art) on Monday? (sets up Pair 5).
Pron. watch
Silent letters: history, chemistry, geography, literature, design; stress: ge·OG·ra·phy, tech·NOL·o·gy. Also PE = /piː iː/, ICT = /aɪ siː tiː/.
Listening
Subskill — Identifying the type of listening (SB p.17 box): before you listen, think people (how many?), place (where?), language (formal / informal?).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is chemistry a science or a language?” → science. · “Do you write essays in maths or English literature?” → literature.
“Is PE a creative subject?” → no, it’s sport. · “Physics or PE — which one is science?” → physics.
Listening: “Two friends chatting — is that formal or informal?” → informal → so it’s a dialogue, not a radio interview.

Assumptions: countries, family, classroom words & Present-simple affirmative/negative are known from the Starter & Pairs 1–3. Learners read confidently but this line (new starters) still needs picture + word-bank scaffolds and slower audio. Days of the week known. Teams: 3–4 per team; mingle for the whole group.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Silent letters pronounced (“ge-o-GRA-phy”, “che-MIS-try”)Cross the silent letter out on the board; choral drill the family words slowly, then at speed.
Subjects confused: physics/PE, English language/literaturePresent as contrasting pairs; anchor with the subject-family map (notebook insert).
Audio feels too fast / learners freezeRead the questions first, play twice, pause between speakers; answers on mini-whiteboards so everyone commits.
Opinion answers drop the reasonFrame on board “… because …”; a turn only counts if it carries because.
Weaker starters can’t recall the word to speakWord-bank strip + subject cards stay on the desk during speaking; pair a stronger with a weaker learner.
Classroom management — signals
“3–2–1, eyes here.” — countdown to stop & face the board.
Boards up — “Show me.” → all raise mini-whiteboards at once = everyone answers.
Volume dial — hand raises/lowers = louder / quieter for pair work.
“Pens down.” — settle signal before instructions.
Mingle music — music on = move & talk; music off = freeze & find a partner.
ICQ first — one check question before every task (“Do you write or say it?”).

Pronunciation · silent letters
board → seats

Notice (SB p.116, Unit 1, ex.1). Play the words: history · design · technology · literature · chemistry · geography. Which letters do we NOT say? Learners cross them out on their card.

Rule. In English we sometimes write a letter but we don’t say it — a silent letter. Model & back-chain the tricky ones: …gra-phy → geo-gra-phy → GE-og-ra-phy. Choral ×3.

Extend (ex.2). Quick pair-hunt for silent letters: chocolate, cupboard, guitar, white, interesting. Boards up to check.
