🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The 17 subjects come in groups with gestures (sciences · arts · languages · humanities) — the whole list at once would drown them. For newcomers the listening is a two-step with a note table: first «who is talking and where» (a safe question — you can hardly be wrong), then the facts. The product is their own dream timetable for one day, from a frame.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Seventeen subjects — art, drama, ICT… Some you love, some you'd delete forever. Today you learn to name them ALL in English. Then — a real story: Ansa from Finland spent a month in a British school. Was it better? Worse? Listen and find out. And at the end — build your own dream Monday.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: 17 subjects in English + the real story of Ansa from Finland in a British school; at the end we build our own dream Monday from a frame.

Subjects in groups
desks → standing

WHAT WE DO: 4 groups with gestures (Kinesthetic Symbols): sciences → arts → languages → humanities + other; recognition → naming

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Group by group: picture → chorally → gesture: biology — leaf! drama — mask! ICT — type-type!
  2. Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols: I invent the gesture and show it first, the class copies it three times chorally — and from then on the word lives together with the gesture. The hand remembers faster than the head, which is why 17 words do not drown.
  3. Recognition: I show the gesture — you shout the subject.
  4. ex.1 p.16: find 2 sciences, 1 arts subject and 1 foreign language in the timetable (key: chemistry · drama/art · French).
  5. I take the key through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: I go through the first item as a model, then the crew confers (heads together), and the number I call out answers — so everyone is ready to explain.
  6. Spelling station on the 6 frequent ones: maths · history · art · music · PE · English.

👦 Students do: they take them in groups with gestures. Expect: ICT and citizenship are the newest

Listening · Ansa's story
screen · desks

WHAT WE DO: 3 steps: type of recording → facts with a note table → a favourite detail

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1: Don't catch facts. Just: how many people? where? friends or strangers? → check.
  2. Step 2 (note table: Finland | Britain): Fill what you hear. Second listening helps!
  3. Step 3: Which fact surprised you? Tell your crew.
  4. I give the model phrase on the board (I was surprised that…) and launch Kagan RoundRobin: round the circle inside the crew, one surprising fact each, and you may not repeat someone else's — so they listen to everybody right to the end of the circle.
  5. If they are drowning — a third play with pauses.
📺 On screen: audio · note table (Backup: I read the script aloud twice, at the same pace)
👦 Students do: they listen step by step without panicking. Expect: everyone gets the type, the facts come on the 2nd play

WB Unit 1
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name school subjects in groups, catch the type and key facts of a real conversation, and build a dream-Monday timetable with a frame.
  • Subsidiary. Subskill: the type of recording (people · place · register) as the first step of listening; a note table as support; comparing the schools from a frame.
  • Personal. Audio is scarier than a text — I play it three times if I have to; after the first play only safe questions (how many voices? where?). I never ask for a fact after the 1st listening.
By the end, students can…
  • name 17 school subjects (by groups, with gestures)
  • say who is talking and where before catching facts
  • catch 3+ facts from a conversation with a note table
  • build and present a dream-Monday with a frame

Spelling of chemistry/geographyThe spelling station covers only the 6 frequent ones; the rest stay at recognition.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
