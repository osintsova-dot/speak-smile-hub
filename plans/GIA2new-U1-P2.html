Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read an online article for gist and detail and to apply the subskill “Predicting from the title and the pictures” before reading (SB pp.14–15, “A day in the life of an online student”).
  • Subsidiary. Recycle daily-routine lexis from P1 in reading context; learn word-work vocab (grades · subjects · library · log on · classmates · school trips); notice Present simple in context; critical thinking — compare online vs traditional school and justify an opinion.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; give an ICQ before every task; pre-teach hard words & hand out sentence frames so every newcomer can speak (supportive tone, easy stars).
By the end, pupils can…
  • predict a text's content from its title & pictures;
  • read for gist, then re-read for detail (True/False, complete sentences);
  • use the 6 word-work items in a sentence;
  • give & justify an opinion: online vs traditional school.

Reading subskill
Predicting from the title and the pictures — look at photos + title before you read (SB p.14, Subskill box).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Do we read the whole text first, or look at the pictures first?” → pictures & title first.
“If I don't know a word, do I stop and give up?” → no → read around it, guess from context.
“Classmates — people in your class or your teachers?” → students in your class.

Assumptions: daily-routine verbs met in P1 (lessons 9–10, pp.12–13); pupils decode short texts but the newcomers line needs vocab pre-taught & frames. Present simple is recognised in context only — it is formally taught in P3, so don't drill forms here. Groups: pairs for reading; teams of 3–4 for word games (10=3+3+4 · 12=3×4 · 8=2×4).

Subskill: Predicting
board → pairs

Read the Subskill box (SB p.14). Good readers look at the title and pictures BEFORE they read. It helps you guess what's in the text.

I Do — think-aloud (books on p.15, text still covered). Model your own thinking: Hmm… the title says “A day in the life of an ONLINE student”. I see a clock — Monday 11 a.m. — and a girl with headphones at a laptop at home. So I predict… this text is about studying from home during the day.

We Do → You Do — SB p.14 ex.3. In pairs, answer the 3 prediction questions from title + pictures: 1 What day and time is it? 2 Why are the students at home? 3 Which daily routines do you think they do — and which not? Take 2–3 predictions on the board. Let's see if you're right! — do NOT confirm yet.

Critical thinking · discuss
pairs → corners

SB p.15 “Critical thinking / Super Skills” — frames on the board. Give the sentence frames first: One advantage of an online school is… · A disadvantage is… · A (traditional) school is better because…

1 Understand. ▶ Think–Pair–Share with a mini Venn (online / both / traditional): Find two things that are the SAME and two that are DIFFERENT. 1 min think, 2 min pairs, share.

2 Evaluate. Which things are an ADVANTAGE of online school? Which is a DISADVANTAGE? Pairs sort, using a frame each turn (RallyRobin).

3 Create → mini-debate. ▶ Corners: Online school or traditional school — which would YOU choose? Go to your corner. Each corner prepares three reasons with the frames; 2–3 speakers per side share. Support, don't score — everyone who uses a frame earns a star.

① PREDICT box — “From the title + pictures I guessed…” (re-write today's prediction, tick ✓ if it came true). ② Venn — online / both / traditional (2 items each, from the discussion). ③ Word bank — glue the 6 word-work words with a mini-picture. ④ My opinion — one frame sentence: I think ___ school is better because ___.

Circulate, help newcomers; this is the writing they carry home from a reading lesson.
