🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): For newcomers the mission is simpler and about themselves: build your own day in English and see that elsewhere it is done differently. That gives them a safe product (I talk about myself, from a model, with a sheet in my hand) and onboarding at the same time: today they see for the first time that a lesson ends with a product made by the crew. Comparing countries is a bridge to curiosity, not a knowledge test.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at the map. In France — two hours for lunch. In Ghana — school at four in the morning. Around the world school days are VERY different. Today, two things: your crew learns about ONE country — and each of you builds your own day in English, six lines. At the end, speakers present: my day + one difference from our country. You can hold your notes — this is not a test. Let's start with MY day…” (and I show the model on the timeline straight away)
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Two things today: we find out how children learn in one country of the world, and we build our own day in English — six lines. At the end we present with the sheet in our hands. This is not a test.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can describe their school day in 6 lines using 15 daily-routine collocations, and name two differences between their day and a school day abroad.
  • Subsidiary. Onboarding into the Get Involved format: crews, roles, score, «the product of the pair». The WDYT map on p.12 with a prompt sheet; Video skills (Japan) with a task set before watching.
  • Personal. I never put anyone on the spot without a draft. Every speaking task comes AFTER the written fixing. The word bank stays on screen for the whole lesson.
By the end, students can…
  • say and write 15 daily-routine phrases
  • describe my school day in 6 sentences
  • understand key facts about school days in other countries
  • work in a crew: role, score, product

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«I wake up or I get up — is it the same thing?» → wake up = your eyes open, get up = you leave the bed.
«have breakfast or have THE breakfast?» → no article, it is a fixed collocation.
«Can the day go in any order?» → no: the timeline (morning → afternoon → evening) holds the story together.

Assumptions: Students joining from outside: the language is roughly A1+, but the format (crews / score / product) is new to them. The first pair of the unit is onboarding as well as teaching. The pace is slower than with «ours» — that is normal.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Afraid to speak in front of strangersThe rule of the pair: first we write in the crew, then we read to a partner, and only volunteers with a finished text go to the board.
They do not understand the format (score, roles)The first 6 minutes are «how our lesson works» with examples; I hand out the roles myself, in simple words.
They ignore the word bank and go silentThe word bank stays on screen for the whole lesson; use the «look up» gesture instead of feeding them the word.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
