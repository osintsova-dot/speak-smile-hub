A2 teens (~12–14, readers) · 90 min · full skills cycle (lead-in → present → practice → prepositions → production → video) · desks + open floor
Week 14.09–20.09 · Pair 5 (KTP) · SB pp.12–13✨ NEW group · line GIA2new~10–12 pupils · pairs + 3 teams🧩 Brain-break ~45′
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name ~18 daily-routine actions and talk about their typical day in the present simple, using time prepositions at / in / on — e.g. I get up at half past six. On Mondays I start school at eight.
  • Subsidiary. Reading for gist & detail (a Chinese student's day, SB p.13); viewing skills (vlog, SB ex.9); speaking fluency (tell a partner about your day) supported by heavy→light sentence frames; time prepositions at / in / on presented in context.
  • Personal. New group — keep it low-anxiety: every open answer is rehearsed in a pair first; model on the board before I ask; keep the day-strip visible as a permanent support; praise attempts, not just accuracy.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say and understand the daily-routine collocations (get up, have a shower, pack my bag…);
  • order the actions of a typical day and put times to them;
  • choose at / in / on correctly for times, parts of day and days;
  • speak for ~30 sec about their own day using the frames.

Structure
Present simple in context: I get up at seven. · She starts school at eight.  ·  Time: at + time · in + part of day · on + day(s) · BUT at night.
Form
Chunks, not single words: have/go/get + noun (have breakfast · go to bed · get dressed). 3rd-person -s in context (gets, has, does) — light touch, grammar comes in Pair 3.
Pron. watch
wake /weɪk/ vs walk /wɔːk/; linked get_up /ˈɡe.tʌp/; weak a in have a shower; at is weak /ət/ before a time.
Register
Everyday chunks — this is talking-about-your-life language, informal & personal. Push for full sentences, not one word.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“7 o'clock — is that a time or a day?” → a time → at 7 o'clock.
“the morning — a part of the day?” → yes → in the morning.
“Monday — a day?” → yes → on Monday.
“And night?” → the exception → at night (not in night).

Assumptions: pupils can tell the time and know numbers; know the present-simple I-form and a few routine verbs (get up, go to school) from Starter; can read short texts. New line: slower pace, more modelling, frames stay on the board; times/clock visuals provided so telling-the-time doesn't block the language. Teams: pairs for talk, 3 teams of 3–4 for the games.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Wrong collocation verb (“make breakfast”, “do my bed”)Teach as fixed chunks on the day-strip; colour have · go · get · make · do and drill the whole phrase.
Preposition overgeneralised (“in Monday”, “in night”)Rule box + colour code (at=blue, in=green, on=red); Quiz-Quiz-Trade forces repeated choices.
3rd-person -s dropped when talking about othersRecast, don't lecture: “She gets up…”; grammar is Pair 3 — keep it light here.
New group — silence / fear of speakingAlways rehearse in a pair before open class; give the heavy frame first, remove support gradually.
Telling-the-time gaps slow the talkHand out clock/time cards; accept “at 7” before “at quarter past seven”.

Volume control — raise/lower arm = louder/quieter after a mingle.

0–8′
Lead-in · Warm-up
board → pairs

Hook — school days around the world (SB p.12). Books shut. Project / read out four facts: In France we have two hours for lunch. In Finland, less than four hours of class a day. In Brazil some students start school at 7 and finish at 12. In Ghana I wake up at four and walk 6 km to school.

▶ Mix–Pair–Share: Stand up, find a partner. Which school day would YOU like — and why? 40 sec each way, swap once. Take 2–3 ideas open class.

Draw out the theme: Every school day is different. Today we learn to talk about your day — from wake up to go to sleep.

10–24′
Presentation · Vocabulary
board → desks

I do. Hold up U1 routine cards one by one. Model your own morning with gesture: I wake up. I get up. I have a shower. I brush my teeth. I have breakfast. I pack my bag. Chorus each chunk ×2, then a few pupils individually.

Tier the chunks (say it as you group them). Point out the “engine” verbs: have (a shower · breakfast · a break · lunch · dinner), go (to school · home · to bed · to sleep), get (up · dressed · changed), plus make my bed · do homework · brush · clean · pack · tidy · wake · start · finish. The verb and the word travel together — learn the whole chunk.

We do — sort by time of day (SB p.12 ex.1). Three columns on the board: Morning · Afternoon · Evening. ▶ Numbered Heads: teams decide where each action goes; a numbered pupil comes up and places the card. When do you usually do homework? Morning, afternoon or evening? (Accept differences — routines vary.)

Check. CCQs on two tricky pairs: “get up” or “wake up” first? (wake up → get up) · “have breakfast” or “make breakfast”? (have breakfast — fixed chunk).

24–32′
📓 Interactive notebook
desks

Glue-in day strip — MY SCHOOL DAY (anchor for the whole unit). Hand out the A5 strip: four blocks — MORNING (wake up · get up · make my bed · have a shower · brush my hair · clean my teeth · get dressed · have breakfast · pack my bag · go to school) · AT SCHOOL (start school · have a break · have lunch · finish school) · AFTER SCHOOL (go home · get changed · tidy my room · do homework · have dinner) · NIGHT (go to bed · go to sleep), with the prepositions at 7:00 · in the morning · on Monday.

Steps: 1) Glue the day strip in — it holds the whole unit. 2) Pencil your own time next to every action. 3) Read your day aloud in a whisper, watching at / in / on. 4) Cross out two actions that never happen in your day.

New-line support: this strip stays open for the rest of the lesson — pupils lean on it in every speaking task. No memorising under pressure.

32–42′
Controlled practice · Living day-line
open floor, teams

Up you get — we're going to build a day. Give each pupil 1–2 routine cards. ▶ Living day-line: the team lines up physically in the order of a typical day (SB p.13 ex.6 idea): wake up → get up → … → go to sleep. Each pupil says their action aloud as they slot in: I make my bed… then I have a shower…

Add the times (SB p.13 ex.5). Second round: hand out time cards; pupils re-line to match times to actions, saying I wake up at quarter past six. Fastest correct line wins the round.

Everyone active: no one watches — each pupil holds a card, speaks, and moves. Reset with Freeze! if it gets loud.

open floor

▶ Mime & Guess (teams): one pupil draws a routine card and mimes it; their team calls the full sentence — You clean your teeth! / She has a shower! — not a single word. Correct sentence = a point. Fast rotation so everyone mimes once. Energiser + hidden drill of the chunks.

48–62′
Language in context · at / in / on
board → mingle

Notice the rule (SB p.13 rule box). Put three model sentences on the board and underline the prepositions: I get up at seven. I clean my teeth in the morning. We finish school early on Fridays. Elicit the pattern → build the box together: at + time · in + part of day · on + day(s) · BUT at night. Colour-code (at/in/on). Run the CCQs from the Target-language card.

Controlled (SB p.13 ex.7). Pupils complete the five sentences with at / in / on in pairs, then check with ▶ RallyCoach (one writes, one coaches). Board-check answers, drill the weak at /ət/.

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (mingle). Each pupil gets a card: “___ Monday morning”, “___ half past six”, “___ the evening”, “___ night”… Mix, pair, quiz your partner, coach if needed, trade cards, find a new partner. 3–4 minutes — every pupil keeps choosing at/in/on.

62–78′
Freer production · My typical day
desks → pairs

Model first (I do). Tell your own day in 4–5 sentences using the day-strip and the prepositions: On schooldays I wake up at quarter past six. In the morning I have a shower and pack my bag. I start school at eight and finish at three…

Frames — heavy → light (new line: start supported, then strip it away). Give pupils 2 min to prepare aloud with the frame that fits them:

• Heavy: On schooldays I wake up at [___]. In the morning I [___] and [___]. I start school at [___]. In the evening I [___]. I go to bed at [___].
• Mid: I [___] at [___], and then I [___]. On [___] I [___].
• Light: just the day-strip + at / in / on — no frame.

▶ Timed Pair Share (SB p.13 ex.8): A speaks ~40 sec about their day, B listens and asks one question; swap. Then B reports one thing back: What is the same? What is different? Take 2–3 to open class. Note good/faulty language for feedback.

Video skills · consolidation
screen

Watch (SB p.13 ex.9). Watch the vlog. Which daily activities do you see the students do? Tick them on your day-strip as you spot them. Play once; pupils tick. Compare in pairs, then class.

Quick discuss (SB ex.10). How is this school day the same as yours? How is it different? One sentence each in pairs — recycles the target chunks in a settler after the mingle.

85–90′
Feedback · Reflection · Exit
desks → door

Feedback. Put 3 lines you heard on the board — 2 good, 1 to fix (e.g. I go to bed in night ✗ → at night ✓). Pupils correct the one error in pairs. Praise the good chunks.

Exit ticket (at the door). Each pupil says one true sentence about their day with at / in / on: I do homework in the evening. / I have a break at eleven. Correct → out.

Great first day of the unit — your day-strip is your map for everything next.
