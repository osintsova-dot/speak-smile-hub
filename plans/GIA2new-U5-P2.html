🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The first long text of the unit. For newcomers it is taken apart in chunks: first six new words BEFORE the text (otherwise they drown in it), then prediction from the photos and the flags, then the text one dish per paragraph, and only after that the questions. The subskill (identifying key words) is taught as a physical action: underline it in the task → find it in the text. The product is four lines of opinion from a frame — safe and doable.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Five dishes you must try before you die. Chips with cheese and hot sauce. A cake with no chocolate in it. Chicken from the street in Jamaica. Purple ice cream. Noodles a government invented. We read about all five — one at a time, step by step — and at the end each of you says which one you'd try and which one you'd never touch. Four lines. The frame is on the screen. First, six words you'll need…”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: five dishes you “must try”: chips with cheese, a cake with no chocolate, street chicken, purple ice cream, and noodles invented by a government. We read one dish at a time, and at the end everyone says four lines: what they would try and what they never would.

WHAT WE DO: six new words before reading: the definitions of ex.7 + the examples of ex.8

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The TB technique the other way round (for newcomers): first I show the word + a picture/gesture, then the definition. delicious — mmm! · mix — stir! · smell — nose! · takeaway — box in your hand! · add — one more spoon! · food stalls — street market!
  2. ex.7 in pairs, the definitions are already familiar. TB key: 1 delicious · 2 mix · 3 smell · 4 takeaway · 5 add · 6 food stalls.
  3. ex.8 in writing, then checked. TB key: 1 delicious · 2 takeaway · 3 smell · 4 mix · 5 add · 6 food stalls.
  4. TB hint: What TYPE of word fits in each gap — a thing, an action, or a describing word?
  5. Consolidation: first I demonstrate the move myself with one card (I ask a student, answer for them, swap), then Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: everyone holds a “word — picture” card, quiz your partner — answer theirs — trade cards — move on. You may peek at the definition on the back, there is no shame in that.
📺 On screen: SB p.62 ex.7–8 · word cards · word bank
👦 Students do: they take the words before the text — inside the text the words are already theirs. Expect: takeaway and food stalls stick through the gesture

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 fast reading (which dish is in which photo) → ex.5 the table, one dish at a time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Comprehension check: facts with the line
Comprehension check
crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.6 (track 31): five statements, all of them false — we correct them with a hint

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I do the first correction out loud myself, finger on the line: 'Poutine is good in summer' — find the word 'winter'. There it is.
  2. TB hint: Look at the little words: 'there isn't any chocolate', 'there aren't any chips'. They carry the answer.
  3. The other four in crews via Kagan Numbered Heads Together: the crew hunts for the line of evidence and agrees on the correction, everyone puts a finger on that line — the number I call answers. The rule: if the whole table is not ready, nobody is ready. TB key: 1 it is a winter dish · 2 there is NO chocolate in pastel de nata (a cake made of eggs and butter) · 3 jerk chicken is eaten with rice and peas, not with chips · 4 halo-halo is BETTER than ice cream · 5 beef and chicken are an optional extra.
  4. ex.9 quickly: Change the underlined words so the sentences are TRUE for you.
📺 On screen: SB pp.62–63 ex.6, 9 · audio track 31 (backup — I read the guide's text aloud myself and the students follow the line with a finger)
👦 Students do: they correct with the line in front of them. Expect: no.4 and no.5 are hard — we do them together, and that is fine

PRODUCT · my opinion about a dish
solo → crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: the ladder: choose a dish → notes → 4 lines from the frame → read it in the crew → volunteers at the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1: Which dish would you try? Which one never? Notes only.
  2. Step 2 — the frame on the screen: I'd like to try … . It's from … . There's … in it. I'd never try … because …
  3. Step 3 — Kagan RoundRobin: round the crew clockwise, everyone reads their four lines off the sheet, no interrupting. Once the circle is done the crew ticks each other for clarity.
  4. Step 4: volunteers to the board with the sheet. The audience: Thumbs up if you agree!
  5. Critical thinking 1–2 briefly: Put the five dishes in order — favourite to least favourite.
📺 On screen: the frame · word bank · timer
👦 Students do: they write from the frame and then speak — the fear is gone. Expect: everyone has 4 lines

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can find specific information in a guidebook using key words, use 6 new words (delicious, mix, smell, takeaway, add, food stalls), and say in 4 lines which dish they'd try and why.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 track 30 (five food descriptions) with a prompt sheet; ex.3 flags and countries; ex.5 the “dish — country — ingredients” table in chunks; ex.6 correcting statements with a hint; ex.7–9 Word work; Critical thinking 1–2.
  • Personal. I give the words BEFORE the text, not after. Every paragraph is read with a concrete task — “read in order to find”, not “read”.
By the end, students can…
  • underline key words in a task before reading
  • fill a table with dish, country and ingredients
  • use six new words in a sentence
  • say in 4 lines which dish I'd try and why

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
smell — with your nose or with your tongue? → with your nose.
takeaway — is it a restaurant? → no: food you carry away to eat somewhere else.
food stalls — is it a shop? → no: a stand in the street or at a market.

Assumptions: SB pp.62–63, KTP pair 39. The text is long, but it splits into five independent chunks — one per dish. All 6 Word work items are in the official Wordlist.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“The text is too long”We read one dish at a time; after each one, one line goes into the table. Five small wins instead of one big task.
The new words drown in the textThe six Word work items are dealt with BEFORE reading, through pictures and definitions — in the text they are already recognisable.
They do not get ex.6 (“every sentence is false”)I do the first correction out loud myself and put my finger on the line that proves it. TB hint: look for the negatives there isn't / there aren't.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
