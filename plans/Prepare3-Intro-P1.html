🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Say five true things about yourself in full sentences and ask another person three questions about their summer — the present simple for what is always true, the past simple for what happened in July.
What is genuinely hard
Not the grammar. The hard part is the size of the answer: after ten weeks of silence a teenager answers Sochi and stops, and every following lesson of the year inherits that habit. The second hard part is that half the room may not know the other half's names.
Typical error to expect
I go to Sochi for a finished July, and the missing auxiliary in the question — Where you go? Neither is corrected on the spot today: both go on your correction sheet and come back as the first three minutes of lesson 2.
Why the lesson is built this way
This is the only lesson of the year in which the group itself is the material, and it is built backwards from a single question: what must be true in this room by October? Three things — the room speaks English, nobody is ever exposed alone, and an answer has a verb in it. So the contract is written by the class rather than announced by the teacher, the score is explained down to its three conditions, and the four Kagan structures are named out loud so that from lesson 4 each of them costs one sentence instead of ninety seconds. The book appears only in the second half, and even then as a hunt rather than a tour: SB p.4–9 answers six questions about the year faster than any teacher can. No test today, no paper, no measurement of any kind — that starts next lesson, and saying so out loud is part of the design.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Seventy-two lessons, twenty units, and I do not yet know what any of you can do. Today none of that gets measured. Today we do three things: you tell six different people about your summer, we write down the four rules this room runs on, and you find out what is actually in this book. By the end you will be able to say five true things about yourself without stopping — and that is the skill the whole year is built on.”

0–5′
Opening · the door and the first sentence
door → desks
Why: The first minute of the year sets the language of the room for nine months. If the first thing every student says in this doorway is English, the room is English by default and you never have to win that argument again.

WHAT WE DO: meet them at the door, one English sentence each before anybody sits down

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand in the doorway. Nobody comes in silently: one question each, one answer, in English. Hello! Say one thing you did this summer — one sentence.
  2. Accept anything with a verb in it. If the answer is one word, give the sentence and have it repeated: Swimming. Good — «I went swimming». Say it. Then in they come.
  3. On the board while they sit down, in your own handwriting: Welcome back. 72 lessons. 20 units. Let's start.
  4. Bridge (30 seconds): Everybody in this room has just spoken English. That is how every lesson this year starts.
📺 On screen: the three lines written up in chalk as they come in — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: produce one sentence each at the door. Expect four or five one-word answers out of twelve and expect them to repeat the full sentence happily. Typical error: I go to my grandmother for a finished July — log it, do not correct it
board · teacher's correction sheet
5–8′
Lesson frame
board
Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: name the language aim of the lesson and the standard an answer has to reach

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write one sentence on the board and leave it there all lesson: An answer has a verb in it.
  2. Explain the aim in 40 seconds: today you will tell six different people about your summer, we will write the four rules this room runs on, and you will find out what is inside this book.
  3. Say what today is NOT: Nothing is measured today. Nothing goes in the register. Next lesson there is a paper, and it does not go in the register either.
  4. Set the bar: Five true sentences about yourself, without stopping. That is the standard by the end of today.
📺 On screen: the one standard sentence in chalk — it stays up all lesson and everything else today is measured against it, so the board is the right medium and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: listen and read the board. Expect one student to ask about marks straight away — answer it now rather than later. Typical problem: somebody announces that they have forgotten all their English over the summer; agree cheerfully and move on

8–20′
🎯 Line-up · how was your summer
two rows, standing
Why: Two rows facing each other, one question, thirty seconds, then everybody moves one place. In twelve minutes each student has spoken to six different people — which is more talking than a whole first lesson of the old kind, and it does the job of an ice-breaker as a side effect rather than as a game.

WHAT WE DO: Kagan Mix-Pair-Share in a moving line: six partners, one question, thirty seconds each

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two rows facing each other. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: thirty seconds each way, then the row on the left moves one place and the last person runs to the front.
  2. Give the question and the frame together, and write the frame up: How was your summer? — Last summer I ___. I went with ___. The best thing was ___.
  3. Round 2 adds the questions, and this is where the real language is: Now you also ASK. Three questions: Where did you go? What did you do? Who did you go with?
  4. Six rotations, 30 seconds each way — that is six partners and twelve turns of speaking in eleven minutes.
  5. Stand at the end of the row so the last pair is always with you, and listen for two things only: the missing did and the one-word answer.
📺 On screen: the three-line frame and the three questions on the board — the board is the medium here and it stays up for the whole activity, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: speak to six different people. Expect the first two rounds to be slow and rounds four to six to be fast and loud — that is the whole point of the format. Typical errors: Where you go? and I go to my grandmother; both go on the correction sheet and neither is corrected aloud today
board · a visible timer · teacher's correction sheet
20–30′
Who is in this room
threes
Why: The group has to be a group by the end of today or the structures will not work in October. An interview in threes gives every student one person whose story they are responsible for reporting — which is a much stronger bond than a name game.

WHAT WE DO: Kagan Three-Step Interview: everybody ends up responsible for reporting one other person

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Threes. Kagan Three-Step Interview: A interviews B while C writes down three facts; then rotate, twice, so everybody has been all three.
  2. Four questions on the board, and no more: What's your name and how do you spell it? · What are you good at? · What are you bad at? · What is one thing nobody in this room knows about you?
  3. Then Kagan Team Statements: each three produces ONE sentence that is true about all three of them. All three of us ___.
  4. Every three reads its sentence out. Write the best five on the board — they are the first thing this group has made together and they should be visible.
  5. Keep the note sheets: they are your class list with something human on it, and they are worth more in October than any register.
📺 On screen: the four interview questions written on the board, and the note sheets are plain paper — nothing here can break down and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: interview, take notes, then agree one sentence in threes. Expect the spelling question to take longer than you think and the «nobody knows» question to produce the best English of the lesson. Typical error: the team statement comes out as three separate sentences — push for one
paper · board
30–42′
The class contract
class → teams
Why: Four rules, agreed rather than announced, and written where they stay all year. The point is not discipline: three of the four rules are language rules, and they do most of the teaching that the plans then take for granted.

WHAT WE DO: four rules, argued and agreed, written where they stay until May

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Do not announce the rules. Ask the question and let them build them: You have been in classrooms for seven years. Name one thing a teacher does that makes it easy to speak — and one thing that makes it impossible. Three minutes, in threes.
  2. Take four or five answers, then put up the four rules you were always going to put up, and connect each one to something they just said:
    • English first — An idea may start in Russian. The point is given for the English version — so say it again in English and the point is yours. Nobody is ever told off for the Russian; the Russian simply does not count.
    • Nobody answers alone — Everything goes to the board through a pair or a team first. You never stand up with an answer you have not already said to somebody. This is the rule that makes the room safe, and it is the reason for every structure we use.
    • An answer has a verb in it — «Football» is not an answer. «I play football on Saturdays» is an answer. This one sentence is worth more marks in five years than anything else on this list.
    • The timer is the boss — When the timer runs, we work; when it stops, pens down. It is not me hurrying you — it is the clock, and the clock is fair to everybody.
  3. Argue the second rule properly, because it is the one they will not believe: You will never be sent to this board alone with an answer you have not already said to somebody. That is a promise, and it is the reason for every strange thing we do this year.
  4. Everybody signs — literally, on one sheet of A3 that goes on the wall. It sounds childish and it works: a rule you signed is much harder to argue with in November.
  5. Write the four rules into the diary as well, on the first page, in English.
📺 On screen: the four rules written large on A3 in marker — paper on the wall is deliberately the medium here, because it must survive the year and cannot be switched off or closed at the end of the lesson, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: argue first, then agree, then sign. Expect real engagement on the «English first» rule and expect somebody to test it within five minutes — which is useful, because the answer is «say it again in English and the point is yours». Typical problem: one student refuses to sign; do not force it, ask them again at the end of the month

How the points work · and why the structures
board → teams
Why: Teenagers work hard for a score they understand and will not work at all for one that appears from nowhere. Ten minutes spent on the rules of the score now buys attention for the whole year, and naming the four structures out loud turns each of them into a one-sentence instruction from lesson 4 onwards.

WHAT WE DO: the three conditions of a point, and the four structures named out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Put the three conditions on the board and do not soften them: Points are for the TEAM, never for the person. A team gets a point when its answer is in English, has a verb in it, and every member of the team can repeat it. That third condition is the whole system: it makes the fast student teach rather than shout.
  2. Say what does NOT earn points, because this is the surprise: What does NOT earn points: speed, volume, being first, being right on your own. Say this explicitly in the first lesson — in most classrooms these four ARE the currency, and a teenager who has learnt that will otherwise spend three weeks playing the wrong game.
  3. Then name the four structures — this is the ten minutes that buys back six minutes a week from lesson 4 onwards: Four structures carry the year and they are named out loud from lesson 1, because a structure a class can name is a structure a class can start in ten seconds: Think-Pair-Share (think alone, say it to one person, then to the room) · Quiz-Quiz-Trade (a card each, quiz a partner, swap cards, find a new partner) · RallyCoach (one pen between two: one writes, the other coaches, then swap) · Numbered Heads Together (the team agrees the answer, then I call a number and that person answers for the team). The reason to name them is not decoration: «Quiz-Quiz-Trade, three minutes» costs one sentence, «now stand up, take a card, find someone…» costs ninety seconds, and ninety seconds four times a lesson is six minutes a week.
  4. Demonstrate one of them immediately with something trivial so the name sticks to a memory: Kagan RallyRobin, 60 seconds, «name English words that have to do with school, one each, back and forth, no repeats».
  5. Close with the reason, in one sentence: Teenagers work for a score they understand. What they will not work for is a score that arrives from nowhere. So the three conditions go on the wall in lesson 1 and stay there until May.
📺 On screen: the three conditions and the four structure names on the board — they stay up for the rest of the lesson and go on the wall next to the contract, so the board and the wall are the medium and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: listen, then run one RallyRobin round. Expect the «points are for the team» rule to produce an objection from the strongest student — answer it honestly: the team point is what makes their explaining worth something. Typical problem: they try to score the demonstration round; let them, and then point out that nobody wrote anything down

52–54′
Move break
standing
Why: A stirrer that also forces one English sentence. Teenagers who have been sitting for forty minutes need to stand before the second half, or the second half dies.

WHAT WE DO: two minutes of movement and a switch from the group to the book

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up. This corner is «I went abroad this summer». That corner is «I stayed in Norilsk». Go. Then one sentence from each corner.
  2. Second round, and it is the bridge: This corner — «I know what is in this book». That corner — «I have no idea». Go. Nobody is wrong, and everybody is now holding the book.
📺 On screen: instructions by voice, nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: move twice and produce one sentence per corner. Expect the second round to put almost everybody in the same corner, which is exactly the set-up for the next stage

The map of the year · SB p.4–9
pairs → class
Why: The book answers the questions instead of the teacher. A hunt through the contents pages gives them the shape of the year — twenty units, a check every four, an exam section at the back — in a form they will actually remember, because they found it themselves.

WHAT WE DO: a six-question hunt through the contents pages and «Welcome to Prepare»

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books open at p.4. Do not read the map to them — set the hunt: Do not read the map to them. Give them a hunt with six questions and let the book answer: Find the unit about films. What page? · Find the unit where you learn to write a biography. · How many units are about travelling? · Find the page where the year is checked — there are five of them. · Which unit has the strangest title? · Find a unit you actually want to do. Six minutes, in pairs, answers written down.
  2. Take the answers with Kagan Numbered Heads Together so nobody sits out. Then say what they have just found: What they should be able to say at the end of the hunt, in their own words: twenty units, each one four pages; a check page after every four units (Review 1 p.30 · Review 2 p.52 · Review 3 p.74 · Review 4 p.96 · Review 5 p.118); and sixteen pages at the back (120–135) that are pure exam technique.
  3. Now p.8–9, four minutes: SB p.8–9 names the seven things that come back in every unit — About you · Pronunciation · Talking points · Prepare for the exam · Prepare to write · Life Skills · Review. Ten minutes here is worth an hour later: a class that knows what «Talking points» means never has to have it explained again.
  4. And be honest about what is not in our year, rather than letting them discover it in October: ⚠️ Two of the labels on p.8–9 are NOT in our year and you should say so rather than let them find out in October: Culture (five spreads) is out — seventy-two lessons will not hold it without cutting language — and the videos need the interactive whiteboard app, so they appear only when the room has it. Life Skills stays but comes inside review lessons.
  5. Finish with one question that is not on the list: Which unit are you most looking forward to? Put a pencil dot next to it. Take four answers and remember them.
📺 On screen: SB p.4–9 on the screen so the whole class hunts on the same page (Backup: it is printed in their own books on p.4–9 — the pairs work straight from the paper page and you write the six hunt questions on the board)
👦 Students do: hunt in pairs, then report by number. Expect «how many units are about travelling» to cause an argument, which is good — make them justify from the page. Typical problem: they read the map instead of searching it; give a time limit per question and it stops

66–78′
Goals · three lines that stay in the folder
solo → pairs
Why: A goal a student wrote themselves and can see in December is the only kind that survives. Three lines, in English, on a card that lives in the folder next to the diagnostic.

WHAT WE DO: three written goals in English, on a card that lives in the folder all year

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out one card each. Three lines on the board as the frame: By May I want to be able to ___. · The thing I most want to stop getting wrong is ___. · One English thing I will do outside this room is ___.
  2. Three minutes alone, in silence. Then Kagan Timed Pair Share: one minute each way, read your card to your partner.
  3. Make the goals concrete or they are decoration: «Learn English» is not a goal. «Talk for two minutes without stopping» is a goal. Give two examples and make them rewrite anything vague.
  4. Collect the cards, or have them stapled into the folder — either way, you need them in December and in May.
  5. Read three of them out anonymously to close the stage. Hearing that somebody else wants the same thing is worth more than any encouragement from you.
📺 On screen: the three sentence frames on the board and blank cards on the desks — paper and chalk are the medium here on purpose, because the cards have to survive nine months, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write three goals alone, then read them to one partner. Expect the second line to be the hardest and the most useful. Typical error: goals written in Russian — give them the English frame again and let them keep the Russian underneath

78–86′
🎯 Speaking out · three true things and a lie
groups of 4
Why: The productive outcome of lesson 1 has to be spoken and personal: four sentences about your own summer, said to people who are listening for a reason. The listeners' question before the vote is what makes it language rather than a party game.

WHAT WE DO: four sentences about your summer, three true and one false, and the group votes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Explain the mechanic in thirty seconds: Four sentences about your summer: three true, one false. The group hears all four, then votes. One point for spotting the lie, one point for every person you fool. The listeners may ask one question each before voting — and that question is the part that makes it a language task rather than a party game.
  2. Two minutes to write the four sentences — and the rule that makes it a language task: All four must be past simple. All four must be about this summer.
  3. Then play, in fours, four rounds. One point for spotting the lie, one point for every person you fool, and the listeners' one question each before the vote.
  4. Teams total their points and shout the number. First scores of the year — this is the moment to point at the three conditions on the board and check they were met.
  5. Log errors silently on your correction sheet; correct nothing aloud. The feedback slot is the first three minutes of the next lesson.
📺 On screen: the scoring line written on the board (spot the lie = 1 · fool a person = 1) — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write four past-simple sentences and play four rounds. Expect the invented sentence to be the most fluent one they produce all lesson, which is worth saying out loud. Typical error: the question before the vote comes out without did — this is the single most useful item on your correction sheet today

Wrap-up + homework
circle
Why: Self-assessment against the aim they heard at the start, so the lesson closes the loop it opened. Today's homework is the September writing sample — it is set once and read in May.

WHAT WE DO: an “I can…” self-check, the September writing sample set as homework, a teaser

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to the standard from the lesson frame: An answer has a verb in it. Five true sentences about yourself — who can do it now? Take one volunteer and time them.
  2. Three lines on the board; students point at the ones that are true for them: I can say five things about myself. · I can ask three questions about someone's summer. · I know the four rules of this room.

Structure
I'm Kirill and I'm thirteen. · I live in Norilsk with my mum and my brother. · I'm good at maths and I'm bad at getting up. · Last summer I went to Kazan and stayed there for three weeks. · Where did you go? What did you do there? Who did you go with?
Form
present simple for what is always true (I live · I'm good at) against past simple for July (I went · I stayed); questions with did, and the verb going back to its plain form after it.
Pron. watch
the two ways of saying -ed that come up today — /t/ in stopped, walked, watched against /d/ in stayed, played, travelled. Do not teach the rule; just say the words correctly and let them copy.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Last summer I go to Kazan.» Is the summer finished? → yes. So is the verb finished too? → went.
«Where you went?» Which word is missing? → did. And once did is there, what happens to went? → it goes back to go.
«Sochi.» Is that an answer in this room? → no. Why not? → there is no verb in it.
ICQ before the line-up: Do you write anything? (no) How long do you speak? (thirty seconds) What happens when the timer stops? (this row moves one place)
«Points are for the team.» So if I am the only one in my team who can say the sentence, does the team get the point? → no. That is the rule that makes you teach the others.

Assumptions: Syllabus lesson 1, the first lesson of the year. Most of the group has come up from Prepare 2 and has met both present tenses and the past simple; a small number are new to the school and their level is unknown until lesson 2. ⚠️ There is no coursebook unit for this lesson and there is no workbook page — the Workbook opens at Unit 1 on p.4, which is lesson 4. The only book pages used today are the contents map (SB p.4–7) and «Welcome to Prepare» (SB p.8–9). Nothing is measured today; the measuring starts in lesson 2.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
One-word answers, from everybody, for the first twenty minutesExpected, and it has one fix that works: do not ask for more, give the sentence. Sochi. Good — now the whole thing: «I went to Sochi with my grandmother.» Say it. Three of those in the line-up and the rest of the room self-corrects.
Two students who already know each other refuse to swap partnersThe line-up format removes the argument: the rows move on the timer, not on friendship. Announce it before they stand up, and stand at the end of the row yourself so the last pair is with you.
A new student who understands very little and is visibly frightenedDo not single them out and do not simplify out loud. Put them second in the line-up so they hear the answer five times before they give it, and give them the interview role of note-taker in the threes. Lesson 2 will tell you what they actually have.
«Are we going to have a test?»Answer it straight, because the question is really about safety: Not today. Next lesson there are thirty questions and the lesson after that a hundred — and neither of them goes in the register. They are for you, not for me.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the OGE — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Speaking task 2 (a dialogue in which the student answers six questions about themselves, six marks) and task 35 (a personal email, ten marks).

Everything today's lesson is made of — say who you are, say what you did in the summer, ask another person about theirs — is the raw material of both. The single mark most often lost in speaking task 2 is lost for a one-word answer, and the standard set today (an answer is an answer only if it has a verb in it) is exactly the fix. Nobody in the room needs to hear the word «exam» for that to work.
