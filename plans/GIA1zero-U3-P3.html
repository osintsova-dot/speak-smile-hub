WHAT WE DO: we go through Jake's lunchbox (ex.2): what you can count and what you can't — intrigue without a term

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I open the lunchbox picture (p.40 ex.2): Jake's lunchbox! What's inside? Shout! (two sandwiches, some tomatoes, a biscuit, some juice, water, an apple)
  2. Intrigue: Watch my hands: TWO sandwiches — I can count! One, two! Now count the juice… one juice? two juices?.. PROBLEM! — theatrically “I can't count it”.
  3. Question to the class: What else can we NOT count here? (juice, water — TB key)
  4. No term yet: English has TWO families of words. Let's meet them. Teams, points board.
📺 On screen: the SB p.40 ex.2 (or the lunchbox slide) · points board (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they name the contents, laugh at “one juice” — the problem is felt BEFORE the rule. Expected: they say “you can't count liquid” themselves. Typical error: “two waters” — a theatrical pause: “two… WATERS?”

12–22′
Input: countable and uncountable (the “1-2-3” test)
Discover · countable/uncountable
board → desks

WHAT WE DO: the “1-2-3” test with a gesture; ex.1 sorting by teams; ex.3 rules built together with support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I introduce the test: The 1-2-3 test! Can you count it on fingers — one banana, two bananas? YES → countable family. One rice, two rice?.. NO → uncountable family. A finger gesture.
  2. TB-warmer in reverse: Odd one out: bananas–biscuits–meat–peas? (meat!) — the test works.
  3. Ex.1 sorting by teams, 3 min: 10 words into 2 columns. Key: C banana·biscuit·orange·potato / UC bread·cheese·coffee·juice·soup·tea. TB hint: 4 countable, 6 uncountable.
  4. Ex.3 rules with support, in chorus: a/an — with a singular countable noun; uncountable — with NO plural; some — with uncountable nouns and plurals.
📺 On screen: board: 2 “family” columns + the 1-2-3 test · the SB p.40 · timer
👦 Students do: they test words with their fingers, sort by teams, build the rules with support. Expected: the test gesture becomes a habit. Typical error: juice → C (“two juices!”) — “two BOTTLES of juice — we count the bottles, not the juice” (TB note).
💪 Support (TB Reinforcement): the “4 and 6” hint + physically lay the word cards into the columns.

A5 grammar card — COUNT IT OR SCOOP IT?: countable (one apple → two apples · a sandwich · an orange · three biscuits) against uncountable (some bread · some cheese · some rice · some water — no plural, no a/an), then the quantifier routes SOME in affirmatives, ANY in negatives and questions, A LOT OF for a big amount with either type
  • Glue the card in; pupils sort ten words from the P1 food map into the two columns.
  • Say the three quantifier models aloud, changing only the food word.
  • Mark the trap: no a/an and no plural on the scoop side.
  • The card stays open through the quantifier duels as the answer key.

WHAT WE DO: a race: grocery cards into the “a/an” and “some” baskets; fixing ex.4 with a reason

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out card sets (12 groceries) and 2 “basket” sheets to the teams: a/an and some.
  2. Sort the shopping into baskets — three minutes, race! Checking in chorus: An apple! Some bread!
  3. Fixing ex.4 (one “case” per team): Kelly's got two banana and a water — TWO problems! Fix! (bananas · some water; TB key).
  4. Report with the test: Why some water? — Can't count! +2 for a fix with a reason.
📺 On screen: card sets · basket sheets · the SB p.40 ex.4
👦 Students do: they lay out cards against time, fix sentences with a reason. Expected: physical sorting builds intuition. Typical error: an before a consonant — the chant “an apple, an orange — AN before a vowel SOUND”.

Input: some, any, a lot of (rules built together)
Discover 2 · some/any/a lot of
board → desks

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: the some/any + a lot of traffic light; ex.5 rules by matching; ex.6 blitz on mini-boards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Traffic light on the board: some = green (+) · any = red (− and ?) · a lot of = “a mountain” (a lot, with both families).
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYBODY writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift them all together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. Examples from the book in chorus: We've got SOME cheese. We haven't got ANY tomatoes. Have you got ANY bread? A LOT OF schools have Meatless Mondays.
  4. Ex.5 rules by matching on mini-boards (1b·2c·3d·4a) — boards up.
  5. Ex.6 blitz: 5 rounds of 10 sec, boards up (any·some·any·a lot of·a lot of/any). The traffic light helps.
📺 On screen: board: the traffic-light poster (stays up till the end of the unit) · mini-boards · the SB p.40
👦 Students do: they build the rules by matching, flip the blitz by the traffic light. Expected: red any in a question — the anchor works. Typical error: some in a question — I point at the traffic light silently.

A logic puzzle (who drinks what?)
Puzzle · Brain teaser

WHAT WE DO: ex.8 puzzle with hints on the TB protocol: table → ticks → the final hint

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I launch it: Three people. Three drinks. Three fruits. Who's got WHAT? Teams — go!
  2. Hint 1 (after 1 min, TB): build a 3×2 table. Hint 2: read sentence by sentence, put ✓/✗. Hint 3 (final, TB): Alex has got juice.
  3. First to finish +5, after that +2. Key: Alex — juice + an orange · Ben — tea + an apple · Carla — water + a banana.
  4. I praise the LOGIC out loud: any = “none at all” — that's why Carla has no juice.
📺 On screen: the SB p.40 ex.8 · timer
👦 Students do: they solve it with a table, using the hints. Expected: 3–4 min with the hints. Typical error: they read “hasn't got any juice” as “a little” — any = zero!

WHAT WE DO: a change of scenery: a mall map + 11 icons with pictures; the possessive 's by chant; TB bingo

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The midpoint ritual: Grammar lab CLOSED. Field trip! We're going to… THE MALL. (a 5-sec “drive” is fine — a steering wheel, music)
  2. Ex.1 map: 5 food places in chorus with pictures (café · baker's · supermarket · restaurant · food stall). TB pronunciation: /ˈrestrɒnt/ — 2 syllables!
  3. Ex.3 icons A–K as a team race, 4 min (TB key: newsagent's·clothes shop·shoe shop·phone shop·hairdresser's·cinema·sports shop·chemist's·bookshop·games shop·stationery shop). TB hint: all the “…shop” ones first, then the rest.
  4. The 's chant: the baker's — the chemist's — the hairdresser's — the shop of the person!
  5. TB bingo: a 6-cell grid, places of their choice → my definitions (a place to buy medicine…) → BINGO!
📺 On screen: the SB p.41 · timer · bingo grids
👦 Students do: they “drive to the mall”, race through the icons, chant the 's, play bingo by definitions. Expected: bingo cements it without drilling by rote. Typical error: chemist's with a /ch/ sound — choral /k/: /ˈkemɪsts/.

screen · desks

WHAT WE DO: the subskill “notes in words”; pass 1 — the order of the questions; passes 2–3 — a ready-made table

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The subskill, simply: When you listen — NO full sentences. Words only. ‘+’ = and. Like a spy. TB model: clothes shops → we write “clothes”.
  2. I hand out a READY-MADE table (Westfield · Mall of America × shops / eat / other).
  3. Pass 1 (ex.5): Only the ORDER of questions a–d! Key: 1c·2d·3a·4b.
  4. Passes 2–3 (ex.6): notes into the table. Between passes, pairs check with each other: Compare notes with your partner.
  5. Checking cell by cell with the TB key (Westfield: clothes+shoe shops · restaurants, cafés, food stalls · cinema / Mall of America: sports shops, games shop · restaurants · cinema, concerts, mini golf). +1 per cell — any correct note.
📺 On screen: audio 26 (Backup: I play it myself from the TB pp.132–133 script) · ready-made table forms
👦 Students do: they listen three times with a growing task, write words into the ready-made table, check in pairs. Expected: 60–80% of cells — a success for the first time, we celebrate it. Typical error: sentences → skipped — “words only, spy mode!”

WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin: pairs ask from the ex.7 frame: favourite shopping place; a memory round with place cards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.7 frame on the board: What's your favourite place to go shopping? — My favourite place is… It's got a lot of… Drill ×2.
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we alternate one answer at a time — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!

  4. Memory: place cards face down, pairs turn over 2 at a time — a match = a pair + the word out loud.
  5. Partner rotation for memory — 2 rounds.

👦 Students do: they ask from the frame about real shopping, play memory with the word said aloud. Expected: “Arena! It's got a lot of shops!” Typical error: silent memory — “say it or lose it!”

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use a/an, some, any, a lot of with countable/uncountable food words in frames and name 12+ shopping-centre places, taking first notes from a street interview.
  • Subsidiary. Full input of C/UC (the “1-2-3” test) and the some/any/a lot of system; drill games with groceries; a Brain teaser with hints on the TB protocol; the shopping centre with pictures + bingo; step-by-step listening with a notes template (subskill: taking notes).
  • Personal. A double pair by the KTP — hold the midpoint break as a “trip to the mall”: the change of scenery gives a second wind. Grammar only in game shells; the rule always comes AFTER the examples (inductively, as in the book).
By the end, students can…
  • sort food into countable/uncountable with the 1-2-3 test
  • use a/an, some, any in frames
  • name 12+ places in a shopping centre
  • write first listening notes into a ready grid

Structure
I've got some water. · We haven't got any tomatoes. · Have you got any juice? · There's a lot of sugar in juice. · Where do you buy bread? — At the baker's.
Form
the “1-2-3” test: can you count it without packaging? → countable; some=+ · any=−/? · a lot of=a lot (C and UC); possessive 's: baker's, chemist's.
Pron. watch
restaurant /ˈrestrɒnt/ — 2 syllables (TB) · chemist's /k/ · stress on the 1st syllable: BOOKshop.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Can you count water — one water, two waters?” → No → uncountable → some water.
“Have you got SOME juice?” → In a question — any!
“Baker's — why the 's?” → the baker's SHOP — the baker's shop.

Assumptions: a/an/some are already in their ears after the P1 frames — today it becomes a SYSTEM. The mall set is mostly new (chemist's, stationery shop). Notes by ear — for the first time: a ready-made table template is a must.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
