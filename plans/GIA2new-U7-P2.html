🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The main fear a new student has in reading is unknown words. So the six new words are dealt with BEFORE the text (meaning + part of speech + example), and the subskill of the unit itself (understanding new words) is practised on four safe words at the end. The text is read in chunks with stops, never in one go. The product is three facts about the heroes of the article, written, on a frame.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Texas, one morning. The streets are rivers. No drinking water, no electricity. And four boys — one of them is fifteen — take a small fishing boat and go out. Fifty people. Even more dogs and rabbits. Today we read the real newspaper article. Don't worry about new words: we'll learn six of them BEFORE we read, and I'll show you how to guess the rest. At the end: three facts about the boys, written, in your own words.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: we read a real newspaper article about four teenagers who took their neighbours out of a flooded city. Six new words are dealt with in advance, and we go through the text in chunks. At the end — three facts about the heroes, written, on a frame.

WHAT WE DO: six new words before reading: meaning in English + part of speech + gesture + example

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I show each word on a card with a gesture: electricity — the energy that makes light work (I flick a switch) · brave — a person who does something in a dangerous situation (fist to the chest) · citizens — people who live in a town (I sweep a hand round the class) · safe — not dangerous (hands into a roof) · neighbours — people who live near you (I point at the next desk) · rescue services — organisations that help in danger (a siren).
  2. The part of speech out loud (this is the subskill itself): Noun or adjective? Electricity — noun. Brave — adjective. (TB key ex.6)
  3. ex.8 in writing with the cards as support (TB key: 1 electricity · 2 safe · 3 brave · 4 neighbours · 5 citizens · 6 rescue services).
  4. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade on the six cards: word ↔ meaning.
📺 On screen: cards with the six words on the board (Backup: I write the same six words in chalk with the gesture as a prompt — the cards are not essential)
👦 Students do: they copy the gestures and write the words in. Expect: brave and safe land instantly through the gesture. Typical problem: citizens is heard as “citadel” — we catch the false friend

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2 as a warm-up + ex.3 choosing the headline after a quick skim

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 with the word bank: Which jobs help other people? (suggested answers, TB: teacher, nurse, dentist, lawyer, scientist).
  2. ex.2 from the photo on p.87: What was the weather like? What problems did people have? (suggested answers, TB: it was wet, a lot of rain; it was impossible to drive, water was standing in the houses).
  3. ex.3 a 2-minute skim, for the headline only: Don't read every word. Look at the pictures and the first line of each part. (TB key: B — TEEN HEROES RESCUE NEIGHBOURS).
  4. Why not A and not C: A is only about the police, C is about the whole of Texas.
📺 On screen: SB pp.86–87 · a 2-minute timer (Backup: I write the three headlines on the board and students hold up fingers A/B/C)
👦 Students do: they predict, then check. Expect: many go for C — we take it calmly, it is a useful mistake

30–45′
Reading the article in chunks
desks

WHAT WE DO: track 45 in three goes: we listen and follow with a finger, one question after each chunk

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Chunk 1 (the storm and the city): we listen and follow with a finger. Question: Where was the storm? What did people NOT have? (TB key ex.4: 1 in Texas · 2 they had no drinking water and no electricity).
  2. Chunk 2 (the boys and the boat). Question: Who were the heroes? How did they rescue people? (3 Thomas Edwards, his friends Richard and Liam and his brother Declan · 4 in a small fishing boat).
  3. Chunk 3 (how many they rescued, and the photographer). Question: How many people? Did they only rescue people? (5 more than 50 · 6 no, animals too).
  4. The rule: Answer AND put your finger on the line.
📺 On screen: audio track 45 · SB pp.86–87 (Backup: I read the article aloud myself in chunks, slowly, and students follow with a finger)
👦 Students do: they listen, follow with a finger and answer after each chunk. Expect: in chunks everybody copes. Typical problem: they lose their place — I stop and point at the paragraph

PRODUCT · three facts about the heroes
solo → pairs → stage

WHAT WE DO: written first: three facts about the boys on the frame, then read in pairs and to the class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The frame on screen: The heroes were … . They were … years old. They took a … . They rescued … people and … . They were very … .
  2. Six minutes of silent writing; I monitor and help individually.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share: 40 seconds reading to my partner → swap.
  4. Speaking with the page in hand: Three facts each. You may hold your notes.
  5. To finish — Critical thinking 2 with support: Can we be heroes at school? One example each: helping someone who is lost, helping with schoolwork, defending someone.
📺 On screen: the frame on screen · paper (Backup: I write the five lines of the frame on the board and leave them to the end of these two lessons)
👦 Students do: they write, read in pairs and speak with the page in hand. Expect: everybody manages three facts. Typical problem: “They was” — corrected individually; the past simple is the topic of P3

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can find specific information in a newspaper article, use six new words (electricity · brave · citizens · safe · neighbours · rescue services) and write three facts about the people in the text.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 jobs that help people; ex.2 predicting from the photo; ex.3 the headline; ex.4 (track 45) six questions; ex.5 six sentence endings; ex.6–8 Word work; Critical thinking 1–2 with support.
  • Personal. I don't translate the words straight away — but I don't torture them either: they get a support card with the meaning in English and a picture-gesture. Every answer is backed by a line, so nobody guesses.
By the end, students can…
  • choose the right headline for a text
  • answer questions about a text and show the line
  • say what type of word a new word is (noun, adjective)
  • write three facts about people in the text

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“brave” — is it about a person or about a place? → about a person.
“safe” — is it dangerous or not dangerous? → NOT dangerous.
“neighbours” — where are the letters you can't hear? → gh: /ˈneɪbə(r)z/.

Assumptions: SB pp.86–87, syllabus pair 55. The jobs from P1 are active. The storm topic can be sensitive — keep the focus on what the people did, not on the damage.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The text looks enormousWe read it in chunks: three stops, one question after each. The text is never read whole in these two lessons.
An unknown word paralyses themThe six key words are dealt with BEFORE reading and stay on the board with a picture-gesture. Every other word they are explicitly allowed to skip.
They answer at randomThe rule: an answer plus a finger on the line. No line found — the answer doesn't count and we look for it together.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
