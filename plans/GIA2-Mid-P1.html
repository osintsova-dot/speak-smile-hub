🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): A midterm is the easiest lesson in the year to ruin. There are two ways to ruin it: turn it into a scary exam (then the paper measures stress, not knowledge), or turn it into a formality (then there is no point running it at all). So the frame is a half-time report: half the season is played, we look at the numbers and decide what changes in the second half. Three things make this lesson work. First: the scale goes up BEFORE the paper, not after — when the rules are known in advance, anxiety drops and the score goes up. Second: Section E of the paper repeats the September entry check word for word, and after marking every student physically puts the two sheets side by side — that is the only way to show a teenager they have grown, because they do not believe “you've got better”, but they do believe their own two sheets. Third: we work through four errors, not twenty — the rest goes into the personal error log. Twenty errors for everyone means nothing gets fixed.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Half-time report. Half the season is played. In a real team, at half time, nobody shouts — they look at the numbers and decide what changes in the second half. That's today. Seventy points, forty minutes, everything from September to December — and nothing else. Here's the scale, on the board, BEFORE you start, so nobody guesses. Then you mark your own paper with the key, and you write three lines: what you fix before Unit 4. One more thing — your September sheet is in my folder. After the marking, you get it back and compare. Warm-up first. Books open at page 21.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: a report at half time: half the year is played, we look at the numbers. 70 marks, 40 minutes, only what has been covered since September. The scale is on the board BEFORE the paper. You mark yourself against the key, you write three lines of “what I fix before Unit 4”, and at the end you compare with your September sheet.

🎯 GATE · three-unit blitz
solo · mini-boards

WHAT WE DO: 6 minutes: a blitz gate across three units — 60 seconds per unit, in writing, books closed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three 60-second rounds, books closed: Round one, Unit 1: as many school subjects and daily routines as you can. GO!
  2. Round two, Unit 2: sort into do / go / play. Six sports minimum.
  3. Round three, Unit 3: clothes and one adjective for each. GO!
  4. They count their own score and write it in the margin. Three numbers. Nobody else sees them.
  5. Close it with no feedback and no comment on levels: Keep those numbers. Your weakest round is the station you go to first.
📺 On screen: 60-second timer · mini-whiteboards or exercise books (Backup: write “60” on the board and count down aloud from ten; mini-whiteboards are not essential here)
👦 Students do: students write silently through three rounds. Expected: Unit 1 (subjects and routines) produces the most words, Unit 2 (do/go/play) the fewest — the sorting is where it breaks. Error: “do swimming” — do not correct it aloud, that is exactly what the station fixes

⚡ Pre-flight check · three stations
crews · stations

WHAT WE DO: three 3-minute stations on the three Quick review pages — you go FIRST to the one you failed in the gate

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three stations, three minutes each. Start at your weakest round. The sheet leaves the station filled in.
  2. Station 1 · SB p.21 (Unit 1): present simple — the table of forms, the -s/-es/-ies rules, Do/Does + short answer, object pronouns. Mini-task: five sentences, put the verb in the right form, two of them on -ies.
  3. Station 2 · SB p.33 (Unit 2): adverbs of frequency (after be, before every other verb), like/love/hate + -ing, the shapes of Wh-questions. Mini-task: four sentences — put the adverb in its place, one of them with be.
  4. Station 3 · SB p.45 (Unit 3): present continuous, -ing spelling, the contrast signalled by the time marker, the short answer with no contraction. Mini-task: five forms, two of them on doubling and on dropping the -e.
  5. Kagan Showdown when each station checks its answers: everyone writes in silence, then all boards go up together on your call.
  6. The last 60 seconds: Everyone back to seats. Books closed. Quick reviews closed. From now on it's your head only.
📺 On screen: SB p.21, p.33, p.45 (one spread per station) · station record sheets · timer (Backup: three stations = three corners of the room, each with an A4 sheet carrying the mini-task and the Quick review page left open in the book)
👦 Students do: students go to the stations starting with their weakest. Expected: station 2 is the busiest — adverbs and -ing after like sag for most of the class. Error: they follow their friends instead of their own gate result — send them by round number, not by preference

HALF-TIME REPORT · the paper
solo

WHAT WE DO: 40 minutes: the 70-mark paper, eight sections (specification in the reference at the end of this plan)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Instructions in exactly 40 seconds, then silence: Forty minutes. Seventy points. Easy points first. Stuck? Mark it, move on, come back. A blank line is a guaranteed zero — always write something.
  2. Put the time on the board and update it three times: 30 · 15 · 5 minutes left. Do not announce it aloud — just change the number.
  3. Play the listening (Section G) at minute 25 of the paper, twice, with a 30-second pause between the two plays: Pens down, questions in front of you, thirty seconds to read them. Then twice.
  4. The writing (Section H) comes last, and say so before the start: Leave eight minutes for the writing. Set your own alarm in your head.
  5. For the fast ones, the quiet bonus task on the back: describe a classmate without naming them, six sentences, two in present continuous and two in present simple with an adverb. It counts for the leaderboard, not for the mark.
  6. Sit down and do your own work. Get up only for a raised hand: hovering over students costs the class 3–5 marks for nothing.
📺 On screen: the papers (built from the specification at the end of this plan) · audio for Section G · a timer on the board (Backup with no equipment: read the listening text yourself from the audioscript, twice, at the same pace — it does not affect the marks, and the class knows this in advance)
👦 Students do: students work in silence for 40 minutes. Expected: sections A–B are done in 12 minutes, section D (the simple/continuous contrast) eats the most time, and 3–4 students leave the writing unfinished — hence the eight-minute warning. Error: they skip items instead of writing something — repeat the rule “a blank line is a guaranteed zero” before the start

WHAT WE DO: students mark themselves against the key in their own hand, circle every slip and write THREE lines of “what I fix before Unit 4”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Keys out. Mark your own paper. Circle EVERY slip — including the -s in the third person, the apostrophe and the capital letter.
  2. They add up their own marks and check them against the scale on the board — it has been up there since minute one, so there are no surprises.
  3. Now the part that actually matters. Three lines at the top: what you fix before Unit 4. Not ‘grammar’. Not ‘everything’. Write: ‘-s in he/she/it’, ‘adverb before the verb’, ‘-ing after like’.
  4. While they write, walk the rows quickly and note down YOUR OWN map of the class: who will need prompts in Unit 4, who can be pushed straight away.
  5. Collect the papers only AFTER the error log has been copied into the exercise book: The log stays with you. The paper comes to me.
📺 On screen: printed keys, one per desk · the scale on the board · exercise books (Backup: read the key aloud item by item and students tick — slower, but it works; what matters is that the student does the marking)
👦 Students do: students mark themselves, circle slips and write three lines. Expected: for most of them the self-marked score is within ±2 of yours — the gaps are usually in the writing, which you re-check at home. Error: “I nearly had it right” — the rule of circling every slip removes that

WHAT WE DO: September → December through the matching format + a clinic on exactly four errors of the term

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Then vs Now. Hand back the September entry-check sheets: Section E of today's paper is the SAME format as your September sheet. Same task, same six lines. Put them side by side.
  2. There is exactly one question, and they answer it in writing, for themselves: What can you write now that you could not write in September? One sentence.
  3. A few volunteers read out — not marks, but exactly “what I can do now”. Do not comment, just repeat after them, louder.
  4. The clinic: four errors, 90 seconds each, anonymously. Write the wrong version on the board, the class fixes it chorally, you write the correct version next to it plus the way to self-check (all four are in the reference at the end of this plan): the -s of the third person · the bare verb after does · the place of the adverb of frequency · time marker → continuous.
  5. These four cost this class more points than everything else together. Four. Not twenty. Fix four and the next paper looks different.
📺 On screen: the September entry-check sheets (kept by the teacher since Starter P4) · the board for the clinic (Backup: if the September sheets are missing — compare with the first page of the exercise book, where they wrote a mini-profile about themselves in September; the comparison is just as visible)
👦 Students do: students put the two sheets side by side and see the difference with their own eyes. Expected: in September most of them wrote 4–6 short sentences, now it is a connected text with two tenses; this works better than any praise. Error: they compare marks instead of texts — steer them with the question “what can you WRITE now?”

Homework

The core task (personal, not shared): 25 minutes strictly on the three lines of your own error log. Where to look is written on the paper itself: a line about present simple → Quick review SB p.21 + Workbook p.11 · a line about adverbs, like + -ing or Wh-questions → SB p.33 + WB p.19 · a line about present continuous and the contrast → SB p.45 + WB p.27 · a line about the base (be · have got · can · possessives) → SB pp.6–10. 📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can use the language of Starter and Units 1–3 together under time pressure (present simple · adverbs of frequency · likes/dislikes + -ing · present continuous and the contrast · be / have got / can), and name three specific things to fix before Unit 4.

And most important: during the error clinic name no student — errors on the board are always anonymous.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He don't like maths” — what is wrong? → after he/she/it it is always doesn't.
“Does she likes PE?” — why not? → does already marks the third person, so the verb is bare.
“I like she” — what has to change? → her: after a verb we use the object pronoun.
“Yes, they're” — is that possible? → no: a short answer takes the full form — Yes, they are.
“Does this count for a grade?” → yes, there is a grade. And the scale is on the board from minute one, so you always know where you stand instead of guessing.
“What if I get a 2?” → a 2 does not go into the register straight away: two weeks to retake it on your own error log. The point of the paper is to fix things, not to record a failure.

Midterm paper — specification (OURS, 70 marks / 40 minutes)
PartWhat is in it, how many marks, and which model to build it from
Section A · Vocabulary, Units 1–3 — 15 marksthree blocks of 5 items. A1 daily routines and school subjects: label the picture / give the word from a definition (format: WB p.11 Vocabulary review, sets from Quick review SB p.21). A2 free-time activities + sort 6 sports into do / go / play (format: WB p.14, set from Quick review SB p.33). A3 clothes and describing them: gap-fill and opposite (format: WB pp.20 and 22, sets from Quick review SB p.45).
Section B · Grammar 1 — Present simple + object pronouns (Unit 1) — 10 marks5 items: put the verb in the right form (aff/neg), two of them on the -es / -ies rule (format: WB p.11 ex.1). 3 items: build a Yes/No question and write the short answer (format: WB p.11 ex.2). 2 items: replace the words in bold with an object pronoun (format: WB p.11 ex.3).
Section C · Grammar 2 — adverbs of frequency · likes and dislikes · Wh-questions (Unit 2) — 10 marks4 items: put the adverb of frequency in the right place, one of them with be (format: WB p.19 ex.1). 3 items: build a sentence with love / like / don’t mind / hate + -ing (format: WB p.19 ex.2). 3 items: find and correct one error in a Wh-question (format: WB p.19 ex.3 — the same page where “Why do Patrick like surfing?” was worked through).
Section D · Grammar 3 — Present continuous and the contrast with present simple (Unit 3) — 12 marks5 items: the present continuous form, two of them on -ing spelling (take → taking, swim → swimming) — format: WB p.27 ex.1. 5 items: choose simple or continuous from the time marker (format: WB p.27 ex.2 and SB p.42). 2 items: a short answer with no contraction (Yes, they are — not “Yes, they're”).
Section E · Starter carry-over — 6 marks6 one-line items: be in a question and a short answer · a possessive adjective or the apostrophe ’s / s’ · have got in the third person · can + bare verb · country and nationality · one family member from a definition. Formats — SB pp.6–10, exactly the ones used in the September entry check: this matters, otherwise the “September → December” comparison means nothing.

Error clinic after the paper — the four biggest errors of the term
What breaksHow to fix it and how to self-check
The -s of the third person goes missingHe go → He goes · She study → She studies. Self-check: try he/she/it — if it fits, the -s is compulsory.
The verb keeps its -s after do/doesDoes she likes PE? → Does she like PE? Self-check: does has ALREADY marked the person — we do not mark it twice.
The adverb of frequency is in the wrong placeShe plays often → She often plays · He always is → He is always. Self-check: with be it goes after, with every other verb before.
Present simple instead of continuous when there is a time markerAt the moment I wear → I'm wearing. Self-check: if you see now / right now / at the moment / today — use -ing.

Work through exactly these four, anonymously, 90 seconds each. Everything else goes into the student's personal error log: going through twenty errors with the whole class fixes none of them.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
