🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The WDYT project from the coursebook is turned into a campaign contest: the crew makes a poster about two endangered animals and pitches it, and the class votes not for 'the prettiest one' but for the one they would actually join. The super skill of the unit (collaboration: listening to other people's opinions) works as a condition for handing the work in — the TB asks you directly to value everyone's contribution. The Quick review is built into the middle of the lesson as stations, as an 'audit before printing': revision for the test without the word 'test'.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Campaign day. Two endangered animals, one poster, one minute to make this class care. The rules come from the model project: description, habitat, population then and now, why it's in danger, how to help. Five blocks — miss one and the poster is incomplete. And the vote at the end isn't 'which poster is prettiest'. It's 'which campaign would you actually join'. One more thing: decisions are made together — 'Why don't we choose the dolphin?' 'Do we all agree?' A crew that doesn't listen doesn't finish. Clock's running.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: a campaign contest: a poster about two endangered animals built from five compulsory blocks, plus a one-minute pitch. The class votes not for looks but for the campaign they would actually join.

WHAT WE DO: STEP 2 PLAN: each student researches their own animal and takes notes on the five blocks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Work individually. Two endangered animals, five blocks each. Notes only.
  2. Sources: phones / the printed file cards you brought (8 species), extracts from the article on p.75, the model poster on p.83.
  3. The constraint: Notes in English. A Russian note doesn't go on the poster.
  4. Back into the crew through Kagan Timed RoundRobin: round the circle, exactly 45 seconds each on their own animal across the five blocks, the others stay silent and listen; when the circle is over they decide together. Back to your desks. Compare — which two animals give the strongest campaign?

WHAT WE DO: 4 stations of 4 min each on Quick review SB p.81: animals and describing · collocations · was/were + there was/were · past simple of regular verbs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before you print, the auditors check the campaign. Four stations, four minutes each.
  2. Station 1: 12 animals by class + 9 body parts + 6 adjectives (tracks 41–42). Station 2: the eight taking-action baskets (track 43). Station 3: was/were, there was/were, questions and short answers. Station 4: -ed (spelling + the three sounds) and didn't + infinitive.
  3. The grammar stations 3–4 run as Kagan RallyCoach: one pen per pair — the first student solves an item out loud and explains the form, the second is the coach (checks it against the key on the back of the card, catches -ed after didn't, praises), on the next item they swap. That way both of them see the gap, not just one.
  4. A personal gap list: Write down what YOU missed — that's your test prep list, and it's short.

WHAT WE DO: STEP 4 PRESENT: a 90-second pitch (both of them speak) + peer review + the vote + the answer to the unit's WDYT question

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ninety seconds per crew. BOTH of you speak. Sections and linking phrases — take your time, a pause is not a problem.
  2. Peer review (ex.10): Which animal do you like best and why? Think of one question to ask. — one question for each crew.
  3. The vote: Which campaign would you actually JOIN? Stickers, and you cannot vote for your own.
  4. WDYT: back to the unit question. So — what CAN we do to help animals in danger? They answer with the actions from the posters, not with opinions (TB technique: compare with the answers given at the start of the unit).

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can research two endangered animals and present a poster with five information blocks, using the language of the unit; the whole unit is revised for the P8 test.
  • Subsidiary. The model presentation on video (ex.1); unpacking the model poster (ex.2–3); choosing a class of animals and researching (ex.4–5); Super skills Key phrases (ex.6–7); building and presenting (ex.8–9); peer review ex.10; Quick review p.81 in stations; Final reflection p.83.
  • Personal. The project is the weaker students' moment of glory: roles matched to strengths (text · numbers · design · voice). Never call the Quick review test preparation — it is 'the audit before printing'. Make sure BOTH partners speak (a TB requirement).
By the end, students can…
  • find and note the five blocks of information about an endangered animal
  • use Super skills phrases to make decisions together
  • present a poster in clear sections with a linking phrase
  • pass all Quick-review stations of Unit 6

Structure
Red pandas live in the forests of China, India, Nepal and Myanmar. · Fifty years ago, there were about 20,000 red pandas, but now there are less than 10,000. · They are in danger because their habitat is disappearing. · You can join a wildlife organisation and adopt one.
Form
three tenses on one poster (a TB observation): present simple — what the animal is; past simple — how many there were; can / need to — what to do next.
Pron. watch
clear numbers in the presentation: thirteen /ˌθɜːˈtiːn/ vs thirty /ˈθɜːti/ — the stress decides; say the linking phrases with a pause: Now let's look at its habitat. ↘
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
A poster about pandas in general — does that work? → no: only about animals IN DANGER (the checking question from the TB).
What are the five blocks? → description · habitat · population then and now · why it is in danger · how to help. 'How the animal helps humans' is NOT in the model poster (TB key ex.2).
Is the Quick review a test? → no, it is the audit before printing: find your own gaps today.

Assumptions: SB pp.81–83, syllabus pair 52. All the unit material has been covered; what is new is gathering information and deciding together. The internet is not essential: keep a pack of printed files on 8 species in case there is no network.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The project eats the revisionA hard frame: the revision stations take 18 minutes IN THE MIDDLE of the lesson, before the poster is built — they are 'the audit before printing'.
One works, the other watchesThe TB requirement: both speak. Share out the roles before the start and check at the pitch — if only one of them speaks, the crew loses a point.
The poster = pictures with no numbersThe five-block rule + a separate judges' flag 'no numbers' (in the model poster both texts contain numbers).
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
