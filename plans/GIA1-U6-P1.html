WHAT WE DO: 8 items in writing in 3 min: 4 objects by definition + 4 prepositions by picture; self-check, score in the margin

Slide: 1 You wash here. 2 It's above your head, inside. 3 You come in through it — the room by the front door. 4 You sit on it at the table. + picture plan: a ball ___ the table (under) · a lamp ___ the table (above) · a chair ___ the table and the wall (between) · a mat ___ the door (in front of).

desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: ex.1–2 photos A–E ↔ rooms (2 extra) + ex.3 “where people do this” for speed + ex.5 a hunt for furniture in the photos; drill /e/–/ɜː/

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 30 sec in crews: What's different about this house? (upside-down; furniture on the ceiling). Culture note in one phrase: the first house like this — Florida, the 1960s; Poland and Japan have their own.
  2. Ex.2 race 90 sec: Photos A–E, seven words, TWO extra. Crews — go! Key: A bedroom · B living room · C kitchen · D bathroom · E hall (extra: dining room, garden).
  3. Ex.3 blitz down the crew chain, 5-sec limit: 1 kitchen · 2 bedroom · 3 living room · 4 bathroom · 5 dining room · 6 garden.
  4. Ex.5 hunt: Three things in ALL photos? Two things in NO photo? Key: 1 floor, ceiling, wall (in all photos) · 2 bath, shower (in none) · 3 bed–A · chair–B · table–B and A · toilet–D.
  5. Drill 60 sec (p.117): Short /e/: bed, next, test. Long /ɜː/: FURniture, girl, word, work. In chorus + a “short/long” gesture.
  6. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!

🔥 Challenge · preposition repair race

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.6 diagrams a–i + ex.7 choose + a “spot the mistake” round on Common problems with prepositions (TB p.84)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.6: Nine diagrams, nine prepositions. Four minutes, crews. Careful — two of them look almost the same. Key: a between · b in · c above · d on · e near · f in front of · g under · h next to · i behind.
  2. I draw an anchor on the board (stays till the end): ON = palm on top · IN = fist into palm · NEAR = “somewhere nearby” · NEXT TO = shoulder to shoulder · IN FRONT OF = palm in front of the face.
  3. ▶ Spot the mistake (Activities bank TB p.7) on mini-boards: I put up 3 errors from TB p.84 — There's a picture in the wall. · The dog is near to the house. · The mat is in front the door. + 2 clean sentences mixed in. Boards up: FIX or OK!
  4. Ex.7 final chain blitz: 1 above · 2 on · 3 between · 4 in front of · 5 next to.
  5. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can give a 45-sec audio gallery walk through their own home plan with 5+ rooms and 5+ prepositions of place used accurately.
  • Subsidiary. The gate exposes the real ceiling — prepositions (in/on · near/next to · in front OF); 16 rooms & furniture and 9 prepositions are drilled for speed; Video skills “Fairy tale house” is watched with maker tasks; WDYT? “What is art?” is opened with a vote; present continuous is NOTICED in context (in yellow), not introduced.

  • place objects with 9 prepositions without in/on and near/next to mix-ups

Target language
Lexis
bathbathroombedbedroomceilingchairdining roomfloorgardenhallkitchenliving roomshowertabletoiletwallabovebehindbetweeninin front ofnearnext toonunder
Structure
There's a bed in the bedroom. · Near the bed, there's a small table with a clock on it. · The chair is between the table and the wall. · This is my flat. It's got three bedrooms.
Form
on = any surface (on the wall/floor) · near WITHOUT to · next to = right beside · in front OF (don't drop the of) · furniture — uncountable: some furniture, NOT a furniture.
Pron. watch
/e/ vs /ɜː/ (SB p.117): bed·next·test — short /e/; furniture·girl·word·work — long /ɜː/. Plus: ceiling /ˈsiːlɪŋ/ (long i:), floor /flɔː/, furniture /ˈfɜːnɪtʃə(r)/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“A picture IN the wall — OK?” → No: ON the wall (on = surface).
“The dog is near TO the house?” → No — near the house, without to.
“In front the door or in front OF the door?” → OF is required, it's “one word”.
“Can I say ‘a furniture’?” → No — furniture uncountable: some furniture / a piece of furniture.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: basic rooms and there is/are are active, house vocabulary partly familiar. The real gaps: precise prepositions, furniture words (ceiling, hall, shower), connected descriptive speech. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (-ing spelling / missing ‘be’ / question word order), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
