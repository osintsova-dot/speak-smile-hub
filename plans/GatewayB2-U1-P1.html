🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the language of school life and higher education (terms, coursework, continuous assessment, grades, assignments, campus, lecture, tutor, undergraduate, student loan, extra-curricular activities) to describe their own education, and read five parallel texts for paraphrase — matching a statement to the person who means it, not to the person who repeats the words.
What is genuinely hard
Two things. First, this vocabulary looks transparent and is not: a Russian «курс» is a course, a year AND a lecture course; «оценка» is a grade, a mark and an assessment; and continuous assessment has no one-word Russian equivalent at all. Second, the reading task never repeats the words of the text — a student who hunts for the matching word finds the wrong club every time.
Typical error to expect
«I study on the second course» for I'm in my second year, «I have good marks on all subjects» for in all subjects, «make an exam» for take an exam. Russian prepositions and Russian verbs are carried across whole.
Why the lesson is built this way
The vocabulary arrives inside a real case study — Emma Johnstone, sixteen, answering questions about her sixth form — so the words are met as answers to questions the students will ask each other five minutes later. The higher-education half comes from a text about what actually happens in a British university's first week, which is genuinely new information and therefore worth reading. The reading then does the exam job: five short forum posts about five extremely odd clubs (underwater hockey, robot football, a dog society, Model United Nations, a charity jailbreak) and five statements that paraphrase them. The lesson ends on opinion — which club would be worth your time — because that is where the new words become the student's own.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One of today's clubs plays hockey at the bottom of a swimming pool, holding their breath. Another gives you 36 hours to get as far from campus as you can with no money. Today: the English words for school and university — and how to find the right text when it never uses the words of the question.”

Vocabulary in context · school life
desks
Why: The words are met as answers to real questions, so the collocation comes with the word. Self-correction against audio is faster and less humiliating than a teacher's correction.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.6 ex.1–2: ten words dropped into a real case study, then checked by ear

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set up ex.1 exactly as the Teacher's Book says: Read the whole question AND the whole answer before you write anything. The clue is usually in the answer.
  2. Do gap (a) together: How many … does your academic year have? — Three. So what kind of word do we need? A plural. → terms.
  3. Students work alone, three minutes. Do not check yet — the Teacher's Book is right: checking now kills the listening.
  4. Play track 01. Students check and correct their own gaps, then listen and repeat the ten words for stress.
  5. Board the four that Russian gets wrong: take an exam, hand in an assignment, get a grade, do coursework. Elicit the Russian only if someone is lost — then go back to English.
📺 On screen: SB p.6 open on the board app if you have it, otherwise nothing — the page is in their hands. Backup for the audio: if track 01 will not play, read the ten answers aloud yourself at natural speed twice — the key is in the plan below (SB p.6 ex.2).
👦 Students do: fill the ten gaps alone, self-correct against the recording, then repeat the words for stress

Quiz-Quiz-Trade + the case-study interview

Why: Kagan PIES: every student speaks in every round, and the cards mean nobody waits for a turn. The interview then puts the word into a whole question, which is how it will be needed.

WHAT WE DO: the ten words go into the mouth, not the notebook

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the ten word cards (one word per card, the definition on the back). Quiz-Quiz-Trade: stand up, find a partner, quiz them on your word, swap cards, find a new partner. Four minutes, everybody moving.
  2. Stop them on a clap. Straight into SB p.6 ex.3 in pairs: Ask your partner the SIX complete questions from exercise 1. Not the words — the whole question.
  3. Monitor for the three Russian carry-overs (on the second course, marks on subjects, make an exam) and write what you hear on the board — unnamed, no author.
  4. Thirty seconds of correction at the end: the class fixes the three boarded sentences together.
📺 On screen: the timer on the board for the four minutes of Quiz-Quiz-Trade. Backup: if there is no timer, use a phone stopwatch face up on the desk.
👦 Students do: quiz five or six different partners standing up, then interview one partner with the six full questions

WHAT WE DO: SB p.6 ex.4–5: what actually happens in a British university's first week

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the reading paragraph by paragraph, as the Teacher's Book advises: read one paragraph, stop, agree on the bold words with your partner, go on.
  2. Feed in the culture, briefly: freshers' week is the week before classes start; the freshers' fair is a hall full of societies trying to recruit you; a student loan is money from a bank you repay after you graduate.
  3. Pronunciation: write lecture, culture, future, sculpture. The -ture ending is /tʃə(r)/. Not /tuːr/. Listen: lecture, culture, future. Whole class repeats twice.
  4. Ex.5 in pairs — complete the three questions with words from the text, then interview your partner with them.
  5. Two answers taken from the floor via Numbered Heads Together so the reporting is not volunteer-driven.
📺 On screen: the four -ture words on the board in big letters with the phonemic ending under them. Backup: none needed — board and voice.
👦 Students do: read in paragraphs with a partner, then ask and answer the three completed questions

48–60′
Reading for paraphrase · the exam skill of the lesson
desks
Why: This is the exam skill named in the aim, and it only becomes a skill when the proof line is compulsory. Coaching in pairs means every student explains at least five answers aloud.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.7 ex.3: five statements, five people, and not one shared word

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the Exam tip aloud, then make it concrete: The words of the question are NOT in the text. If you find the same word, be suspicious — it is usually the wrong text.
  2. Model item 1 in full. Which person thinks the name of their club clearly reflects what people do in the club? Key words: name, reflects, what people do. Answer d, and the proof is four words long: The name says it all. Show that not one word is shared.
  3. Students do 2–5 alone, five minutes, writing the proof line next to each answer. The answer alone is not accepted.
  4. RallyCoach in pairs: A reads the answer and the proof, B agrees or challenges; then swap.
  5. Whole-class check: for every item, ask for the proof line, not the letter. Key: 1 d · 2 a · 3 c · 4 e · 5 b.
📺 On screen: the five statements projected while the students work, so eyes stay on the text and not on the rubric. Backup: write the five key-word groups on the board instead — name/reflects, winning/matters, alter/personality, duration, learn from.
👦 Students do: answer alone WITH a proof line, then coach a partner through their five answers

WHAT WE DO: SB p.7 ex.5: eight expressions guessed from the line they sit in

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Do not look them up. Read the whole sentence, then the sentence before it, and write your guess in Russian or English — a guess is fine.
  2. Pairs compare guesses for take your mind off, have a bite to eat, staff, reckon, role-play, current affairs, speech, jailbreak.

🎯 Which club would be worth your time
open floor → desks
Why: The words only become theirs when they are used to say something the student actually means. Reporting the partner makes listening compulsory.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.7 ex.4 and ex.6: opinion with evidence, the productive outcome of the lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the task precisely: Choose ONE club. Say how beneficial it would be FOR YOU and give one fact from the text and one fact about yourself. Give the frame on the board: … would be perfect for me because … . It says in the text that … . On the other hand, … wouldn't work for me because …
  2. Two minutes of silent note-making. Nobody speaks yet.
  3. Timed Pair Share: 45 seconds each way, listener silent. Then change partners and repeat once — the second telling is always better than the first.
  4. Four reports from the floor, and the rule is: report your PARTNER'S opinion, not your own.
  5. Put any new word that came out of their mouths on the word wall, written by the student who used it.
📺 On screen: the sentence frame on the board for the whole stage. Backup: if you have no board space, dictate the frame and have them write it at the top of the page.
👦 Students do: make notes, speak twice to two different partners under a timer, then report a partner's view

WHAT WE DO: seven minutes: the paraphrase habit transferred to the shape of the Russian reading paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say it plainly, once: The task you have just done is task twelve to eighteen of the Russian exam, in English clothes. Same skill, different paper.
  2. Board one exam-style question about the forum texts: The writer of post b believes that … A the club is more serious than it looks. B the club is a good place to meet people. C the club teaches more than his lectures do.
  3. Thirty seconds alone, then hands up with the proof line (C — «I learn as much from this club as from my science lectures»).
  4. Name the one habit they take away: Underline the words that prove it. In the exam, an answer you cannot prove is a guess.
  5. This is a teacher-facing frame, not an exam lesson. Seven minutes, then it is over — do not turn the class into a test-preparation group.
📺 On screen: the one exam-style question on the board. Backup: read it aloud twice if the board is full.
👦 Students do: answer one three-option question in thirty seconds and read out the proof line

Workbook p.4–5
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can describe their own education in precise English using about twenty words for school life and higher education, and match statements to parallel texts by recognising paraphrase.
  • Subsidiary. Listening to check vocabulary (track 01); reading five short forum texts for gist and then for detail (track 02); guessing eight items of vocabulary from context; the /tʃə(r)/ ending in lecture and culture.
  • Personal. Open the unit WORD WALL in two columns — SCHOOL LIFE and HIGHER EDUCATION — on the side board. It stays up for the whole unit and every new word goes on it, written by the student who first used it correctly.
By the end, students can…
  • describe their timetable, terms, coursework and assessment in English
  • explain what a campus, a tutorial and a student loan are, without Russian
  • match five statements to five texts that never repeat the words of the statement
  • give an opinion on an extra-curricular activity and justify it with an example

Structure

Form

Pron. watch
the ending -ture is /tʃə(r)/, never /tuːr/: lecture, culture, future · word stress moves and changes the word: undergraduate vs undergraduate is wrong — undergraduate · qualifications · education · weak forms in the perfect: I've been /aɪvbɪn/, not /aɪ hæv biːn/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Continuous assessment» — is that one exam at the end of the year? → no, the opposite: marks collected all year from coursework.
Emma says «80% of our mark depends on exams». Is coursework an exam? → no. Coursework is work you hand in during the term.
ICQ before the reading: are the words of the question in the text? → no. So what do I look for? → the same IDEA in different words.
«I'd love to do a degree in medicine» — does she have the degree? → no, she wants to study for it.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They translate the words instead of learning the collocationEvery word goes on the wall inside a phrase, never alone: not «assignment» but «hand in an assignment». When a student offers a bare word, you say the phrase and they repeat it.
The matching task is done by hunting for identical wordsKill it in the first item, publicly: read statement 1, find the words in text d, and show that d does not contain a single word of the statement. Then every answer has to be justified with the line that proves it.
Two or three students dominate the discussion of the clubsTimed Pair Share with an actual timer: 45 seconds each, the listener says nothing. Then the LISTENER reports the partner's opinion, not their own.
Fast finishers in the vocabulary stageThe Teacher's Book task: mark the stress on qualifications, undergraduate, education and accommodation, check it in a dictionary and teach it to the class in thirty seconds.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 10–18 (the whole reading section) and tasks 26–31 (word formation). Education is the single most frequent topic in the Russian paper.

Exercise 3 on page 7 is task 12–18 in disguise: five statements have to be matched to five texts, and not one of them repeats the words of the text. That is the exam skill in its pure form — recognising the same idea in different words. The vocabulary is exam currency too: higher education, continuous assessment, coursework, undergraduate and campus print in the lexical section and are needed the moment the speaking card says «education».

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
