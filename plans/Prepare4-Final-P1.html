🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Meet the B1 Preliminary for Schools format and survive it calmly: one exam-format reading (questions matched into a gapped text), one exam-format listening (two plays, no rescue), and a paired speaking with examiner-style questions and a photo each.
What is genuinely hard
Not the language — the conditions. Two plays and no third; a matching task with two spare questions designed to tempt; a minute of speaking that must be FILLED. The skill of the day is staying in the task when it stops being comfortable.
Typical error to expect
A blank on a multiple-choice paper is the only unforgivable answer: a guess scores sometimes, a blank scores never [exam craft, said calmly]
A one-word reply does not count as an answer at all: an answer needs a verb in it [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
An honest design note first: the course folder has no complete B1 Preliminary sample paper, and the syllabus does not ask for one — it asks to MEET the format, train it and remove the fear. So the session is built entirely from the publisher's own exam-format worksheets (Cambridge 2019, with keys and scripts) plus the official Speaking bank. Listening goes first because its clock belongs to the recording; the reading's exam task is done solo in silence; the paired speaking runs all pairs simultaneously, sampled rather than chaired — that is how a class of teenagers produces a speaking sample inside one lesson. Each block opens with the worksheet's own primer, because that is what the publisher built the primers for.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Next year some of you sit a Cambridge exam. Today you meet it — and it is smaller than you think. One reading, one listening played exactly twice, and a real paired speaking. Nothing today gets a mark. Everything today gets you information: what already works, and what we fix next lesson.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls back the one thing that is needed today rather than everything that was ever taught.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a two-minute exchange of exam knowledge and myths, and the calm naming of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — format words, Kagan Think-Pair-Share: three words on the board — examiner · task · options. What do you already know about Cambridge exams? Twenty seconds, tell your partner, I take two answers.
  3. Collect calmly; correct nothing today except terror.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): name the day plainly: Today is the mock. Three parts, no marks, all information. By five to the hour you will have met the exam — and it is smaller than you think.
📺 On screen: three words in chalk — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: share what they know and fear. Expect myths («you fail and that's it») — park them for the briefing

WHAT WE DO: what B1 Preliminary is, what today imitates, and the three rules

WHAT WE DO: the worksheet's own lead-in: the first-aid kit words

20–40′
Listening paper · two plays
solo, exam conditions
Why: The listening goes first because its clock belongs to the recording: everyone starts and stops together, which settles the room — and a lapse of attention here cannot be recovered, so it gets the freshest minutes.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 on play one, ex.4 on play two — no pauses, no rescue

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pens ready. Play one — questions one to five, short answers. Nobody catches everything. Go. Play the mp3 straight through.
  2. Ninety silent seconds to complete answers. Then: Play two — new job: the six hikers' rules on ex.4. Did Mark follow them? Straight through again.
  3. Three minutes to finish notes; pairs compare ONLY ex.4 wording (the content answers stay solo).
  4. Check both against the key, out loud, no drama: ex.3: 1 call an ambulance · 2 yes · 3 on the mountain, under a tree · 4 summer · 5 a mountain rescue team — and ex.4: ex.4: 1 no — he was alone · 2 he HAD good boots but took them off · 3 yes, he can make a fire · 4 yes, he had food and water · 5 no — no first-aid kit, not even painkillers · 6 yes — his flatmate knew where he had gone
  5. One craft line to close: who got more on play two than play one? Everyone. That is what play two is for.
📺 On screen: the mp3 TOR_B1_L_WS_1_Ex03_A02.mp3 in the whiteboard app (Backup: the FULL Kerry-and-Mark script is printed in the teacher's notes — perform it in two voices at natural speed, twice, and the paper works identically)
👦 Students do: listen twice, write short, compare craft not answers. Expect item 5 (mountain rescue team) to need play two — by design. Typical slip: answers of a whole sentence where 1–4 words are asked

Reading primer
pairs
Why: Six housing words unlock the blog; pre-teaching them is not cheating — the real paper assumes exactly this level of lexis, and the worksheet ships the task for that reason.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the six housing words that unlock the blog

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs, 3 minutes: suburbs, semi-detached, to dock, running water, tank, launderette. Check fast: ex.1: 1 suburbs · 2 semi-detached · 3 To dock · 4 running water · 5 tank · 6 launderette.
  2. Frame the text in one line: Leo is sixteen and lives on a houseboat. People keep asking him questions — your job is to put the right question over each answer.
  3. The craft line, once: two questions are spares. The wrong one reuses Leo's WORDS; the right one matches his MEANING.
📺 On screen: the sheets are paper — no backup needed
👦 Students do: unlock the lexis, hear the craft line. Expect launderette to amuse — let it; a remembered word is a found answer later

48–68′
Reading paper · the exam task
solo, exam conditions
Why: Questions matched into a gapped text with two spares is the B1 Reading Part 4 shape: reading for structure, not for words. Done solo and in silence, it is the most honest reading sample of the year.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 (A–H into six gaps, two spares) + ex.3 true/false with corrections — in silence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Fifteen minutes, solo, silence. ex.2 first — read ALL eight questions before the text. Then ex.3; when a sentence is false, write WHY in your own words. Go. Visible timer.
  2. Walk twice, answer nothing about content; the frame card of the three rules stays on the board.
  3. Two-minute pair comparison at the end — answers may change ONLY if the pair can quote the line that proves it.
  4. Check aloud: ex.2 — ex.2 (the exam task): 1 H · 2 G · 3 E · 4 C · 5 D · 6 A; ex.3 — ex.3: 1 F (they wanted a house with a garden) · 2 T · 3 T · 4 F (the neighbours are unfriendly) · 5 T · 6 F (the holiday apartment has luxuries his home lacks).
  5. Fast finishers: the sheet's own ex.4 ranking (what a home needs), solo notes.
📺 On screen: visible timer counting down 15 minutes (Backup: the end time chalked in the board corner and updated by hand — the paper itself needs nothing electric)
👦 Students do: read the questions first, match by meaning, prove corrections. Expect gaps 4–5 (parents' feelings / winter) to swap in weaker papers — the quote rule settles it. Typical slip: F/B chosen because they repeat «neighbours» and «bathroom»

🎯 Speaking · pairs, exam shape
pairs, simultaneous
Why: The paired speaking is the half of the exam nobody can sit alone at home — and the half the syllabus names for today. Every pair works simultaneously; the teacher samples, not chairs.

WHAT WE DO: Part 1 question sets + Part 2 a photo each way — all pairs at once, teacher sampling

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the mechanics from the bank: run BOTH halves in pairs simultaneously — A examines B with a question set, swap; then each pair gets one photo each way. The teacher orbits with the examiner phrases and samples two or three pairs closely.
  2. Round 1 — Part 1 (6 minutes, Kagan Timed Pair Share discipline): A examines B with one printed set, swap sets and roles. The sets: Speaking bank Part 1: examiner-style personal questions in three printed sets (name and home · yesterday evening, English in your future, a place you'd like to visit, your house · free time, your English teacher, another country, your home town, transport, last weekend). Model: Maria on audio 01–02; key language box — frequency adverbs, past time phrases, going to, I'd like to…, actually/also/because.
  3. Round 2 — Part 2 (8 minutes): a photo each way, about a minute each, frames on the desk card. Speaking bank Part 2: describe a photo for about a minute. Key language box — The picture shows… · at the front / in the background · present continuous · It's a kind of… · He looks like… / It might be… Practice photos on the bank's own pages (students in a canteen, a family meal, cyclists, an art class); model on audio 04–06.
  4. You orbit with a sampling sheet: hear at least three pairs closely, note fluency and the one-word-answer habit — names go in your log, not on the board.
  5. Close with the exam's own kindness: you just did the paper most adults fear most. In pairs, in English, for fifteen minutes. Noted.
📺 On screen: the question sets and photos are printed from the Speaking bank — paper is the medium; the model audio 01–06 is optional (Backup: skip the models entirely — the printed key-language boxes carry the task)
👦 Students do: examine each other, describe, escape missing words with the frames. Expect the strongest speaking of the year — pairs protect each other. Typical slip: photo described in the past tense — the frame card's present continuous fixes it silently

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Two of the questions are not needed» — do I still read them? → yes: they are traps built from the text's own words; rejecting them IS the task.
«The recording is played twice» — what is play one for? → the sure answers; play two checks and fills the rest. Nobody writes everything on play one.
«Describe the photo for about a minute» — what if I don't know a word? → say it another way: it's a kind of…, it looks like… — the exam rewards the escape, not the silence.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Panic at the speed of the recordingName it in the briefing: nobody catches everything on play one — the task is built for two plays. Sure answers first, gaps second.
The two spare questions in the reading trap half the classOne line of craft before the silence: the wrong option reuses the text's WORDS; the right one matches its MEANING. Check item 1 together if the class is fragile — then silence.
Speaking pairs dry up at twenty secondsThe frame card stays on every desk (The picture shows… · In the background… · He seems…). A dried-up speaker reads the next frame aloud and continues — that is the trained exam move.
A strong student finishes the reading ten minutes earlyThe worksheet's own ex.4–5 (rank what a home needs; would you live on a houseboat) are the quiet second job — solo notes, to be spoken in the closing minutes if time allows.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

What today gives a student who sits that exam next year is not the format — it is the experience of working through exam-shaped paper with nobody helping, plus a paired speaking with real reaction language. The B1 Preliminary wrapper differs from the OGE wrapper; the stamina and the reading discipline underneath are the same muscle.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
