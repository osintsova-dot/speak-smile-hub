Challenge: the ideal classroom with arguments
🔥 Challenge · dream classroom
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: p.8 ex.4–6: classroom words as a blitz → Kagan RallyRobin of reasons “which classroom is better?” → ex.6 check via Kagan Numbered Heads Together

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4, 90 sec: 8 objects across two classrooms (key: clock · window · board · door · poster · computer · desk · chair).
  2. Ex.5 via RallyRobin: Pairs, ping-pong: A — one reason for classroom A, B — one reason for B. Your turn — my turn, till the timer! TB model on the board: I prefer… because… · I agree/disagree with… because…
  3. Ex.6 via Numbered Heads Together: numbers 1–3 in the trios; Heads together! over 5 gaps in the text; I call a number — they answer for the team (key: chairs · desk · board · computers · posters). Follow-up TB: Which classroom is this? — B!
  4. Fast finishers: a list of everything in OUR classroom, in plurals.
📺 On screen: SB p.8 · frame I prefer… because… / I agree with…
👦 Students do: they match objects, argue about the classrooms with reasons, fill in the text. Expected: a lively A vs B argument. Typical error: a reason in Russian — I point at the frame, repeat in English.

this/that/these/those accuracy
desks → standing

WHAT WE DO: p.9 ex.7–8: the table in writing → a live drill pointing round the real classroom; this/these accuracy

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.7 solo, 2 min: the table (key: That's · These are · Those are). Anchor gestures: this — finger close, that — finger far, these — palm close, those — palm far.
  2. Minimum pronunciation drill: this /ðɪs/ — these /ðiːz/, short-long.
  3. Ex.8 live: stand up, Student A points at a classroom object — B names it: That's a clock. These are our desks. SWAP, 6 objects each.
  4. “Catch the mistake” on mini-boards: This are my books · Those is a door — they fix them in writing.
📺 On screen: SB p.9 · the real classroom · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they fill in the table, point and name standing up (everyone moves), fix mistakes. Expected: gestures clear up the confusion. Typical error: this are — gesture: “palm = are”.

A5 grammar card — POINT & NAME: the NEAR/FAR × ONE/MORE grid (this · that · these · those) with a model for each, plus the two helpers — a/an by sound (a bag · an object) and the plural spelling routes (+s · -ch/-sh/-s/-x/-z +es · consonant+y → ies)
  • Glue the card in; pupils draw the NEAR/FAR arrows onto the grid themselves.
  • Stand up: point at a near object, then a far one, and say the right sentence.
  • Test the a/an helper by sound, not by letter — an hour, a university.
  • Write four sentences about the real classroom, one per cell of the grid.

WHAT WE DO: p.9 Instructions ex.1–2 + Grammar ex.3–4: command icons → the imperative rule → a volley of don't-forms; audio 4

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1, 2 min: 8 icons ↔ 8 commands (key: 1e 2b 3d 4a 5c 6g 7f 8h). TB: opposite of open? — close; listen — the t is silent!

66–74′
Preparing the command session
Prepare the product
desks · trios

WHAT WE DO: we prepare a 60-sec command session: 5+ commands, at least 2 negative, + a this/these pointer; a run-through in the trio

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The task: Your product: run the class for sixty seconds. Five+ commands, two negatives, one pointer sentence (These are… so don't…!). Keywords only.
  2. My own example: Stand up! Look at those posters! Don't touch the computers — these are our computers! Sit down. Thank you!
  3. A run-through in trios with roles (Commander / Soldier / Referee), rotation ×3.
  4. I walk around, fixing only don't + inf. and the register of politeness (please/thank you — a commander, not a dictator).
📺 On screen: rubric · command bank
👦 Students do: they build the session out of the bank, run it through as a trio. Expected: creativity (“Don't look at Misha!”). Typical error: a dictator's tone without thank you — “commander, not dictator”.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — forms are clean (1–3) · Richness — details, colours, numbers, connectors (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		prep slips

Own assignment (no WB pages for the Starter): 1) Draw/photograph your classroom (or room) and label 6+ objects with arrows: close ones — This is… / These are…, far ones — That's… / Those are… (a trick from TB Homework). 2) Write 4 commands for a “home lesson” for your parents: 2 ordinary + 2 with Don't. 3) Spelling practice: Wednesday · Thursday · Tuesday — 3 times each, rematch in Pair 4.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can run a 60-sec instruction sequence for the class (5+ imperatives, ± don't) and describe a classroom with this/that/these/those accurately.
  • Subsidiary. Days of the week: pronunciation targets nailed (Wednesday!); classroom words → a full description of the ideal classroom; this/these accuracy in writing; a mini-gate on classroom English.
  • Personal. The lesson hands power to the kids: not the teacher commanding — them. That's exactly the teen format: output earlier, responsibility faster. Keep the commands 100% in English.
By the end, students can…
  • say the days with clean pronunciation (Wednesday, Tuesday, Thursday)
  • describe a classroom and their preference with reasons
  • use this/that/these/those accurately in writing
  • give and follow 5+ classroom commands, incl. negatives

Structure
I prefer classroom A because… · This is a chair. — That's the board. — These are our desks. — Those are our computers. · Open your books! · Don't talk!
Form
this/these = close, that/those = far; is/'s + singular, are + plural (still chunks — be comes in U1!); imperative = infinitive without to; negative = Don't + inf.
Pron. watch
Wednesday — the d is silent! · Tuesday /ˈtjuːz/ · Thursday /ˈθɜːz/ · listen — the t is silent · don't /dəʊnt/ (the t is often swallowed: Don'(t) talk)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Wednesday — how many syllables?” → two: /ˈwenzdeɪ/, the d is silent.
“I point at the far chairs: this or those?” → those — far AND many.
“Imperative — do you need you?” → No: Open your books, no you.

Assumptions: Days and classroom words are known orally from GMF; gaps: spelling Wednesday/Thursday, this/these in writing, the habit of following commands is there, but GIVING commands isn't. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling! numbers by ear! alphabet at speed!), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Likely problemWhat you do

Simon Says turns into chaosA Referee role in every trio; one clap — reset; commands only from the list on the board.
Someone's shy about commanding the classFirst they command the trio (safe), for the whole class — volunteers; no pressure.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
