WHAT WE DO: Showdown for revision: greeting them at the door; a U1 blitz revision (nationalities + be) for points; a bridge into the new unit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: Hi! One word — how are you? Mood fingers 1–5.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everybody writes at the same time, boards face down, on the call No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up together — I see every answer, nobody can hide.
  3. U1 revision ping-pong: Russia! — Russian! Japan! — Japanese! 5 rounds in chorus.
  4. 2 rounds written on mini-boards: Spell: SPANISH! Boards UP! +1 for a clean one.
  5. A volley of be: Are you 11? Is our town big? — in chorus, full short answers.
  6. Bridge: Unit 1 — the world. Unit 2 — the people you love. Today we meet a family with a secret… and a game that is one hundred years old.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · my list of countries
👦 Students do: they ping-pong nationalities, write spelling, answer politely. Expected: U1 skills are alive. Typical error: a bare “Yes!” — the class buzzer from U1 still works.

Hook · WDYT wall

WHAT WE DO: everybody sticks a note on “what makes a family a family”, teams, the Unit 2 points board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out sticky notes, open the unit question: What is a family? People who live together? Love? Help? Fun? — 4 columns on the board.
  2. Command: ONE sticky note — stick it on YOUR answer. Go!
  3. I read out the winning column, and promise: We'll come back to this question at the end of the unit — with a project.
  4. I show the Seven Families card: This game is from France. It's over 100 years old. Today — we play it in English!
  5. Teams of 3 (the line-up is planned), a new Unit 2 points board, I carry over the entry points.
📺 On screen: board: “What is a family?” + 4 icon columns · Seven Families card (slide/printout) · an empty board (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they stick their notes — everybody moves; they sit down in teams. Expected: an argument over “love or fun” — I quiet it with the “Eyes up in 3-2-1” signal.

10–21′
Discovery: the Canes family tree
Discover · the Canes tree
board → desks

WHAT WE DO: I build the Canes family tree on the board with choral repetition; the /ə/ chant; teams complete ex.2 + the Language note

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I draw the Canes family tree (from SB p.25): Victor+Louise → Sandra+Paul · Anna+Pierre → Martin, Lena, Hugo. Photo-names as we go.
  2. I say a word — the class repeats it ×2; I write the male words in blue, female in green: mother/father · son/daughter · grandmother/grandfather · aunt/uncle · cousin (in both colours!).
  3. The /ə/ chant, finger at the lips: mo-thə, fa-thə, bro-thə, sis-tə, daugh-tə — no ‘r’!
  4. Language note: father + mother = PARENTS. son + daughter = CHILDREN. No brothers, no sisters = an ONLY CHILD.
  5. Task (ex.2): Complete the six sentences about the Canes. Teams, three minutes! Key: mother · sister · son · daughter · brother · father. +1 per item.
📺 On screen: board: the Canes tree, built before their eyes and STAYS up till the end of the lesson; blue/green colour code · SB pp.24–25
👦 Students do: they repeat in chorus, chant /ə/, complete the sentences by the tree in teams. Expected: 5–6 out of 6. Typical error: they look for “daughter” as “dotter” — a finger on daughter in the tree: the letters are tricky, the sound is simple.
board tree · SB pp.24–25
21–30′
Pair race: male ↔ female word
Play · Male–Female match race
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: Quiz-Quiz-Trade: an ex.4 race (8 male↔female pairs) + ex.5 riddles in chorus; sorting into “3 generations”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Race ex.4: Male to female — match all eight! Careful: ONE word is for both. Two minutes!
  2. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the unit's printed cards (handout GIA1-QQT: the question on top, the answer on the back after folding; no printouts — the students write their own cards in 2 min).
  3. Commands: Stand up, hand up, pair up! Quiz your partner — praise — TRADE cards — find a new partner! 3–4 trades, EVERYBODY asks at the same time.
  4. I check in chorus with colour gestures (blue hand/green hand): nephew — NIECE! grandson — GRANDDAUGHTER! cousin — …COUSIN!
  5. Riddles ex.5 in chorus by the 5-second rule: My father's sister is my…? — AUNT! My aunt's son is my…? — COUSIN!
  6. Twist (Fast finishers TB): Sort the words into three generations: grand-level, parents-level, kids-level. Ninety seconds!
📺 On screen: SB p.25 ex.4–5 · timer · board
👦 Students do: they match pairs at speed, answer the riddles in chorus, sort the generations. Expected: cousin trips up half the class — laughter and it sticks. Typical error: nephew↔niece — the colour gesture fixes it.
SB p.25 · timer · the unit's QQT cards (handouts/GIA1-QQT-U2.pdf)
30–38′
Discovery: adjectives about people (with gestures)
Discover 2 · describing people
board → pairs

WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin: 3 adjective pairs with gestures (ex.6) + “my family or a TV show” (ex.7) from the frame

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I introduce the pairs with gestures: tall (hand up)/short (hand down) · dark/fair (pointing at hair) · old/young. The class copies the gesture + word ×2.
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we alternate one answer at a time — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!
  3. Ex.6: photos A–C → pairs name the adjectives (TB key: A short, old · B tall, dark · C young, fair).
  4. Frame on the board: ___ : my ___ (he's/she's ___). TB model: old: my grandfather — he's 79!
  5. Ex.7 in pairs: match a person (family OR a TV show/film — a safe choice!) to each adjective, tell your partner.
📺 On screen: board: 3 adjective pairs with gestures · the ex.7 frame · SB p.25
👦 Students do: they learn the gestures, describe the photos, match their own people/characters to the frame. Expected: “tall: my dad!”, “old: Dumbledore!”. Typical error: “my grandfather is tall and old and big…” — I ask for ONE word per person today.

47–57′
Speaking circle: the first I've got… (no theory)
Try it · have got frames (no theory)
standing circle

WHAT WE DO: speaking circle with a ball: a relative card → “I've got a brother! I haven't got a sister!” — the class echoes; no grammar

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Circle; frames on the board: I've got a ___ . / I haven't got a ___ .
  2. I model it myself, slowly, twice: I've got a brother. I haven't got a sister.
  3. I throw the ball + a word card (brother/sister/cousin/uncle…): caught it — say the TRUTH about yourself by the frame (or about an invented character — your choice), the class echoes.
  4. Everybody catches it 1–2 times; I correct ONLY the family word, I don't touch the grammar: The machine inside these frames — we open it in Pair 3!
📺 On screen: board: 2 frames, big · family word cards · a soft ball
👦 Students do: they catch the ball, tell the truth by the frame, the class echoes. Expected: “I've got a cousin!” with pride. Typical error: “I have brother” — I gently re-ask through the frame, the echo fixes it.
soft ball · word cards

Play · Seven Families

WHAT WE DO: we play “Seven Families” light: build a family from 6 cards by asking “Have you got the (Duffy) father?”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out the decks (prepared before the lesson: 4–6 families × 6 cards: father·mother·son·daughter·grandfather·grandmother, families from SB p.24: the Huangs, the Williams, the Canes, the Duffys…).
  2. I demonstrate with a strong student: Have you got the Duffy father? — Yes! Here you are. / No! Take a card.
  3. ICQ: Do you ask in Russian? — No! No frame — no card!
  4. I start the game in groups of 3–4 (rules SB p.24: collect the whole family); frame strips on the desks.
  5. I walk round, listen for have got, note down 2–3 errors anonymously for P3.
📺 On screen: “Seven Families” decks ×4 · frame strips on the desks · SB p.24 (rules)
👦 Students do: they play the real 100-year-old game, running the Have you got…? frame dozens of times. Expected: excitement + the frame becomes automatic without a “grammar lesson”. Typical error: they silently draw a card — the card doesn't count, the rule of the game drives it home.

Writing 4 sentences about your own family
Build · first writing portion
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: they write 4 sentences about their family (real or invented) by the frames, peer review by 2 checkboxes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model on the board: I've got a brother. His name is Sasha. He's 8. He's short and fair.
  2. I erase everything except the frames: I've got a ___ . His/Her name is ___ . He's/She's ___ . He's/She's ___ and ___ .
  3. I say: Four sentences — YOUR family or an invented one. Five minutes. Timer.
  4. Peer check: Swap. Two boxes: family word from the tree? adjective from today? Tick or underline — the author fixes. +1 for a clean check.
📺 On screen: board: model → frames · slide of 2 checkboxes (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they write 4 sentences in one portion, check a neighbour by 2 points. Expected: clean by the frames. Typical error of the checker: fixes it themselves — “underline it, the author fixes it HIMSELF” (the U1 ritual).

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 17 family words and describe people with 3 adjective pairs: “Martin is Lena's brother. My grandfather is old — he's 79.”
  • Subsidiary. Meet WDYT? What is a family?; the Canes family tree as the anchor visual; male↔female system; Video skills (Jonas) with an active task; first contextual contact with have got in frames (system lesson = Pair 3). Pronunciation /ə/ endings (SB p.116).
  • Personal. Unit 2 rituals continue from U1: check-in + rewind, teams of 3, points board, timers, English-first. Family topic is personal — “tell us about YOUR OWN family” always optional: the Canes and celebrities work as safe material.
By the end, students can…
  • name 17 family words
  • solve who-am-I riddles on a family tree
  • describe a person with tall/short, dark/fair, old/young
  • say I've got… / I haven't got… in a frame

Structure
Louise is Paul's mother. · My sister's daughter is my niece. · He's tall and dark. · Frames (no theory): I've got a brother. I haven't got a sister.
Form
male↔female pairs (nephew/niece…); cousin = both; father+mother = parents, son+daughter = children; only child (Language note SB p.24).
Pron. watch
/ə/ endings without ‘r’: mother, father, brother, sister, daughter (chant with a finger on lips); nephew /ˈnefjuː/, niece /niːs/, aunt /ɑːnt/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Parents — how many people?” → two: mum + dad.
“Cousin — a boy or a girl?” → both! One word.
“An only child has got …?” → no brothers, no sisters.

Assumptions: After U1: be (all forms), classroom routines, mini-boards, peer-review basics are in place. Basic mum/dad/brother known from school; the SYSTEM of 17 words (niece/nephew/grandson…) is new. have got is NOT presented today — only heard in frames.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
17 words feel like a mountainAnchor everything on the Canes tree ON the board all lesson; new words only enter THROUGH the tree, never as a list.
nephew/niece/cousin confusionColour trick: male words blue, female green, cousin — both colours; gestures for each pair.
Family topic sensitive for some kidsRule out loud: we describe the Canes, celebrities or invented families — «your family» is always optional.
Video too fastTask is names-bingo (visual), two plays, pause before Jonas's quiz answers.
Answers in RussianAccept idea, recast in English, team repeats for the point (ritual from U1).
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
