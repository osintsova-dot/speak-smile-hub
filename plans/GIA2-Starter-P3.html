🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): can is the most «already done» structure in their lives, so the lesson is not about can but about a licence: claiming an ability is not enough, you have to PROVE it in 60 seconds or the line gets crossed out. That changes the quality of the language: qualifiers appear (quite well, not really, a bit) that are not in the book — and that is an honest growth area for our students. The second half of the pair is the HQ map: finding your way round the book through a race (SB p.11), so that all year you never hear «where is that?». Test yourself p.10 ex.6 works as a crew race, not as a test.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Passport, file… and now the licence. A licence is not a list of things you SAY you can do. Anyone can say 'I can draw'. A licence says: prove it, sixty seconds, right now. Every claim you can't prove gets crossed out. And second job: this book is our HQ — by the end of today you find anything in it in under ten seconds, or your crew loses the race. Licences on the desk.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The third part of the registration is the skill licence. Saying «I can» is not enough: you have 60 seconds to prove it, and anything unproven gets crossed out. Plus we learn the book as the map of the base: a «find it in 10 seconds» race.

🎯 GATE · can-sprint
solo

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: as many «I can … / I can't …» sentences as possible with a qualifier; only lines with a qualifier count

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ninety seconds. Sentences with 'can' or 'can't'. But careful: 'I can swim' scores ZERO. 'I can swim quite well' scores one. Every line needs a qualifier. GO!
  2. The qualifiers go on the board before the start: quite well · very well · a bit · not very well · really badly.
  3. The score stays private. Keep the number. Rematch at the end.

👦 Students do: they write against the clock. Expected: 4–7 lines — noticeably fewer than they expected from an «easy topic»; the qualifier is forgotten in half the lines. Mistake: «I can to draw» — silently into the notebook, for the P4 clinic

Sprint: the skills market (action verbs)
⚡ Base sprint · skills market
crews

WHAT WE DO: p.10: ex.1 ten verbs ↔ pictures as a race → audio 6 (sound scenes: can or can't?) → the collocation market

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: Ten verbs, ten pictures, ninety seconds. GO! The key round-robin style across the crews.
  2. Audio 6 as field recordings: Six recordings. Which verb — and CAN they do it or not? Careful with number three: two people, two answers. Key: 1 sing – yes · 2 play the guitar – yes · 3 speak Chinese – yes (the first one), no (the second) · 4 swim – yes · 5 spell – no · 6 dance – yes.
  3. The collocation market: a bare verb is worth nothing on the market. 'play' is worth nothing. 'play the guitar' — that's a skill. Each crew writes 8 collocations (ride a bike · speak Chinese · run fast · spell your name…).
  4. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade on the collocations: question → answer → swap cards, 3 minutes.
📺 On screen: SB p.10 ex.1–2 · audio 6 · blank cards for the collocations (backup with no sound: I act the scenes out myself — sing, play an imaginary guitar, «swim»; the students guess the verb and decide can/can't)
👦 Students do: they close the pictures, listen, collect collocations. Expected: trap no.3 (two people, different answers) catches almost everyone — a good moment to talk about listening carefully. Mistake: «play guitar» with no the — it shows up on the card

Challenge: the licence exam (can)
🔥 Challenge · licence exam
crews · mini-boards

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 they build the can table from a–d themselves → ex.4 the forms → ex.5 pair survey → a 5-second round, a broken form burns

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3: Four sentences, four gaps in the table. Close it. Two minutes. Key: 1 d · 2 a · 3 b · 4 c. They formulate the rule: can is the same for everybody, the verb is bare, the question needs no do.
  2. ex.4 in writing, then Kagan Showdown: boards up all at once.
  3. ex.5 a pair survey on their own answers: How many of your answers are the same?
  4. The licence exam, 5 seconds per turn: 'he / play the guitar / negative' → 'He can't play the guitar.' Broken form — the licence burns, next crew. 8 rounds.
  5. Pronunciation as a separate run: 'I can /kən/ swim' — weak. 'Yes, I CAN /kæn/' — strong. Say both.
📺 On screen: SB p.10 ex.3–5 · mini-boards · 5-sec timer
👦 Students do: they build the table themselves and fire the forms. Expected: «Do you can…?» hangs on longest in 1–2 students, and the burning turn knocks it out in 3 rounds. Mistake: «He cans» — the neighbour catches it on the mini-board

The product: the skill licence
PRODUCT · skill licence
solo → crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: the licence: 5 lines of abilities with qualifiers and proof → a 60-second defence → Find Someone Who + Test yourself p.10 ex.6

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 6 minutes of writing: Five lines. Each line: what you can do + how well + PROOF. At least two lines with contrast (claim + limit).
  2. Kagan Find Someone Who, 5 minutes: Find someone who can cook. Find someone who can't swim. Find someone who can speak three languages. One name per person — you have to move.
  3. On stage: Sixty seconds. Read your licence and prove two lines. Judges: any claim without proof gets crossed out — say it out loud.
  4. The finish — Test yourself (SB p.10 ex.6) as a crew race for points: six micro-tasks (6 colours · 10 animals · 3 objects beginning with 'c' · counting back 20→1 · spelling the teacher's name · the days of the week). One point for each one completed — the points go to the crew.
  5. I hand out the licences: Licence GRANTED — for 5 proven lines.
📺 On screen: the licence form (a sheet with 5 lines and a PROOF column) · timer · the Find Someone Who cards (backup: the students draw the licence frame in their notebooks in 30 seconds from the model on the board)
👦 Students do: they write, mingle, defend, race through Test yourself. Expected: crossing out the unproven lines makes the language concrete — instead of «I can draw» you get «I can draw anime faces, I've got a sketchbook». Mistake: proof given in Russian — it does not count

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, agents can claim, question and prove abilities — building affirmative, negative and question forms with can at speed, with correct short answers, and defend a 5-line skill licence in 60 seconds.
  • Subsidiary. The 10 action verbs are covered through the pictures (SB p.10 ex.1) and checked against audio 6; the can table is built from the examples (ex.3); Test yourself p.10 ex.6 is done as a crew race; finding your way round the book (SB p.11 ex.1–4 + Classroom challenge) — so that nobody hunts for pages all year.
  • Personal. My own focus is the qualifiers. A bare «I can draw» is not enough at this level: I drill quite well / not very well / a bit / really badly. And I hold the «prove it» principle: I never let an ability count without a demonstration or something concrete.
By the end, students can…
  • name 10 action verbs and their common collocations
  • build can / can't statements and questions with correct short answers
  • qualify an ability (quite well · not very well · a bit)
  • find any feature in the Student's Book in under ten seconds

Target language
Lexis
cookdancedrawplayriderunsingspeakspellswim
Structure
I can draw. · She can swim. · They can speak Chinese. · I can't sing. · He can't spell. · They can't speak French. · Can you play the guitar? — Yes, I can. / No, I can't. · Can he sing? — Yes, he can. / No, he can't. · Can they cook? — Yes, they can.
Form
can is the same for every person: he can (not «he cans») · after can comes the bare verb, no to: I can swim (not «I can to swim») · the question needs no do/does: Can you swim? (not «Do you can swim?»)
Pron. watch
can in a statement is weak /kən/, in a short answer it is full /kæn/: «I can /kən/ swim» but «Yes, I CAN /kæn/» · can't /kɑːnt/ — a long /ɑː/, not «kent» · verbs: draw /drɔː/ · swim /swɪm/ · spell /spel/ · dance /dɑːns/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«He cans swim» — what's the extra bit? → the -s: can doesn't change with person.
«Do you can dance?» — why not? → can asks its own question: Can you dance?
«I can to cook» — what do we drop? → to: after can the verb is bare.

Assumptions: SB pp.10–11, KTP pair 3 (lessons 5–6). can and the action verbs are familiar from A1+; what is new is weak /kən/ vs full /kæn/ in the answer, the qualifiers and finding your way round the book. ⚠️ There are NO flashcards for the action verbs in our set — we work with gestures and mime.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«can? seriously?» — motivation drops on the doorstepThe topic is never named. What is named is: the licence, the proof, the crossed-out line. And the gate shows them: writing 10 abilities with qualifiers in 90 seconds is not something everyone manages.
Everyone claims «I can do everything» and the product loses its valueThe licence rule: anything unproven is crossed out by the judge. Proof = a demonstration, something concrete or a fact (I can cook — I make pancakes every Sunday.).
The demonstrations wreck the discipline (one sings, another runs about)The format of the demonstration is agreed in advance: 10 seconds, from your seat, no props; sport and dancing — description or mime only.
The race round the book turns into flicking through at randomAs the Teacher's Book says: when checking the answers, every crew OPENS the page and points at the place — that is how the book is remembered physically.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
