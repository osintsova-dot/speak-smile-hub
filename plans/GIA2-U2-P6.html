🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A pair made of two broadcasts: a spoken one (asking about a course — a skill that works both in an exam and in life) and a written one (the first informal message of the unit). The «club fair» framing: half the class behind the desks, half signing up — the Key phrases become a working tool rather than a list. and/or/but is served as «the editor's standard»: broken sentences go back to the author.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Tonight the club fair opens. Half of you work the desks: photography, movie-making, chess, robotics. The other half walk in with one mission — sign up for something. The desk answers only real questions: What time does it start? How much is it? Can I sign up for it? Then, round two: a message arrives from another school, and you write the reply. Editors return messages with broken and, or and but. Desks — open!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: A club fair: half of you behind the desk, half signing up for courses with real phrases. Then the second broadcast: a message arrives from another school and you write the reply. The editor sends back any text with broken and/or/but.

🔥 The fair · the live club fair
stations

WHAT WE DO: half the class behind the desks (5 courses, each with its own timetable and price), half going round with a form; then roles swap

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out the desk cards (Photography Mon 6:00–7:30 · Yoga Tue 4:30 · Painting Wed 4:00 · Movie-making Thu 4:30 · Coding Fri 7:00) and the visitor cards with their constraints.
  2. Visitors: you must sign up for ONE course. But you're busy on Tuesdays and you've got $60.
  3. The visitors work as Kagan Find Someone Who: with their constraint card they go round the desks looking for a course that fits both the timetable and the budget — ask, note the answer, move on. Two desks minimum! At each desk they write down the day, the time and the price.
  4. Constraint: phrases from the Key phrases only; Russian costs the crew a point.
  5. Roles swap after 6 minutes. We count: who signed up and who left with nothing.
📺 On screen: desk and visitor cards · the Key phrases on screen (Backup: the same written on the board before the lesson)
👦 Students do: they walk around and talk for real. Expected: the information gap keeps them in English with no reminders

Connectors · the editorial standard
pairs

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 find and/or/but in the model and work out the rule → ex.5 as a race in pairs with a coach

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4: Find every and, or and but in the reply. Then finish the rules yourselves. (TB key: 1 but — a different idea · 2 or — an alternative · 3 and — a similar idea).
  2. ex.5 through Kagan RallyCoach, 3 minutes: one student puts the connector in OUT LOUD and says why («the ideas disagree — so it's but»), the other is the coach — checks it against the rule and praises; on the next item they swap roles. TB key: 1 or · 2 but · 3 and · 4 and · 5 or · 6 but.
  3. The upgrade for our students: I love team sports, but I hardly ever watch them on TV. — their own line with an adverb AND a connector.
📺 On screen: SB p.33 ex.4–5 · timer
👦 Students do: they draw the rule out of the model. Error: but where and is needed — we sort it out by ear: «do these ideas disagree?»

PRODUCT · the reply
solo → pairs

WHAT WE DO: THINK (choose message A or B, write out the 3 questions) → WRITE (5–7 lines) → peer review → the wall

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: Message A or B. Write down the three questions the person asks. That's your plan.
  2. WRITE: Five to seven lines. Answer all three. Use at least two frequency words and two connectors.
  3. Peer review (ex.7): the partner checks 4 points — all three questions? the adverbs in place? -ing after like/love? and/or/but used correctly?
  4. Publication: the messages go on the unit wall. Replies are LIVE!
📺 On screen: the editor's checklist · the unit wall · timer
👦 Students do: they write in class off a fresh model. Expected: the peer review catches 80% of the errors before publication

WB Unit 2 · the messages on the unit wall
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask for information with 6 Key phrases in a real sign-up dialogue and write a 5–7-line reply answering three questions, linked with and, or and but.
  • Subsidiary. The video with Maria; ex.4 completing the dialogue; Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; the model messages A/B/C and the model reply; ex.4–5 on the connectors; peer review.
  • Personal. We write in class, not at home — I see the process. The editorial rule: a message that does not answer all three questions is not published, and the author fixes it, not me.
By the end, students can…
  • ask for information about a course (time, day, price, sign-up)
  • act out a sign-up dialogue without reading it
  • write a reply that answers all three questions
  • link ideas with and, or and but

Structure

Form
and — adds a similar idea · or — adds an alternative · but — adds a different (contrasting) idea · US: Do you have…? / UK: Have you got…?
Pron. watch
silent letter in sign /saɪn/ · stress in photography /fəˈtɒɡrəfi/ · a polite falling intonation in How much is it? ↘
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Can I sign up for it?» — is that about a signature? → no, about enrolling on a course.
and, or, but — which one adds a DIFFERENT idea? → but. A similar one — and. An alternative — or.
The message is finished but there is no answer to the second question — do we publish it? → no, the editor sends it back.

Assumptions: SB pp.31–33, KTP pair 18. The skeletons of the dialogue and the writing come ready from the book — the mission only frames them and adds a real audience.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They read the dialogue instead of playing itRole cards: the desk has its own timetable and its own price, the visitor has their own constraints (busy on Tuesdays, a budget). The information gap makes the dialogue real.
They copy the message off the modelThe editorial rule: the skeleton of the model — yes, the facts of the model — no. Their own three questions give them their own material.
They slap the connectors in at randomThe editor's check on 3 points: where the contrast is, where the alternative is, where the addition is. They swap texts — it is not me correcting.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
