WHAT WE DO: rituals: high five, the calendar question; rewind P2: commands with the body + the days in a chain; bridge

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A high five at the door + How are you?; the duty student: What day is it today?
  2. A rewind TPR: 4 commands with the body in a burst (Stand up! Look at the clock! Don't talk! Sit down!); the days in a chain round the circle.
  3. Bridge: “We know the days. But the school number? Your age? The price of ice cream? All of that is NUMBERS. Today — up to a hundred. And secret codes.”
📺 On screen: calendar
👦 Students do: they go through the rituals, carry out commands, race through the days. Expected: the ritual part is already automatic.

WHAT WE DO: a confidence hook: we count in chorus what we know (almost everyone can count to 10!); a demo of the system “20 words = 100 numbers”

10–22′
Discover: the number system with gestures
Discover · numbers system
desks + standing

WHAT WE DO: introducing it as a system: 1–12 in chorus → 13–19, the -teen tail with a gesture → tens, -ty → the 21+ builder; ex.1–3 with support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 1–12 in chorus with fingers ×2 (most already know it — a brisk pace).
  2. 13–19: thirTEEN! fourTEEN! — fist forward on the tail. 20–90: TWENty! THIRty! — a clap on the head. A contrast drill in pairs: thirteen-thirty · fifteen-fifty · eighteen-eighty.
  3. The builder: 21 = twenty-one (a hyphen in writing!); we build 34, 58, 77 in chorus from my cards.
  4. Ex.1–2, pairs with support: order + gaps 1–20; ex.3 together: the tens in words (forty with no u — tricky word #2!).
  5. A stand-up-sit-down game: even — stand up, odd — sit down (numbers by ear, the body answers).
📺 On screen: SB p.10 · number cards · -teen/-ty gestures
👦 Students do: they master the system in portions with gestures and the body, fill it in with support. Expected: the builder is understood; the gesture settles 13/30. Typical error: fourty — tricky word #2 goes on the board next to Wednesday.

WHAT WE DO: a light audio 5 dictation (3 numbers) + personalising numbers by Kagan Mix-Pair-Share (age, flat number, a favourite number)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A light audio 5: we catch ANY 3 numbers out of six (key: 20 · 80 · 12 · 85 · 38 · 54); catch 3 = a win; a second run to pick up the rest.
  2. Frames on the board: How old are you? — I'm eleven. · My lucky number is… · My flat number is…
  3. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share (the ritual is already familiar from P1): MIX! … PAIR UP! Ask your three questions — numbers with your VOICE, not fingers! → a new partner → again.
  4. 2–3 answers for the class: Vika is eleven! Her lucky number is seven! (his/her from P1 lives on).
📺 On screen: audio 5 · number frames (Backup: I read the numbers aloud myself)
👦 Students do: they catch numbers by ear at their own level, talk about THEIR OWN numbers in pairs. Expected: the personal touch lights them up. Typical error: “I'm 11” with fingers — “voice, not fingers!”

WHAT WE DO: p.10 ex.6–7: “words you already know” — sorting by Kagan Numbered Heads Together; a pronunciation upgrade

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hook: I show panda, pizza, Wi-Fi: English? Russian? — BOTH! International words — you know MANY.
  2. Ex.6 by Numbered Heads Together: numbers 1–3 in the teams; Heads together — sort the eight words!; at check time I call a number — THAT student answers a category for the team (key: panda · pizza, banana · hospital, hotel · tennis · Wi-Fi, camera). 3 min.
  3. Ex.7 as a top-up: 9 more words (lion · chocolate · park, school · football, golf, rugby · computer, phone).
  4. A pron upgrade: the word's the same — the music is different: hoTEL! Wi-Fi /waɪ/! chocolate /ˈtʃɒklət/ — 2 syllables! Choral echo.
  5. + their own: What other international words do you know? (café, taxi, metro…)
📺 On screen: SB p.10 · a categories table on the board
👦 Students do: they sort the familiar, add their own, fix the pronunciation. Expected: the day's main confidence lesson — “I know 30+ words!”. Typical error: Russian stress — a choral “retuning of the music”.

The alphabet: in portions of 5 letters
Discover · the alphabet in portions
desks · chorus

WHAT WE DO: p.10 ex.8 (audio 7): the alphabet in portions of 5 letters with a support card; trap letters on their own; a “silent test”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Portions of 5 (a TB technique): ABCDE in chorus ×2 → FGHIJ → KLMNO → PQRST → UVWXY+Z. After every portion — an “echo check”.
  2. Big traps: G /dʒiː/ — J /dʒeɪ/ · E /iː/ — I /aɪ/ · A /eɪ/ — R /ɑː/. Minimal pairs in chorus.
  3. A “trap” game: I say a Russian “es” — the class fixes it: /es/! Laughter legalises self-monitoring.
  4. A “silent test”: I mouth a letter — they write it on a mini-board, boards up.
📺 On screen: SB p.10 · audio 7 · an alphabet card for everyone (the support stays up for good) (Backup: I say the letters myself, no track)
👦 Students do: they drill in portions with the card, catch the traps, write the silent letters. Expected: portions + the card lift the panic. Typical error: G/J — the minimal pair once more, calmly.

WHAT WE DO: a personal victory: spelling YOUR OWN name from the card → pair exchange → cracking the ex.9 code as a team (2 words out of 5)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone prepares the spelling of their own name from the card (60 sec quietly, whispering to themselves).
  2. Pairs: How do you spell your name? — D-A-S-H-A! The partner writes it down, the author checks it. SWAP. A new partner by Mix-Pair-Share.
  3. The code part: we reveal the judo (10.21.4.15) together; the teams crack 2 words from ex.9 of their choice (key: cinema · internet · pasta · hockey · elephant) — with the alphabet card it's manageable.
  4. +1 for every cleanly cracked one; for fast finishers — a third word.
📺 On screen: alphabet cards · sheets · SB p.10 ex.9
👦 Students do: they spell THEIR OWN name (a personal victory!), crack codes as a team. Expected: clean names for 90%; the codes are absorbing. Typical error: the Cyrillic look-alike letter for I — I point at the card: /aɪ/!

Homework

Own homework: 1) Write out the spelling of two family members' names (M-A-M-A for real: mum's name in the letters of the English alphabet!). 2) Code your own name with A=1…Z=26 — bring it in, your partner will crack it. 3) Find 5 “English” words at home (on packaging, on your phone) — write down which ex.6 categories they fall into.

notebook · a photo of the board on your phone — that's fine
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can count and say numbers to 100 (tens + compounds), spell their own name and sort international words — with support from “I already understand a lot of English”.
  • Subsidiary. The -teen/-ty distinction is laid down with a gesture; the alphabet in portions of 5 letters; a calm tour of the coursebook + the Classroom Challenge IN PAIRS WITH SUPPORT; the “I CAN” final #3.
  • Personal. A lesson of confidence: international words and familiar numbers give the feeling that “English is manageable”. The alphabet is NOT a fast little song, but portions with support. Spelling your own name is a personal victory for everyone.
By the end, students can…
  • count 1–20 and say the tens to 100
  • spell their name with the alphabet card
  • sort international words into 5 categories
  • find a page in the coursebook with a partner

Target language
Lexis
numbers 1–100international words: Animals · Food · Places · Sports · Technologythe alphabet (in portions of 5)
Structure
How old are you? — I'm eleven. · What's your phone number? · How do you spell your name? — D-A-S-H-A. · What page? — Page 122!
Form
numbers 21+ = a tens word + a number (twenty-one, with a hyphen in writing); -teen a stressed tail, -ty a stressed head; spelling = the names of the letters.
Pron. watch
-teen stress on the SECOND syllable (thirTEEN) vs -ty on the first (THIRty) · hotel — stress on the 2nd syllable · Wi-Fi /ˈwaɪfaɪ/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“thirTEEN — is that 13 or 30?” → 13: the -teen tail is stressed (fist forward!).
“Is the letter C read “es”?” → No: /siː/; Russian letter names are banned when spelling.
“I don't remember the word in English — but is it international?” → Say it however you know it — it's often ALREADY English (pizza!).

Assumptions: After P1–P2: the rituals work, hello/things/days/commands are active. Many know numbers 1–10 from life/games; the alphabet is patchy (from usernames and brands). International words are a goldmine of confidence. Course rule #1 (said out loud EVERY Starter pair): mistakes are how we learn — here nobody laughs at mistakes, here we grow from them. The teacher makes mistakes on purpose and fixes them out loud.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
