🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use nine verb+noun sports phrases (enter a competition, win a prize, score a goal, lose a match, beat the other team, have a go at something, join a club, give someone the chance, miss an opportunity) and choose between the present simple and the present continuous by reading the signal in the sentence.
What is genuinely hard
The vocabulary is not vocabulary — it is collocation. Russian uses one verb where English uses three, so «win a competition» arrives instead of «enter a competition» with complete confidence. The grammar breaks in two places: state verbs pushed into the continuous, and the present continuous for arrangements replaced by will.
Typical error to expect
«I won a competition» for «I entered a competition» — the verb decides, not the noun. Russian uses one verb where English uses three (enter / win / lose), and the exam item is always the verb.
«I am thinking you are right», «I am liking this song» — state verbs do not take the continuous. Russian has no such class of verbs, so the error is produced with total confidence.
Why the lesson is built this way
The words arrive as pairs from the start, because a single word from this set is useless on its own. Three recordings, then an email that uses six of the pairs in context, then a survey where every student has to say them. The reading is the strongest text in the block — Ntando Mahlangu, a silver medallist at fourteen — and it is read the exam way (Reading Part 3): question first, evidence underlined, the other three options killed. The grammar then comes out of that same text: five sentences the class read ten minutes ago, sorted into five rules, and the productive stage is a thirty-second presentation of a real athlete that cannot be given without both tenses.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“At fourteen he won silver at the Paralympics. Two years before that he couldn't walk far enough to play football with his friends. Today: the English verbs that go with competitions, medals and clubs — and why «I'm meeting Maria later» is not the future you were taught.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a 90-second repair of the block test's weakest point, and the bridge into Unit 3

WHAT WE DO: About you + ex.1–3: photos, three speakers, then the nine collocations

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer on the exact thing the test asks for

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two walls: VERB and NOUN. You call a word from the block (enter, prize, join, opportunity, beat, goal, miss, club, score, chance) and everyone walks.
  2. After each word, one student on the correct wall says the whole pair aloud: enter a competition.
  3. Speed up round by round. The last three words come with no thinking time at all.
  4. Freeze and sit: Page twenty-one. Now a man who did all of this at fourteen.

Reading · the exam questions
individual → pairs → class
Why: Reading · the exam questions

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 = B1 Reading Part 3: five four-option questions, done the exam way

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the exam information aloud: Reading Part 3 — one long text followed by five four-option multiple-choice questions. It tests detail, global meaning, and the writer's purpose, attitude and opinion. The last question is almost always about the text as a whole.
  2. Do question 1 together, in public, in three steps: read the question · find the part of the text that answers it · underline it · then kill A, B and C one at a time, aloud.
  3. Questions 2–5 individually, six minutes, clock on the board.
  4. Then pairs: for each answer, show your partner the underlined line. If you cannot show a line, the answer does not count yet.
  5. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together. For each item, two questions: which line, and why is the nearest wrong option wrong? Question 5 (what would another athlete say?) is the whole-text question — slow down there.
📺 On screen: the three-step method on the board, permanently: QUESTION → LINE → KILL THE OTHER THREE. Backup: none needed, book and board only
👦 Students do: work alone under time, then prove every answer from a line. Everyone answers at least once because the check is by number
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 10–18 (the long text with questions). Cambridge Reading Part 3 and the Russian long-text section test the same three things: detail, global meaning and the writer's attitude.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: after the book task, give the same text with two extra true / false / not stated statements for five minutes in pairs. The word-harvest stage can run two minutes shorter.

Grammar · present simple and continuous
class → pairs
Why: Grammar · present simple and continuous

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3: five rules from the text they just read, then two practice sets

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books at page 22. Warmer from the Teacher's Book, thirty seconds, books closed: put these words in order — meeting Juan I meet every I'm Saturday on. Two sentences come out (I meet Juan every Saturday · I'm meeting Juan on Saturday). Name the tenses and the difference.
  2. ex.1: the five examples are all from Ntando's story. Match to the rules in pairs, then check on the board and write the five signals next to them — regularly, at the moment, next month, a fact, a state verb.
  3. Box the state verbs on the board: think, know, believe, like, want. Say it as a law: These five never wear -ing.
  4. ex.2 as a team competition, four minutes: small teams, two points per correct choice. It is a choose-the-form exercise, so speed is safe here.
  5. ex.3 in RallyCoach pairs — three of the eight sentences have no mistake at all, which is what makes it a thinking task. Item 8 has two defensible answers; say so.
  6. Grammar reference is SB p.149 for anyone who wants the full picture.
📺 On screen: the five signals and the boxed state verbs on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: extract the rules, then compete and coach. Everyone writes all eight items in their own book

🎯 Speaking out · the thirty-second athlete
individual → pairs → class
Why: 🎯 Speaking out · the thirty-second athlete

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: a real sportsperson presented in thirty seconds, both tenses compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4: each student chooses a real athlete and writes five notes — where they are from, what they do, one fact, what they are doing at the moment, what they are doing next month. The model on the page (Garbiñe Muguruza) shows exactly this shape.
  2. Four minutes of note-making. Circulate and check that every set of notes contains at least one at the moment and one next month — those two lines are what force the continuous.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share: thirty seconds each way, twice, with different partners. The listener's job: count the tenses and say which rule each one used.
  4. Three presentations to the whole class. The class holds up one finger for present simple and two for present continuous as they hear them — silent, instant, and it keeps everyone listening.
  5. Log two or three state-verb errors silently; they open lesson 2.
📺 On screen: the five note headings on the board for those who write slowly — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write notes, present twice in pairs, then listen actively with a counting job. Nobody sits passively through the presentations

Structure
This organisation gives young people the chance to do something new. · He often takes part in competitions. · He's making excellent progress at the moment. · Some athletes say they're running at the next Paralympics.
Form
present simple for facts, systems and regular events and with state verbs (want, like, believe, know) · present continuous for now, for temporary situations and for fixed future arrangements.
Pron. watch
competitor /kəmˈpetɪtə/ — the stress is on the SECOND syllable, not the first · canoeist /kəˈnuːɪst/ has three syllables · spectator /spekˈteɪtə/ · and the whole point of the suffix lesson: -er and -or are the same weak sound /ə/, so sailor and climber rhyme perfectly and are spelled differently
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I'm meeting Maria later today.» Have I decided just now, or was it arranged before? → arranged before. That is why English uses the continuous, not will.
«Do you think you know all your friends?» Can I say «Are you thinking»? → no. Think here is a state verb — an opinion, not an activity.
«He entered the competition» and «He won the competition» — same thing? → no. One is starting, one is finishing. The verb carries the whole meaning.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Students learn the nouns and forget the verbsThe wall takes PAIRS only. Any single word offered as an answer is handed straight back: Give me the pair. The Teacher's Book cooler does the same job in reverse — you say the verb, they write the noun.
The exam questions are answered from memory of the storyNo answer is accepted without an underlined line. Ask Which words? before you accept a letter, every single time, including when the answer is right.
«I am thinking that…» survives to the end of the lessonName the class of verbs out loud and put five on the board (think, know, believe, like, want) with a box around them: these five never wear -ing. Then plant one deliberately in the presentation stage and let the class catch it.
Fast finishers in the grammarThe Teacher's Book task: match every answer in ex.2 to the rule in ex.1 that explains it. It is harder than it sounds and it produces the metalanguage you want in feedback.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 26–31 (multiple-choice cloze, where verb + noun collocations are the commonest item type) and tasks 10–18 (reading a long text for detail and attitude).

Nine verb+noun pairs — enter a competition, score a goal, beat the other team — are exactly what the lexical cloze tests: not the word, the partner. And the Ntando text is read the exam way: the question first, the evidence underlined in the line, the other three options killed one by one. That last habit is worth more marks than any vocabulary list.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
