🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that the vocabulary and grammar of two units work without the book: money and shopping words, pay/charge/cost, twelve food adjectives, look/taste/smell, the present perfect with been/gone, the perfect-vs-simple gate and for/since — and correct spelling.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the gap between oral and written: out loud the class knows almost everything, on paper receipt grows a p in the wrong place, delicious loses letters, and tenses get picked by feel instead of by the time marker.
Typical error to expect
receipt (silent p!) · delicious · disgusting · juicy — the block's misspelling traps; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
Plus tenses chosen without looking for the date, and for/since swapped under time pressure.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built as retrieval practice, not re-teaching: two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it — and shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which form still breaks. The official Cambridge game sits between the stations and the briefing on purpose: it hides revision inside a sentence-building race, and a class that has just competed walks into a test calmer. Only the Unit 9–10 circles of the game are legal today — the body and wildlife circles belong to the next block. Nothing new is introduced anywhere today, and the error lists that leave this room tonight are the revision plan for the half-year lesson in two days.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. Everything on these two papers you have already done in this room — I can name the lesson for every line. First forty-five minutes we play it back. Then forty-five minutes you show it. Your score is not the point: the point is your own list of five mistakes — because in two days that list is your ticket into the half-year paper.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast card round of the block's words, and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — hot round. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the block's word cards (money words + food adjectives + the five verbs), two trades each: Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest: Everything you just said is in today's test.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, call definitions and pairs answer chorally
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety dropping. Typical stumbles: receipt and disgusting — both spelling, both in the stations

Lesson frame
board
Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: put the test sections up in advance — transparency takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the six sections of the two papers on the board, one line each: money words · pay/charge/cost · been/gone · present perfect ×2 · food adjectives + look/taste/smell · perfect vs past simple + for/since.
  2. Look at the board. Have we done all of this in class? Yes or no? The class answers chorally — the answer is always yes; if anyone doubts, name the lesson for that line.
  3. The rule of the day: Your score goes in the register. Your five mistakes go in your notebook — and that list is your TICKET into the half-year lesson. Bring it.
📺 On screen: the six section lines in chalk — the board is the medium and it stays up for the whole first half, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the sections and answer chorally. Expect visible relief. Typical problem — they don't believe «we have done all of it»: point line by line at the lesson it came from

8–20′
Review station 1 · words
teams
Why: Retrieval practice rather than re-teaching: they pull the block's vocabulary out of memory in short bursts, which is what makes it stay.

WHAT WE DO: the block's vocabulary: 30+ words, against the clock and in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, oral: definitions at speed — the paper that proves you paid? (receipt) · kept very cold? (frozen) · not cooked? (raw) · money the assistant gives back? (change) · extremely nice to eat? (delicious). Twelve items.
  2. Round 2, subjects: you name who or what, teams fire the verb — the boots! (cost) · the café! (charges) · you at the checkout! (pay). Six items.
  3. Round 3, written: Kagan Showdown — you say the meaning, teams WRITE the word. Load the traps deliberately: receipt, delicious, disgusting, juicy, special offer.
  4. Every misspelling goes into a «watch out» column on the board — it stays there right up to the test.
📺 On screen: definitions read aloud, answers on mini-whiteboards — voice and boards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: say, fire, then write the words. Expect almost everything orally and a visible drop in the written round — which is exactly why the column exists. Typical error: reciept

20–32′
Review station 2 · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar — and it shows you, twenty minutes before the test, exactly which form still breaks.

WHAT WE DO: the block's grammar: ten mixed sentences with the reason every time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ten mixed sentences, one at a time: three on the perfect (ever/never + participles), two on been/gone, three on the gate (While…? No — a date! «Yesterday I ___ (buy) it»), two on for/since.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the REASON, not the answer — Say WHY, not just the answer.
  3. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Sentence six — number fours, boards up!
  4. Boil every explanation down to the step: a date → past simple; no date, just life → perfect; still there → gone; length → for; starting point → since.
📺 On screen: the ten sentences written on the board before the lesson and uncovered one by one with a sheet of paper — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with an explanation, then report by number. Expect the gate sentences to be solid and for/since to wobble under speed — slow exactly that down. Typical error: since three weeks — a line of its own in the watch-out column

32–40′
Review game
teams of 2 vs 2
Why: The official Cambridge game hides revision inside a reason to listen to each other; a class that has just competed walks into a test calmer.

WHAT WE DO: «Four in a row!» — the official Cambridge review game, our block's circles only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Two teams per group, one cut sheet each (word grid + Topics ticklist). On each turn a team picks a circle from the grid and a topic from the ticklist and makes one sentence that uses the word ON that topic — Food and drink → pay → «My father always pays for my food.» If the other team accepts the sentence, the circle is coloured in the team's colour and the topic is ticked off until all topics have been used once. The sheet's own aim is four circles in a row.
  2. ⚠️ The grid covers Units 9–12, so today only the Unit 9–10 circles are legal: save up · checkout · charge · cost · price · pay · afford · ever · never · raw · juicy · tastes · smells · disgusting · fresh · delicious · sweet · spicy · tasty · bitter (about twenty of the forty-two). The body, wildlife and might/could/probably circles are Units 11–12 — they are saved for the next block, so skip them. With half the grid off-limits a four-in-a-row may be impossible — play for MOST circles won in eight minutes instead, and say so up front.
  3. Sharper groups: the «you give me the topic» version from the teacher's notes — team B picks the topic team A must build on.
  4. Set the bar: The other team judges your sentence — so listen like judges: does it show the meaning, yes or no?
📺 On screen: one cut sheet pair per group, run off in advance — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed; with no printer, write the twenty legal words on the board and teams claim them by sentence, ticking topics off a shared list
👦 Students do: pick a circle and a topic, build the sentence, judge each other, score. Expect the judging to be as useful as the building. Typical stumble: a sentence that USES the word without SHOWING it (It's not easy to afford) — the teacher's-notes example, worth quoting

40–45′
Test briefing
class
Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to the «watch out» column: the five words that were misspelt. Look at these five words for thirty seconds. Then I close them.
  2. Instructions: two official papers, Unit 9 then Unit 10, about 40 minutes total; phones in the bag; finished early — five food sentences on the back.
  3. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  4. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect silence to settle in 20 seconds if the flat hand goes up before you start talking

47–85′
Block test
solo, silent mode
Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats, official papers. Everything in them has been done in class, so the test measures learning, not surprise.

WHAT WE DO: the official Unit 9 and Unit 10 tests, back to back — 73 items in about 38 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. Unit 9 first. You have thirty-eight minutes for both papers. Start.
  2. Walk the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  3. Write the time left in chalk: 20 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  4. Anyone who finishes early writes five food sentences on the back and sits quietly.
  5. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling — receipt, delicious, disgusting.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; nothing at all is put up in the room while the test is running
👦 Students do: work in silence for 38 minutes. Expect the vocabulary sections to be closed fast, the U9 question-building (ex.5) and the U10 email cloze to slow them down. Typical error: short answers rushed in U9 ex.5 — mention it in the briefing

85–90′
Test wrap-up
circle
Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the half-year briefing.
  3. Homework: once the papers come back — rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly; that list is the ENTRY TICKET to the half-year lesson, collected at the door.
  4. Teaser: In two days: the whole half-year in one lesson — ten units, one official paper, and the scale on the board before you start.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is full, read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to name for/since and the U9 short answers most often — that is the plan for the half-year briefing

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students have shown control of Units 9–10 vocabulary and grammar on the official Cambridge unit tests and have their own list of five mistakes to fix — which doubles as their revision plan for the half-year lesson.
  • Subsidiary. Revision through stations (vocabulary, grammar) and the official review game; keeping test anxiety low through transparency.

By the end, students can…
  • recall the block's 30+ words without the book — spelled right
  • choose the tense by the time marker and justify it
  • pick for or since via the length/starting-point step
  • write five of their own mistakes in the correct form (after papers return)

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Nerves before the testSay it out loud and early: nothing will come out of thin air, every format has been done. Put the section list on the board before you start.
Someone finishes both papers in 25 minutesThe rule: hand them in, turn the sheet over and write five sentences about food you have and haven't tried — two perfect, three past simple. It is read, not marked.
After the test they want to compare answers out loudCollect the papers in silence; the analysis is next lesson. Today, once a paper is in, the only thing left is personal reflection.
The game's four-in-a-row is impossible on the reduced gridNamed up front, not discovered mid-game: with half the circles off-limits we play for MOST circles in eight minutes. The Cambridge rules otherwise stand.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
