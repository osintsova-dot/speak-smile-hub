Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name 6 adjectives (shiny · expensive · modern · heavy · powerful · interesting) and form the superlative of long adjectives: “The blue rocket is the most expensive. The red rocket is the heaviest.”
  • Subsidiary. Sing «The best rocket in the world» (CD2 23); discover the rules — long adjectives take the most (the most powerful), and -y → -iest (shiny → shiniest, heavy → heaviest); contrast with the short-adjective -est from L2; play a true-or-false rocket game (AB follow-up).
  • Personal. Insist on the most + adjective for long words (not “expensivest”); keep the -y → -iest rule visible; model each adjective with a big gesture; keep the game moving — short turns.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name 6 adjectives (shiny · expensive · modern · heavy · powerful · interesting);
  • form long superlatives: the most powerful / the shiniest;
  • compare rockets: “The … rocket is the most … .”

Structure
The blue rocket is the most expensive rocket.  ·  The red rocket is the heaviest.  ·  It's the most powerful / the shiniest / the most interesting rocket in the world.
Form
Long adj → the most + adjective (the most modern · the most powerful · the most expensive · the most interesting). Adj ending -y → -iest (shiny → shiniest, heavy → heaviest). Contrast: short adj -est (L2).
Pron. watch
expensive /ɪkˈspensɪv/; powerful /ˈpaʊəfl/; shiniest /ˈʃaɪniɪst/; heaviest /ˈheviɪst/; interesting /ˈɪntrəstɪŋ/ (3 syll.).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“expensive — a long word. The expensivest or the most expensive?” → the MOST expensive.
“shiny → shini___ — shinyest or shiniest?” → shiniest — y becomes i!

Assumptions: Superlative -est (short adj) is active from L2; the 6 adjectives are new but very picturable (rockets). The switch to the most for long words + -y → -iest is today's challenge. Readers can read short adjective words. (Book teaches 6 adjectives — note КТП listed a different set that mixes in L2's short adjectives.)

Presentation · 6 adjectives
carpet → board

Listen, point and say (act. 1, CD2 22). Adjective cards: Shiny! Expensive! Modern! Heavy! Powerful! Interesting! Choral ×3 + a big gesture each (heavy = strain, shiny = sparkle, powerful = flex). Speed rounds — you say, they SHOW.

Notice long vs short. Board two columns: SHORT (-est: big → biggest) · LONG (the most: powerful → the most powerful; -y → -iest: shiny → shiniest). Sort each card.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: Stand up if this adjective takes THE MOST… interesting! — flash fast, everyone reacts (trick them with -y words!).

💪 Support: gesture carries the meaning. ⭐ Extension: read the adjective without the picture.

27–37′
Rocket true-or-false game
desks, pairs → 2 teams

Model first. Show the 6 coloured rockets. Ask 2 pupils publicly: The orange rocket is the most modern. — That's true! / That's false — it's the blue one.

▶ Talk Partners true or false (act. 4). Pairs take turns: A makes a superlative sentence about a rocket, B says true or false, then corrects. 6 exchanges.

▶ Listen & hold up (team relay). You say a superlative — teams race to hold up the matching rocket card & say the full sentence. Point per correct.

✔ Assess: circulate — is it “the most” for long words and “-iest” for -y? Recast, note names.

💪 Support: rocket cards have the adjective printed. ⭐ Extension: add: but the red rocket is the heaviest.
