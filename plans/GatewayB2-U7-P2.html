🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Report statements and questions accurately: move the tense one step back, change the pronouns and the time and place words, choose between say and tell, and drop the auxiliary in reported questions. Plus eleven compound nouns from the film industry (blockbuster, cliffhanger, box office, sell-out, turnout, drawback, feedback, breakthrough, masterpiece, outcome, soundtrack).
What is genuinely hard
Russian does not backshift. «Он сказал, что он занят» keeps the present tense, so «He said he IS busy» feels correct to every student in the room and the error survives into the exam email. Reported questions are worse: the mind hears a question and produces question word order with an auxiliary, which is exactly what English forbids. The compound nouns are a separate, purely mechanical problem — one word or two, and the stress on the first part.
Typical error to expect
«He said he is busy», «She said she will come» — Russian keeps the original tense in reported speech, English moves it one step back: he said he WAS busy · she said she WOULD come. This is not a rule to understand, it is a habit to automate — and it leaks into the exam email as well.
«She asked me where do I live», «He asked what is my name» — a reported question is not a question: no auxiliary do/does/did, no inversion, statement word order. She asked me where I lived. The Student's Book plants the trap on page 86 and the unit test collects on it.
«He said me», «She told that…» — tell takes a person (tell ME), say does not (say to me). Russian has one verb for both, so the two must be taught as a pair, not separately.
Why the lesson is built this way
The grammar is DISCOVERED, not delivered: the Student's Book prints three pairs of sentences (direct → reported) and six questions about them, and the class works out the rule from the data. Then the backshift is made physical — a ladder drawn on the board that every disputed sentence has to climb down. Reported questions come second and are taught as the opposite of an instinct: three options on the board, two of them wrong, and the class explains WHY. The compound nouns sit in the middle as a change of gear, and the lesson ends with a real survey: five questions asked to five people, then reported back — which is the only reason to learn reported speech at all.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two sentences. «He said he is busy.» «He said he was busy.» One of them is worth a mark and one of them isn't — and in Russian they are the same sentence. Today: how English reports other people — and at the end you interview five of your classmates and report what they said.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every student says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, lesson-1 vocabulary recycled, and the target error captured live from the class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: One sentence: what was the last thing you watched or listened to? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — rewind, woven into today. Kagan Think-Pair-Share, thirty seconds each way: Tell your partner about the last thing you watched — plot, ending, one adjective from the wall. Yesterday's vocabulary, used before it cools.
  3. 1 minute — the trap, sprung early. Ask two students to repeat what their partner just said, out loud, to the class. Write the first sentence on the board exactly as it comes: it will almost certainly be She said the plot IS predictable. Do not correct it. Circle the verb and leave it.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): point at the circled verb: Page eighty-six. By the end of this lesson you will know why that verb is wrong — and it costs a mark in the exam.

Lesson frame · the backshift ladder
board
Why: Backshift is a movement, and movements are learnt spatially. The ladder turns an abstract rule into something a student can point at, which is what makes it survive into speech.

WHAT WE DO: name the aim and draw the ladder the whole lesson refers to

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Draw the ladder down the side of the board, six rungs, and leave it up all lesson: present simple → past simple · present continuous → past continuous · present perfect → past perfect · past simple → past perfect · will → would · can → could.
  2. Beside it, a short list that does NOT move: could · would · should · might · past perfect · infinitives · gerunds.
  3. Say the shape of the lesson in thirty seconds: the ladder, then questions, then the words English writes as one, then a real survey of this class.
  4. Set the bar: Every time you report something today, you point at the ladder and say which rung you came down.

Why: Grammar · discovery from the data

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: the rule worked out from three pairs of sentences, not delivered

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: three students read sentences 1a to 3b aloud. Make the relationship explicit: The b sentences report the a sentences. Watch the verbs.
  2. Then questions a–f, one at a time, whole class, eliciting after each. Answers: a the tense goes one step back · b could, would, should, might, the past perfect, infinitives and gerunds do NOT change · c no, after a present or present perfect reporting verb the tense stays · d I → she, this → that, my → her · e tell needs a person, say does not · f «that» is optional.
  3. For b, give the two examples the Teacher's Book gives, on the board: She said she SHOULD do her essay before they went shopping. · He told me he HAD JUST FINISHED his homework when I called.
  4. ex.2, three minutes in pairs: the eleven time and place words. Answers: ago → before · here → there · last → the previous / the … before · next → the following · now → then · these → those · this → that · today → that day · tomorrow → the next/following day · tonight → that night · yesterday → the day before / the previous day.
  5. Write these on a second small board and keep them: they are half the marks in the workbook and on the test.
📺 On screen: the eleven time and place words listed on a second board. Backup: everything in this stage is on p.86 — no technology is used at all
👦 Students do: read the paired data, answer six analytical questions, then build the time-word list in pairs

WHAT WE DO: ex.3–5: say or tell, back into direct speech, and six sentences reported

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3, two minutes, individually: said or told, six items. Answers: told, said, said, said, told, told. For weaker classes write the Teacher's Book prompt on the board first — Use TOLD when you know who receives the message.
  2. ex.4, four minutes in pairs: turn the six reported sentences back into direct speech. This is the reverse gear and it is worth more than another forward drill — students who can reverse it have understood it. Do number 1 together as a model.
  3. Kagan RallyCoach for ex.4: one writes, the other coaches out loud and holds the pen for the next item. Nobody works silently in parallel.
  4. ex.5, four minutes, individually: six sentences reported. Elicit the three things to change before they start — verb tense, pronouns, words of time and place.
  5. Check on the board in WRITING, with the ladder pointed at each time. The Teacher's Book extra, if there is time: dictate go, will, am going to read, can, saw, must, might, had forgotten, yesterday, now, here, last year and collect the reported versions.

30–34′
Move break · the quote relay
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Sixteen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that drills backshift with the whole body

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands. You hand out (or say) one direct-speech quote per student — use the film titles and the wall vocabulary: «I've seen that film three times.» · «The soundtrack is amazing.» · «I'll download the album tonight.»
  2. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: mix to the music, freeze, pair with the nearest person. A says the quote, B reports it aloud, then they swap quotes and mix again. Three rounds.
  3. The rule that makes it work: you may not report until you have pointed at the ladder. It looks silly and it stops the guessing.
  4. Freeze and sit: Now the harder half. Questions.
📺 On screen: quote cards or quotes read aloud — no technology, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: every student reports at least three quotes aloud, standing, with a different partner each time

Grammar · reported questions
class → individual → pairs
Why: Grammar · reported questions

WHAT WE DO: ex.7–8: the instinct broken on the board, then a whole conversation reported

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed. Write the Teacher's Book test on the board: «Where are you going?» I asked Kyle. — A: I asked Kyle where was he going. B: I asked Kyle where he was going. C: I asked Kyle where are you going?
  2. Take the vote, then take the REASONS. B is correct. Push for why A and C fail — that discussion is the lesson.
  3. Books open, ex.7: sentences 1a to 4b read aloud, then questions a–e whole class with an example from the data for each answer. Answers: a yes, tenses and pronouns change the same way · b no auxiliary do · c no, subject before verb · d no question mark · e if or whether.
  4. Write the two-line summary and box it: A reported question is not a question. Statement order, no auxiliary, no question mark.
  5. ex.8, six minutes in pairs: the Olivia and Tom conversation about seeing Zendaya at a film festival in France — fourteen lines reported with said, told, asked and wanted to know. Model the first line together: Olivia asked Tom if he had ever seen a famous actor in person.
  6. Remind them what else moves: time expressions, place, possessives. Check three or four lines aloud, not all fourteen — the rest is checked in pairs against the key in this plan.
📺 On screen: the three-option test and the boxed two-line rule on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: vote and justify, then report a fourteen-line conversation in pairs

WHAT WE DO: Developing vocabulary ex.1–2: noun or adjective, and where the stress falls

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed warmer, ninety seconds. Write: firefighter · rainfall · cowboy · earring · greenhouse · haircut. What do these six words have in common? They are compound nouns. Ask for two more from the class.
  2. ex.1, whole class, one minute: which of best-selling, download, eco-friendly and screenplay are nouns and which are adjectives? The test to teach them, because it works everywhere: Can you put A or AN in front of it? Nouns — download, screenplay. Adjectives — best-selling, eco-friendly.
  3. ex.2, track 38: listen and mark the stress. The rule they should produce themselves: in compound NOUNS the stress is on the first word, in compound ADJECTIVES usually on the second. Drill six of them by clapping.
  4. Pre-teach the eleven compounds of ex.3 quickly, with one-line definitions: blockbuster (a huge hit) · breakthrough (an important positive event, often a surprise) · cliffhanger (the moment where you don't know what happens next) · drawback (disadvantage) · outcome (result) · sell-out (an event you can't get tickets for) · turnout (how many people came).
  5. Every compound goes on the wall with a dot over the stressed syllable.
📺 On screen: the compound-noun column on the wall with stress dots. Backup: if track 38 fails, say the eleven words yourself and have the class clap the stress — the point is the pattern, not the recording

standing → pairs
Why: 🎯 Survey · five questions, five people

WHAT WE DO: ex.9: a real interview round that produces the raw material for reporting

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the prompts on the board: Have you ever …? · How often do you …? · What's your favourite …? · When did you last …? · Would you rather … or …?
  2. Three minutes, silent: each student writes FIVE questions about films or series, using the wall vocabulary. Check two sets before the standing part starts — a bad question wastes the whole activity.
  3. 🎯 Nine minutes standing, Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade style: ask your five questions to at least five different people and WRITE DOWN the answers, in note form, with the person's name. The note is what gets reported later, so it has to be accurate.
  4. Circulate with one job only: listen for the questions themselves, not the answers. Note two good ones and two broken ones on paper for the correction slot.
  5. Thirty-second warning, then everybody sits with their notes.
📺 On screen: the five question prompts on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone writes five questions and interviews five people standing; nobody waits for a turn

🎯 Report back · what they said
pairs → class
Why: 🎯 Report back · what they said

WHAT WE DO: the survey notes turned into reported speech, out loud and in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four minutes, individually and in writing: take your notes and write FIVE reported sentences. Rule on the board: every sentence uses said, told, asked or wanted to know, and the verb has come down the ladder.
  2. 🎯 Kagan Timed Pair Share, ninety seconds each way: read your five to a partner. The listener has one job — catch any verb that did not move.
  3. Then the whole class round, four minutes: each student reads ONE sentence about somebody in the room. The person named confirms or denies it: Yes, that's what I said. / No, I said I HAD seen it. That confirmation is the best listening task in the lesson and costs nothing.
  4. Delayed correction, two minutes: put the four sentences you collected during the survey on the board, unattributed. The class repairs them.
  5. Correct only backshift and say/tell today. Nothing else.
📺 On screen: the four collected sentences written up anonymously for repair — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write five reported sentences each, read them aloud, and hear them confirmed or corrected by the person quoted
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text) and task 37 (the personal email). Structure
«The vending machines are great!» → He said (that) the vending machines WERE great. · «Why have you stopped reading novels?» → The journalist asked Paresh why he HAD stopped reading novels. · «Do you prefer reading or gaming?» → They wanted to know WHETHER we preferred reading or gaming.
Form
reported statement: reporting verb in the past → the reported verb goes one tense back · could, would, should, might, the past perfect, infinitives and gerunds do not change · say has no personal object, tell must have one · reported question: no do/does/did, no inversion, no question mark, and if/whether for yes-no questions.
Pron. watch
compound NOUNS take the stress on the first word (BLOCKbuster, CLIFFhanger, SOUNDtrack, BOX office) while compound ADJECTIVES take it on the second (best-SELLing, eco-FRIENDly) — that is exactly what track 38 tests · clichéd /ˈkliːʃeɪd/ keeps the French vowel and the class will say «kli-CHED» · genre /ˈʒɒnrə/ · lyrics /ˈlɪrɪks/, not «LYE-rics» · and in the emphatic I really DO think that… the stress lands on do, which is the whole point of the structure
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«He said he was busy.» Is he busy NOW? → we don't know — the past tense here is grammar, not time. That single question prevents half the confusion in the room.
«She asked me where I lived.» Where is the question mark? → there isn't one. Why? → because it is not a question any more, it is a statement about a question.
«Tell» or «say»? → who are you speaking TO? If the person is in the sentence, it is tell. If not, it is say.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«They already did this in Year 8»Agree, and change the target: You know the rule. Today we make it automatic under time pressure — that is a different skill. Then use the timer visibly; speed is the new challenge, not novelty.
Reported questions keep the auxiliaryThe board test, used every time: cover the reporting verb with your hand and read what is left. If it still sounds like a question, it is wrong. Physical, instant, and they can do it themselves.
Backshift forgotten in free speech (it survives in exercises only)That is why the survey stage exists. In it, correct nothing except the tense — one focus, named in advance, and everyone knows what is being listened for.
Compound nouns written as two wordsMake it visible and slightly ridiculous: any compound written as two words on the wall gets a red line through the gap. It takes three seconds and they stop doing it.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 — grammar in a text. Reported speech is a named line of the codifier and it is one of the few places where the Russian paper can force a tense change that Russian never makes.

Russian reports speech by keeping the original tense: «Он сказал, что он занят» = «He said he WAS busy». English moves the verb one step back, and every candidate who does not automate it writes «He said he is busy» in task 37 as well. The compound nouns are quieter but paid for twice: they print in the lexical section and half of them are one word, which is a spelling mark.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
