🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The fifteen holiday activities are introduced not as a list but as a first-aid kit against boredom: the listening has a real character — Ed, whose summer has collapsed (his friends have all gone away, the family isn't going anywhere) and who calls out of sheer boredom at ten in the morning. The crew builds him a week. The subskill (listening for feelings) works like a scout's working skill: hear the MOOD first, the facts second. Going to and present continuous are in every line of the audio, but they are NOT announced as the topic of the lesson — that is the work of Pair 5.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Someone is having the worst summer of his life. His name is Ed. Karl's at summer camp, Piet's in Scotland, and his parents just told him the family isn't going anywhere this year. He called at ten in the morning to ask if you were still asleep. That's how bored he is. Your crew builds Ed a week — seven days, seven activities, and not one of them costs real money. And here's the catch: before you can help anyone, you have to HEAR them. Three calls. Three moods. Ears on.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: Ed's summer has collapsed: his friends have gone away, the family isn't going anywhere, and he rings at ten in the morning out of boredom. The crew builds him a week — seven days, seven activities, all of them free. But first they have to learn to HEAR a mood: three calls, three states.

WHAT WE DO: track 50: six lines — six feelings; producing the intonation before recognising it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The warm-up from the TB (PRODUCE first, recognise second): the line “The concert is tomorrow” — read it bored · confused · excited · surprised · nervous · sad. By crew, against the clock.
  2. The marker words out loud (TB hint): bored = 'This really isn't interesting.' · confused = 'I don't understand.' · excited = 'I can't wait!' · nervous = 'I feel a bit scared.'
  3. ex.5 track 50, six short lines (key, TB: 1 confused · 2 excited · 3 surprised · 4 sad · 5 nervous · 6 bored). Mini-whiteboards at the same moment — Kagan Showdown: the line plays, everyone writes the feeling and keeps the board face down, and on Showdown! they all go up together. Copying the neighbour is impossible, so everybody listens.
  4. Feedback: Which WORDS told you? Which was the intonation only?
  5. The constraint: Eight seconds per answer. No word or intonation named — the point burns.
📺 On screen: audio track 50 · mini-whiteboards (Backup: read the six lines yourself from TB p. 136, with honest intonation, twice each)
👦 Students do: they produce the intonation, then recognise it. Expect: they mix up 3 (surprised) and 5 (nervous) — sort it out through the words “You're kidding!” and “Are you sure it's safe?”

PRODUCT · rescue Ed's summer
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a seven-day plan for Ed: seven activities, different engines, zero roubles + a 60-second delivery

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes to prepare: Seven days, seven activities. All three verbs must appear: go on, go to, go for. And one day must work in the rain.
  2. Roles: every member of the crew delivers their own days.
  3. Sixty seconds. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness — cards up!
  4. The class listens with a task: Would this actually fix Ed's week? Vote with thumbs. And name the ONE day you'd steal for yourself.
  5. A challenge from the class: You said 'go sightseeing'. Where exactly? Name the place.
📺 On screen: 60-second timer · judges' cards · the rubric · the seven-cell calendar
👦 Students do: they perform in their roles and the class votes. Expect: the “rainy day” slots come out best — that is where the games room, the museum and the video games appear

WB Unit 8 · the three engine columns stay on the wall until the end of the unit
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can use 15 holiday activity phrases with the right verb (go on / go to / go for) and identify a speaker's feelings and plans from a phone message, then present a seven-day rescue plan.
  • Subsidiary. The blog “Awesome things to do in the holidays!” (ex.1–4); ex.3 the three collocation engines; the listening-for-feelings subskill; track 50 (six lines); track 51 (three calls) — ex.6, 7, 8; ex.9 about their own holidays.
  • Personal. Say nothing about going to and present continuous, even though they are in every line. Log errors like “we meeting at 8” — that is the material for Pair 5.
By the end, students can…
  • say 15 holiday activities with the correct verb (go on / go to / go for)
  • hear a speaker's feeling from their words and intonation
  • catch who is planning what, when and where from a phone message
  • present a seven-day plan of free activities

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“go to a bike ride” — can we say that? → no: go FOR a bike ride. The engine decides everything.
“sunbathe” — is that lying in the sun or swimming? → lying in the sun; swimming is swim.
How do you read a feeling from a recording when you cannot see the face? → from the WORDS and the INTONATION (the unit subskill).

Assumptions: SB p.101, syllabus pair 65. Some phrases are familiar (go for a walk, hang out); new are go on a day trip, go sightseeing, sunbathe, look after a pet, visit a museum or art gallery, have a barbecue.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The verb engine drifts (go to a walk)The three columns go on / go to / go for stay up all lesson; students put each new phrase into a column out loud.
They guess the feeling at randomThe TB routine: take the neutral line “The concert is tomorrow” and ask them to read it bored, confused, excited, nervous — produce it first, recognise it second.
“The audio is too fast”Three passes with three different tasks: 1st — mood only, 2nd — who is planning what, 3rd — the details. One task at a time, not three at once.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
