🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use ten words for technology and how it is powered (invention, fuel, pump, plug in, charge, power, satellite, display, connection, experiments) and choose between the four future forms — will, going to, present continuous and the future continuous — by asking what KIND of future it is.
What is genuinely hard
Russian has one future where English has four, and the four are not stylistic variants: an arrangement, a plan, a prediction and an action in progress are four different meanings. Students will use will for everything, because it is the one that looks like a future tense. The future continuous is the newest form and the easiest — it is the CHOICE that has to be built.
Typical error to expect
«Tomorrow I will meet my friend at six» for an arrangement that is already fixed — English uses the present continuous for that (I'm meeting him at six). Russian has one future, so will swallows all four meanings unless they are separated by hand.
environment, endangered, population — three words the whole unit stands on and all three are misspelled by half a group. They are worth a written drill, not an oral one.
Why the lesson is built this way
The unit opens with a quiz, not a word list: five questions about driverless cars that nobody in the room can answer confidently, then a podcast that settles them. Curiosity first, vocabulary second. The reading is the best story of the block — American teenagers who took a 1967 car, put a lithium battery in it and drove it across the country on social-media fuel — and it is a genuine exam task, four options per question. The grammar then arrives as a sorting problem with the four forms on the board and the class deciding which is which, because a rule that is derived is remembered and a rule that is read is not.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“A group of teenagers took a car built in 1967, took the engine out, and made it run on likes and tweets. It crossed America. Today: the vocabulary of technology — and the four different futures English has, which is three more than Russian.”

Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that spends all ten words

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands. You name a device (a phone, a satellite, a petrol car, a torch, an electric bike, a laptop); pairs have ten seconds to say how it gets its power, using a word from the wall: You plug it in. It runs on fuel. It charges from the sun.
  2. Six devices, six exchanges. After each one take one answer aloud and make it a full sentence.
  3. Last thirty seconds: Name one invention you use every day and could not live without.
  4. Freeze and sit: Page fifty-five. A car with no petrol and no plug — it ran on social media.

👦 Students do: everyone speaks six times in four minutes, to a neighbour rather than to the teacher

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: predict from the title and photograph, then read for the shape

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the background in two sentences (it is worth it): Minddrive is an after-school programme in Kansas City. A group of teenagers took a Volkswagen built in 1967, removed the petrol engine and put in a lithium-ion battery and modern electronics.
  2. Then the hook, as a question rather than a statement: The car only moved when people liked and tweeted about it. How can that possibly work? Sixty seconds in pairs, three guesses on the board.

  4. Pairs, ninety seconds: put the project in five stages on paper — the old car, the new battery, the social-media fuel, the journey, the arrival. That timeline is the tool for the exam questions.
  5. Optional: play track 36 (the article is recorded) while the two weakest readers follow with a finger.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 = B1 Reading Part 3: five questions, and the fourth option is the new difficulty

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the difference before they start: Four options, not three. The extra one is always true — it is just not the answer to the question.
  2. The method on the board, same four steps as block 4: read the question · underline the key words in all FOUR options · find the place in the text · say why the other three are wrong.
  3. ex.3 individually, six minutes, clock visible. Rule: write the line number next to every answer.
  4. Pairs compare — line numbers first, letters second.
  5. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: 1 C, 2 A, 3 B, 4 D, 5 B. For every item take one rejected option and ask why it fails; the Teacher's Book explains all of them and those explanations are the actual lesson.
  6. ex.4–5: the highlighted words, two minutes in pairs.
  7. Talking points if the clock allows: Do you think everyone will have a driverless car one day? How would you feel about being in one? Do inventions interest you — or only the ones you can use? — pairs first, then the floor

Why: Grammar · four futures, sorted

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–4: the four forms placed in the four boxes, then the future continuous

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed for two minutes. Twelve words on the board, jumbled: I / 'll / after / phone / 'm / tonight / phoning / him / going / to / later / lunch. Groups of three make three sentences from them.
  2. The three sentences go into three of the four boxes: I'll phone him tonight (promise) · I'm phoning him later (arrangement) · I'm going to phone him after lunch (plan). The class does the placing, not you.
  3. ex.1: the four rules matched to the four examples — 1 b, 2 c, 3 a, 4 d. Confirm and write the label inside each box.
  4. ex.2: the future continuous. Draw the timeline (a dot for tomorrow at seven, a wavy line through it) and fill the fourth box. Elicit the form: will be + -ing.
  5. ex.3 individually, four minutes: six items from the box, two verbs unused. Check in writing on the board — 'll be waiting, will be studying, 'll be holding, will be taking, won't be seeing, will be driving.
  6. ex.4, three minutes: six items where all four forms compete. This is the real test of the lesson; check it by asking for the BOX before the form.
  7. Grammar reference is SB p.155 for anyone who wants the whole picture in writing — name it, do not read it aloud.
📺 On screen: the four boxes, now labelled and full of the class's own sentences — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: build three sentences from jumbled words, place them in boxes, then produce twelve forms in writing
📓 Interactive notebook — 04_U9_Grammar_Future_Forms_A5
A5 grammar card — FUTURE FORMS + FUTURE CONTINUOUS: four ways in one place — WILL (a general prediction, an offer or promise: Driverless cars will become common.) · BE GOING TO (a personal plan, an evidence-based prediction: Look at those clouds — it's going to rain.) · PRESENT CONTINUOUS (a definite arrangement with time and place: We're meeting the engineers tomorrow.) · FUTURE CONTINUOUS (will be + verb-ing, an action in progress at a future time: This time tomorrow, we'll be driving across the USA.), with the WHICH ONE TO USE? strip and the reminder that will takes the base verb

Why: 🎯 Speaking out · at seven o'clock this evening

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: three sentence stems finished honestly, then swapped and challenged

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the three stems on the board: For my next birthday I … · At seven o'clock this evening I … · When I'm old enough I …
  2. Three minutes, silent, individually: finish all three in writing. One rule — the three sentences must use THREE DIFFERENT forms, and the box is written in the margin next to each one.
  3. 🎯 Kagan Timed Pair Share, two minutes each way: read your three sentences and say which box each one is in. The listener's job is to disagree if the box is wrong.
  4. Then the challenge round, four minutes, new partners: the listener asks ONE follow-up question about each sentence, which forces a second future sentence out of the speaker.
  5. Collect six sentences to the floor — two per box — and correct only the choice of form, nothing else today.
  6. Close with the honest count: Everyone in this room has just said at least six future sentences, and not all of them were «will».
📺 On screen: the three stems and the four boxes on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write three sentences with three different forms, label them, then defend the labels twice

Form
will for predictions, offers and promises · be going to for personal plans · present continuous for definite arrangements · will be + -ing for an action in progress at a future moment.
Pron. watch
satellite /ˈsætəlaɪt/ — three syllables with the stress on the first, and the class will put it on the last · experiment /ɪkˈsperɪmənt/ · the future continuous swallows its auxiliary in speech: /wɪlbiˈweɪtɪŋ/, not «will be waiting» in four separate pieces · and going to is /ɡənə/ in every natural sentence, which is why students do not hear it
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I'm seeing the dentist at four» — have I arranged it? → yes, it is in the diary. That is why it is the present continuous, not will.
«I'll help you» — did I plan that before you asked? → no, I decided now. Will is the form for a decision made at the moment of speaking.
«This time tomorrow I'll be flying» — is the flight finished or in progress? → in progress. That is what the future continuous is for.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The grammar half is where marks actually move: will, going to, present continuous and the future continuous are four different meanings that Russian expresses with one form.
