teacher desk + 3 stations

WHAT WE DO: the checkpoint one at a time (2 min: About me from the frame → 3–4 familiar questions → 1 question for ME); the class — a supported carousel

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The 1-on-1 checkpoint (≈2 min): 1) Your About me — go! (the frame in hand is fine); 2) 3–4 questions from the bank of FAMILIAR formats (Where are you from? Have you got…? What time…? Do you like…?); 3) the final twist: Now YOU ask ME one question! (their favourite moment); grid: fluency/vocabulary/clarity 1–4, + notes.
  2. Station A, “Interview gym”, by Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade (the GIA1-QQT-Mid deck; the support answer is on the card upside down): Ask — answer — check — TRADE — new partner inside the station.
  3. Station B, the “review board game”: a half-year game board (task squares: name 3 colours! tell the time! describe the monster!), a die, supports on the board.
  4. Station C, “A letter to May”: they quietly start a letter to themselves from the frame (they finish it in the I CAN block).
  5. Rotation ~12 min; checkpoints run continuously; after the checkpoint — a quiet return to the station.
📺 On screen: grid · a bank of question cards · the board game · letter frames
👦 Students do: everyone talks to me for 2 min (with support — successfully!) and works at the stations with no idle time. Expected: “a question for the teacher” lifts the fear best of all; the grid shows growth for EVERYONE since September. Typical error: one-word answers — I gently draw them out: Tell me more!

WHAT WE DO: bridge: everyone's had their say — now let's pay the test back: with a game

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. I hand back the marked papers face down: “the number is your own; the mistakes on the board are nobody's”.

47–62′
Review game: “nobody's mistakes”
🎲 Review game · “nobody's mistakes”
teams

WHAT WE DO: an anonymised review by Kagan Showdown: 6 “nobody's” mistakes — EVERYONE fixes them on mini-boards, boards up on command; we check our own tests quietly

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 6 slides = 6 “nobody's” mistakes (real, typical, anonymised from the tests): EVERYONE writes on their mini-board; on the command SHOWDOWN! — boards up all at once (+2 for the fix, +1 for a “rule word”: helper! -s! big letter!).
  2. We fix it IN CHORUS after each one — the course's ritual.
  3. After the round, 20 sec of quiet: Look at YOUR test — did this trick catch you? Tick.
  4. Into the notebook: “my 1 trap + how I fix it” (for a zero-level group — one, not two: manageable).
  5. As I walk round, into everyone's hands — 1 strength of their test, OUT LOUD: Your writing part — super!
📺 On screen: 6 error slides · mini-boards · their tests (Backup: the 6 errors read out and written on the board one at a time)
👦 Students do: they fix the “nobody's” mistakes as a team, check their own quietly, hear 1 personal compliment from me. Expected: a review with no tears — the format works; they recognise the traps with a smile — “oh, that was mine!”. Typical error: comparing scores — “a personal number!” + turning the focus back to the fixes.

desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: a big Starter–U4 I CAN list, with ticks; finishing the “May-me” letter into an envelope; 1 goal for the half-year

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. An I CAN list (a handout, 10 lines with pictures): I can say hello · count to 100 · talk about my family · order food · say the time… — ticks: ✔ / ~ / not yet.
  2. I unfold their September: “Remember the first lesson: a cloud of 20 words. Now — look at this list.”
  3. They finish the letter to May from the frame: Dear May-me! Now I can… My favourite was… In May I want to… — an envelope, I keep it until May.
  4. 1 goal for the half-year from “my trap” — a specific one.
📺 On screen: I CAN lists with pictures · envelopes
👦 Students do: they assess themselves, write the letter to May, set 1 goal. Expected: the “September-December” contrast is moving; they take care over the letters. Typical error: a cloudy goal — I help narrow it down to a verb + a topic.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, every learner has passed the supported speaking checkpoint (About me from the frame + familiar questions + teacher support), worked through the test in an anonymised review game, and closed the half-year with an “I CAN” self-assessment and a celebration.
  • Subsidiary. Dialogues from frames as the checkpoint format; progress logged in the grid (we'll compare in May); an anonymised review (“nobody's” mistakes fixed as a team); a half-year wrap-up: a big I CAN list + a letter to yourself in May; a festive finale.
  • Personal. The zero-level group's last meeting of the year: the main feeling they should leave with — “in 4 months I started speaking English from zero”. The checkpoint is a warm chat with me, not an exam. Review is anonymised only. The celebration is earned.
By the end, students can…
  • talk to the teacher for 2 min with the frame
  • answer familiar questions about themselves
  • fix «nobody's» mistakes in the team game
  • assess their half-year with the I CAN list

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is the checkpoint scary?” → No: a chat with me on familiar questions, the frame in your hands is allowed.
“If I make a mistake at the checkpoint — a minus?” → No: I'm listening for what you CAN do; mistakes are my notes for planning, not penalties.
“Whose mistakes are in the review game?” → Nobody's! Anonymised — we fix them as a team, points for fixes.

Assumptions: The test is marked before the lesson; typical mistakes are collected anonymously; “About me” from the frame is prepared at home (the frame was handed out). The pre-New-Year mood is fuel for the finale. Course rule #1 (said out loud EVERY Starter pair): mistakes are how we learn — here nobody laughs at mistakes, here we grow from them. The teacher makes mistakes on purpose and fixes them out loud.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Fear of a 1-on-1 conversationThe frame in hand is legal; I start with what they prepared; smile/nods; I give a prompt myself after a 3-sec pause — no agonising.
A queue for the checkpointA carousel of 3 quiet, supported stations; I call them one at a time; a helper role at the stations.
Review = “mine's worse than everyone's”ONLY anonymised mistakes on the slides; personal results go into everyone's own hands, in a whisper, with one strength said out loud.
Pre-holiday overexcitementThe celebration is strictly at the end, as a magnet; before it — the structure of the stations.
Those who missed the testThey write a mini-version at station C during the carousel; the checkpoint is in the shared queue.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
