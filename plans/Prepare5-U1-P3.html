🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use twelve personality adjectives (annoying, anxious, easygoing, jealous, loyal, reliable, sensible, sensitive, silly, sociable, talented, thoughtful) to describe real people, and use the -ing form after verbs like enjoy and don't mind, after adjective + preposition (good at, afraid of, keen on), and as the subject of a sentence.
What is genuinely hard
The adjectives come in confusable pairs that Russian keeps apart with different roots — sensible is not sensitive, silly is not funny, anxious is not angry. And the grammar breaks in one predictable place: after a preposition English insists on -ing, where Russian takes an infinitive.
Typical error to expect
«I'm good at to swim», «I enjoy to read» — after a preposition and after enjoy / don't mind / can't stand English takes the -ing form. Russian takes the infinitive in both places, which is why this error survives so long.
Why the lesson is built this way
The vocabulary arrives inside a quiz about the students themselves, which is the fastest legal way to make twelve adjectives personal — they choose five statements that are true, then read what the quiz says about them and disagree with it out loud. Only then do the definitions appear. The reading is an article they have opinions about before they read it (can online friends be as good as real ones?), and the grammar is lifted from that same article — hugging is good for us, are you good at keeping in touch — so the rules are extracted rather than delivered. The productive stage is a mingle in which nobody can take a turn without producing an -ing form.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“A scientist spent two years asking one question: can an online friend be as good as a real one? His answer surprised him. Today: the twelve words English uses for the kind of friend you are — and the form of the verb that follows every one of them.”

Why: Vocabulary · what kind of friend are you?

WHAT WE DO: About you + ex.1–2: the quiz, then the three friend types

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. About you, 90 seconds in pairs: How important are friends to you? What makes a good friend, in your opinion? If you use the video («Best friends forever», video 01), play it here and set two questions before it starts.
  2. ex.1: read the twelve statements and choose the FIVE that are most true for you. Four minutes, timed, silent. Tell them to guess the blue words rather than ask — that is the point of the stage.
  3. ex.2: read the description that matches your colour. Then the speaking task that makes it a language lesson: Do you agree with what it says about you? Which part is wrong?
  4. Hands up by type — orange, yellow, green. Two students from each explain, in English, what the quiz got wrong about them.
📺 On screen: the video if you use it, otherwise the page. Backup: if the video will not play, go straight to the About you questions on the page — the quiz does not depend on it
👦 Students do: choose, read, disagree. Everybody has a result and everybody has an opinion about it, which is the fastest route into personal language

Move break · describe and guess
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that spends the twelve adjectives immediately

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: walk until you meet someone, then describe a person you BOTH know — a teacher, a character, a footballer — using two adjectives from the wall, without the name.
  2. The partner guesses, then swaps roles. Walk again on the signal.
  3. Three rounds, about seventy seconds each. Keep the pace fast and do not let anyone stand still.
  4. Freeze and sit. One student describes somebody to the whole class and the class guesses — thirty seconds, no more.
📺 On screen: the twelve adjectives on the wall as the only support — no gadgets, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: everyone describes and guesses at least three times. The «no name» rule forces the adjectives to do all the work

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: their own list first, then the article (track 05)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: ex.3–4: five questions answered from the line, then the harvest

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3 in mixed-ability pairs: five questions, and every answer has to be underlined in the text before it is said. No underline, no answer.
  2. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: pairs agree, you call a number, that student answers for the pair. It removes the always-the-same-hand problem in one move.
  3. The oxytocin question (3) is the one worth slowing down for — it is also the sentence the class will quote for the rest of the week.
  4. ex.4: match the five highlighted words. Technique stated again — put the meaning into the sentence and check it still works. unfriend is the gift here: a verb built by the internet.
  5. Talking points, three minutes in pairs: Do you think you will have the same online friends in five years' time? The same real friends? What are the disadvantages of having online friends? — pairs first, then the floor
📺 On screen: the five questions on screen or on the page. Backup: the book is the primary source; no technology is required
👦 Students do: underline, agree in pairs, answer when your number is called. Everybody is accountable because nobody knows who will be asked
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 10–18 (reading for detail). Answering five questions from an underlined line is precisely the discipline the long-text section rewards.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: give the same article with three true / false / not stated statements for five minutes. Not stated is the Russian exam's own trap and it is worth meeting early.

Grammar · -ing forms
class → pairs
Why: Grammar · -ing forms

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–4: three rules extracted from the article, then three practice sets

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books at page 16. ex.1: the three examples are sentences from the article they have just read. Match them to the rules as a class, on the board.
  2. Make the second rule physical: underline the prepositions at, of, on, about, in in a different colour and say it as a law — After these words the next verb always wears -ing. Always.
  3. ex.2 individually (six spellings — watch preparing and visiting), then ex.3 in RallyCoach pairs: one writes, the other coaches by naming the preposition first.
  4. ex.4 is the error clinic in advance: seven sentences, all wrong, all wrong in the way this class will be wrong. Do item 1 together, then pairs.
  5. Check ex.4 by asking for the RULE, not the answer: Why -ing there? Grammar reference is SB p.148 for anyone who wants the full picture.
📺 On screen: the five prepositions underlined in colour on the board — it stays up for the mingle, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: extract, practise, coach each other, then repair seven broken sentences and justify every repair

Why: 🎯 Speaking out · find someone who

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: ten personal sentences, then a mingle to find people who match

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5 first, four minutes, silent: complete the ten sentence starters so they are true for you (I'm good at… · I'm afraid of… · I'm tired of… · …is boring for me).
  2. Circulate and check the form on paper before anyone speaks — this is the moment to catch a missing -ing while it is still cheap.
  3. Then the mingle, six minutes: find a classmate whose sentence is similar to yours, for as many of the ten as you can. Write the name next to the sentence. Rule: You may not write a name until you have BOTH said the whole sentence.
  4. Sit. Report in the third person, which is where the grammar pays twice: Dasha and I are both good at drawing. Nobody in this room is afraid of speaking English.
  5. Log two or three preposition errors silently — they open lesson 4.
📺 On screen: the ten sentence starters on the board for the ones who write slowly — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write ten true sentences, then say them to as many classmates as possible. Everyone speaks continuously for six minutes

Error clinic

Why: Error clinic

WHAT WE DO: four sentences from the mingle, repaired by the class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write four sentences you heard, two correct and two broken, without names.
  2. Pairs, sixty seconds: which two are wrong? Repair them.
  3. Take the repairs, rub out the wrong versions, and leave the two correct ones on the board for the wall.
  4. Name the pattern once: Every mistake tonight was the same mistake — a preposition with an infinitive after it.

Structure
He enjoys finding out about friendship. · Hugging is good for us. · Are you good at keeping in touch with your friends? · I'm tired of waiting. · She's crazy about playing online games.
Form
verb + -ing after enjoy, hate, don't mind, can't stand · preposition + -ing (good AT swimming, afraid OF losing) · -ing as the subject of a sentence (Writing letters is old-fashioned).
Pron. watch
anxious /ˈæŋkʃəs/ — two syllables, no «ks» · sensible /ˈsensəbl/ against sensitive /ˈsensətɪv/ (different words, not a pair) · jealous /ˈdʒeləs/ · and the prefix stress rule that the test rewards: un- is unstressed (unKIND), dis- carries the stress (DISagree, DISappear)
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«She's very sensible.» Does she cry easily or does she make good decisions? → good decisions. Sensitive is the one who cries easily.
«I'm good at swimming.» Which word comes before the verb? → at, a preposition. And after a preposition, the verb always ends in…? → -ing.
«Reading is boring for me.» What is the subject of this sentence? → reading — a verb doing a noun's job.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The -ing forms after verbs and prepositions (good at swimming, tired of waiting) are a named grammar line — Russian learners produce the infinitive there almost by default.
