🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Consolidate the block: the work verbs, as and like, the three types of clause, the verbs of ambition, the phrasal verbs and the two-object word order — and then produce all six under test conditions on the two official Cambridge papers.
What is genuinely hard
Two sections punish a right idea expressed slightly wrongly. Unit 19 Grammar 4 asks for the MATCH and the LINKER together, so a correct pairing with the wrong linker scores nothing. Unit 20 Grammar 4 forbids adding any words, which means the prompt itself decides the order: if to or for is in the jumble the thing comes first, and if it is not, the person does.
Typical error to expect
«tell to me», «send to me a photo», «explain me» — with an indirect object FIRST, English uses no preposition at all: tell me, send me a photo. The preposition only appears when the order flips: send a photo TO me. Russian marks the recipient with a case ending, so the to is imported to do a job English does with word order.
«believe to», «depend of», «join to» — a phrasal verb is one word with a preposition welded on, and the preposition cannot be reasoned out. Believe IN, depend ON, join IN, miss out ON. They are learned as chunks or they are lost.
Why the lesson is built this way
Forty-five minutes of review, forty-five of testing, and the review half completes Review 5 honestly — the four sections and the exam text that were deliberately left for today when block 9 played the other half. The order is vocabulary, then the two grammar sections, then the error-correction section, which is the closest thing on the page to the test. The review game is the other half of the same pack the class played five weeks ago, so no time goes on explaining rules. The briefing names both traps out loud, by section number, because a surprise on a test day is a lost mark rather than a lesson.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Last review of this course, and the page finishes the job we started five weeks ago — the half of Review 5 we left on purpose. First forty-five minutes: we play the block back. Second forty-five: you write it down on your own.”

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer between the review and the game

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands in one line down the room, in any order.
  2. You call a word from the block — install, whereas, as, dream, keep on, lend, in order to, believe in. The line passes it along: each student in turn says a WHOLE sentence containing it, and it may not repeat anyone else's.
  3. Two passes down the line, eight words, everybody speaks at least twice. Anyone stuck says Pass and gets the next word.
  4. Freeze and sit in groups of three: Last game of the course. Same one as five weeks ago, other half of the pack.

WHAT WE DO: the block's own review game with the Units 19–20 cards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. «CRAZY SENTENCES!» — PREPARE_5_Review_game_Units_17-20.pdf, the same consequences game as lesson 60 and this time with the OTHER half of the pack. Groups of three or more, one set of cut-up sentence cards per group, one blank A4 sheet and a pen each. One player reads the FIRST part of a card aloud, everyone secretly writes a word for the gap at the top of their own sheet, folds the paper over and passes it to the left. Three gaps per card, three passes, then the sheets are unfolded and each crazy sentence is read out.
  2. Which cards to use today. Lesson 60 played the Units 17–18 cards; today take the ones that were put aside — the gaps that live in Units 19–20 (the work verbs, as and like, the linking words, the verbs of ambition, the phrasal verbs, verbs with two objects). Scoring is optional and the Teacher's Notes say so: at the end each group reads out one sentence and the class awards points for originality, humour, vocabulary and — this is the part that makes it a language game — grammar and spelling. The printed follow-up is worth three minutes: with only the gaps in front of them, students rebuild the whole sentence from memory in their notebooks. It is the last game of the course, so play it properly and finish on the laugh.
  3. No demonstration needed — they played it in lesson 60. Say the three rules in one line (write, fold, pass) and start.
  4. Play four cards, about eight minutes. Then three minutes of reading out and voting.
  5. Award the points out loud and name the reason each time — originality, humour, vocabulary, and grammar. The grammar point is what makes it a review rather than a party, and it is the last time this year you get to say that.

WHAT WE DO: thirty-two minutes of silent testing, both papers, no support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start both papers together and write the finish time on the board. Unit 19 first, Unit 20 second; anyone who finishes 19 early moves straight on to 20.
  2. Halfway call at sixteen minutes, said once and quietly: Half the time. You should be starting the second paper.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
