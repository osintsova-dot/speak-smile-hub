Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name objects in space (planets · comet · meteorite · rocket · astronaut · satellite · asteroid belt · rings + the 8 planets) and describe the sky: “There are eight planets. There's a comet.” — meeting the superlative in the song (“Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun”).
  • Subsidiary. Sing «In our solar system» (CD2 16) & say the vocabulary chant (CD2 15); brainstorm what's in the day sky vs the night sky (Learning to learn, act. 1); play the planet guessing game (act. 4); recycle There is / There are from Unit 1 applied to space; early literacy — read space words from clues and write them (AB).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; stirrer→settler rhythm — the guessing game is lively, plan the freezes. Today is NAMING — the superlative (“the biggest / the closest”) is only heard in the song; it's drilled properly in L2, so don't over-correct it now.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name the 12 space words and the 8 planets;
  • describe a space scene with “There is / There are…”;
  • read space words from clues and spell them (AB).

Structure
There are eight planets.  ·  There's a comet / a rocket / an astronaut.  ·  heard in the song: Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun. Jupiter is the biggest planet.
Form
There is + singular (a comet, a rocket, one Sun) · There are + plural (eight planets, rings, asteroids). Superlative the …est — receptive only today (drilled in L2).
Pron. watch
asteroid /ˈæstərɔɪd/; satellite /ˈsætəlaɪt/ (3 syll.); meteorite /ˈmiːtiəraɪt/; astronaut /ˈæstrənɔːt/; Jupiter /ˈdʒuːpɪtə/; Uranus /ˈjʊərənəs/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“eight planets — There IS or There ARE?” → count them, plural → There ARE.
“a comet — one or many? There is or there are?” → one → There IS a comet.

Assumptions: Unit 3 (prehistoric times · past simple · could/couldn't) is just finished; base adjectives (big/small/hot/cold/close/far) are known — they feed the superlative from L2. There is / There are is active from Unit 1 (recycled today). The 12 space words + planet names are largely NEW. Readers — word cards work alongside pictures.

10–20′
Presentation · 12 space words
carpet → board

Present with flashcards + chant (act. 2, CD2 15). Each card: What's this? It's a comet! A rocket! An astronaut! Choral ×3 + one gesture (rocket = launch, comet = whoosh, rings = circle round).

There is / There are. Board: two columns — There is (a comet, a rocket, an astronaut, an asteroid belt) · There are (eight planets, rings, satellites). Hold each card — class chooses the column, then says: There's a comet. / There are eight planets.

Order the planets. Chain round the circle, Sun outwards: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. Keep the planet strip on the board.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: Stand up if you can SEE it at night… a satellite! — flash cards fast, everyone reacts every time.

💪 Support: answer with the gesture + word. ⭐ Extension: full sentence — There's a shiny rocket in space.

Song · In our solar system
desks

To your tables! Open your books at page 40. Sing (act. 3, CD2 16). Listen & point to the planets in order; 2nd run — sing with orbit gestures.

Notice the superlative (in context, no lecture). Read the song lines: Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun. Jupiter is the biggest planet of them all. Chorus — plants L2's grammar. Freeze between verses.

28–36′
Planet guessing game
carpet → desks, pairs

▶ Guessing game (act. 4, Talk Partners). A gives a clue from the book: It's next to Earth. It's the fourth planet. B guesses: Is it Mars? — Yes, it is. Well done! Swap. Point per correct guess.

ICQ: Do you say the planet's name in your clue? (→ no — it's a secret!)

✔ Assess: stand behind one pair — is it a full clue and a “Is it…? Yes, it is” answer?

36–44′
Space word trade
mingle

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade. Each child holds a space card. Music → mingle; off → pair: A shows the card, What's this? Is there one or many? B answers It's a comet. There's one comet. then swap, TRADE cards, new partner. 5–6 exchanges — everyone speaks many times.

Whole-class check: Hands up if you had something you say with THERE ARE!

My space scene · write
desks

Speak. ▶ RallyRobin: Take turns: There's a… / There are some… — things in space.

Write + draw (AB p.38 format). Two frames from the board: There is a ___. There are ___. Each child draws a quick space scene & writes 3 sentences about it. Fast finishers add an adjective.

💪 Support: copy the frame + choose from the word cards. ⭐ Extension: add a colour/adjective: There's a shiny satellite.
