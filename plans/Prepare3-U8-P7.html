🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that two units work without the book: twenty-five words and phrases, the for/since choice, and the choice between the present perfect and the past simple.
What is genuinely hard
The tense contrast from lesson 5. Everything else in this block is knowledge; that one is a decision made under time pressure, and it is the part that decays first. Twenty minutes of retrieval before the paper is worth more than any re-explanation.
Typical error to expect
I have seen this film yesterday — the present perfect with a finished time marker. yesterday, ago, last year, in 2019 always take the past simple [high priority, A2/B1]
I've lived here since three years / for 2015 — for and since swapped. Russian uses one word for both, so the choice has to be made consciously every single time [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built as retrieval practice, not re-teaching: the two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it and what shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which forms still break. The book's own Review is used honestly and partially — Review 4 covers Units 13–16 and its entire vocabulary half belongs to Units 13–14, so only the grammar items and the two exam tasks are ours, and the class is told that in one sentence rather than left to discover it. The review game is restricted the same way: eight of the twenty-one cards. The test itself is ours, because Cambridge published no Unit test for 15 or 16, and saying so out loud costs nothing and buys trust.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show. Everything on today's paper has been done in this room, and the specification is going on the board in three minutes so you can check that yourself. One warning: the part that decides the score is the one with two tenses in the same text.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast trading round on the whole block, and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — block rewind, at speed. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with cards for the whole block: the fifteen free-time activities, the collocations, the ten language-learning words. Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Two trades each, then sit down on a clap. Be honest with them as they sit: Everything you just said is on today's paper.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show. The specification goes on the board now.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the whole block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, read the words out from a list and students give the phrase back
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety visibly dropping. Typical problem: they stumble on photography and exercises, which is useful information ten minutes before the spelling round

WHAT WE DO: put the test specification up in advance — transparency is what takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the eight parts of the paper on the board in two groups. VOCABULARY: free-time activities · collocations about having fun · words to describe language learning. WHAT WE DO: the block's vocabulary pulled out of memory, plus a written spelling round

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, books closed: you say a definition, teams write the word. Ten items from the two units — getting and keeping lots of one kind of object (collecting) · using a camera (photography) · to find a word in a dictionary (look up) · to say what a word means in another language (translate) · to think you know but not be sure (guess).
  2. Round 2, on the page: the eight items of Review 4 Vocabulary that are ours — there are none, and say so. ⚠️ Review 4 covers Units 13–16, and the whole VOCABULARY half of it (ex.1 units of measurement, ex.2 outdoor activities, ex.3 shops) is Units 13–14 — nothing from our block at all. Do not photocopy the page and hope. Only the items below are ours; the vocabulary of Units 15–16 is revised in the stations instead.
  3. Round 3, so the station has its written half: Kagan Showdown — you say the word in Russian, teams write it in English and raise the board on command. Put the traps in deliberately: photography · instrument · exercises · brilliant · translate.
  4. Every misspelling goes into a «watch out» column on the board and stays there right up to the test.
  5. Round 4, ninety seconds: the collocations. Four different ways to say you had a good time. Go.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column building up in chalk as the rounds go on — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed; the Review 4 page is printed in the students' own book
👦 Students do: produce words from definitions, then spell against the clock. Expect almost everything orally and a visible drop in the written round. Typical error: photografy and exercices — which is exactly why the column is needed

Review station 2 · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar — and it shows you, twenty minutes before the test, exactly which form still breaks.

WHAT WE DO: the Units 15–16 items of Review 4 Grammar (SB p.97) — the two tenses and for/since

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Grammar ex.1, items 3, 4, 7 and 8 only: choose, then correct. 3 …had it for one year · 4 Last night my sister left her bag · 7 My mum has played the violin since she was six · 8 I texted you an hour ago but you didn't answer
  2. Grammar ex.2, items 2, 3 and 6: write the question, then answer it for yourself. 2 How long have you lived in this town? · 3 Have you ever visited Russia? · 6 How long have you known your best friend? — then students' own answers
  3. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the reason and not the answer — Which word decided it?
  4. Grammar ex.3, all eight gaps: past simple or present perfect in one connected text. Every one of these is ours, and it is the single most useful five minutes of the review half. 1 have loved · 2 was · 3 spoke · 4 began · 5 moved · 6 went · 7 learned · 8 haven't started
  5. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Gap five — number twos, boards up!
  6. Boil it down to two lines on the board and leave them: amount of time → for; starting point → since. Time word inside the finished box → past simple; outside it → present perfect.
📺 On screen: the Review 4 grammar exercises on the screen with the Units 13–14 items already crossed out (Backup: they are printed on SB p.97 — put a pencil cross against items 1, 2, 5 and 6 of ex.1 before the lesson and run the rounds from the page)
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with a reason, then report by number. Expect ex.3 to be the one that sorts the class. Typical error: gap 1 (have loved) — there is no time word at all in that clause, so they default to the past simple

Review game
teams of 2–3
Why: A game that hides revision inside a reason to speak; nerves before a test drop when the last thing they did was laugh.

WHAT WE DO: the game «List it! (Review game Units 13–16, Cambridge, PHOTOCOPIABLE)» — five items, fast, and the spelling counts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Twenty-one cards, each of them «List 5 …». Teams of one to three. A player takes a card and reads it out; every team races to write five items. The first team to finish shouts Listed! and everyone else stops. You check quietly that the five are valid — if they are not, you say Keep writing and the race goes on. The winning team reads its five out; the others tick anything they also had and ask about anything new.
  2. Scoring: One point for winning the round · one bonus point for every item nobody else had · one extra point if all five are spelt correctly. Arguments about whether an answer counts are part of the game — you are the referee, and students are allowed to make their case.
  3. ⚠️ Important, and do it before you photocopy: ⚠️ The pack covers four units. Take out the Units 13–14 cards before you photocopy. Ours are eight cards: «things that help you learn a language» (U16) · «good places to listen to music» (U15) · «typical spelling mistakes in English» (U16) · «things that make people feel glad» (U15) · «activities you enjoy with friends» (U15) · «good places to read books» (U15) · «activities to do on a smartphone» (U15) · «things you can do online» (U15). That is enough for eight rounds, which is more than the slot holds. The other thirteen cards (fossils, shops, units of measurement, things that come in pairs or slices) are Units 13–14 — leave them for the block 7 review.
  4. Run six of the eight cards, not all eight — eight minutes is six rounds at most and stopping while they still want more is the right way to end a game.
  5. Keep the referee role visible and light. Arguments about whether chess counts as «an activity you enjoy with friends» are the language work, not an interruption of it.
📺 On screen: one printed set of the eight relevant cards per group, cut along the dotted lines in advance — paper is the medium here, so no backup is needed; with no printer, read the card out yourself and let every team race on paper
👦 Students do: race to five items per round and argue about validity. Expect the liveliest stage of the review half. Typical error: they write four items and shout — the referee check is what catches it, and it should be visible when it does

Test briefing
class
Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions for the paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go back to the «watch out» column: the five or six words that were misspelt. Thirty seconds. Look at these. Then I wipe them.
  2. Instructions: 35 minutes, eight parts, 63 marks; phones in the bag; finished early — turn the paper over and write four sentences about your own free time.
  3. Part 8 is read aloud by you and it happens FIRST, at the very start, so that everybody hears the eight numbers once and then works alone.
  4. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  5. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; everything else in the room is switched off so there is nothing to look at
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect the thirty-second look at the spelling column to be worth two or three marks each. Typical problem: someone asks whether the pronunciation part is really a listening — yes, and it is your voice

WHAT WE DO: the block 8 test: eight parts, 63 marks, 35 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Part 8 first, from your voice: read the eight ‑teen / ‑ty numbers aloud, twice each, in a shuffled order, 3 minutes. Then everybody works alone.
  2. Turn the paper. You have thirty-five minutes from now. Start.
  3. Walk round the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  4. Write the time left in chalk: 20 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  5. Anyone who finishes early turns the paper over and writes four sentences about their own free time with for and since.
  6. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check part six — is every verb in the tense the time word asked for?
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board and nothing else — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed; the only part of the paper that has to be heard is read out in your own voice, which cannot fail
👦 Students do: work in silence for 35 minutes. Expect the vocabulary parts to be closed fast and part 6 to take a third of the time. Typical error: they leave part 7 (find the mistake) until last and rush it — worth one line in the briefing next time

Test wrap-up
circle
Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework, and the bridge to the exam lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip of paper, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the feedback at the start of the next lesson.
  3. Homework: once the papers come back, rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly. For tonight — the Vocabulary or Grammar worksheet for Units 15–16 if anything is still open.
  4. Say the next step plainly, and make it sound like the reward it is: Next lesson is the only lesson this block with no new grammar in it at all. Three minutes of talking about yourself — and the Russian version of exactly the same thing.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed; if the board is already full, simply read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to be about the two tenses most often — that is the diagnosis you carry into Prepare 4

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
