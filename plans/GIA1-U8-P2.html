WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.4 the rule for questions and short answers → ex.5 build 6 questions for speed (everyone writes on mini-boards)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4 p.100: Did + subject + infinitive. Short answers: Yes, I did / No, I didn't — the DID comes back, not the verb. A mini short-answers poster on the board.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, no hiding.
  3. Ex.5 ▶ order-the-words race STANDING (after two seated blocks I get the class up): I show a set of words → everyone builds it on a mini-board → Boards up! 6 rounds, +1 to the crew for a clean volley; we sit back down only at minute 26.
  4. Key: Did you watch TV yesterday evening? · How did you come to school today? · Did you do any homework last weekend? · What did you have for dinner last night? · When did you meet your best friend? · What did you do last weekend?
  5. Pronunciation: did you = /ˈdɪdʒə/ — say it fast: 'didja watch TV?' (TB p.113).

🔥 Challenge · THE INTERROGATION (alibi)
standing · pairs → crews

WHAT WE DO: the alibi interrogation: everyone has their own “yesterday”; detectives ask Did/Wh-questions, catch the mismatches; everyone talks at the same time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out alibi cards (or the kids invent their own “yesterday from 4 to 8 pm”: where they were, how they got there, what they did).
  2. Ex.6 p.100 first in writing, 2 min: Answer the six questions so they're TRUE for you. — this is their testimony.
  3. ▶ Interrogation in pairs: the detective asks 5 questions (at least 2 Wh-), the suspect answers with short answers + a detail. Rotation: new detective, same testimony — if the story changes, you're busted!
  4. Round 3: crew against crew — 90 sec, whoever catches the mismatch gets +2.
  5. Challenge (TB p.113): follow-up questions — What did you watch? When did you do your homework?

👦 Students do: everyone interrogates and defends at the same time, swapping partners twice. Expected: laughter + growing fluency in questions. Typical error: “Where you went?” — the detective-partner penalises it themselves (+1 to whoever catches it).

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can interrogate and defend an alibi in the past simple: form negatives (didn't + infinitive) and Yes/No + Wh- questions with short answers, and deliver a 60-sec radio segment about a school travel week using 6+ transport collocations.
  • Subsidiary. The gate exposes “did went”; the system negative → questions → short answers goes in over 45′ (KTP pair 52); the Leonardo blog as a sniper checkpoint for written accuracy; Brain teaser; contracted forms p.117; collocations go straight into a journey chain; Listening with the subskill “choosing the best summary”.
  • Personal. The unit's main enemy — “did went / didn't wanted”. I plant the anchor DID EATS THE PAST and don't let go of it all unit. Grammar exactly 45′: after minute 45 — only lexis and listening.
By the end, students can…
  • make past simple negatives with didn't + infinitive, no ‘didn't went’
  • build Did-questions and Wh-questions and answer with true short answers
  • use 10+ transport collocations (buy a ticket, change trains, wait in a queue…)
  • choose the best summary of a radio programme and fill 6 detail gaps

Structure

Form
didn't (= did not) + INFINITIVE · Did + subject + infinitive? · Wh- + did + subject + infinitive? · short answers repeat did/didn't, NOT the main verb.
Pron. watch
did you → /ˈdɪdʒə/ (TB p.113) · didn't — a contraction, SB p.117 audio 75 · /aɪ/ in drive and ride (TB p.114).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He didn't wanted — is that right?” → No: didn't eats the past, WANT stays.
“Did you go? — Yes, I went?” → No: Yes, I DID. A short answer repeats did.
“How did it started?” → How did it START — after did it's always the infinitive.

Assumptions: Past simple affirmative (regular + irregular) was covered in U7; negatives and questions are familiar from GMF4, but written accuracy is shaky. Transport set 1 is active after P1. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (didn't + infinitive / question word order / superlative spelling), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
