WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share on repeat: entry; rewind P3 is woven in: describe a workshop photo from a frame; a bridge to questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Entry: Fist bump — one clothes word!
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A talks for their time against the timer, B is the active listener (counts pauses on their fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's feedback starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. Rewind P3: I show the photo on p.77. Pairs take turns — one sentence each, 60 sec. Frame on the board: She's ___ing a ___. They're wearing ___.
  4. Bridge: You can SAY what people are doing. But how do you ASK? That's today.
📺 On screen: photo p.77 · board: description frame
👦 Students do: take turns building sentences in pairs (Kagan RallyRobin). Expected: 4–6 sentences per pair. Typical error: a missing be — the frame holds it up.

WHAT WE DO: physical input of word order: word cards on magnets; ex.1 rules; choral drill

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I write on the board: You are wearing glasses. I hand the same words out as cards to one student.
  2. Make it a QUESTION. Move ONE word. The student moves are to the front, the class calls it out in chorus. We repeat with 3 other sentences (She is reading · They are running · He is playing).
  3. Formula on the board: BE + subject + verb-ing ? Chorus ×3.
  4. Ex.1 together, one item at a time. Key: 1 before (be goes BEFORE the subject) · 2 with the verb be · 3 don't use (in a positive short answer — NO contraction: Yes, he is) · 4 use (in a negative one — WITH contraction: No, they aren't).
  5. Chorus of short answers ×5: Yes, I AM. No, I'M NOT. Yes, she IS. No, she ISN'T. Yes, they ARE. No, they AREN'T.
📺 On screen: word cards on magnets · board: question formula + short answers table (stay up the whole lesson)
👦 Students do: physically rearrange the words and drill the answers in chorus. Expected: “by hand” lands faster than any explanation. Typical error: “Yes, she's” — the paradox rule big on the board.

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.2 six questions in writing with a check after each + a mini-board blitz

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.2: we do the first item together on the board, the rest — in teams, one at a time with a general check. Key: 2 Are they listening to music? · 3 Are we travelling by bus? · 4 Are you watching a film? · 5 Am I speaking Spanish? · 6 Is she playing the guitar?. Reinforcement (TB p.90): first we decide which form of be and which -ing form, then we write.
  2. Mini-board blitz: I give “he / read / a book” → everyone writes a question → Boards up! 5 rounds.
  3. After each round — the short answer in chorus (+ and −).
  4. Points to the teams: +1 for a clean question, +1 for a clean answer.
  5. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.
📺 On screen: SB p.78 ex.2 · mini-boards
👦 Students do: write questions at the same time and answer in chorus. Expected: 4–5 out of 5 clean by the end of the block. Typical error: they forget the question mark — the class shouts in chorus “question mark!”

Play · “What am I doing?” + Guess Who
standing · pairs

WHAT WE DO: a question drill game: mime with Yes/No questions, then a pair game with character cards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ What am I doing?: the one performing mimes an action, the class asks ONLY Yes/No questions: Are you cooking? Are you playing football? The performer answers with short answers. 5 rounds.
  2. ▶ Guess Who in pairs: everyone has a character card (what they're doing + what they're wearing). Ask Yes/No questions. Six maximum — then guess!
  3. Rotate partners ×2 — everyone asks and answers.
  4. Support on the desks: a list of question starters (Are you… ? Is he… ? Are they… ?).
📺 On screen: action cards · character cards (one each) · support on the desks
👦 Students do: mime, ask, guess — everyone talks at the same time in pairs. Expected: by the end of the rotation the questions come naturally. Typical error: a Wh-question instead of Yes/No — the rule of the game brings it back.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Formula train on the board: Wh + BE + subject + -ing ? — we count the carriages on our fingers in chorus.
  2. Ex.4 together, one question at a time. Key: 1 Where are you sitting? · 2 What language are you speaking? · 3 What grammar are you studying? · 4 What is your teacher wearing?.

📺 On screen: SB p.78 ex.4–6 · board: formula train + answer frame
👦 Students do: build Wh-questions by the formula and answer about themselves. Expected: 4/4 in ex.4 after the train, 4–6 out of 6 in the round-up. Typical error: “What she is doing” — we count the carriages on our fingers.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask Yes/No and Wh- questions in the present continuous and give short answers (Are you wearing a jacket? — Yes, I am. / What are you doing?).
  • Subsidiary. Word order is introduced physically (word cards); short answers are drilled in chorus; Wh-questions by formula; a supported Grammar round-up; in the second half — Key phrases “asking for information” from a video model and a dialogue from a support.
  • Personal. Questions are the hardest spot in the new structure. Don't skimp on time for physically rearranging words and choral short answers: it's exactly those that give confidence in the roleplay in the second half.
By the end, students can…
  • put be before the subject to make a question
  • answer Yes, I am / No, she isn't correctly
  • ask Wh- questions with the right order
  • ask for information about a place using Key phrases

Structure
Are you having a good time? — Yes, we are. · Is she wearing glasses? — No, she isn't. · What are you doing? · I'd like some information about this exhibition.
Form
Wh- questions: question word + be + subject + verb-ing? → What is she doing? · Where are you going? · Short answers: Yes, he IS (full form!) / No, he ISN'T (contracted).
Pron. watch
ABCD TB p.92: in Wh- questions the intonation FALLS, with stress on the information word: What TIME is it open? · How MUCH does it cost? · HOW can I get there?
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“You are wearing? — is that a question?” → no: ARE YOU wearing? (be goes first).
“Yes, he's — is that OK?” → no: Yes, he IS.
“What she is doing?” → no: What IS SHE doing?
“I'd like some information — is that polite or rude?” → polite, it's a phrase for the information desk.

Assumptions: The affirmative and negative forms were set up in Pair 3; questions with be came up in U1, but not with -ing — that's new. Key phrases for the information desk are a completely new functional block.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
