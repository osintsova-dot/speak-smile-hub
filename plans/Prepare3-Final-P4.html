🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Sit the official Cambridge End-of-year test on the language of Units 1–20, and close the year on what the group can do rather than on a number.
What is genuinely hard
Breadth. Eight grammar and vocabulary tasks sweep twenty units, and the sets that were learnt in September — the expedition equipment of Unit 7, the shops of Unit 14 — are the ones most likely to have gone quiet.
Typical error to expect
becouse · tomorow · alot · wich · beatiful · intresting — the most frequent misspellings; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
My brother play football every day instead of plays — the dropped ‑s in the third person singular [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
Two clean halves, and the first exists entirely to serve the second. The revision is built backwards from the paper: the four vocabulary tasks decide what the word half revises, the four grammar tasks decide what the grammar half revises, and nothing decorative gets in. The specification goes on the board at minute five and stays there — a paper whose shape is public is a paper nobody is frightened of. The scale goes up with it, so no score is a surprise. Task 12, the speaking, is not sat today: the group sat a complete A2 Key Speaking paper one lesson ago, which is a far better speaking sample than five prompted questions, so the five questions are used instead as the last thirty seconds of the revision half. And the lesson does not end on the paper. It ends on September against May, and on the sentence that takes them into Prepare 4 — because a year with the same children should not close on a mark.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Last paper of the year, and there is nothing on it you have not done. Look at the board: eleven tasks, and I will tell you which unit every one of them comes from. Forty-five minutes to remember, forty minutes to show. No speaking today — you did the real speaking paper last lesson and it was better than anything five questions could show me. And we are not finishing on a number: we are finishing on what you can do now that you could not do in September.”

0–5′
Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls back the one thing that is needed today rather than everything that was ever taught.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of the whole year said out loud, and the bridge into the last lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and leave the desk empty. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the year in one round. Kagan Round Robin round the circle, one word each, no repeats: One word from this year. Any unit. Go.
  3. Go round twice if the group is quick. Write nothing down — the point is that twenty units get said out loud in ninety seconds and the room hears how much there is.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Last paper of the year, and there is nothing on it you have not done. Forty-five minutes to remember, forty to show.
📺 On screen: nothing on display and nothing playing — the circle is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: produce one word each, twice round. Expect the last block's words first and the September ones last, which is exactly the map of what needs the revision half. Typical problem: a repeat — no penalty, just move on, the round is a warm-up and not a game

5–8′
Lesson frame
board
Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: the specification and the scale go up before anything else happens

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the eleven tasks on the board in two groups, with the unit next to each. VOCABULARY: the internet (Unit 10) · shops (Unit 14) · language learning (Unit 16) · expedition kit (Unit 7). GRAMMAR: the tenses · future forms · determiners · everything else.
  2. Then LISTENING (five speakers), READING (a travel blog) and WRITING (a reply to an email, about fifty words).
  3. Look at the board. Have we done all of this? Yes or no? The class answers chorally, and the answer is always yes.
  4. Say the one absence out loud: There is a speaking task on this paper and we are not doing it. You did the real speaking paper last lesson, and that told me more than five questions could.
  5. The scale goes up next to the specification and stays there: 67–74 — 5 — the year holds end to end · 55–66 — 4 — solid, with two or three named things to take into Prepare 4 · 41–54 — 3 — half of it holds; the summer list is short and specific, and it is written today · 40 and below — 2 — not entered in the register on the spot: a retake in September on your own sections, not on the whole paper.
  6. And the honest answer to the question that is coming: Yes, this one goes on your report. That is why the scale has been on the board since minute five.
📺 On screen: the eleven-task specification and the scale in chalk, and they stay up until the papers are handed out — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the specification and answer chorally. Expect the visible scale to lower the temperature rather than raise it. Typical problem: someone asks what happens if they get a 2 — answer it from the scale, out loud, once: a retake in September on their own sections
board
8–22′
Express revision · words
pairs → teams
Why: Retrieval practice rather than re-teaching: they pull the year's vocabulary out of memory in short bursts, which is what makes it stay, and it shows you thirty minutes before the paper exactly which sets have gone.

WHAT WE DO: the four vocabulary sets the paper actually tests, and nothing else

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade, six minutes, with cards for the four sets only: internet words (Unit 10) · shops (Unit 14) · language learning (Unit 16) · expedition equipment (Unit 7). Five trades each.
  2. Then Kagan Showdown, books closed, four rounds of spelling — one from each set, chosen because they are the ones that get misspelt: message board · chemist's · exercises · compass.
  3. Every misspelling goes into a «watch out» column on the board and it stays there until the papers go out.
  4. Last three minutes on the set that always goes quiet: the expedition kit of Unit 7. Eight things. Sixty seconds. Write. Then check against the book.
  5. And the thirty seconds that replace the speaking task: the five tourist-office questions, built orally from the prompts, nothing written. 1 Where is the History Museum? · 2 When does it open? · 3 How much is a family ticket / does a family ticket cost? · 4 Where can we buy (some) postcards? · 5 What do they sell / is sold in the café?
📺 On screen: the four sets of word cards in their hands and the «watch out» column in chalk — paper and the board are the media here, so no backup is needed; if the cards were not printed, read the definitions aloud and students write the word
👦 Students do: trade cards, then spell against the clock. Expect the internet set to be intact and the expedition kit to be the one that has gone. Typical error: chemist's without the apostrophe — which is why it is a Showdown item and not a card
word cards for Units 7, 10, 14, 16 · mini-whiteboards · board
22–36′
Express revision · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar, and deliberately built from the eight grammar tasks the paper actually contains, so that nothing in the revision half is decorative.

WHAT WE DO: the four grammar tasks of the paper, in the order the paper asks them

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round one, the tenses (task 5): eight sentences on the board with two options each, one per tense the paper tests. Kagan RallyCoach — the partner asks for the reason, not the answer.
  2. Round two, the future (task 6): Sure, not sure, or arranged? Three columns on the board, and every sentence goes into one of them — will, may/might, present continuous.
  3. Round three, determiners (task 7): the six that carry the marks — another against other, much against many, both, a slice of. Sixty seconds each, Showdown.
  4. Round four, everything else (task 8): one sentence per area, said out loud and repaired chorally — an adverb, a possessive pronoun, don't have to, a gerund, who, a first conditional, a reflexive pronoun, a passive.
  5. Boil each one down to a single line and leave all eight on the board: finished time → past simple · unfinished time → present perfect · forbidden → mustn't · not necessary → don't have to · after a preposition → ‑ing · the passive needs be.
📺 On screen: the four rounds written up one at a time in chalk, with the eight one-line summaries left standing — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with a reason, then show on the boards. Expect the future round to be cleanest and the determiners to be the weakest. Typical error: another used with a plural — the three-column layout catches it in one look

Test briefing
class
Why: The errors from the revision half get named and fixed while the paper is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: the «watch out» column read one last time, then the rules of the paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to the «watch out» column: the five or six words that were misspelt in the revision half. Thirty seconds. Then I wipe it.
  2. The rules, in one breath: forty minutes, eleven tasks, phones in the bag, task 9 is the listening and it runs once for everybody at the start.
  3. Finished early: Task 11 — add two sentences. Then check every spelling in tasks one to four.
  4. The two rules from the mock, and they are the same two: check before you hand it in; never leave a box empty.
  5. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. Hand the papers out face down, Standard and Plus without any announcement of which is which: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column in chalk, wiped in front of them just before the papers go out — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect the wiping of the column to focus the room more than any instruction. Typical problem: somebody notices their neighbour has a different paper — nothing is said, and nothing needs to be

44–84′
The End-of-year test
solo, silent mode
Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats, and an official Cambridge paper on the language of this course. Everything in it has been done in class, and the specification has been on the board since minute five.

WHAT WE DO: the official Cambridge End-of-year test, tasks 1–11, 40 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. Forty minutes. Task nine is the listening and we do it first, together. Everything else is yours.
  2. Play the audio for task 9 once, at the start, so that the whole room is on the same clock: the five speakers, about two minutes. Then they work alone.
  3. Walk the room in silence. To any question about content the answer is only Read the instruction again.
  4. Write the minutes remaining on the board — 20 · 10 · 5 — in chalk, never out loud.
  5. Anyone who finishes early adds two sentences to task 11 and then checks the spelling of tasks 1 to 4. Nobody leaves and nobody talks.
  6. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling. And check there are no empty boxes.
📺 On screen: the audio file PRE-FT-S_ex.10.mp3 played once from the whiteboard app for task 9, and the minutes remaining written on the board (Backup: the tapescript of the five speakers is printed on page 2 of the answer key — read it aloud yourself, twice, at natural speed; the rest of the paper needs no technology at all)
👦 Students do: work in silence for forty minutes. Expect tasks 1 to 4 to be closed fast and task 7 (determiners) to be the one that is left till last. Typical error: task 11 skipped as an optional extra — which is why it is named twice, in the specification and in the briefing

84–90′
The end of the year
circle
Why: The year does not close on a number. It closes on what the group can do now that it could not do in September, and on the sentence that takes them into Prepare 4.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, September against May one last time, and the bridge into Prepare 4

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, with no comment on anybody's work.
  2. Then close the books and change the subject completely: Forget the paper. One sentence each: something you can do now that you could not do in September. Kagan Round Robin, round the circle once, no repeats.
  3. Take them without commenting. Repeat each one back, louder. That is the whole technique and it is the right last thing this group hears from the course.
  4. Marks: Your paper comes back to you on the last day, privately. Nobody's score is read out, today or ever.
  5. Homework, and it is genuinely for the summer: three lines about what you will keep doing in English — one book or one series with subtitles, ten minutes of the app a week, and the Prepare 4 word list in September. Nothing is checked.
  6. The last line of the year, and it is worth preparing: Twenty units, seventy-two lessons. In September you could write five sentences about yourself. Today you sat a whole paper in English and most of you finished it. That is what the year was for. See you in Prepare 4.
📺 On screen: nothing is displayed and nothing is playing — the circle is the medium here, and the three summer lines go on the board in chalk, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hand in the paper and say one sentence about the year. Expect the round to be slower and more serious than any other round of the year, and let it be. Typical problem: somebody asks for their score — the answer was given in the briefing and it does not change on the last day

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Somebody finishes in twenty-five minutesThe instruction is fixed and it is on the board with the specification: Go back to task 11 and add two sentences. Then check every spelling in tasks 1 to 4. The writing is the only part with no ceiling.
The audio for task 9 will not playThe tapescript is printed on page 2 of the answer key — five short monologues. Read them aloud yourself, twice, at natural speed, in the same order. It is the same test.
A Plus paper lands on a student who cannot hold itSwap it without comment and without discussion, at the desk, in the first two minutes. Decide who gets which before the lesson, not during it, and never announce the split out loud.
«Does this go on my report?»Yes, and say so plainly at the start rather than letting it be asked mid-paper — with the scale already on the board, so the question answers itself.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the OGE — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: The grade scale: 2 = 0–28 · 3 = 29–45 · 4 = 46–57 · 5 = 58–68 out of 68.

Today's paper is not that scale and must not be presented as it. It is a Cambridge end-of-year test on the language of this course. The reason to know the Russian scale is the opposite one: so that a teacher can say honestly, in Year 9, that 29 marks is a pass and that the distance from here to there is two years of language and not two years of fear.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
