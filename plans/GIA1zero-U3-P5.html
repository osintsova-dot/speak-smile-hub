🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Writing for beginners — the most fragile skill: 6–8-minute portions, frames on the board for the WHOLE pair, we praise completing the step, not “genius”. Quick review — no racing, with peer help: it's a radar, not a competition.

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: input of and/but by gestures; ex.5 rules; ex.7 practice on mini-boards on the traffic-light principle

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Gestures: and = palms together (a similar idea) · but = palms apart (a different one). Examples from the book in chorus with gestures.
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYBODY writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift them all together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. Ex.5 rules in chorus with support: and = similar · but = different · a comma BEFORE but (I draw a big comma).
  4. Ex.7 on mini-boards: 6 rounds of 10 sec, boards up (TB key: and·and·but·but·and·but). The gesture is a hint.
  5. TB extra: and before the last item in a list: bread, cheese AND milk.
📺 On screen: board: the and/but gesture poster + a big comma · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they learn the linkers with gestures, flip 6 rounds. Expected: the gesture hints at the choice on its own. Typical error: no comma before but — I draw a comma in the air.

WHAT WE DO: a dish bank by brainstorm → everybody chooses 2 (not 4 — less for beginners!) → notes from the 4-axis template

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB-warmer: Typical dishes of our country! Shout! — the bank on the board: borsch · pelmeni · bliny · syrniki · okroshka… (stays up).
  2. THINK: Choose TWO dishes — yours. (the model writes about 4 — 2 is enough for us, say it without apologising: “two, but perfect”).
  3. PREPARE — the notes template on the board: ingredients · where · when · with what. Words, not sentences! Four minutes.
  4. I walk round, help with words (cabbage? sour cream? — I give them on cards).

👦 Students do: they brainstorm the bank, choose 2 dishes, write notes in words. Expected: “how do you say smetana?” — the sour cream card goes into the bank. Typical error: notes as sentences — I cut them down to words.

WHAT WE DO: portion 1: the opening + dish number one from the book's frames; quiet writing against the timer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin: a 60-sec physical warm-up + a gesture-check of your own portion: is there an and or a but?

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Warm-up: Stand up! Swap seats if you wrote about borsch… if your dish has meat… if it's delicious!
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we alternate one answer at a time — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!
  3. A seated gesture-check: Look at your portion one. Is there an AND (palms together) or a BUT (apart)? No? Add ONE now — one minute.
📺 On screen: timer
👦 Students do: they move, then add a linker to their own portion. Expected: the warm-up recharges them for portion 2. Typical error: and stuck in without meaning — their neighbour catches it later in review.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write a 5–6 sentence blog post about food in our country in portions, joining ideas with and/but, and check it with a 3-point list.
  • Subsidiary. A model blog step by step (general idea — an echo of P2); input of and/but (+ the comma before but); a bank of support phrases; writing in two portions against the timer; peer review by boxes; Quick review with peer help — a calm run-up to the P6 test.
  • Personal. Writing for beginners — the most fragile skill: 6–8-minute portions, frames on the board for the WHOLE pair, we praise completing the step, not “genius”. Quick review — no racing, with peer help: it's a radar, not a competition.
By the end, students can…
  • name world dishes and their countries
  • join two ideas with and/but (comma before but)
  • write a blog post from frames in 2 portions
  • check a partner's work with 3 ticks kindly

Structure

Form
and = a similar idea · but = a different idea · a comma before but (SB p.44 rule box); the post skeleton: opening → dishes → descriptions.
Pron. watch
delicious /dɪˈlɪʃəs/ — we clap the syllables · stew /stjuː/ · we say the dishes of our own languages proudly, as they are.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“It's tasty ___ it isn't healthy — and or but?” → but (a different idea) + a comma.
“Is a post = 6 sentences in one go?” → No — two portions, step by step.
“Is peer review a red pen everywhere?” → No — 3 boxes + 1 compliment.

Assumptions: All the unit's language has been input (P1–P4) — today it's assembled into a genre. and is intuitively familiar, but and the comma are new; writing longer than 4 sentences — for the first time: frames are a must. Handwriting is slow — we keep the volume to 5–6 sentences.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“I don't know what to write”A dish bank by brainstorm BEFORE writing + a notes template with 4 axes; choosing from a ready-made list takes away the block.
They write and between contrastsA gesture anchor: and = palms together (alike), but = palms apart (different); the poster stays up till the end of the pair.
They get tired on portion twoBetween portions — a 60-sec physical warm-up (swap seats); portion 2 is shorter than portion 1.
Peer review hurts feelingsThe “be kind, be exact” rule: a tick/underline + ONE compliment is mandatory.
Quick review turns into pre-test panicA peer-help format: pairs settle disputed questions, then the key; “the test = this page on paper”.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
