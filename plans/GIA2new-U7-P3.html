🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): With new students the grammar is named openly: the topic of the lesson is written on the board, and that takes the anxiety away. The discovery starts from the text they already read in P2 (Thomas woke up, saw, had), then we word the rule together and put it up as a poster. The key distinction: the past lives EITHER in the verb OR in didn't. Everything is written before it is spoken, and a self-checked mini-test closes the lesson.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today's topic is on the board: the past simple of irregular verbs. Look at the story we read: Thomas WOKE UP. He SAW houses under water. He RANG his friends. Not one of those verbs takes -ed — they change completely. There are eight of them in this unit and by the end of today you'll have all eight. And one rule that saves you every time: after DIDN'T the verb goes back to normal. Let's build the table together.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the topic is announced openly: the past simple of irregular verbs. We discover it in the familiar P2 text, build the table together and put up the poster. The main rule: after didn't the verb goes back to the infinitive.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2 on the familiar text: which verbs took -ed and which broke

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WB Unit 7 · the poster of eight verbs stays on the wall to the end of the unit
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can give the past form of eight irregular verbs (began · came · gave · rang · saw · spent · took · woke up) and build negatives with didn't + infinitive.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 find the irregular ones; ex.2 the table; ex.3 eight forms checked against pp.126–127; ex.4 four sentences; ex.5 turning the text round; ex.6 about yourself; ex.8 Emma Watson; ex.9 Brain teaser; the memory techniques from the TB.
  • Personal. The poster with the eight verbs goes up at the start and stays to the end of the unit. The switch gesture (didn't ↔ clean verb) is repeated instead of a spoken correction.
By the end, students can…
  • say the past form of eight irregular verbs
  • tell a regular verb from an irregular one
  • make a negative: didn't + infinitive
  • write six sentences about yesterday

Target language
Lexis
begin/begancome/camegive/gavering/rangsee/sawspend/spenttake/tookwake up/woke up
Structure
Thomas woke up early. · He didn't wake up late. · He saw houses under water. · They didn't have electricity. · I didn't spend two hours doing homework.
Form
affirmative — the past form of the verb (woke up, saw, had). Negative — didn't + infinitive (didn't wake up, didn't see, didn't have). The past lives EITHER in the verb OR in didn't — never in both.
Pron. watch
rhyme chains: began–ran · came–became · rang–sang · took–shook · spent–went. Three choral repetitions each — that is how the form gets into the muscles.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He didn't woke up” — what is wrong? → after didn't comes the infinitive: didn't wake up.
“She goed” — correct it. → went.
“decided” — does it break? → no, ordinary -ed.

Assumptions: SB p.88, syllabus pair 56. The past simple of regular verbs is familiar; was/were came in Unit 6. The irregular forms are recognised but not produced — today is when we set them.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The eight forms don't stayPoster on the wall + choral repetition in rhymes three times each + Quiz-Quiz-Trade cards. The form settles through the mouth, not the eye.
didn't + past form (“didn't saw”)The switch gesture: one hand on didn't — “the past is already here”, the other opens — “the verb is clean”. I repeat the gesture all lesson.
The list on pp.126–127 frightens them by its sizeI use the TB technique: today we work with eight only, and six of them are in the most-frequent list. The rest comes later.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
