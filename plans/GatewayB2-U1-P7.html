Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. Fairly measure each pupil's mastery of Unit 1 across all skills — B2 Unit 1 Test, 100 points (Listening · Vocabulary · Grammar · Use of English · Reading · Writing · Speaking) under standardised, low-stress conditions.
  • Subsidiary. Assess the target language of the unit — school & higher-education lexis, do/make collocations, present tenses & state verbs, present perfect simple vs continuous, gerunds/infinitives — and exam sub-skills (listening for gist/detail, cloze, timed reading, an informal email 140–190 words, spoken interaction with reasons).
  • Personal. Keep it calm and validity-first: model each task type with my own examples (never the test items), give ICQs, protect timing, and frame the test as a check-point they are ready for — not a threat.
By the end, pupils will have…
  • completed the written paper (Listening → Vocab & Grammar → Use of English → Reading → Writing);
  • done the paired Speaking task on video for the /10;
  • a reliable per-skill score that tells them and me exactly what to consolidate before Unit 2.

Settle & framing
seats, front

Calm start. Desks spaced, one pupil per desk. Today is the Unit 1 check-point. This is exactly the language and the skills we've been doing for three weeks — nothing new. You're ready.

Self-efficacy. Frame it low-stakes and specific: You get a score for each skill — listening, vocabulary, grammar, reading, writing, speaking — so you'll see your strengths and the one or two things to polish before Unit 2. It's information, not a verdict.

Board the plan & the START / FINISH times. Phones away / face-down at the front.

6–13′
Model-first walkthrough
board

One model per task type — my own examples, not the test. Fast board demo so the format is never the obstacle:

Word-box gap-fill: “Please ___ your essay by Friday.” → hand in. Tip: read the whole box first, use each word once, cross it off. do/make: do the shopping · make a decision. Definitions match: read all options before you commit.

Grammar: state verbs / present — “Water boils at 100°C. Look — he's running.”; always + continuous for annoyance — “He's always losing his keys!” Correct-the-mistake: “I have went → I have gone.” — tick the right ones, fix the wrong ones. Present perfect continuous: “How long have you been waiting? — Since 3 p.m.” Gerund/infinitive: “I enjoy reading; I decided to go.”

Use of English cloze: read the whole text once before choosing. Listening & Reading: read the questions first, underline key words. Email: board a skeleton — Hi (name), / thanks + your news / a question / advice / arrange to meet / See you soon, (name) — informal register, 140–190 words. Speaking model: “I'd rather study in a big city — there are more opportunities and I love the energy.” (answer + reason + personal detail).

16–82′
Speaking corner — in parallel
far corner, camera

Runs alongside the written paper. From the moment the listening ends, call pairs one at a time to the corner while the rest write. Camera on a tripod for the record.

① Publisher's Speaking task (Ex. 15, /10): Student A & B ask/answer the printed preference & would-you-rather questions (free time, exams vs continuous assessment, home vs student accommodation, university vs job…). Give a reason and a personal detail every time. Recorded for the mark.

② S&S oral pair check (1:1, parallel): straight after, a quick oral run of unit chunks — teacher/partner says the item, pupil renders it the other way, English ↔ Russian, 1:1: continuous assessment ↔ накопительная оценка · to hand in coursework ↔ сдать курсовую работу · postgraduate ↔ аспирант · to make an appointment ↔ записаться на приём. Fast, oral, reinforces the speaking score and confirms the lexis lives in production.

Pair back to their desks silently; tap the next pair. Rotate until all pairs are recorded.
