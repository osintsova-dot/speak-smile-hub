Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /e/ sound spelt e and ea (Seven heavy Mexican elephants… on a feather bed), talk about what England is famous for, and read & plan a travel brochure — three mini-blocks, one “around the world” thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that e and ea can both spell /e/ (recognise → say → sort); culture — What's England famous for? (Wimbledon · rugby · afternoon tea · double-decker buses · Hamleys) + Channel 4 clip, 21C digital skills: getting information from different sources; literacy — text type a travel brochure, rhetorical questions (“Are you ready for an adventure?”), read Italy & plan a brochure.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (chant/action bridges); the Seven-heavy-elephants tongue-twister is play; cap each block hard so the brochure actually gets planned.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the /e/ twister and sort e / ea words;
  • name 2–3 things England is famous for and compare with ours;
  • find the parts of a travel brochure and plan one with a partner.

Structure
Seven heavy Mexican elephants bounce on a feather bed.  ·  England is famous for (its royal family).  ·  brochure: Come and have fun in… Are you ready for an adventure?
Form
/e/ = e (seven, elephant, bed, Mexican, egg) AND ea (heavy, feather, weather, bread, sweater, head). Brochure layout: place name · climate · food · sights · activities · rhetorical question to persuade.
Pron. watch
/e/ clean & SHORT in seven/heavy/feather/bed (not /iː/); Wimbledon /ˈwɪmbldən/; Hamleys /ˈhæmliz/; brochure /ˈbrəʊʃə/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“bed and bread — same /e/ sound? Same letters?” → same sound, different spelling (e vs ea)!
“A travel brochure — does it try to make you WANT to go?” → yes — it persuades (Come and have fun!).

Assumptions: Countries, holiday activities & present perfect are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from earlier GMF4 phonics is enough to enjoy the e / ea noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short brochure texts with support. NB: ea also spells /iː/ (sea) — today we notice only the /e/ words.

7–23′
Block 1 · Phonics /e/ (e · ea)
carpet → desks

Speak and spell (act. 1, CD4 23). PB p.97: Seven heavy, Mexican elephants bounce on a feather bed. Remember! fish · elephant. Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort the /e/ spellings. Hoops/columns: e (seven, bed, elephant, egg) · ea (heavy, feather, weather, bread, sweater). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card.

Notice the rule. Bed and bread — same short sound /e/, different letters! e is one letter; ea is two letters for the same sound. (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more /e/ word to a hoop (leg, head).

✔ Assess: who still reads ea as /iː/? Note them — extra RallyRead next time.

39–52′
Block 3 · Literacy · a travel brochure
carpet → desks, pairs

Read a brochure (PB p.98, act. 1–2, CD4 25). «Italy»: How will you get there? What can you see and do in Italy? Read/listen; find the climate, food, sights (Rome, Pisa, Venice gondola) & activities (beaches, skiing, hiking).

▶ Brochure scavenger hunt. Race in pairs to find on the PB p.99 «Mexico» brochure: What's the climate like? What food can you eat? What can you do in Mexico? First hands up read the answer aloud — everyone scans.

Writing tip (PB p.99). A brochure asks rhetorical questions to make you want to go — “Are you ready for an adventure?” “What are you waiting for?” We use “for” for a period of time. Chorus two rhetorical questions with big expression.

▶ Plan a brochure (Cooperative learning, AB p.96 format). Pairs choose a country & make notes: Country · Climate · Food & drink · Main cities · What you can do · What you can see · a rhetorical question. 2 pairs share one line aloud.

💪 Support: fill the plan template with word/phrase support. ⭐ Extension: write two full brochure sentences with a rhetorical question.
