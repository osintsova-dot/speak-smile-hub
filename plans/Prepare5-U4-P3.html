🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Describe a building with adjectives of age and opinion (historic, brand new, traditional, original, cosy, spectacular, narrow, stylish) and compare buildings with comparatives, superlatives and (not) as … as: the narrowest house in the world, less traditional than I thought, not as big as ours.
What is genuinely hard
The adjectives split into two families that Russian does not split — AGE is factual (historic, modern, brand new), OPINION is not (spectacular, cosy, unusual) — and the exam task on the same page is decided by exactly that difference. The grammar looks familiar and is not: the spelling rules (bigger, cosier, thinner) are where the marks actually go, and (not) as … as is the one form nobody produces spontaneously.
Typical error to expect
«more taller», «the most best» — one comparison per adjective. Russian marks the comparison twice without sounding wrong, so this survives into B2 unless it is named.
«He is best guitarist in our school» — a superlative takes the. Russian has no article at all, so the loss is invisible to the student and completely visible to the examiner.
Why the lesson is built this way
Three photographs of real houses and three descriptions that do not name them: the class matches, and in matching has to notice which words are facts and which are judgements. That sorting IS exercise 2. The reading is a genuine exam task — five people with five sets of requirements and eight homes, including the narrowest house on earth (122 cm wide) and a house built inside a giant skate pipe — and it is done the exam way: underline what each person NEEDS first, then hunt. The grammar comes out of the same eight texts, which are full of comparatives already, so exercise 1 is a hunt rather than a rule.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One of these houses is a hundred and twenty-two centimetres wide. Another one is a skateboard ramp you can sleep in. One is a lighthouse. Today: the adjectives that describe a building, and the grammar that lets you say which one is the strangest.”

WHAT WE DO: About you + ex.1–2: match, then sort into the two columns

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. About you in pairs, 90 seconds: What is your favourite building? Do you prefer old or modern buildings? How important is it to have local facilities near your home? — facilities means parks, sports clubs, leisure centres, cinemas, libraries, shops
  2. ex.1: three descriptions, three photographs. Pairs, two minutes. The rule that makes it work: Find the WORD that proves it. Answer 1 a, 2 c, 3 b.
  3. Check by asking for the proof word, not the letter: solar panels and brand new for a, narrow and stylish for b, historic and cosy for c.
  4. ex.2: the fourteen adjectives sorted into AGE and OPINION on the wall. Students come up in threes and write. Answers: AGE — recent, brand new, historic, modern, traditional, original; OPINION — unusual, fresh, spectacular, classic, cosy.
  5. Drill the two that are always mangled: spectacular (four syllables) and traditional (stress on -DI-). Chorally, twice.
📺 On screen: the wall filling with fourteen adjectives, written by students — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: match with proof words, then physically build the two columns

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that spends all fourteen adjectives

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands. You call an adjective; the class steps LEFT for age and RIGHT for opinion. No talking, no hesitating.
  2. Fourteen adjectives at speed, then the harder round: you say a sentence (It was built in 1780) and they call the adjective (historic).
  3. Last thirty seconds, in pairs: One adjective for the building you are standing in.
  4. Freeze and sit: Page forty-seven. Eight homes, five people, and one of them wants to skateboard indoors.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 = B1 Reading Part 2: five people, eight texts, three not used

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

class → individual

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3: hunt the forms in the text, then build the rules and produce them

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books still at p.47 for ninety seconds. The hunt: Find three comparatives and two superlatives in the eight texts. Thirty seconds. Go. They are everywhere — narrowest, smaller, greener, most original.
  2. ex.1: the eleven examples labelled C or S. Fast, by nomination — 1 S, 2 C, 3 C, 4 S, 5 S, 6 S, 7 C, 8 C, 9 C, 10 C, 11 C.
  3. ex.2: the six rules built from those examples. Write the three SPELLING rules separately and box them, because that is where the test marks are: big → bigger (double), cosy → cosier (y → i), long adjectives take more / most.
  4. ex.3 individually, four minutes, eight items. Check on the board in WRITING, not aloud — quieter, happier, bigger, the most popular, the most/least intelligent, further, the best, cheaper.
  5. Grammar reference is SB p.154 for anyone who wants the full table — name it, do not read it.
📺 On screen: the three spelling rules boxed on the board, left up for the rest of the block — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: hunt forms in a text they have just read, then produce eight of them in writing
📓 Interactive notebook — 10_U8_Grammar_Comparatives_Superlatives_A5
A5 grammar card — COMPARATIVES + SUPERLATIVES in three levels: COMPARE TWO (small → smaller than · cosy → cosier than · modern → more modern than · less expensive than · good → better · far → farther/further) · THE EXTREME IN A GROUP (the smallest · the cosiest · the most modern · the least expensive · the best · the farthest) · EQUAL / NOT EQUAL (as + adjective + as · not as + adjective + as), with a RULE CORNER (one syllable -er/-est · consonant-vowel-consonant doubles · -y → -ier/-iest · long adjectives more/most) and the warning NOT «more nicer»
  • Glue the card in and colour the three levels.
  • Copy the rule corner into the margin — the spelling is what fails.
  • Compare two buildings you know, then name the extreme one in your town.
  • Say the same idea twice: X is smaller than Y / Y is not as small as X.

Structure
The Keret House is the narrowest house in the world. · It isn't as big as our flat. · The design was less traditional than I thought. · Homes with sea views are the most expensive. · Our old house was further from the shops.
Form
-er / -est for short adjectives (with doubling and y → i) · more / most and less / least for longer ones · irregular good → better → the best, far → further · (not) as + adjective + as for equality.
Pron. watch
spectacular /spekˈtækjələ/ — four syllables, not five, and the class will insert a vowel after the k · traditional stresses the -DI- · ancient /ˈeɪnʃənt/ · exhausted /ɪɡˈzɔːstɪd/ with a /ɡz/, which is the hardest sound in the unit · and the comparative -er ending is weak: /ˈnærəʊə/, never /nærəʊˈer/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«historic» — is that a fact or my opinion? → a fact, it is about age. «spectacular»? → my opinion. Two columns, no middle.
«more bigger» — what is wrong? → the comparison is marked twice. Once only, always.
«He lives in ___ oldest house in the street.» What is missing? → the. A superlative always takes it.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Age and opinion adjectives get mixedThe wall has two columns and NO middle column, deliberately. Every new adjective is placed by a student who says which question it answers: how old? or what do I think?
The matching task is done by topicForce the exam method: underline the FIVE requirements each person has before opening the texts. A text that matches two out of three is the trap, and it is in every one of these tasks.
Spelling collapses in the comparativesThree spelling rules on the board with one example each (big → bigger, cosy → cosier, narrow → narrower), and every disputed answer is written on the board rather than said aloud.
Fast finishers in the readingThe Teacher's Book task: write a description of a sixth person to match one of the unused texts (C, F or H), then swap and solve.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 10–18 (matching people to texts is the shape of the first reading task) and tasks 19–25 (comparative and superlative forms).

Reading Part 2 gives five people with five sets of requirements and eight texts, three of which are traps built to match on one point out of three. The Russian heading-matching task is decided by the same discipline: underline what the person actually needs, then reject any text that satisfies only part of it. The comparatives come back in the grammar cloze, where the two reliable losses are the double comparative and the missing the in front of a superlative.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
