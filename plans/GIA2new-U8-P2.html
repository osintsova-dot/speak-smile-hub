🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The text is long, so we do not read it whole and we do not read it at once: first six new words, then a prediction from the photos, then paragraph by paragraph with a check. The subskill of the unit (fact or opinion) is given as one simple test — “can you check it?” — and is practised on their own sentences first and only then on the text. The product is safe and personal: my opinion about a VR holiday from a frame, 6 lines.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Imagine a holiday with no passport, no plane and no sun cream. You put on glasses and you're on Everest. Or under a waterfall. Or swimming with dolphins. Four hundred dollars, and it never rains. Sounds perfect? The blog says it's amazing. But today we learn one very useful skill: telling FACTS from OPINIONS. And at the end each of you tells us — would YOU go on a Virtual Reality holiday? Six lines, with your notes. Glasses on.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: a holiday with no passport and no plane: put the glasses on and you are on Everest. The blog says it is wonderful. Today we learn to tell facts from opinions, and at the end everyone says whether they would go on a VR holiday. Six lines, with notes in hand.

Words BEFORE the text · Word work
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: six new words with pictures and gestures BEFORE reading

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six words one at a time, with a picture and a gesture: parachute — jump! · waterfall — water comes down! · nightmare — a very bad situation! · passport — you need it at the airport · book — reserve a table · climb — up the mountain.
  2. The pronunciation traps straight away: climb /klaɪm/ — the b is silent; nightmare /ˈnaɪtˌmeə(r)/.
  3. ex.7 as a check, not as a discovery (TB key: 1 parachute · 2 waterfall · 3 nightmare · 4 passport · 5 book · 6 climb).
  4. ex.8 in pairs (TB key: 1 climb · 2 book · 3 passport · 4 waterfall · 5 parachute, nightmare).
  5. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with cards: word — definition.
📺 On screen: cards with the six words and pictures · word bank on screen (Backup: the six words large on the board + a rough drawing of a parachute, a waterfall and a mountain; do not rub the word bank off before the end of the lesson)
👦 Students do: get the words in advance — the text stops being frightening. Expect: book meaning “reserve” surprises them, explain it with the restaurant example

Predicting from photos and facts
pairs

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 three Holiday facts + ex.2 predicting from the photos → ex.3 checking against the first paragraph

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 read the three facts together: Disney World — over 20 million visitors a year; the most expensive room in Geneva — about $80,000 a night; the first space tourist paid $20 million.
  2. ex.2 in pairs (TB sample answers): What can you see in the photos? What connects them? (a person in VR glasses and a view from behind a waterfall; the second photo is what he SEES).
  3. Run ex.2 as Kagan Think-Pair-Share and say all three steps out loud: first YOUR model — I can see glasses. I think he's not really there. Then THINK: half a minute in silence looking at the photo and writing one word in the notebook (Russian is fine). PAIR: turn to your neighbour, the one on the left speaks first, the second adds their own. SHARE: the pair gives the class one joint answer — either of the two may speak.
  4. ex.3: read ONLY the introduction and the first paragraph and check your guess. Just two paragraphs. Nothing more yet.
📺 On screen: SB pp.98–99 · slide with the three facts
👦 Students do: predict and check in small steps. Expect: the guess about the glasses turns out right, and that gives them confidence

Reading the blog in chunks
crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 (track 49) the summary → ex.5 five true/false statements with the paragraph named

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 read and listen at the same time (track 49), then choose the summary (TB key: b — Virtual Reality will change how people buy and experience travel). Talk through out loud why the other two do not fit.
  2. ex.5 one statement at a time, and you name the paragraph (TB routine): Number one — look at the introduction. Number two — paragraph two.
  3. TB key: 1 True · 2 False (the virtual hotel tour is needed BEFORE the trip) · 3 False (you can swim with dolphins, but the person is not in the water) · 4 False (in the same room the experience is different) · 5 True (about the 80-year-old grandmother).
  4. The rule: Show me the line with your finger. Then say the answer.
  5. Do the first statement together — you find the line yourself and read it out loud so they can see how it is done. The other four go through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: the crew looks for the line together, all four put a finger on the same line and check each other that the finger is in the same place. Then you call a number — Number twos: True or False, and read me the line. The crew knows in advance that anyone may answer, so they show the line to everyone.
📺 On screen: SB pp.98–99 · audio track 49 (Backup: read the blog text out loud paragraph by paragraph while students follow with a finger)
👦 Students do: read in chunks and point at the lines. Expect: items 2–4 are hard — talk each one through out loud, without rushing

Fact or opinion: step by step
Fact or opinion · step by step
solo → crews

WHAT WE DO: the rule as one test → five sentences of their own → ex.6 on the text

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The rule on the screen in one line: Can you CHECK it? Then it's a FACT. If it's just someone's idea — it's an OPINION.
  2. The opinion signals as a list: amazing · fantastic · terrible · I think · I'm sure · In my opinion.
  3. Practice on their own world: Our school has three floors. (fact) · Our school is beautiful. (opinion) · It's minus 30 in January. (fact) · Winter is terrible. (opinion) · I'm sure summer will be hot. (opinion — 'I'm sure')
  4. ex.6 on the text (TB key: Fact — 2, 3, 5, 6 · Opinion — 1, 4, 7). Take item 7 separately and slowly: 'VR glasses will get cheaper.' Can we check the future? No. And he says 'I'm sure'. So — opinion.
📺 On screen: slide with the rule and the signals (Backup: the rule and the six signals on the board — do not rub them off before the end of the lesson)
👦 Students do: practise on the obvious first, then on the text. Expect: item 7 confuses many — that is exactly why it is taken last and in detail

WHAT WE DO: a stretch

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up. Thumbs up if you'd try it in VR, thumbs down if never. Climbing Everest! A parachute jump! Swimming with dolphins!
  2. This is Kagan Take Off–Touch Down. Model it yourself the first time: you say climbing Everest — you stand up, which means “I would try it”. PRODUCT · my opinion about VR
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: product: 6 lines “would I go on a VR holiday” from the frame; the speaker holds notes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame on the screen: I think VR holidays are … . One good thing is … . One problem is … . I'd like to try … . I wouldn't like … . So I would / wouldn't go on a VR holiday.
  2. Support — the Critical thinking section of the book (TB sample answers): pluses — cheaper, no passport needed, open to people who cannot travel; minuses — you are not really there, no tastes and smells (you can't eat the pizza), you don't meet real people, you have to pay for the glasses.
  3. Write for 6 minutes, then read it in pairs, then the crew comes out with notes.
  4. A class vote (as the TB asks): Hands up: who would go on a Virtual Reality holiday?
  5. Praise something specific: Good reason! Clear opinion!
📺 On screen: the frame as support · timer · students' notes
👦 Students do: speak with notes, the crew beside them. Expect: the “you can't eat the pizza” argument turns up in every second piece of work — and that is excellent

WB Unit 8 · the cards with the six words stay in the classroom
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can tell a fact from an opinion (and point at the signal word), use 6 new words, and give their own opinion about VR holidays in 6 lines.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 three Holiday facts; ex.2–3 predicting from the photos and checking; ex.4 (track 49) choosing the summary; ex.5 true/false; ex.6 fact or opinion; Word work ex.7–8; Critical thinking 1, 3 and 4 in a simplified form.
  • Personal. Give the words BEFORE the text, not after. Look for every answer together and name the paragraph (the TB routine for less confident classes). The mini-test is self-checked — the mistake is found privately.
By the end, students can…
  • say if a sentence is a fact or an opinion
  • find the signal word that shows an opinion
  • use six new words (parachute, waterfall, nightmare, passport, book, climb)
  • give my opinion about VR holidays in 6 sentences

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“The hotel room costs $80,000 a night” — can you check it? → yes, so it is a fact.
“Camping is terrible” — can you check it? → no, it is somebody's opinion (the word terrible).
“book” here — a noun or a verb? → a verb: to reserve.

Assumptions: SB pp.98–99, syllabus pair 63. The text is long — we read it in chunks with support. All 6 Word work items are in the official Wordlist.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The length of the text frightens themRead it paragraph by paragraph with a task for each; after every paragraph one question, not five.
They can't find the answer in the textTB routine: give the number of the paragraph to look in. Plus the “finger on the line” rule — the answer is pointed at.
Fact and opinion get mixed upOne test instead of a theory: “can you check it?” On their own five sentences first, then on the text.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
