🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): For students new to us the mission is simple and about themselves: put your own weekend together in English and compare it with American teenagers. That gives a safe product (I talk about myself, from a frame, with the sheet in my hand) and at the same time feeds in the 18 words of the set. The statistics on p.24 are not a task but an excuse: the numbers are clear without translation and start an argument straight away.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at these numbers. American teenagers spend 4.3 hours a day on media, 3.8 hours on hobbies and almost 11 hours sleeping. Really? Today two things: we learn to name every free-time activity in English — and each of you writes your own weekend, six lines. At the end you present with your notes in your hand. This is not a test. Let's start with MY weekend…” (and I show the model straight away)
In simpler words, if the class needs support: we look at the American numbers, learn to name free-time activities in English and build our own weekend — six lines. At the end we present, sheet in hand.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 18 free-time activities and describe their own weekend in 6 lines with hours, comparing one thing with US teenagers.
  • Subsidiary. The four-column table (ex.2–3) with a prompt sheet; ex.4, the text about the twins, done from a model; Video skills (Ryan's vlog) with a task set before they watch.
  • Personal. I never put anybody on the spot without a draft. Every speaking task comes AFTER it has been written down. The word bank stays on screen the whole lesson.
By the end, students can…
  • say and write 18 free-time activities
  • sort them into four groups
  • describe my weekend in 6 sentences with hours
  • compare one thing with US teenagers

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“take photos or make photos?” → take photos, and nothing else.
“play chess or play the chess?” → no article: play chess.
“go to gym or go to the gym?” → the is needed: go to the gym.

Assumptions: SB pp.24–25, KTP pair 13. Some of the words are familiar (play football, listen to music); new — collect things, practise the piano, play in a band/team, use social media.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Afraid to speak in front of people they don't knowRule of the pair: first we write, then we read it to a partner, and we come to the board with the sheet in our hands.
18 words at once will not fitI feed them in four portions, by the columns of the table, with a gesture for each; recognition first (point at it), naming second.
They ignore the word bank and go quietThe word bank stays on screen the whole lesson; a “look up” gesture instead of feeding them the word.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
