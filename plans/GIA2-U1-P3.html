A2 teens (~12–14, continuing line) · 90 min · Grammar — guided discovery → controlled → freer production · pairs + teams
Week 21.09–27.09 · Pair 7Grammar 1~10–12 pupils · pairs / teams of 3–4SB p.16 · WB p.5
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to make affirmative and negative present-simple statements about routines, using the correct 3rd-person form and spelling (-s / -es / -ies, have → has) — e.g. Jack wakes up at seven. Abi doesn't see her teachers.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle daily-routine & school-subject lexis in context; guided discovery of the rule from a text they know; delayed error correction; fluency in a personalised mingle.
  • Personal. Keep TTT low, elicit before you tell; give one clear ICQ per task; run the room during controlled practice and log errors for a delayed board.
By the end, pupils can…
  • complete the affirmative & negative present-simple tables and explain the rule;
  • spell 3rd-person verbs (studies, watches, goes, has) correctly;
  • write & say 5+ true present-simple sentences about their own school day.
Target language
Structure
Affirmative: I/You/We/They + base · He/She/It + verb-s.  ·  Negative: I/you/we/they don't + base · he/she/it doesn't + base.
Model
I get up at eight. · Jack wakes up at seven. · They don't go to school. They stay at home. · Abi doesn't see her teachers.
Form / spelling
3rd person: most +s; after -s,-sh,-ss,-ch,-x,-o +es (goes, watches); consonant+y → -ies (study→studies); irregular have→has. After doesn't the verb is base (doesn't already carries the -s).
Lexis (recycled)
finishget upgohavemakeplaystudywatch + daily-routine & school-subject sets
Pron. watch
3rd-person ending: /s/ gets · /z/ plays · /ɪz/ watches, finishes. doesn't /ˈdʌznt/, don't /dəʊnt/ — weak, one beat.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Jack wakes up at seven — is it me and you, or he?” → he → verb + s.
“Abi doesn't see her teachers — does she see them?” → No → negative.
“After doesn't — do we write sees or see?” → see (base).
“Every day, or right now?” → every day (a routine/habit).

Assumptions: learners met these routines in P1–P2 (SB pp.12–15) and can read the Jack/Abi article; they know be present simple from Starter; they are teens — adult tone, no mascot. Groups: pairs for RallyCoach; teams of 3–4 for Showdown. Sizes 10=2+2+3+3 · 12=3×4 · 11=3+4+4.

Classroom management — signals
“3–2–1, eyes on me” — countdown to full attention & silence.
Silent hand-up — you raise a hand → they finish the sentence, hands up, quiet.
Noise level 0–3 — name the level before each task (0 silent · 1 pair-whisper · 2 group · 3 share-out).
“Pens down, look up” — reset before instructions.
“30 seconds” — timed-task warning to keep pace.
“Swap” — role change in pairs (RallyCoach solver ↔ coach).

Activate. On the board: My perfect school day… Learners jot 3 things they do every school day (from P1–P2 routines). ▶ RallyRobin in pairs — take turns, one action each: I get up at seven. I catch the bus. I have lunch at one.

20–33′
⚡ Base sprint · tables + flips
seats

Clarify form (I Do → We Do). Build a colour-coded box: he/she/it row in one colour with a big +S. Motto for negatives: “doesn't” already carries the -s, so the next verb is naked — base form. Contrast: He doesn't wakes up ✗ → He doesn't wake up ✓.

Spelling sketch — think-aloud (SB p.21 Spelling rules open). Sort the ex.3 box (finish · get up · go · have · make · play · study · watch) into three buckets aloud: watch ends in -ch, so… watches. Study ends in consonant + y, so drop y, add… studies. Have is the odd one — has. Learners call the ending before you write it.

📓 Interactive notebook — “Present simple: aff / neg” (A5 grammar card). Hand out / stick the card: two mini-tables (aff: I/you/we/they + base · he/she/it + verb-s; neg: don't / doesn't + base) and the SPELLING SKETCH (+s · +es after -s,-sh,-ss,-ch,-x,-o · consonant+y → -ies · have→has) with the warning doesn't already carries the -s → base verb next. Learners complete the three buckets with their own example verb in each.

Controlled practice · near→varied
seats, pairs

Warm start (near transfer) — SB p.16 ex.2. Choose the correct option (don't go/doesn't go…). Do #1 together as the model, then ▶ RallyCoach: A solves aloud, B coaches & ticks; Swap each item. Whole-class check with fingers (a=1, b=2).

Step up — SB p.16 ex.4 (complete with the present simple, brackets, mixed aff/neg & 3rd person). Noise level 1, pairs continue RallyCoach. ICQ: After “doesn't” — base or -s? (→ base).

Live monitoring. Circulate; don't correct on the spot — log 4–5 real errors on your sheet for the delayed board later. If 80%+ correct on ex.2 → move on; if not, re-model one line.

Make it true — SB p.16 ex.5. Learners rewrite the six sentences so they are true for them: I don't go to an online school. I go to a state school. Write 5; monitor for form.

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (Kagan mingle). Each learner turns one true sentence into a question on a card (What time do you finish school?). Stand up, find a partner, quiz, quiz, trade cards. 3–4 partners. 70–80′
Production · two truths, one lie
teams of 3–4

▶ Two truths & a lie — routines. Each learner writes 3 present-simple sentences about their week (aff & neg), one of them false: My brother plays the guitar. · I don't do homework on Fridays. · My dad makes breakfast. Read to the team; team asks a follow-up and votes on the lie.

Push accuracy: the team may only accept a sentence if the 3rd-person -s and don't/doesn't are correct — Grammar police! Is it “plays” or “play”? Winner = spots most lies with correct grammar.
