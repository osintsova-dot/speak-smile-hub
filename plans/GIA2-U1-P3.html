🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): This is the grammar pair, but the topic is NOT 'the Present simple' — the topic is 'the Fact-Check Bureau': a file about a real person's day, where one error of form = the file goes in the bin. That way -s/-es/-ies, doesn't and word order work under time pressure rather than in an exercise. The Hogwarts text from the Round-up is the Bureau's final check.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Welcome to the Bureau. We check facts about people's days. One rule: a fact-file with ONE grammar error goes in the bin — the court won't accept it. Today you get your Bureau licence: write fast, write clean. Third person is our weapon: he wakes, she studies, it starts. Licence test in three minutes.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We are a fact-checking bureau: we build a file about someone else's day, and a file with one error of form is not accepted. The licence = fast clean writing in the 3rd person.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can write 6+ clean 3rd-person sentences about a real person's day at speed, applying -s/-es/-ies spelling and doesn't + bare verb automatically.
  • Subsidiary. Both Present simple tables closed as a sprint; the -s/-es/-ies spelling sorter; an error clinic on Pairs 1–2 (collected silently); the Grammar round-up text.
  • Personal. The first grammar pair of the track: I do NOT re-explain the rule — the students build the table out of the examples themselves (guided discovery) and I only validate it. Error correction is anonymous.
By the end, students can…
  • complete both present simple tables from examples
  • sort verbs into -s / -es / -ies at speed
  • write a 6-line zero-error fact-file in the 3rd person
  • spot and fix a 3rd-person error in 5 seconds

Structure
I/You/We/They get up at eight. · He/She/It gets up at seven. · I/You/We/They don't go to school. · He/She/It doesn't see her teachers.
Form
most verbs: + -s (read → reads, wake up → wakes up) · after -s, -sh, -ss, -ch, -x, -o: + -es (go → goes, teach → teaches, mix → mixes) · consonant + y: y → -ies (study → studies) · exception: have → has
Pron. watch
the weak /z/ in goes /ɡəʊz/ · studies /ˈstʌdiz/ — not spelled out letter by letter
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'He don't like maths' — what's wrong? → after he/she/it it is always doesn't.
'She studys' — is that right? → no: consonant + y → studies.
After doesn't, does the verb take -s? → no: doesn't see, not doesn't sees.

Assumptions: SB pp.14–15, KTP pair 7 (both halves are grammar). The material is familiar from A1+ — the load is in the speed and the writing, not in the understanding.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
'The Present simple again, we know it'I don't argue: the writing-speed gate shows their real ceiling in 60 seconds. After that it is the Bureau that drives them, not the rule.
studys / gos when they rushThe -s/-es/-ies sorter as a separate station + a 'spelling' flag with the judges.
The class gets tired of writingI alternate: writing → a flip on the mini-boards → an oral duel → writing.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
