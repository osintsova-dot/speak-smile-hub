🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Editing drags on — hold the timeboxes hard: the «minimum»: scene order + trimming + 2 titles. Effects and music — only if time is left. The premiere is sacred: don't touch the last 45 minutes.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Order… cut the start… cut the end… title: ‘Our English Room’. Done. FOUR minutes.” “Effects and music? — Only AFTER titles, if time. Minimum first!”

WHAT WE DO: an editing sprint: the editor cuts against the timeboxes, the team works in parallel on the language check, the teaser, the intro speech

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Timer launch: ORDER — eight minutes, go! Then CUT, TITLES on the whistle.
  2. I go round the editors: I only fix «stuck in a menu»; creative decisions stay with the team.
  3. Language check in parallel: speaker+writer listen to every take through an earphone/quietly — a checklist tick: are the phrases clean? has the title spelling been checked by neighbours?
  4. The director writes the premiere's intro speech (2 sentences: Our guide is about… Enjoy!).
📺 On screen: phones · language checklist · title drafts (swapped for a spelling check)
👦 Students do: Students edit and check language in parallel — every role is busy. Expected: by minute 33, most teams have it assembled. Typical error: the editor dives into effects — the timebox pulls them back.

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Showdown (mini-boards): a language rewind of the year — everyone writes their own answer, the round's captain commands «Showdown!», we reveal at the same time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Showdown: everyone has a mini-board, the team has a round captain (rotating). Question up → EVERYBODY writes, no talking! Captain: ‘SHOWDOWN!’ — boards up together, compare, agree on the best answer.
  2. I mix up questions from the season's and the year's language: Say a guide phrase for a tour! · A connector for facts! · How do you order food? (U3) · Ask about last weekend! (U7)
  3. A clean team volley (all boards correct) = +2; the captain changes every round.
  4. 6–8 rounds, high pace — hands move, everyone works.
📺 On screen: a mini-board for everyone · a list of questions (from the P1 banks)
👦 Students do: EVERYONE writes at the same time and reveals on the captain's command — nobody hides behind a strong classmate. Expected: excitement builds towards the end of the pair. Typical error: copying before the showdown — «no talking till the call!»

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, every team has a finished 1.5–2 min video guide (scenes in order + titles in English) and it has premiered to the class with peer review against the axes.
  • Subsidiary. Minimal phone editing (trimming, order, titles) done by the editor with the team's language control; a premiere judged on Layout/Content/Language/Visuals and voted on; nominations for video day.
  • Personal. Editing drags on — hold the timeboxes hard: the «minimum»: scene order + trimming + 2 titles. Effects and music — only if time is left. The premiere is sacred: don't touch the last 45 minutes.
By the end, students can…
  • cut and order scenes in a phone editor
  • write English titles (title + credits)
  • run a language check on their own video
  • review a film with the four axes

Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Is editing all about special effects?» → No: order + trimming + titles. A minimum that still watches well.
«Can the titles be in Russian?» → Titles are in English: that's part of the Language axis.
«Can I vote for my own team?» → No — a token only goes to someone else's video.

Assumptions: The footage was shot in P4 (+homework re-shoots); editors have any standard phone editor (iMovie/CapCut/built-in — doesn't matter, the operations are the same). No Wi-Fi needed: everything is local, playback from a phone via cable/AirPlay, or simply passed round the rows if a projector isn't available.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The editor drowns in the editor for 40 minutesTimeboxes on the board: order 8′ → cut 8′ → titles 6′. The timer is visible; no time for effects — they weren't needed anyway.
The rest of the team is bored during editingParallel roles: speaker+writer do a language check against the checklist and prepare a 15-sec «trailer announcement» in words; the director prepares the premiere's intro speech.
The video won't open/is missingA check at the start of the lesson: does everyone have their footage? Fallback plan — a «live guide» premiere: the team runs the guide live round the classroom (the show is saved).
Titles with spelling errorsWe read the titles out loud + a neighbouring team's spelling check BEFORE inserting them (swap title drafts).
Hurtful comments at the premiereReview only against the axes and only in the «favourite part + next time try» formula; laughing AT — a stop signal, laughing WITH (bloopers) — that's fine.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
