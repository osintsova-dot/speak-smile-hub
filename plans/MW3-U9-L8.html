Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, children will have made a bug and added it to a class “Our bugs” diorama — then described the diorama together: “I like the butterfly! It's got wings. It's got spots. Do you like the snail? — Yes, I do!” — a joyful, collaborative end to the unit AND the whole course.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle all six bugs, the four features & Has it got…? / It's got… / Do you like…? through a creative product; sing Genki «Creepy Crawlies» (73) & «We want to get some honey» (CD4 T23); take turns presenting; Phonics Hero finale — a reading celebration of favourite stories; a happy end-of-course gallery & goodbye.
  • Personal. Project = process & pride, not perfection. Model the making & the presenting sentences first. Keep making calm; keep presenting short (one bug each) so no long waiting. This is the LAST lesson of the year — make the goodbye warm & celebratory: they can name, describe, sing AND read.
By the end, pupils can…
  • make a bug & add it to our class diorama;
  • describe my bug — “It's got wings. It's got spots.”;
  • say “I like the butterfly! Do you like the snail? — Yes, I do!”

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Do we all make ONE bug for the class diorama, or each keep our own?” → each make one, all together = our diorama!
“Your friend shows their bug — what do we say?” → I like it! / Well done!

Assumptions: All Unit 9 language is consolidated (L7 review done). Children can name the six bugs, describe them, ask Has it got…? and read whole stories. They know the making & presenting routines from earlier projects. This is the final lesson — a celebration.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Can't decide which bugShow the bug flashcards / templates laid out; each child points to the one they'll make first.
Making takes over the lessonReady templates + tissue balls / clay / crayons; time-cap with a visible timer + countdown.
Freezes when presentingSay it together with the puppet first; showing the bug + one feature is enough.
Audience not listeningGive a job: everyone says I like it! after each child.
Long waiting to presentAdd bugs to the diorama in fast small groups; describe together, not one-by-one.
Classroom management — signals
“Class? — Yes!” — you: “Class!” → pupils stop & echo “Yes!”
“WiFi” 🖐 — say “WiFi” + raise a spread hand → pupils silently mirror & tune in.
Volume control — raise/lower your arm = louder/quieter → settler.
“Give me five” — eyes · ears · mouth · hands · feet.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1… pencils down.”
“Freeze!” — statue, mid-movement reset.

Make it. Children choose a bug & make it — colour, stick tissue-paper balls, or use clay on the template. Add legs, spots, stripes, wings.

Rehearse as they work. go round: What's it got? What colour is it? help each child rehearse describing their bug quietly at the table.

💪 Support: make & name the bug with the puppet. ⭐ Extension: describe it fully — It's got six legs. It's got spots. It's red and black.
