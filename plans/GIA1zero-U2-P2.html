WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin for revision: a word about the weekend; ping-pong “male→female” + 2 words written (P1 revision); bridge “switching on the TV”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: One word about your weekend!
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we alternate one answer at a time — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!
  3. P1 revision ping-pong with colour gestures: father! — MOTHER! nephew! — NIECE! cousin! — COUSIN!
  4. 2 rounds written: On boards: DAUGHTER! — boards up, spelling matters, +1.
  5. Bridge: You know family words. Now — TV time: a family SO big you won't believe it. Twenty-six people. One house.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · colour gestures from P1
👦 Students do: they ping-pong the word pairs, write the spelling. Expected: daughter is already cleaner. Typical error: “dauther” — a finger on the tree (still on the board/slide).

Bets before reading: believe / don't believe
Hook · prediction bets
screen

WHAT WE DO: the photo from p.27: big or small family? 4 “believe/don't believe” bets written down, face down

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I open the photo p.27 (or the coursebooks): Look — the Putmans. Big family or small? Why unusual? Team guess — thirty seconds!
  2. I put up 4 statements: 26 people live in ONE house · each child has got a bedroom · they've got three cars · dinner is at ONE big table.
  3. Command: Believe or not? Write T or F — face down! We check by READING.
  4. (Key for me: T/F/F/F.)
📺 On screen: photo p.27 · slide of 4 statements · bet slips (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they bet in writing before reading — everybody moves. Expected: “three cars” catches out many. We don't say the bets out loud!

10–18′
Reader's move: predicting before reading
Discover · predicting (subskill)
desks

WHAT WE DO: the move “photos and headlines speak first”: page type (TV guide) by look; first reading with audio 17 — only checking the bets

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I teach the move: Pro readers PREDICT: photos, titles, buttons — first. Reading — second. That's the subskill.
  2. 30 sec WITHOUT reading: What page is it? Tabs? “Add to watchlist”? — a TV GUIDE! +2 for a clue.
  3. First reading with audio 17: Read along. Check ONLY your four bets. Three minutes.
  4. We open the bets in chorus: One house — TRUE! Three cars — FALSE… they've got a BUS!
📺 On screen: SB pp.26–27 · audio 17 (Backup: I read it myself) · bets on the desks
👦 Students do: they identify the page type, read with the bets in mind, open them. Expected: “BUS!” with delight. Typical error: line-by-line translation — “Scan for YOUR bet!”

18–32′
Text quest: a fact hunt
Play · Fact Hunt stations
4 stations

WHAT WE DO: jigsaw: everybody raids THEIR OWN station-paragraph with a hunt card (3 facts), returns and dictates to the team — all 12 are needed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before the lesson — 4 paragraph stations in the corners: №1 family (26·names·“B”) · №2 the house (small·2 bathrooms·share) · №3 transport+school (bus·parents drive) · №4 evenings (homework help·dinner 6·three tables).
  2. I hand out hunt cards (3 short questions per station).
  3. ICQ: Copy sentences? — No! Short answers: bus, six, Montana!
  4. I start it: Four minutes — GO! Return: they dictate to the team, a team sheet of 12 facts; the first complete one +5.
  5. Checking against the key ex.3 (TB): Montana, USA · house · grandparents · four, 16 · small · bus · six.
📺 On screen: 4 stations · hunt cards · team sheets · timer
👦 Students do: they raid their own stations, dictate short answers to the team. Expected: the Amsterdam skill from U1 carries over: words, not sentences. Typical error: they copy out the whole sentence — the model “bus” in one word.
⭐ For the fast ones: a “Fact+” envelope: a number, a name and a “B”-name nobody used.		stations · hunt cards · timer
32–41′
True/false on mini-boards
Check · true/false showdown
desks · mini-boards

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: statements ex.4 → everybody writes T/F at the same time; false ones fixed with a haven't got sentence (a plant for P3)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I put up statement 1 (Bill and Barb have got two daughters).
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everybody writes at the same time, boards face down, on the call No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up together — I see every answer, nobody can hide.
  3. I command: T or F on boards — five seconds — UP!
  4. We fix the false ones in chorus with two moves: They HAVEN'T got two daughters — they've got ONE daughter and three sons!
  5. I go through all 6 (TB key: F·T·F·T·F·F).
  6. The plant: Hear the move? HAVE got — HAVEN'T got. Remember it. Pair 3 — we open this machine.
📺 On screen: slides of statements one by one · mini-boards · key (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: everybody commits to T/F, false ones fixed in chorus with haven't got. Expected: “They haven't got a car — a BUS!”. Typical error: “they no have” — I echo it back through the frame, I don't dig deeper (the system lesson is P3).

Word work round

WHAT WE DO: envelopes: 6 words from the text ↔ 6 definitions at speed; choral echo; a sentence about yourself

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out envelopes: unusual · reality show · share · lonely · together · fun + 6 definitions (ex.5).
  2. I start it: Match! Three minutes!
  3. I check with a choral echo: Sad because you aren't with people? — LONELY! Use with another person? — SHARE!
  4. Ownership: ONE sentence about YOU with any word: I share a room with… / My favourite reality show is… — tell the neighbouring pair.
📺 On screen: Word work envelopes · key on the slide (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they match, echo the answers, own a word about themselves. Expected: “I share a room with my sister”. Typical error: lonely=alone — a CCQ from the reference note.

WHAT WE DO: 3 online comments (ex.8): who is an only child, who is happy and why; “who is like you?” by the frame

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pre-teach (TB): annoying = makes you a little angry. My little brother sings ONE song all day — annoying!
  2. Teams read the 3 comments (90 sec): Who is an only child? Who's happy? Who isn't — and why?

60–71′
Big or small family: pros and cons
Critical thinking · big or small?
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: pros/cons of a big family from the text (Understand) → a small one from their own heads (Apply) → voting on “the ideal size” (Evaluate)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Understand: Two GOOD things + two HARD things about a big family — FROM the text! Three minutes. (help: never lonely · homework help / share a room · two bathrooms · noisy).
  2. Apply: Now a SMALL family — your ideas, the frame helps: You've got your own room. It's quiet.
  3. Evaluate by voting: The ideal number of people in a family? Write YOUR number — boards up! Defend in one frame-sentence.
  4. Frames on the board: A big family is ___ because ___ . / In a small family you've got ___ .
📺 On screen: board: a “good/hard” table + frames · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they pull arguments from the text, add their own, vote a number and defend it. Expected: numbers 3–6, arguments by the frames. Typical error: an argument in Russian — it doesn't make it into the table (the rule was announced).

WHAT WE DO: everybody writes a 3-sentence verdict (big or small family — for me + 2 facts), peer review

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. We write for 5 minutes, timer.
  3. Peer check: Swap. Two boxes: real fact from the text? have got correct? Fix and return. +1 for a clean check.
📺 On screen: verdict frame on the board · slide of 2 checkboxes · team fact sheets (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they write 3 sentences leaning on the facts they found. Expected: “Fact 1: they've got a bus!”. Typical error: an invented “fact from the text” — the checker asks “Which paragraph?”.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can find facts in a TV-guide article and report them with simple have got sentences: “They've got a bus! The children haven't got their own bedroom.”
  • Subsidiary. Reading subskill: predicting what a text is about (bets before reading); Word work — 6 text words; online comments (Eli·Clara·Toby); Critical thinking Understand→Apply→Evaluate with heavy scaffolds.
  • Personal. Never read the page aloud frontally — bets and hunts do the reading. Timer visible in every hunt. Praise fact-finding speed, not translation. Seed haven't got corrections for Pair 3.
By the end, students can…
  • predict text content from photos and check
  • find a named fact within a time limit
  • match 6 new words to definitions
  • say one advantage and one disadvantage of a big family

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Predicting = guessing after reading?” → No — BEFORE. Photos and titles speak first.
“Lonely = alone?” → No: alone = alone (physically), lonely = sad about being alone.
“Share a room = a room of your own?” → No — one room TOGETHER.

Assumptions: 17 family words fresh from P1 (rewind retrieves them). Reading a long authentic page is scary — success = find facts, not understand every word; say it out loud. have got frames from P1 keep working receptively.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
