🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Hear what a short recording is really about rather than which words it contains, and match five speakers to a list with more options than speakers.
What is genuinely hard
Both tasks are built out of the same trap and it is a good one: every wrong option is made of something that was genuinely said. In Part 4 the word that matches is almost always in the wrong answer; in Part 5 there are three options nobody uses, and a half-remembered answer gets forced onto one of them.
Typical error to expect
A word from the option was said in the recording → they tick it. A matching word is not a correct answer; meaning decides [exam report]
In a matching task they forget that one option is never used, so a half-remembered answer gets forced onto the leftover heading [exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
This is the one lesson of the block where the exam format IS the topic, and it is built the way an exam lesson has to be built: strategy before task, task in real conditions, then analysis of the wrong options — which is where the learning actually is. Listening Part 4 comes first because it is five separate short recordings and therefore forgiving: a lost answer costs nothing on the next one. Part 5 comes after the move break because it is one long recording and five matches held in the head at once, which needs the fresher attention of a second start rather than a tired middle. Both get a worked example done out loud by the teacher, and both get the question that teaches — not «what is the answer» but «which words proved it». The stage at 62–70′ is deliberately about the option nobody used, because understanding why option E was there is worth more than understanding why option G was right. The last twelve minutes open the Russian version of the same job in the school trainer, so the two wrappers sit side by side and the difference turns out to be trivial.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Five people talk. Six things they could be talking about. One of the six is never used. That is today, and it is the same task in both exams. The trap is always the same: the word you heard is usually in the wrong answer.”

0–5′
Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: papers returned, five personal errors written down and one sentence from everybody

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — test feedback, and it is the reason this stage exists. Hand back the paper. No marks read out loud. One instruction only: Find your five. Write them on the inside cover of your notebook.
  3. Then one question round the room, fast: One thing you will never get wrong again. One sentence each.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): state today plainly: Today is a listening lesson, and there is one trap that decides half the marks.
📺 On screen: no equipment at all — the marked papers are the material and they are already in their hands
👦 Students do: take the paper back, find five errors, say one sentence. Expect the irregular participles to appear on at least half the lists. Typical problem: somebody compares marks out loud — stop it once, quietly, and move on

WHAT WE DO: the map of the lesson: one skill, two wrappers, and what actually loses marks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Draw two boxes on the board and one arrow between them. Left box: 5 speakers → 8 things they might mean. Right box: the same thing, in Russian: 5 speakers → 6 headings.
  2. The skill is one. The wrapper is different. Today we do the English one properly, and in the last twelve minutes I show you the Russian one.
  3. Then the trap, written in the corner of the board where it stays all lesson: the word you heard is usually in the WRONG answer.
  4. One sentence of honesty, because half the group will never sit the Russian exam: Whether you take that exam or not, hearing what somebody is really talking about is the most useful listening skill there is. That is why we do it.

10–20′
Listening Part 4 · format
class → pairs
Why: The question is about the whole meaning, not about a word — and that has to be said before the first recording, because everything in the task is designed to make them listen for a word.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.131: what the task is, and the worked example on question 1

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books open at p.131. Five short recordings, each with one three-option multiple-choice question. The question is never about a detail — it is about the main idea, the message, the gist or the topic. Each recording is played twice. Read the EXAM INFORMATION box aloud with them.
  2. Read the tips and put the first two on the board. the question tells you what to listen for — read it before the recording starts;; you are listening for the WHOLE meaning, not for a word;
  3. TB p.257, for question 1: ask them to read the question and the answers first, then «If the girl just wanted a snack, what answer could it be?» (A or B). Play it, let them choose, then compare in pairs, and finish with the question that names the skill: «Which word in the conversation means the same as very hungry?» (starving).
  4. Kagan Think-Pair-Share on that last question: twenty seconds alone, then the partner, then the class. Finding the paraphrase IS the task, and naming it once here saves ten minutes later.
  5. Drill three words before anything else is played, because they are fast and unstressed in the recordings: starving · a tiny bit · not very well known.
📺 On screen: track 156 and the five questions on screen (Backup with no sound: the audioscript is on TB p.306 — read the first conversation aloud yourself in two voices. The worked example is teacher talk in any case)
👦 Students do: read the question, listen once, then hunt for the paraphrase. Expect the starving question to be the moment the task clicks. Typical problem: they want to answer all five now — hold the line

20–32′
Listening Part 4 · exam conditions
solo → pairs
Why: Five short recordings under time, twice each: the strategy only counts if it survives real conditions.

WHAT WE DO: questions 2–5 in silence, twice through, then checked with the evidence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. One minute to read questions 2–5 and underline the question word in each.
  2. Silent mode. Palm down. Play track 157 once, straight through. Confirm nothing.
  3. Thirty seconds alone to finish anything they can still remember.
  4. Play track 157 a second time. Tell them what the second play is for: to CHECK, not to answer.
  5. Kagan RallyCoach for the pair check: A says the answer AND the words that proved it, B coaches, then swap.
  6. Class check. Key: 1 C (she is hungry but doesn't want the other things her mother offers her — she wants something big) · 2 C («There was so much action – I wasn't bored for a minute») · 3 A («Just because of a tiny bit of rain») · 4 B («I need to go to the ticket office first so why don't you wait for me there?») · 5 B («The writer's not very well known but the story …») — TB p.257
  7. TB p.257, one comprehension question per recording, asked AFTER the check, because they prove the answer rather than replace it: «What is mum cooking?» (roast chicken) · «How long was the show?» (two hours long) · «What happened to the boy's boots?» (they got very wet) · «What are the friends going to?» (a music festival) · «Is the writer famous?» (no).
📺 On screen: track 157 in the whiteboard app, played twice (Backup with no sound: the audioscript is on TB p.306–307 — read the four conversations aloud yourself in two voices, twice, at natural speed. The five questions are printed on SB p.131)
👦 Students do: listen twice in silence, then check in pairs. Expect question 3 (the boots and the rain) to be the one that splits the class. Typical error: choosing the option that names something clearly mentioned in the recording

32–40′
Where the marks go
pairs → class
Why: Analysis of what went wrong turns into a checklist the students wrote themselves, which is the only kind of checklist anybody uses.

WHAT WE DO: turn the mistakes into a checklist the students wrote themselves

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Where marks go: they hear one word that matches an option and stop listening. In a gist question the matching word is almost always in the WRONG option — that is what it is there for.
  2. In pairs, 3 minutes: Write three rules for this task. Rules you would give to somebody who has never done it.
  3. Collect them on the board and merge them into four. Expect them to produce «read the question first», «listen for the whole meaning», «the same word is usually a trap» and «use the second play to check» — which is the official tip list rediscovered.
  4. Kagan Numbered Heads Together: call a number, that student reads one rule out and gives an example of breaking it.
  5. Write the final four rules in the corner of the board. They stay there for the rest of the lesson and are used again at 70′.
📺 On screen: the four rules written on the board as they are agreed (Backup: none needed — this stage is chalk, and the chalk version is the one that stays up)
👦 Students do: write three rules, argue, agree on four. Expect one group to produce a rule about guessing, which is worth keeping: an empty answer is always zero, a guess sometimes is not

Why: A stirrer that also forces one English sentence. Teenagers who have been sitting for forty minutes need to stand before the second half, or the second half dies.

WHAT WE DO: a two-minute stirrer that also resets the ears before the long recording

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody up. I say a word, you say a word that means the same. Starving. Enormous. Freezing. Exhausted. Four of them, fast, and accept any reasonable paraphrase.
  2. Then the sentence tax: Before you sit down — one thing you did in the holidays.
📺 On screen: no equipment at all — this stage is deliberately technology-free, and nothing can fail
👦 Students do: stand, paraphrase four words, name one holiday activity, sit. Expect the holiday answers to preview half the options of the next task, which is why the question is that one

42–50′
Listening Part 5 · format
class → pairs
Why: Eight options for five people means three are never used. Naming that before the recording is what stops a leftover option being forced onto a speaker.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.132: eight options for five people, and the example worked through

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB p.258, books closed: in pairs, compare how you spent your half-term holiday and decide who had the more interesting time. Two minutes, and it is the exact content of the recording.
  2. Books open at p.132. One recording, five people, and a list of eight options to match them to — so three options are never used. The recording is played TWICE. Read the EXAM INFORMATION box aloud.
  3. People: 0 Jasmin (example) · 1 Sophie · 2 Sam · 3 Joe · 4 Emily · 5 Gemma. Activities: A cooking · B going online · C music practice · D photography · E shopping · F studying · G sport · H travelling.
  4. Write on the board, and leave it there: 8 options · 5 people · 3 left over.
  5. Read the eight options aloud and check they know what each one means — thirty seconds, and it is the stage everybody skips and everybody should not.
  6. The example is worked through together (track 158): Jasmin's grandmother asks how her half-term was, and Jasmin says I played the guitar a lot. I'm in the school concert next week and I need to get ready for it. Have them underline the part of the audioscript that gives the answer — that underlining is the strategy of the whole task.
  7. Read out the two tips that matter most. remember three of them are never used — do not force a leftover option onto a speaker;; listen for what the person actually DID, not for the words they use;
📺 On screen: track 158 and the two lists on screen (Backup with no sound: the audioscript is on TB p.307 — read the example exchange aloud in two voices and let them underline the proving line in the printed script. The two lists are on SB p.132)
👦 Students do: compare half-terms, read the eight options, watch the example. Expect the underlining of the proving line to be the move they remember. Typical problem: they skim the eight options in five seconds — make them read them out loud

50–62′
Listening Part 5 · exam conditions
solo → pairs
Why: Timed, twice through, then a partner check on one thing only — which words in the recording proved the match.

WHAT WE DO: the five matches in silence, twice through, then checked with the evidence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. One minute to re-read the eight options and cross out nothing yet.
  2. Silent mode. Palm down. Play track 159 once, straight through. Confirm nothing.
  3. Thirty seconds alone: cross off the options you have used and look at what is left.
  4. Play track 159 a second time. The second play is for the ones they left blank and for checking the ones they did not.
  5. Kagan RallyCoach for the pair check: A gives a match and the words that proved it, B coaches, then swap.
  6. Class check. Key: 1 G (she played hockey) · 2 H (he went away with his parents) · 3 D (he spent a lot of time taking pictures) · 4 B (she went on the internet a lot) · 5 A (she spent the whole week baking) — the example 0 is C. TB p.258
  7. TB p.258, six questions after the check, and they are what turn a right answer into understanding: «Why did Jasmin do a lot of music practice?» (she has a concert next week) · «What sport did Sophie do?» (hockey) · «Who did Sam travel with?» (his parents) · «What is Joe going to do with his best photos?» (enter a competition) · «Why didn't Emily go shopping?» (she didn't have enough money) · «What did Gemma plan to do?» (study).
  8. The best question of the stage, and it is the book's own: «Who had the most interesting half-term?» and «What advice would you give to someone like Emily, who doesn't have much money but still wants to have an interesting time?»
📺 On screen: track 159 in the whiteboard app, played twice (Backup with no sound: the audioscript is on TB p.307 — read the five turns aloud yourself, twice, at natural speed and in five different voices if you can manage it. The task is identical)
👦 Students do: listen twice, cross off used options, then check. Expect speaker 4 (Emily) to be the one that goes wrong, for exactly the reason the next stage is about. Typical error: all eight options used

62–70′
The option nobody used
pairs → class
Why: Explaining why option E was never used teaches more than confirming the five answers, because the unused options are built from exactly what a careless listener hears.

WHAT WE DO: explain the three unused options — that is where the learning is

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Where marks go: Emily talks about shopping and does not go shopping. The word shopping is in the recording and option E is never used at all — that is exactly the trap, and it is the same trap as in the Russian task 1.
  2. In pairs, 4 minutes and one question: Three options were never used. Which three, and why was each of them put there?
  3. Collect the answers and name the mechanism out loud: every unused option is built from a word that WAS said. That is what makes it plausible, and it is why hearing a word is not an answer.
  4. Kagan Showdown to finish: you read three short speaker turns of your own, each containing a word from a wrong option (I wanted to go shopping but I stayed at home and read), and everybody writes the answer and reveals at Boards up!
  5. Add one rule to the list on the board: if you have used all the options, something is wrong.
📺 On screen: the four rules from 32–40′ still on the board, now growing to five (Backup: none needed — chalk, three spoken turns and mini-whiteboards)
👦 Students do: identify the three unused options, then catch three distractors. Expect the third spoken turn to catch somebody, and expect them to laugh about it — which is the point

Why: The same job in the Russian wrapper: more headings than speakers, one never used, the recording twice. Experiencing that is what stops the exam looking like a different subject.

WHAT WE DO: open the Russian version of the same job in the school trainer and compare the two

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Open the trainer on the screen: https://speakandsmile.online/oge.html#/hw?listening:g2 — no password, no sign-up, it opens straight away.
  2. One complete OGE listening set, eleven items in the order of the real paper: item 1 — five speakers matched to six headings, one heading extra; items 2–5 — three-option multiple choice; items 6–11 — write in the missing word or number. The recording is played twice, as in the exam.
  3. Only item 1 is done in class — it is the Russian twin of Listening Part 5 on SB p.132. The rest of the set is left for whoever wants it at home. The announcement is from a sports shop celebrating its tenth anniversary — presents for customers, water sports, camping and cycling. The block's own topic, so no new vocabulary gets in the way.
  4. Before anything is played, the same move as at 42–50′: read the six headings aloud and be sure what each one means. Two minutes in pairs.
  5. Play it once, then once more. They do item 1 on paper, not on the screen — it is faster and it keeps everybody involved.
  6. Then put the two side by side and ask for the differences. Two differences, and only two. The headings are in Russian, and there are five speakers instead of five items in a list. Nothing else changes — not the skill, not the strategy, not the traps.
  7. And the sameness, which is the message of the lesson: Exactly the same job as SB p.132: more options than speakers, so one option is never used, and you have to hear what the speaker is really talking about rather than which words they happen to say.
  8. Close it with the five rules on the board: read them out and ask which of them changed. Not one of them did.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can choose a three-option gist answer and match five speakers to eight options without forcing an unused one.
  • Subsidiary. Reading the options before the recording; underlining the proving words in an audioscript; explaining why a distractor is a distractor.
  • Personal. Confirm nothing on the first play, either task. The second play is the strategy, and if you confirm early it never gets used.
By the end, students can…
  • say what a short conversation is about, not just which words were in it
  • match five speakers to a longer list of options
  • leave an option unused without panicking
  • explain why a wrong option was put there

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
The recording says «shopping» and option E is «shopping». Is E the answer? → not necessarily. Emily TALKS about shopping and does not go shopping. E is never used at all.
«Which word in the conversation means the same as very hungry?» → starving. Finding the paraphrase is the task; recognising the repeated word is the trap.
«There are eight options and five speakers. How many do I not use?» → three. If you have used all eight, something is wrong.
«I missed number 3 on the first play. What do I do?» → leave it and go on. The second play is for exactly that — and stopping to think costs you number 4 as well.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
