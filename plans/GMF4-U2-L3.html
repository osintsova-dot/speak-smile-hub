Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to follow, read and act out the story «The trick that went wrong» and talk about a past event: “I visited the wax museum when I was seven. / Lily decided to play a trick.”
  • Subsidiary. Value — considering your friends' feelings before playing tricks; break Luke's code (→ WAX MUSEUM, the story setting); recycle was/were & adjectives from L1–L2; story skills — predict → listen → read along → Read & answer → act out; prepare the past-simple activities for L4.
  • Personal. Let the spooky museum story breathe — big voices, torch-mime, pauses, prediction; the past-simple verbs are NOTICED today, drilled properly in L4 — don't over-correct. Give every child a story role.
By the end, pupils can…
  • retell 2–3 things that happen in the story;
  • read their role's lines from the book;
  • answer comprehension questions in full: “They go to the wax museum.”

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Lily plays a trick to frighten Ravi & Josh — does it work?” → no — she frightens herself!
“Before a trick, should you think about your friends' feelings?” → yes — that's kind.

Assumptions: was/were & adjectives are active from L1–L2; the alphabet-number code was learnt in the Starter (recycled in act 2). Act-out routine is established from Unit 1. Readers follow the text with a finger.

Listen 1 — gist (CD1 33, books closed). Listen: whose trick goes wrong? → Lily's — she frightens herself!

Listen 2 — read along (books open pp.20–21). Finger under the text. Pause per frame: The children arrive at the museum at six o'clock. … They try to frighten each other with scary stories. … Lily decides to play a trick… Suddenly, the attendant turns around!

Values (act. 4, whole class). Was it kind of Lily to trick her friends? How did they feel? Do YOU think about your friends' feelings before you play a trick? 3–4 hands, recast key words.

Read & answer · comprehension
carpet → desks

Read and answer (act. 3, Talk Partners). Questions: Where do Josh, Lily and Ravi go? What do they use to explore? Who decides to play a trick? Who does the attendant frighten? Pairs answer in full: They go to the wax museum. Every pair speaks.

Who said it? (Showdown). Mini-whiteboards: you read a line — “Let's frighten each other with scary stories!” Who says it? — all write the name, Show me! 4 rounds.

✔ Assess: the boards are your check — who follows the story? The rest sit with you in the board game.

💪 Support: point to the right frame in the book. ⭐ Extension: add a second true fact about that moment.

28–40′
Act out
carpet, groups of 4

Model a frame. You + 3 pupils play the attendant-turns-around frame with big drama: Boo! — Aaargh! It's ALIVE!

Groups of 4 (Josh · Lily · Ravi · attendant). Rehearse 2 frames with books as scripts. Rotate roles once.

Showtime. Each group performs ONE frame (30–40 sec — fast carousel, no long waits). Audience task: shout who got frightened at the end (“Lily!”).

💪 Support: shortest lines / the “Aaargh!” reaction for the shyest child. ⭐ Extension: add an extra line from the frame.

Break the code · where was Luke?
desks, groups

Use the code (act. 2). Luke's message: Yesterday, I was at the… Decode with the A=153 key: 871-153-923 501-849-759-245-849-501 → WAX MUSEUM! That's where our story happens!

▶ Code-breaker teams (RallyCoach). Each group decodes a short word (shy · kind · tidy) with the key; partner A reads a number, B finds the letter, then swap. Everyone reads the word aloud.

🎮 Projector option: show the code machine on screen; a rotating child spins to a number, the WHOLE class calls the letter.
