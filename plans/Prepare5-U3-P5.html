🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Retrieve and repair everything from Units 5 and 6 — the making verbs, time adverbs, both past tenses, health verbs, the some/any/every/no grid and the modals — and then produce it under test conditions in the two official Cambridge unit tests.
What is genuinely hard
The Unit 6 grammar section is the hardest thing in the block: eight same-or-different judgements about modal pairs, where the whole question is whether mustn't and don't have to mean the same thing (they do not). The Unit 5 test hides its difficulty in the time adverbs, which look easy until eight items in a row need the right one.
Typical error to expect
«You mustn't come if you're busy» for «you don't have to come» — mustn't is a prohibition, don't have to is a free choice. Russian uses one negative for both, and this is a marked exam item.
«I didn't buy something», «Is there something to eat?» — negatives and questions take any-. The Russian equivalent does not change shape, so this survives into B2 unless it is drilled.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is retrieval, not revision: three timed stations where every student writes and speaks, then the official Cambridge review game for Units 5–8, filtered down to the squares that belong to our two units. The second half is the real paper, briefed honestly — sections named, minutes named, and the one genuinely confusing feature of the Unit 6 key (several answers are acceptable per item) explained BEFORE the papers turn over rather than afterwards.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty-five minutes to get it all back, then forty-five to show it. One warning before we start: on the modal section, two different answers can both be right. Do not panic when your neighbour has something else.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of retrieval with a spelling penalty, and the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what's the best thing about today so far? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the block in twelve words. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs: one pen, one paper, ninety seconds — write twelve words from Units 5 and 6, taking turns, spelling each one aloud as you write.
  3. Swap papers with the pair behind and mark. One point per word, minus one for a spelling error — announced before they start, because that is exactly how the paper works.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): name the day honestly: First half: get everything back. Second half: show it. Nothing new today.
📺 On screen: the two word walls, still up from the whole block — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write in turns, then mark someone else's list against the wall

Frame + test briefing
board
Why: A specific briefing removes the fear that comes from not knowing the shape, and the multiple-answer warning prevents twenty minutes of second-guessing during the paper.

WHAT WE DO: what is on the two papers, how long they take, and the modal warning

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Put the day's four blocks and the clock on the board so the class sees the whole 45 minutes at once.
  2. Brief the tests specifically, from the specification table in this plan: Unit 5 — the nine verbs, the time adverbs, and past simple against past continuous twice. Unit 6 — eleven health verbs, the some/any/every/no grid, then the modals in two sections.
  3. The warning that prevents panic, said now: On the modal section, several answers are correct for the same item — must OR have to, shouldn't OR ought not to. If your neighbour wrote something different, you may both be right.
  4. Explain why Review 2 in the book is not being used: It covers Units five to eight, and we've only done two of those. We'll open it after Unit eight.
  5. Promise the keys: Papers back next lesson with the answers.
📺 On screen: the timetable and the test sections on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: listen and ask; two students repeat the timings and the modal warning back to the room

WHAT WE DO: making verbs, health verbs and the grid retrieved on paper, at speed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three tables, three sheets, four minutes each, groups rotate on your signal.
  2. Table A — making and mending: nine definitions, write the verb (from SB p.32 ex.4). Table B — health: eleven definitions, write the verb. Table C — the grid: a blank twelve-cell grid to fill, then five sentences that need a grid word.
  3. One rule at every table: Whole word, correct spelling, or it doesn't count. Each group marks the previous group's sheet before writing its own.
  4. Fast finishers at every table write two extra items for the next group.
📺 On screen: three prepared A4 sheets, printed before the lesson — nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write, rotate, mark. Every student writes about forty words in twelve minutes

Station 2 · grammar, out loud and on paper
three groups, rotating
Why: Station 2 · grammar, out loud and on paper

WHAT WE DO: the two past tenses, the time adverbs and the modals

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Same groups, three new tables, four minutes each.
  2. Table D — past tenses: ten sentence halves to join, each one needing while or when and both tenses. Table E — time adverbs: a six-sentence story with the adverbs removed, to be rebuilt in order. Table F — modals: eight pairs of sentences, same meaning or different? (the exact shape of the test's hardest section).
  3. Kagan Showdown at each table: everyone writes privately, all reveal together on the signal, disagreements settled before moving on.
  4. Monitor for the two block errors and repair on the spot: the long action in the simple, and mustn't used for don't have to.
  5. Last ninety seconds at each table: the group says its hardest item aloud to the group arriving.
📺 On screen: prompt sheets at three tables — printed in advance, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone writes, everyone reveals at the same moment, and each group hands its hardest item to the next

Review game · «What's the word?»
two teams
Why: Review game · «What's the word?»

WHAT WE DO: the official Cambridge review game, filtered to Units 5–6

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it up in ninety seconds: «WHAT'S THE WORD?» — PREPARE_5_Review_game_Units_05-08.pdf. Two teams, A and B, one sheet each, and the sheets must not be visible to the other team. Team A reads out a sentence from their sheet, pausing at the gap (they do NOT say the word printed in capitals — that is the answer). Team B chooses the word from the box at the bottom of their own sheet. Three points if they get it first time, two at the second attempt, one at the third. One word in each box is never used. Versions: No crossing out (you may not cross off the words you have used) · Not in the box (the answer has to be a word that is NOT in the box, judged by the players with the teacher).
  2. Announce the filter and why: the sheets cover Units 5–8, so today play only the sentences whose answer belongs to our two units: yawn · exhausted · freezing · injure · burn · cough · breathe · original · terrible · absolutely · incredibly · customise · design · suddenly · finally · later · next · don't have to. Sentences answered by gig, concert hall, studio, lyrics, musician, celebrity, perform or live belong to Unit 7, and cosy, spectacular, unusual, traditional, brand new, busier, wider, narrower and modern to Unit 8 — mark those lines on the sheets before the lesson and skip them. The follow-up in the rules is worth two minutes: find FIVE words that could fill the same gap
  3. Play with the scoring exactly as printed — three, two, one — because the descending points are what keep the losing team thinking.
  4. Two minutes before the end, stop and run the follow-up from the rules on one sentence: find FIVE words that could fill the same gap. It is the best vocabulary activity in the pack.
📺 On screen: one A and one B sheet per pair of teams, cut along the dotted line and pre-marked. Backup: if you have only one copy, run it as a whole-class game with you reading team A's sheet
👦 Students do: teams listen to each other and choose from a word box. Everyone contributes because the team decides together before answering

WHAT WE DO: the two-minute ritual that starts a test calmly

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out both papers face down, Unit 5 on top.
  2. Read the rubric of section one aloud yourself — thirty seconds that prevent four marks lost to a misread instruction.
  3. State the two rules: Silence. If you finish, check your spelling — you don't hand in early.
  4. One breath together, then: Turn them over.

Official block test
exam rows, silent
Why: Official block test

WHAT WE DO: Unit 5 Standard and Unit 6 Standard, about 35 minutes with checking time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silence, and you sit down. Do not walk the rows for the first ten minutes — it makes nervous students slower.
  2. Answer only procedural questions, never linguistic ones. The one exception is the modal section: if somebody asks whether two answers are possible, say yes and move on.
  3. Write the remaining time on the board at 15 minutes and at 5 minutes. Say nothing else.
  4. Stronger students who asked for the Plus versions sit those instead — the sections are identical, so the timing does not change.
  5. At minute 30, one sentence: Five minutes. Spend them on the spelling in the vocabulary sections.
📺 On screen: the finish time and the two time-checks on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: work silently for 35 minutes, then check rather than sit idle

Two official Cambridge unit tests (Standard), about 66 items in 35–40 minutes — comfortably inside the 45-minute half, with checking time. Answer keys are in the same two folders. For stronger students swap in the Plus versions — the sections are identical, so the briefing does not change. Two honest heads-ups for the briefing: the Unit 6 grammar section accepts several correct answers per item (must OR have to, shouldn't OR ought not to), so nobody should panic if their answer is not the first one on the key; and the same section tests mustn't against don't have to, which is the one distinction the whole unit is built on. Every format in both papers has been done in class — name the lesson for any line a student doubts. Print one copy of each paper per student in advance (course folder → материалы → Tests).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Revision turns into the teacher talkingStations are timed and written. Your job for the first 34 minutes is to hold the clock and answer nothing that the station sheet answers.
The strong students finish every station earlyEvery station has a fast-finisher line: write two more items of the same type for the next group. Their items get used, which is why they write them properly.
The review game is for Units 5–8 and half of it is unknown languageMark the Unit 7–8 lines on both sheets with a highlighter BEFORE the lesson and tell the class why they are skipped. Ten seconds of honesty prevents five minutes of confusion.
Someone finishes the paper in fifteen minutesSaid before the papers turn over: If you finish, check every spelling in the vocabulary sections, then sit quietly. You do not hand in early.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The block test is built the way the exam block is: produce the form, spell it right. A missing to in ought to, or someone written where anyone belongs, scores nothing at all — which is why the briefing names both ghosts aloud before the papers go face down.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
