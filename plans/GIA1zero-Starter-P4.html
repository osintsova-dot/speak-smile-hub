WHAT WE DO: entry rituals; a mini-rewind of three pairs by Kagan Inside-Outside Circle (a new structure — a review carousel); a bridge to the game day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rituals: high five, How are you? What day is it today?
  2. A rewind by Inside-Outside Circle (we learn the new structure with our bodies): Two circles! Inside — face out, outside — face your partner. Rounds of 30 sec: show an object — your partner names it! → the days of the week in turn! → a number with fingers, your partner answers with their voice! Between rounds: Outside circle — rotate two steps!
  3. Bridge: “Everything you can do after three pairs — today it all works inside games. This is not a test. This is Big Game Day. And at the end we'll write OUR rules.”
📺 On screen: calendar · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they fly through a kaleidoscope of topics — a warm-up with no stress. Expected: the speed pleasantly surprises them too.

Stations: four corners of skill
🎲 GAME STATIONS · four corners
stations · teams

WHAT WE DO: 4 stations of 7 min: Hello Theatre · Classroom Commander · Number Hunt · Spelling Code; everyone does their own item, the helper helps

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Station 1, “Hello Theatre”: draw a situation card (friend/teacher/new student) → act out the greeting dialogue in a pair (the P1 frames are at the station).
  2. Station 2, “Classroom Commander”: take turns commanding your trio from the bank (2 do + 1 don't) — the team obeys; and the other way round.
  3. Station 3, “Number Hunt”: number cards spread on the desk: one says a number — the rest grab it (13/30 traps!); then the builder: build 47 from half-cards.
  4. Station 4, “Quiz-Quiz-Trade” (Kagan, the GIA1-QQT-Starter deck): cards spread on the desk — take a card: ask — answer — check (the answer's on the card upside down!) — TRADE! + a bonus: crack one code from home.
  5. I float between the stations, marking confidence into my OWN grid (this is the confidence-check — invisible).
  6. Rotation by bell; after the circle — everyone has been everywhere.
📺 On screen: 4 picture station instruction cards · station props · my confidence grid
👦 Students do: they go through 4 game stations as a team — a retrieval of all of Starter through play; everyone is active at every station. Expected: they name their favourite stations themselves; the grid fills up without a single word “test”. Typical error: the helper does it for the weaker one — “help = prompt, not do it”.

The Fun Facts quiz: numbers at work
⚡ Fun Facts quiz (a loose end from P3)
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: p.10 ex.5 as a guessing quiz by Kagan Showdown (teams write their number guess IN WORDS, boards up all at once) + audio 6

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Showdown format: English is official in HOW many countries? Teams — write your guess in WORDS… wait… SHOWDOWN! — boards up all at once (numbers in words — a workout!).
  2. 6 facts from ex.5 in turn; audio 6/key: 54 · 20 · 85 · 12 · 38 · 80. The closest guess gets +1.
  3. After each one — we read the fact in chorus with the number in words.
  4. Finale: 80% of computer information is in English. That's why we're here! — a motivational point.
📺 On screen: SB p.10 ex.5 · audio 6 · guess slips (Backup: I read out the facts and numbers myself)
👦 Students do: they place their guess, write the numbers in words, are amazed by the facts. Expected: a wow effect + the meaning of “why English”. Typical error: fourty — I remind them of tricky word #2 with a smile.

57–67′
The class contract: rules from experience
Class contract · our rules
circle

WHAT WE DO: a class contract from THEIR experience: “what helped you learn over 3 pairs?” → rules in English with support → signatures

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A question to the circle (Russian is fine): “Three pairs behind us. What HELPED you learn? What got in the way?” — I collect ideas.
  2. I turn the ideas into support-rules on the board: Speak English (try!). · Don't laugh at mistakes. · Help your team. · Listen to your partner. Trios choose/add to them.
  3. A sticker vote for the top 5; the rules go onto a flipchart sheet; SIGNATURES from everyone + mine.
  4. A ritual: rule #1 (mistakes!) moves from the P1 poster into the contract, ceremonially.
📺 On screen: flipchart paper · markers · stickers · the rule #1 poster
👦 Students do: they draft rules from their own experience, vote, sign. Expected: “don't laugh at mistakes” — top of the list from the children themselves. Typical error: a rule that bans everything — we reword it positively together.

67–77′
The first supported peer review
Peer review onboarding (with support)
trios

WHAT WE DO: the first peer review: trios show each other a mini-number (dialogue/commands/spelling) → “2 stars + 1 wish” from support strips

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The trio prepares a 60-sec mini-number OF THEIR CHOICE: a hello dialogue / a commands session / a spelling show (they can already do it all — the choice removes the fear).
  2. The show is trio-to-trio (not to the whole class — safer).
  3. Peer review from the strips: ⭐ I like your voice! ⭐ I like your English! · Try louder! / Try slower! — pick one and read it.
  4. We swap roles; I say it out loud: “A grown-up skill: two good things first, then one piece of advice. That's how teams work.”
📺 On screen: peer review support strips · a timer
👦 Students do: they show their number to a small audience, give their first structured feedback from the supports. Expected: awkward and sweet; the 2+1 formula is learned. Typical error: a wish with no Try — I point at the strip.
💪 Support: strips with ready-made phrases — just pick one. ⭐ Fast finishers: their own wish with their own Try…
📓 Interactive notebook — Get_Involved_A1_Starter_Translation_Match_It_2up_A4 + Get_Involved_A1_Starter_Translation_Translate_It_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to English A–L (greetings, colours, this/that/these/those, possessives, classroom commands), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 written out in English from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the unit's exit line.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners have recycled ALL the Starter language through game stations (a confidence-check, not a test), learned the pair/trio work routines, signed the class contract and run their first supported peer review.
  • Subsidiary. A revision of pp.6–11 in game shells; tying off loose ends (the Fun Facts quiz, the full Classroom Challenge, this/these ex.7 in passing); the peer review ritual “2 stars + 1 wish” with support; the “I CAN” final for the whole of Starter.
  • Personal. The Starter final = a celebration of skills, not an exam. The stations are built so that EVERYONE leaves with “I could do it”. The contract is their document. From next week, Unit 1 — the group must go into it feeling “we're a team and it's working”.
By the end, students can…
  • use all Starter sets in game formats
  • work in pairs/trios by the routine (roles, volume, signals)
  • give supported peer feedback (2 stars + 1 wish)
  • say the class contract rules

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is today a test?” → No! A day of games: we check CONFIDENCE, we don't give grades.
“Peer review — do we say what's bad?” → First TWO stars (what's good!), then one wish with Try…
“Does the teacher make up the class rules?” → No — we do, together, and everyone signs.

Assumptions: Three pairs behind us: the basic sets are active, the rituals work. Today: consolidation + two new rituals (supported peer review, the contract) + the emotional point “we're ready for Unit 1”. Course rule #1 (said out loud EVERY Starter pair): mistakes are how we learn — here nobody laughs at mistakes, here we grow from them. The teacher makes mistakes on purpose and fixes them out loud.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The word “test” scares themThe words “test/check” don't come up at all: it's Big Game Day. The checking happens inside the games, marks go only into their own I CAN list.
Stations = chaos for a zero-level groupRehearse the rotation BEFORE the start (a 60-sec dry run); every station has a picture instruction card; a bell signal.
The weaker ones hide behind the stronger onesStation tasks in the format “everyone does their own item”; the helper helps but doesn't do it for them.
Peer review without the languageSupport strips: I like your voice/spelling/English! · Try louder / Try again! — just pick one and read it.
The contract feels formalThe rules are born from THEIR experience over 3 pairs (“what helped you learn?”); we put it up for good and keep referring back to it.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
