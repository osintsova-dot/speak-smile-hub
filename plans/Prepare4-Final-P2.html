🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Turn the mock into three named, fixable lines per student — an error log — repair the class's four biggest errors together, and see September beside May so the distance travelled becomes visible.
What is genuinely hard
Honest self-marking. The reflex is to count the score and stop; the work is to circle the near-misses, name the pattern behind them, and write the pattern down in words a student can act on alone in a week.
Typical error to expect
She said she is working — the tense left unshifted after a past reporting verb; paper task 7 tests exactly this line [A2/B1]
unless we don't run — a double negative smuggled into unless; the paper's task 7 item 4 is exactly this trap [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The sheets come back unmarked ON PURPOSE: the student who marks the paper learns from it, the teacher who marks it learns instead. Self-marking runs against the keys read aloud; the error log is three lines with a strict shape (weakest paper by name · one wrong answer written out correctly · first thing to revise). The clinic takes four anonymous errors from your sampling sheet — four, not twenty. September-beside-May uses the Diagnostic test from the September archive, because it is the one stage of the year that shows distance travelled. The repair workshop closes the loop: time to use the feedback, today, in pairs.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Yesterday you met the exam. Today you read what it wrote back. Three lines each — your weakest paper, one answer fixed, one thing to revise. Then I show you your September selves, and you will not believe them.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls back the one thing that is needed today rather than everything that was ever taught.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, the group's own map of the mock read back, and the reason the sheets return unmarked

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the corner lines, Kagan RallyRobin: read back (anonymously) five of the one-line answers from the sheet corners — hardest: listening, because fast. Pairs alternate: agree or disagree for their own mock, one sentence each.
  3. Name the pattern the corners show — usually the second play and the spare questions.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): frame the day: the sheets come back unmarked — you mark them, because the person who marks the paper is the person who learns from it.
📺 On screen: five corner lines read by voice from the collected sheets — paper and voice are the media, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hear the group's own map, place themselves on it. Expect relief that everyone found something hard

Lesson frame
board
Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: the aim and the three-line shape on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The log's exact shape on the board, numbered: The three-line error log, written before any sheet leaves the desk: line 1 — my weakest PAPER by name (reading task / listening second play / speaking part 2, not «I did badly»); line 2 — one sentence or answer I got wrong, written out CORRECTLY; line 3 — what I revise first before the final test, in five words.
  2. Set the bar: a line with a name and an example. «I did badly» is not a line — it is a shrug.
  3. Promise the payoff: by the end you hold the only three sentences that matter before the final paper.
📺 On screen: the three-line shape in chalk — it stays up all lesson, so the board is the medium and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the shape, hear the bar. Typical problem — «can I write four lines»: yes, after the first three are sharp

8–24′
Self-marking against the key
solo → pairs
Why: The student who marks the sheet learns from it; the teacher who marks it learns instead. Circling every slip, including the near-misses, turns a score into information.

WHAT WE DO: sheets back, keys read aloud, every slip circled

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Sheets returned face-down. Red or green pen out — not the one you wrote with.
  2. Read the reading keys aloud, task by task, at dictation pace; students tick exact answers and CIRCLE everything else, including near-misses.
  3. Same for the listening keys. Speaking: read out (anonymised) three good moves and two habits from your sampling sheet — students self-assess against them with a margin note.
  4. Kagan RallyCoach for disputes: the partner rules on «does this count», you hold the veto.
  5. Close the stage with the count that matters: count your CIRCLES, not your ticks — circles are tomorrow's material.
📺 On screen: keys read by voice from the teacher's notes pages — paper and voice are the media, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: mark honestly, circle the near-misses, dispute in pairs. Expect inflation attempts at ex.3 corrections — the quote rule from the mock still applies

24–38′
The error log · three lines
solo, silent
Why: The stage the whole review exists for: three named, fixable lines are worth more than any mark, because they are the only feedback a fourteen-year-old can act on alone.

WHAT WE DO: the whole point: three named, fixable lines

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ten silent minutes. Three lines, the shape is on the board. Write them so that YOU-in-July understands them.
  2. Walk and sharpen: every vague line gets a question, not a correction — which task? which play? which word?
  3. Pair swap for sixty seconds: partners read each other's line 1 and ask one clarifying question.
  4. Collect nothing — the log belongs to the student; you note only who needs which workshop station.
📺 On screen: the shape is already on the board — no backup needed
👦 Students do: write, sharpen, swap once. Expect line 3 («what I revise first») to be the hardest — offer the menu: the shift, the passive, unless, short answers, photo language

Move break

Why: A stirrer between two heavy stages. Teenagers who have been staring at their own mistakes for half an hour need to stand before they look at four more.

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds before the clinic

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up. The year in gestures, fast: quote-hands — IF-arms — shield — the three-finger trio — brick hands. I call, you show.
  2. Sit. Four sentences on the board next — none of them has a name on it.
📺 On screen: instructions by voice, nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: shake off the marking, flash the systems, land ready for the clinic

40–52′
Error clinic · four errors
class
Why: Four, not twenty, and ninety seconds each. Anonymity makes the class willing to repair the sentences out loud; a small fixed number makes the list feel finishable rather than damning.

WHAT WE DO: four anonymous sentences from the mock, repaired out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The rule first: The clinic rule, said before the first sentence goes up: Nobody in this room is named — these four sentences belong to all of us.
  2. Four sentences up, one at a time, ninety seconds each — the class finds, fixes, names the pattern; you confirm and move:
  3. She said she is working till 6.30 — the shift dropped — said she WAS working. Task 7 of next week's paper tests exactly this line, so the fix pays twice. · The Olympics held in Brazil — the was/were lost on the way to the passive — WERE held. Do-or-receive: the Olympics didn't do the holding. · We'll miss the train unless we don't run — unless already means if-not — UNLESS WE RUN. One negative per idea. · Home. / Yes. / Pizza. — one-word answers in the speaking — an answer needs a verb and one detail: I usually stay at home, because…
  4. Kagan Showdown on the last one: boards up with the fix, so every hand commits.
📺 On screen: four sentences in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: repair, name patterns, commit on boards. Expect the unless sentence to split the room — that is why it is in the four

52–64′
September beside May
pairs → class
Why: The only stage of the year where students see the distance travelled rather than the distance remaining. No mark can do what this does: it makes next year's effort look like it will work, because last year's did.

WHAT WE DO: the Diagnostic test held against the mock: distance travelled

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Run it as the archive allows: September beside May: if the September diagnostic sheets are in your archive, hand each student their own; if not, project two or three anonymised September answers and let the class fix them at sight. Either way the point lands: the distance travelled is visible and it is theirs.
  2. Kagan Think-Pair-Share: find one thing September-you could not do that May-you did yesterday without noticing.
  3. Collect a round of them aloud — one per pair, full sentences: In September I couldn't understand a recording at that speed.
  4. Land the point once, without syrup: that distance is yours. Nobody can mark it away.
📺 On screen: September sheets from course folder → materials → Tests → Prepare_2ed_4_Diagnostic_test handed back, or two anonymised September answers projected (Backup: no archive, no projector — write two typical September answers on the board from memory; the class fixes them at sight, which proves the same point)
👦 Students do: compare, name the distance, say it aloud. Expect genuine surprise at their own September spelling — let them laugh at it kindly

🎯 Repair workshop
pairs by log line
Why: 🎯 Repair workshop

WHAT WE DO: time to use the feedback: fix your own three lines today

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pair students by LINE 1 (same weakest paper together). Three signposted repair menus on the board: LISTENING — replay craft on the WS1 script, partner reads at speed, short answers only · READING — the spare-option hunt: partner defends the wrong option, you refute it with the quoted line · SPEAKING/GRAMMAR — the frame drill plus the clinic patterns turned into six rapid Showdown items per pair.
  2. Fourteen minutes of work, Kagan RallyCoach throughout: the coach holds the key or the script, the worker earns ticks.
  3. You orbit by need — the quietest mock from the sampling sheet gets you first.
  4. Exit ticket by voice, pair by pair: the line you repaired + proof in one sentence.
📺 On screen: menus in chalk; the WS1 script and keys on paper at the stations — board and paper are the media, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: repair the named line, prove it aloud. Expect the listening pairs to be loudest — a partner reading at speed is a good machine. Typical slip: repairing a strength because it is comfortable — the log line rules

Wrap-up + homework
circle → door
Why: Self-assessment against the aim they heard at the start, so the lesson closes the loop it opened. Homework comes out of each student's own paper.

WHAT WE DO: the log read to yourself, homework, teaser

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silent re-read of the three lines; one edit allowed now that the workshop happened.
  2. Round the room, line 3 only: what you revise first — five words max.
  3. Homework, verbatim: each log line becomes five correct sentences of your own — fifteen sentences, notebooks; the stations next lesson start from them.
  4. Teaser: next lesson the year comes back OUT LOUD — a carousel, the official discussion task, and the last quiz before the paper.
📺 On screen: homework named by voice, diaries open — no backup required
👦 Students do: re-read, declare line 3, note the homework. Expect sharper lines than at minute 30 — the workshop is why

  • Main aim. By the end, every student holds a three-line error log in their own words and has repaired their own top slip in the workshop.
  • Subsidiary. The four class-wide errors named and fixed publicly; the September↔May contrast made visible.
  • Personal. Bring your sampling sheet from the speaking and the four chosen clinic sentences READY — the clinic dies if you pick sentences live. Dig out the September diagnostics beforehand, or prepare the projected alternative.
By the end, students can…
  • mark their own paper against a key without inflating it
  • name their weakest paper specifically, not «everything»
  • write a wrong answer out correctly and say why it broke
  • see and say one thing they can do now that September could not

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«My weakest paper is listening» — is that a log line? → almost: WHICH listening skill? The second play, the short answers, the speed? The line needs a finger, not a wave.
«I got 4 out of 6» — what does the log want instead of the score? → the two I missed, written out correctly, and the pattern they share.
«September me» — why look back at all? → because distance travelled is the only proof that next year's effort will pay.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Scores compared across desks the moment sheets returnReturn sheets face-down and open with the rule: today we trade patterns, not points. You never read a score aloud; the log has no score line.
Self-marking inflated («that nearly counts»)The near-miss rule said once: circle it — nearly is our best material. Circles feed the log; only exact answers tick.
A student whose mock went badly goes quietThe September contrast is the antidote and it is IN the lesson; pair that student for the workshop with a generous coach, and sample their pair first.
The clinic drifts into re-teachingNinety seconds per sentence, timer visible, fix-and-move. The workshop is where depth lives; the clinic only names.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The same skill is tested by: The error-log habit — no single task; this lesson is what OGE preparation looks like when it works.

Not another paper: three named lines and the page that fixes each. Students who learn at fourteen to write «reported speech — the shift» instead of «grammar» arrive in Year 9 able to prepare themselves, which is worth more than any number of practice papers.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
