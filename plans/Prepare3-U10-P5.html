🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Build the past simple passive — was / were + past participle — and decide, sentence by sentence, whether the agent is worth naming with by.
What is genuinely hard
The form is one week old and one word different, so it is not the problem. The problem is by: students either put it in everywhere, because the exercise taught them the word, or leave it out everywhere, because the last lesson said you do not need it. The right answer is «it depends», and it depends on something they can be taught to check.
Typical error to expect
My bike is stolen last year instead of was stolen — the right structure in the wrong tense, because the time expression was never read (SB p.113 ex.5) [high priority, A2/B1]
They were cost £25 / The school was opened five years ago put next to The accident was happened — the passive glued onto a verb that cannot take an object. Only verbs with an object have a passive [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson opens with the class out of the corridor and five things changed in the room, because the past passive is the only natural way to describe what they walk back into — the structure is needed before it is named. The discovery goes straight back to Joe's blog, which they read on Tuesday, so the examples are already understood and only the form is new. The by question gets its own ten-minute stage rather than a footnote, and the book answers it perfectly: of the five passives in Joe's blog only two name anybody. The Summer Palace text is where that decision has to be made six times in a row with a reason each time — it is the hardest stage of the lesson and the one that teaches it. The pronunciation slot is the last of the year and it is the one that most directly feeds reading aloud. The final task ranks five inventions, and every sentence of the argument is a past passive that nobody had to be told to use.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Go into the corridor. Give me ninety seconds. When you come back, five things in this room will be different, and you are going to tell me what was done — not who did it, because you will not know. That is today's grammar, and by the end you will also know when it IS worth saying who.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a five-change hunt round the room, and the bridge into the grammar

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the room has changed. Send the class out for ninety seconds and make five changes: a chair on a desk, Hello! on the board, a bag moved, the blind down, your watch on the other wrist.
  3. Bring them back and ask for the five changes. They will describe them in the active and get stuck; that is the point. Feed the first one in and let them carry on: A chair was put on the desk.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): You just needed a piece of grammar you have not been taught. Books open, page one hundred and thirteen.
📺 On screen: the five physical changes in the room itself — the room is the medium here, so no backup is needed; if the class is too large to send out, make the changes while they close their eyes for thirty seconds
👦 Students do: spot five changes and try to describe them. Expect the first two to be active and the last three to be passive once the model is given. Typical problem: Somebody put a chair — which is correct, and is exactly the sentence the passive improves on, because nobody knows who

WHAT WE DO: name the one structure of the lesson and the standard an answer has to reach

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two sentences on the board, one under the other: Mum's records are sold in the USA. and Mum's records were sold in the USA.
  2. Two questions, thirty seconds each: Which one is passive? (both of them) What is the difference? (the tense of be, and nothing else).
  3. Explain the aim in 40 seconds: one word changes from last week. The lesson is not about that word — it is about the four letters at the end of some of these sentences: by.
  4. Set the bar: Every time you write «by» today, you can say why it is there. If you cannot, take it out.
📺 On screen: the two model sentences in chalk — the board is the medium here and they stay up all lesson, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: compare the two sentences. Expect «both» to take a moment, which is worth waiting for. Typical problem: somebody says the second one is «past simple» rather than «past passive» — accept it and refine it

Why: Guided discovery from the story they read on Tuesday. Only one thing changes from last week — the tense of be — so the discovery is aimed at that and at nothing else.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2 p.113: the rule from two sentences of Joe's blog, and the other three found

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books at p.112, the text they read on Tuesday. Two example sentences are already printed on p.113: Everything was done for us. Mum and I were flown everywhere in a private plane.
  2. ex.1 in pairs, 2 minutes: complete the rule. Key: the past simple of the verb be + the past participle of the main verb.
  3. ex.2: find the other examples in the blog. Pairs, 3 minutes — there are five altogether. The songs weren't written by other people. · A year later, her first album was released. · our food was cooked by someone else · our apartment was cleaned · I was driven to school. — Only two of them name the agent (sentences 1 and 3). It is not always necessary to say who did the action: sometimes nobody knows, and we say it only when the information matters.
  4. Now the CCQs, slowly, because they are the lesson: «Everything was done for us.» Who did it? → we are not told, and the sentence works without it. · «Our food was cooked by someone else.» Why is by someone else there at all? → because that IS the news: the point of the sentence is that they stopped cooking. · «My bike is stolen last year» — what is wrong? → last year is a finished time, so be has to be was.
  5. Ninety seconds at SB p.167 for the participles they will need in four minutes: choose · buy · make · give · take.
📺 On screen: the two example sentences on the screen with the blog beside them (Backup: both are printed on SB p.112–113 — the pairs work from the paper book and you write the five passives on the board as they are found)
👦 Students do: complete the rule, then hunt for five examples. Expect all five to be found and the rule to be written correctly. Typical error: weren't written missed, because it is negative and they are looking for was

Grammar · when to say who
pairs → class
Why: The by question is the part that is genuinely hard, and the book answers it beautifully: in the whole of Joe's blog only two passives out of five name anybody.

WHAT WE DO: the second half of ex.2 p.113: how many of the five name anybody, and why

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The counting task, and it is the whole stage: Five passives. How many of them tell you who did it? (two — by other people and by someone else).
  2. Then the question that turns the count into a rule: Why those two and not the other three? Give them two minutes in pairs before you say anything.
  3. The answer, in their words and then in yours: you say who when it is the news. Our food was cooked by someone else — the whole point of the sentence is that they stopped cooking. Our apartment was cleaned — nobody cares who.
  4. Kagan Think-Pair-Share on a test case: «This school was built in 1974.» Add «by builders». Is the sentence better or worse? (worse — builders build things, that is not news).
  5. Write the rule on the board in six words and leave it: Say who only if it is news.
📺 On screen: the five passives listed on the board with a tick against the two that name an agent — the board is the medium here and the rule stays up all lesson, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: count, then explain the difference. Expect the count to be right and the reason to take the full two minutes. Typical error: «because it is important» with no example — push for the sentence, always

Grammar · practice: the day before the party
solo → pairs
Why: Controlled practice with a picture in it — five things that were done before a birthday. Short, closed, and impossible to get right without was or were.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 p.113: five things that were done before Joe's sixteenth birthday

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Do the example together and make the subject visible: The guests / invite. → The guests were invited. Underline the subject first. Is it one or many?
  2. Solo, 4 minutes, five items. Key: The clothes were made. · The music was chosen. · The cake was decorated. · The food was prepared. · The presents were bought..
  3. Kagan RallyCoach to check: the partner asks Was or were? Why? before accepting anything.
  4. Extension, 2 minutes, and it is the useful part: add two more sentences of your own about the day before the party. The room was decorated. The music was chosen.
  5. Nobody adds by to any of them, and ask why: because at a party everything is done by the same people and nobody needs telling.
📺 On screen: the five prompts on the screen one at a time (Backup: they are printed on SB p.113 — work from the page and write each completed sentence on the board as the class agrees it)
👦 Students do: build five sentences, then invent two. Expect 5 out of 5 — this is the easy stage and it is meant to be. Typical error: The clothes was made, the subject closest to the verb rather than the real one

41–53′
Grammar · the Summer Palace
solo → pairs
Why: The stage where the rule earns its keep: six verbs to make passive and, for each one, a decision about whether the agent is worth naming. That decision is the language point.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 p.113: six verbs turned passive, and six decisions about by

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the context in two lines: a garden in Beijing built by an emperor in the middle of the eighteenth century, destroyed in a war, rebuilt and renamed, and turned into a public park in 1924.
  2. Two comprehension questions first: What was its original name? (the Garden of Clear Ripples) What is it now? (a public park).
  3. The task, and say both halves of it: Change the six verbs in italics into the past passive — AND decide for each one whether you need «by». Solo, 6 minutes.
  4. Key, with the reason for every decision: 1 The Garden of Clear Ripples was designed by the Qing Emperor Qianlong — by is needed, it is new information · 2 some of the garden was destroyed — no by, it does not matter who · 3 the garden and the buildings were built again by the Emperor Guangxu — by is needed, new information · 4 and were given a new name — no by him, Guangxu has just been named · 5 The Summer Palace was used by the Empress Dowager Cixi — by is needed · 6 In 1924 it was changed into a public park — no by someone, that is obvious. The best photo for the new text is the Garden of Clear Ripples.
  5. Kagan Numbered Heads Together to collect, and the question is always the same: Number threes — sentence four. Is «by him» needed? (no — Guangxu was named in the sentence before).
  6. Note the printing quirk: the answer key spells the emperor Quinlong and the text spells him Qianlong. The text is right.
📺 On screen: the Summer Palace text on the screen with the six italicised verbs highlighted (Backup: it is printed on SB p.113 — students work from the page and you write the six passive versions on the board one at a time, with a tick or a cross for «by» next to each)
👦 Students do: rewrite six verbs and justify six decisions. Expect 5 out of 6 on the form and about 3 out of 6 on the by. Typical error: by someone added to sentence 6 — which is the exact overcorrection this stage exists to remove

53–61′
Grammar · error hunt
teams
Why: Correcting other people's sentences is faster and safer than producing them, and the Showdown means every student answers every item instead of one student answering one.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 p.113: five sentences, and every one of them is a different mistake

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Five sentences, 4 minutes. Kagan Showdown: the team agrees a correction, writes it on the mini-whiteboard, and on Showdown! every board goes up at once.
  2. Key: It was given to me by my uncle. · My bike was stolen last year. · They cost £25. (were deleted — there is no passive here) · All those things were sold in yesterday's sale. · The school was opened five years ago.
  3. For every item, one team says WHAT KIND of mistake it was — and there are four different kinds here: the missing be (1), the wrong tense of be (2 and 4), the passive on a verb that cannot take one (3), and the missing passive altogether (5).
  4. Item 3 is the one worth ninety seconds: They were cost £25. What does «cost» do to anything? Nothing. So there is no passive.
  5. Anything nobody catches goes on the board as a line for the review lesson.
📺 On screen: the five sentences one at a time on the screen so the whole class repairs the same one (Backup: write the five sentences on the board before the lesson and uncover them one by one with a sheet of paper)
👦 Students do: correct as a team and reveal together. Expect 3–4 out of 5. Typical error: item 3 — the team «corrects» it to They were costed, which is the mistake made twice

Pronunciation · sounds and spelling
class → pairs
Why: Ten groups of three words that look different and mostly sound the same. It is the last pronunciation slot of the year and it is the one that most directly serves reading aloud.

WHAT WE DO: ex.6–7 p.113 (track 144): ten groups of three, and ten sentences read aloud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. SB p.113 ex.6–7, track 144: ten groups of three words that are spelt differently and mostly sound the same — buy / boy / by · which / witch / watch · hear / hair / here · would / word / wood · know / now / no · meat / meet / met · own / one / won · their / there / they · toe / too / to · were / wear / where. Then ten tongue-twisting sentences to read aloud in pairs. Demonstrate with wait / walk / weight first (TB p.222): walk is the odd one, because its vowel is /ɔː/ while the other two have the diphthong /eɪ/.
  2. Demonstrate the first one on the board and say why: wait / walk / weight — Which is the odd one? (walk) Why? (its vowel is /ɔː/; the other two are /eɪ/).
  3. ex.6 in pairs, 4 minutes: ten groups, one odd word in each. Then play track 144 to check and repeat all thirty words. Key: 1 boy · 2 watch · 3 hair · 4 word · 5 now · 6 met · 7 own · 8 they · 9 toe · 10 were.
  4. ex.7 in pairs, 3 minutes: take turns reading the ten sentences aloud. This is the stage that matters — the sorting was preparation for it.
  5. Two pairs read to the class. Correct nothing except the sounds.
📺 On screen: track 144 in the board app with the ten groups on the screen (Backup: they are printed on SB p.113 — say all thirty words yourself, clearly, in the order printed, and let the class point at the odd one in the paper book)
👦 Students do: sort ten groups by ear, then read aloud. Expect group 4 (would / word / wood) to be the hardest and group 10 (were / wear / where) the second. Typical error: they choose the odd word by spelling rather than by sound, which is the whole trap of the exercise

🎯 Speaking out · what changed the world

Why: The productive outcome: five inventions, ranked and defended. Every sentence of the argument is a past passive, and nobody has to be told to use one.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.139 (Unit 20 ex.8): five things that changed people's lives, ranked and defended

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Put the five on the board: DNA was discovered. The internet was created. Electricity was discovered. The steam engine was developed. Air conditioning was invented. Every one of them is today's grammar and nobody has to be told.
  2. Groups of four, 5 minutes: rank all five from most to least important for life today, and agree on the order. The disagreement is the task.
  3. Then the addition, which is where the language gets interesting: Add one more to the list. It has to be in the same form. (The telephone was invented. Vaccines were developed.)
  4. Each group reports its top one with a reason. The class votes on a single winner.
  5. The standard from the lesson frame: If you say «by», you can say why.
  6. Log errors silently on your correction sheet; do not correct aloud — the feedback slot is the start of the next lesson.
📺 On screen: the five sentences written on the board — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed; SB p.139 has the same five printed with photographs
👦 Students do: rank five inventions and defend the ranking. Expect the internet and electricity to be the final two, and expect at least one group to argue for air conditioning seriously. Typical error: the added sixth sentence written in the active — the instruction «in the same form» is what catches it

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can form the past simple passive accurately and justify including or omitting the agent in a connected text.
  • Subsidiary. Sounds against spelling in ten groups of three words; reading aloud; ranking and defending an opinion in the past passive.

  • decide whether to name the agent, and say why
  • correct the five classic passive mistakes
  • hear the difference between words that are spelt differently and sound the same

Structure
Everything was done for us. · Our food was cooked by someone else. · The songs weren't written by other people. · The Summer Palace was used by the Empress Dowager Cixi. · In 1924 it was changed into a public park.
Form
was / were (+ n't) + past participle. The agent goes at the end with by, and only when it is new or important. Questions: Was St Paul's Cathedral built by Christopher Wren? ⚠️ Only verbs with an object have a passive.
Pron. watch
the weak was inside a passive — /wəz/, not /wɒz/: The school wəz opened in 2005. Plus the ten sound-and-spelling groups of SB p.113 ex.6.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Everything was done for us.» Who did it? → we are not told, and the sentence works without it.
«Our food was cooked by someone else.» Why is by someone else there at all? → because that IS the news: the point of the sentence is that they stopped cooking.
«My bike is stolen last year» — what is wrong? → last year is a finished time, so be has to be was.
«It gave to me by my uncle» — what is missing? → was. The passive is two words.
«They were cost £25» — what is wrong? → cost has no object here, so there is no passive to make. The sentence is simply They cost £25.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
by is put into every single sentenceIt is the predictable overcorrection and the cure is a counting task, not an explanation: send them back to Joe's blog and have them count. Five passives, two agents. Two out of five. Not five out of five.
was and were are chosen by the noun nearest the verbBecause in All those things were sold the singular sale is closer than the plural subject. Make them underline the subject before they write anything — a physical habit, not a rule.
The pronunciation stage turns into a spelling competitionKeep it oral. The point is the SOUND: buy, boy and by are written for the eye and only two of them are the same for the ear. Demonstrate with wait / walk / weight first, which is the TB p.222 model, and let them do the rest by listening.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 20–26 again — this time the form to produce is the past passive.

The past passive is the single most common right answer in that task after the past simple, because exam texts are full of sentences like The museum ______ in 1897. The whole skill is noticing that the subject cannot do the action itself.
