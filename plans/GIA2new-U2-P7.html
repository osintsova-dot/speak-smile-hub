🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The first project of the unit for new students goes strictly by the steps in the book (THINK→PLAN→CREATE→PRESENT), with an organiser and the discussion phrases on cards. The unit revision is built in as stations, framed as “a check before you publish” — preparation for the test without the word “test”. The presentation is the whole crew at the board, the speaker holding the sheet.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today we find out the truth about this class. How much time do we really spend on hobbies? Your group makes a survey — six questions — then you go and ask real people: your classmates. After that, four quick checks (you'll see, you know all of it), and finally your results go on the wall for everyone to read. Real data, real English.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: we find out the truth about our class: the group makes a six-question survey, asks their classmates, goes through four checks and puts the results up on the wall.

Design · building the survey
crews

WHAT WE DO: STEP 1–2: the model survey (ex.2) → topic → 6 questions (4 from the bank + 2 of their own) round the circle on one form

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2 on the model survey SB p.35: Which question is yes-or-no? Which gives options? Which is open? TB key: a — 1 · b — 3 and 4 · c — 2, 5, 6; the words: a Who · b What · c How many · d Why.
  2. Topics (ex.4): creative hobbies · music · social media · socialising · sports.
  3. A bank of ready-made questions on the slide — we take 4 and invent 2. We write through Kagan RoundTable: one form per crew, I write the first question on the board as the model — then the form goes round the circle, everybody writes in one question (from the bank if they like) and passes it on. Six turns — six questions.
  4. The table phrases on cards work as Kagan Talking Chips: everybody has 3 chips, you speak — you put a chip in the middle. What do you think? · Can you write down our ideas? · OK, let's start. Out of chips — you listen; still holding chips — the crew asks for your opinion.
📺 On screen: SB pp.34–35 · question bank · phrase cards · Project planner p.118
👦 Students do: they build the survey leaning on the bank. Expect: the two own questions do not come at once — I help pinpoint by pinpoint

Field work · asking the class
moving

WHAT WE DO: we shuffle the groups: everybody surveys 4 people from other crews and writes the answers down

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. New groups! Ask YOUR questions, write the answers. Short answers are fine.
  2. In the new group — Kagan RoundRobin: I demonstrate with one group how it goes (“asked — everybody answered round the circle — passed the turn on”). Then they do it themselves: clockwise, each person becomes the researcher and asks their questions, the others answer in turn. Nobody skips a turn.
  3. Support: a form with columns (name · answer 1 · answer 2 …) — very little to write.
  4. Back to your groups. Count your answers together.
📺 On screen: survey forms · timer
👦 Students do: they move around and survey. Expect: noisy, but with the form everybody copes

Four checks · revision stations
stations

WHAT WE DO: 4-minute stations with supports: activities · do/go/play · adverbs and like + -ing · Wh- questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four checks. Four minutes each. The posters on the wall help you!
  2. The activities station runs as Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: first I demonstrate the move with one student (“asked — answered — praised — swapped cards”), then they do it themselves: card in hand, hand up, new partner. The prompt is on the back of the card — peeking is allowed.
  3. At every station — a prompt card and the poster from its own pair (the line, the two routes, the three colours, the baskets).
  4. A personal “superpower list”: whatever did not work, I write down for myself.
📺 On screen: SB p.33 Quick review · station cards · the class posters · timer
👦 Students do: they revise the unit with supports. Expect: they recognise their own materials and cope faster than they expect

WB Unit 2 · the test is next pair (introduced gently)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can design a 6-question survey, collect real answers and present the results with a frame, and revise the whole unit at four stations.
  • Subsidiary. Super skill collaboration with the phrase cards; going over the model survey and the model summary (SB p.35); Quick review p.33 as stations with supports; the unit's WDYT question gets closed with an answer.
  • Personal. The project follows the steps in the book, I do not cut it short. At the stations — prompt cards and the posters from the previous pairs. I call the gap list “the superpower list”; the word “test” only comes at the very end of the pair.
By the end, students can…
  • write survey questions of different types
  • ask classmates and note their answers
  • present results with a frame (Most students… / Only four students…)
  • pass four revision stations

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
A survey of six Yes/No questions — will that do? → no, you need different types.
Is the summary all the answers one after another? → no, only the main points.
Are the stations a test? → no, a check before you publish.

Assumptions: SB pp.33–35, KTP pair 19. The first full project of the unit for this line; success = they went through all the steps, not “the best presentation”.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The discussion slides into RussianPhrase cards on the table; the rule: a decision is only taken with a phrase from a card.
They do not know what questions to writeI give a bank of 10 ready-made questions: at least four come from the bank, two they invent themselves.
The stations get squeezed out by the projectThe stations sit firmly in the middle of the pair, on a timer, with the posters and cards — and they are worth as many points as the project.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
