🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): With new students grammar CAN and should be named as the topic — clarity takes the anxiety away. The lesson is built as a discovery with the table on the screen: examples first, then the rule, then drilling, then their own sentences. Five predictions about the future are a safe personal product: I talk about myself from a frame and it is almost impossible to get it wrong. The listening part (I go / I'll go) is given as a game, not as an exam.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today we learn one word that opens the whole future: will. Underwater hotels in 2030. Planes with 360° windows in 2050. And a real scientist says that by 2100 we will travel by teleportation. One word, one form, no changes for he, she or they — this is the easiest tense in English, I promise. At the end each of you gives five predictions about the future. Five lines, with your notes. Let's open 2100.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: today we learn one word that opens the whole future — will. Underwater hotels in 2030, planes with 360° windows in 2050, teleportation by 2100. One form for every person — the easiest tense in English. At the end everyone gives five predictions about the future.

WHAT WE DO: model on the board → ex.1 the table → ex.2 four predictions with support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. MODEL: three predictions of your own on the board: I think it will rain tomorrow. · I'm sure we'll finish this unit on time. · This lesson won't be boring.
  2. Three questions so that students work out the rule themselves (TB routine): 1 Are these about the past, the present or the future? (future) · 2 Do I know for certain? (no) · 3 What word is in every sentence? (will)
  3. The formula on the screen for good: WILL + verb. No 'to'. No '-s'. WON'T = will not.
  4. ex.1 together (TB key: 1 We will eat VR food in the future. · 2 It won't cost a lot of money.).
  5. ex.2 — do the first affirmative and the first negative TOGETHER (TB advice), the rest in crews (TB key: 1 will have · 2 won't need, will have · 3 won't take, will use · 4 will use).
  6. The crew closes the remaining items with Kagan RoundTable: one sheet per crew, and it goes round — you get the sheet, write ONE sentence, pass it on. Before you start the round, model it yourself: take the sheet, say will + verb out loud, write the first line and pass it. The formula is on the screen — copying from it is allowed and expected.
📺 On screen: SB p.100 ex.1–2 · the formula on screen · one sheet per crew for RoundTable (Backup: the formula and the table on the board — do not rub them off before the end of the lesson)
👦 Students do: work the rule out from the three questions. Expect: “one form for every person” is met with relief

Rule · questions with will

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 the tables of questions and short answers → ex.5 building them → ex.6 in pairs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3 with support (TB key: 1 Will it be expensive? · 2 No, it won't. · 3 What will this cost?).
  2. Drill the short answers with ten questions chorally: Will it rain tomorrow? — Yes, it will. / No, it won't. Show them: we do NOT repeat the verb.
  3. ex.5 building questions from words, do the first one together on the board (TB key: 1 Who will you speak to next on the phone? · 2 What will the weather be like tomorrow? · 3 What time will you go to bed tonight? · 4 What job will you have when you leave school?).
  4. The remaining three go in pairs as Kagan RallyCoach. Explain the roles on your fingers: ONE pen per pair. The first student builds the question out loud, saying the order — question word, will, person, verb — and writes it. The second is the coach at that moment: writes nothing, just looks at the table on the screen and checks two things — no stray to and no -s. Question built — hand the pen over and swap roles.
  5. ex.6 in pairs — WRITE your answers down first, then ask each other. Kagan RallyRobin.
📺 On screen: SB p.100 ex.3, 5–6 · the table on screen · timer (Backup: write the table of questions and short answers on the board in two columns and do not rub it off before the end of the lesson)
👦 Students do: write first, speak after. Expect: the question “What job will you have” gets the liveliest answers

WHAT WE DO: Pronunciation p.117 as a game: contractions, then telling them apart

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Tracks 79–80: drill I'll · you'll · he'll · she'll · we'll · they'll, then in sentences I'll see you at 8 o'clock. · She'll be here in 10 minutes. Chorally, then crew by crew.
  2. Track 81 as a game — listen TOGETHER first, work one example through, then on their own (TB key: 1 b · 2 a · 3 a · 4 b · 5 b). Mini-whiteboards: A or B — this is Kagan Showdown: write the letter, hold the board with the text facing you, lift together on the command Showdown! That way nobody looks at their neighbour and changes the answer at the last second — and you can see in a second who still cannot hear the 'll.
  3. Pair game ex.5 p.117: Say one sentence. Can your partner tell which one you said? — three goes each.
  4. Bridge into ex.7: read the text “How will holidays change in the future?” with support (TB key: 1 will become · 2 will look · 3 will have · 4 will we travel · 5 won't take · 6 how will we travel). Pre-teach: teleportation = moving a person from one place to another instantly.
📺 On screen: audio tracks 79–81 · SB p.117 and p.100 ex.7 · mini-whiteboards (Backup: say the pairs of sentences yourself, three times each, and students hold up A or B)
👦 Students do: play at telling them apart. Expect: 2–3 out of 5 the first time, 4–5 after the drill. They like the pair game more than the audio

WHAT WE DO: product: five predictions about the future from the frame; the speaker holds notes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame on the screen: In 2030, … will … . In 2050, people won't … . I think I will … . I'm sure my town will … . In 2100, we will … .
  2. Support — the ideas from the ex.7 text: underwater hotels, planes with 360° windows, space flights, teleportation. Plus their own: school, town, sport, phones.
  3. Write for 6 minutes in silence, then read it in pairs, then the crew comes out with notes.
  4. The class listens with a task: Thumbs up if you AGREE with the prediction.
  5. Praise something specific: Clean will! Good year! Nice detail! — no error feedback at the board.
📺 On screen: the frame as support · timer · the formula WILL + verb on screen (Backup: write the five-line frame and the formula on the board; students copy the frame into their notebooks before they start)
👦 Students do: speak with notes. Expect: the predictions about school and phones are the funniest and the cleanest in form

WB Unit 8 · the formula WILL + verb stays on the wall until the end of the unit
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can make predictions with will and won't, ask a question with will and answer it short, and give five predictions about the future from notes.
  • Subsidiary. The tables in ex.1 and ex.3 with support; ex.2 four predictions; ex.5 building questions; ex.6 in pairs; ex.7 the text about the future of holidays; Pronunciation p.117 (tracks 79–81) as a game.
  • Personal. The topic is named out loud and written on the board. The table stays on the screen all lesson. The self-checked mini-test sits in the middle — before the product, so the mistake is found before the stage.
By the end, students can…
  • make a prediction with will and with won't
  • ask a question with will and give a short answer
  • hear the difference between 'I go' and 'I'll go'
  • give five predictions about the future from notes

Structure
People will have holidays in space. · We won't need a passport. · We will eat VR food in the future. · It won't cost a lot of money. · Will we visit the Moon? — Yes, we will. / No, we won't. · Will it be expensive? — No, it won't. · Where will we travel? · What will this cost?
Form
will/won't + the verb with NO to and NO -s, one form for every person · question: (Question word) + will + subject + verb · short answer: Yes, they will. / No, they won't.
Pron. watch
'll: I'll /aɪl/ · he'll /hiːl/ · she'll /ʃiːl/ · we'll /wiːl/ · they'll /ðeɪl/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“People will to travel” — what is one word too many? → to.
“She will goes” — what is one letter too many? → -s.
“will not” in one word? → won't.

Assumptions: SB p.100 + Pronunciation p.117, syllabus pair 64. They saw will in the blog in P2; new are the question, the short answer and hearing the contraction 'll.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“will to go” and “will goes”One picture for the whole lesson: WILL + a bare verb. It stays on the screen and you point at it instead of explaining.
A short answer with no verb feels incompleteShow it in pairs: Will people go? — Yes, they will (not “Yes, they will go”). Drill it with ten questions.
The listening task frightens themGive it as a pair game AFTER the drilling and after the mini-test, not at the start; listen together first, then on their own.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
