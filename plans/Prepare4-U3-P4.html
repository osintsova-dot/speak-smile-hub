🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Build the eight noun+noun compounds (bus stop, speed limit…), follow a two-voice interview for gist and detail, and agree and disagree politely with the ten Prepare-to-speak phrases in a real discussion.
What is genuinely hard
Compounds are easy to match and hard to OWN — the order flips under pressure (stop bus) because Russian builds the pair the other way with an adjective. And disagreeing is a social skill before it is a language skill: teenagers either nod at everything or say «no» flat — both lose the conversation.
Typical error to expect
An opinion with no reason attached — I think city is better full stop — scores as no opinion at all in the speaking [national exam report]
A one-word reply («Pollution.», «Yes.») does not count as an answer at all: an answer needs a verb in it [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The compound work opens the lesson because it is light and winnable — energy for the listening half. The interview gets three passes (predict → gist → detail) because that is how ears actually work; Bess and Mr Evans mostly AGREE, which quietly models the phrases before Alice and Oliver disagree with them. The speaking is built as two rounds with an error clinic in between — the improvement between rounds is audible within one lesson, which is the cheapest motivation there is. Phrases are compulsory: a discussion without them scores zero in this room.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today I teach you to argue with me — politely. Ten phrases: how to have an opinion, how to ask for one, how to disagree without a war. By the end you'll debate city against village like two radio guests — twice, and the second time you'll hear yourselves getting better.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a three-sentence quantifier repair, and the bridge into compounds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — quantifier clinic. Three anonymous sentences from lesson 3's log on the board (a much/a lot slip, a some/any slip, a countability slip). Pairs find and fix, hands up, repair together.

  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the page: Page thirty-eight, bottom half. Words that come in pairs — then you learn to argue with me.
📺 On screen: three faulty sentences in chalk — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hunt and fix in pairs. Expect the countability slip to be found last. The point is closure on lesson 3, not re-teaching

Vocabulary · compound nouns
pairs → class
Why: Noun + noun with the box-A word first: the pattern is the teaching point. The pairs race makes every compound be said aloud, not just matched.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: build the eight compounds from boxes A+B; ex.2: use them

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 as a pairs race: match box A + box B to photos 1–8 — 3 minutes, first three pairs call it. Key: 1 apartment building (example) · 2 bus stop · 3 pedestrian crossing · 4 post box · 5 recycling bin · 6 speed limit · 7 taxi rank · 8 road sign — the word from box A always comes first.
  2. The rule out loud: the box-A word always comes first — and the stress lands on it. Clap-drill all eight: BUS stop, SPEED limit, TAXI rank…
  3. ex.2 solo, 4 minutes, pair check. Key: 1 taxi rank · 2 speed limit · 3 post box · 4 apartment building · 5 pedestrian crossing · 6 recycling bin · 7 bus stop · 8 road sign.
  4. Thirty-second hangman teaser from the TB cooler list (police station, swimming pool) — proof the pattern makes new words free.
📺 On screen: the eight compounds land on the board as the race resolves — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: race, clap the stress, complete the sentences. Expect pedestrian crossing and taxi rank to be the new ones. Typical error: stop bus under pressure — the clap fixes it

Listening · village interview
pairs → solo
Why: Three passes, three depths: brainstorm predictions, gist (do they agree?), then six detailed choices — the same recording trains three sub-skills.

WHAT WE DO: three passes: predict → gist (do they agree?) → six details

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 predict, pairs, 2 minutes: Living in the country — one advantage, one disadvantage. Two ideas per pair. Collect five onto the board.
  2. Pass 1, track 29, gist: Bess and Mr Evans — in general, do they agree or disagree? Key: in general they agree (listen for: Maybe you're right · I completely agree).
  3. Pass 2, same track, ex.3 detail: six choices solo, pair check. Key: 1 country · 2 lived (in the same place) · 3 sometimes · 4 public transport · 5 worse · 6 recycling — item 3 is the trap: the ANSWER is sometimes, whatever your ears want.
  4. Colour with the extra facts: Mr Evans likes: the village green, knowing the neighbours, lots to do. Bess: more fun in the city, the cinema.
📺 On screen: track 29 in the whiteboard app, played twice (Backup: read the interview from the audioscript, TB p.264–265, in two voices — change position for Mr Evans; the task works identically)
👦 Students do: predict, then listen twice at two depths. Expect 4–5 of 6 on the detail pass. Typical problem: item 3 answered from logic, not the tape — replay just that exchange if it splits the class

Speaking model · Alice and Oliver
solo → pairs
Why: A recorded model shows the bar before students have to reach it: the ten opinion/agree/disagree phrases are sorted by ear, then drilled with attitude.

WHAT WE DO: track 30: sort the ten phrases by speaker, then drill them with attitude

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Look at the two photos: Motorway city, seaside village. Where would YOU rather live? Hold the thought.
  2. Play track 30, ex.2: Alice and Oliver — what do they AGREE on? Key: they agree that in the city you meet more people — and that the crowds, the rush hour and the rubbish are terrible.
  3. ex.3: fill the four menu boxes on the board from the Prepare to speak list, writing A or O next to each phrase as the class assigns them. Key: Personally, I think… O · I (don't) think… A · It seems to me… O · If you ask me,… A · What do you think? O · Do you agree? O · That's true. A · Yes, maybe you're right. O · I completely agree with… O · I'm not sure I agree. A.
  4. Drill the menu with attitude — polite disagreement has a MELODY: I'm not sure I agree… falls soft; Maybe you're right, BUT… rises on but. Choral, then rows.
📺 On screen: track 30 in the whiteboard app (Backup: read the Alice/Oliver dialogue from the audioscript, TB p.265, in two voices; the phrase-sorting works identically)
👦 Students do: listen, sort the phrases into the menu, drill the melody. Expect the menu to demystify «politeness» — it is just phrases. Typical error: disagreeing with a flat «no» in the drill — replay the melody

A5 dialogue map — AGREE OR NOT?: the opinion frame (I think city life is… · In my opinion…) with three response banks — AGREE (I agree. · That's true. · Exactly!) · PARTLY AGREE (Maybe, but…) · DISAGREE (I'm not sure. · I don't agree because…) — a three-turn model discussion about city problems and four topic cards (traffic · pollution · rubbish · green spaces) under the rule OPINION + REASON · LISTEN · RESPOND
  • Glue the map in — an opinion without a reason does not count.
  • Take a topic card and give your opinion with because.
  • The partner answers from one of the three banks, never with a bare yes.
  • Swap cards and run it again — this time start with partly agreeing.

🎯 Speaking out · round 1
pairs
Why: The productive outcome: a real two-way discussion with the phrases compulsory. Round 1 is the diagnosis — you log errors and phrase-dodging for the clinic.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5–6: two topics, assigned positions, phrases compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pairs pick TWO topics from the ex.5 menu: ex.5 topics (pick two): cinema vs film at home · a concert vs headphones · cycling vs public transport · an apartment vs a house · a beach holiday vs a city holiday
  2. One minute solo: two opinions per topic, each with a because.
  3. Round 1, Kagan Timed Pair Share — 2 minutes per topic: LEFT partner defends the less-popular side (assigned!), minimum three exchanges, menu phrases compulsory.
  4. You walk and log SILENTLY: missing reasons, flat «no», abandoned phrases, quantifier slips.
  5. Close: Freeze. Round two is coming — after the clinic.
📺 On screen: the phrase menu and the topic list stay on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: argue assigned positions with the menu. Expect stiffness — normal for round 1; the clinic and the second round exist because of it. Typical error: opinion without because — two fingers up as you pass

68–76′
Error clinic
class
Why: Delayed correction between the rounds: three anonymous sentences from your sheet, repaired by the class — feedback lands while the conversation is warm and there is still a round 2 to use it in.

WHAT WE DO: delayed correction between the rounds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four sentences from your round-1 log on the board, anonymous: one missing reason, one flat disagreement, one quantifier slip, one word-order slip.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs: fix all four, then hands up; repair together fast.
  3. One melody replay: the class disagrees with YOU politely, chorally: I'm not sure I agree…
  4. Announce the round-2 delta: Same game, new partner, free choice of side — and I listen only for phrases and becauses.
📺 On screen: four faulty sentences in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: fix, drill the melody once, reset. Expect recognition laughs at the flat «no» sentence — anonymity keeps it safe

76–86′
🎯 Speaking out · round 2
new pairs
Why: Same task, new partner, higher bar: the improvement is audible within one lesson, which is the cheapest motivation there is.

WHAT WE DO: same topics, free positions, audible improvement

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: stand, mix, new partner, sit.
  2. Round 2 — 2 minutes per topic, free choice of side, phrases and becauses compulsory. You listen only for the delta.
  3. Two pairs perform their best 30 seconds to the class; the class reacts ONLY with menu phrases: That's true! · I don't think so!
  4. Praise the delta by name: Round one you said «no». Round two you said «I'm not sure I agree, because…» — that's the lesson, done.
📺 On screen: the menu stays up; performing pairs stand where everyone sees them — the room is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: re-run the discussion better, react with phrases. Expect audibly longer exchanges — say so out loud. Typical leftover: because-dropping under speed; one reminder, not a correction

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can use the eight compounds and run a two-way discussion using opinion, agreeing and disagreeing phrases — with a reason attached to every opinion.
  • Subsidiary. Listening to the Bess/Mr Evans interview for gist and six details (track 29); sorting the model phrases by speaker (track 30); delayed error correction between the rounds.
  • Personal. In round 1 do not interrupt at all — log. Run the clinic on YOUR sheet, anonymously. In round 2 listen only for the phrases and the reasons: that is the delta you praise.
By the end, students can…
  • build the eight compounds with the right word first
  • catch the gist of a two-voice interview (do they agree?)
  • give an opinion WITH a reason (Personally, I think… because…)
  • disagree politely (I'm not sure I agree · maybe you're right, but…)

Form
compound = noun + noun, box-A word first, stress on the FIRST word (BUS stop, SPEED limit); opinion → reason (because…); disagree = soften first, then counter.
Pron. watch
compound stress: ˈbus stop · ˈspeed limit · ˈtaxi rank · recycling /riːˈsaɪklɪŋ/ · building /ˈbɪldɪŋ/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Personally, I think…» — fact or opinion? → opinion; and what MUST follow it in this room? → a reason (because…).
«I'm not sure I agree» — is that rude? → no: it is how you disagree and keep the conversation.
«Stop bus» — what's wrong? → order: the box-A word comes first — bus stop, and the stress lands on BUS.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Compound order flips under pressure (stop bus, limit speed)Anchor the rule as rhythm, not grammar: clap the stress — BUS stop, SPEED limit. The clap fixes what the rule can't.
The discussion dies in one exchange («City. — Yes.»)The phrase menu is compulsory and visible; the rule of the round: minimum three exchanges each way before you may stop. The listener asks What do you think? to restart.
Everyone agrees with everything — no disagreement to practiseAssign positions for round 1: the left partner defends the village, whatever they privately think. Round 2 is free choice.
Opinions without reasonsThe because-rule: an opinion without because is half a point. You hold up two fingers — the reason is the second finger.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
