🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Name ten things that you read (advert, article, brochure, e-book, graphic novel, note, notice, paper, poster, sticker) and read six real-life short texts — signs, notes and messages — for the writer's exact purpose.
What is genuinely hard
The words look easy and half of them are false friends of nothing — the difficulty is the boundaries: a note vs a notice, a poster vs an advert, a paper vs an article IN a paper. And in the reading, the genuinely hard move is rejecting the two tempting wrong options, not finding the right one.
Typical error to expect
brochure · advert(isement) · imagination · concentration · believe — the block's misspelling traps; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
A one-word reply («Posters.», «Yes.») does not count as an answer at all: an answer needs a verb in it [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
Vocabulary first, because the reading is made of it — every text in the exam-style task IS one of the ten things. The classroom scavenger break makes the words physical (there are notices, posters and stickers in the room right now). The reading runs gist → example → five choices with justification, and texts 2–3 are quietly farmed for next lesson: they already speak in reported speech («Mum said she was working till 6.30»), so today's careful reading is tomorrow's grammar quarry. The productive stage is a personal reading map — printed vs digital — because opinions with reasons are the unit's real-life register.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Count everything you have read today — messages, stickers, posters, the timetable on the door. You read a hundred things a day and most of them are not books. Today: the ten names for all of it, and how to read a sign so fast you know who wrote it and why.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a hot round linking block 9 jobs to what those people read, and the bridge into the unit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — jobs-that-read hot round (block 9 words meet the new unit): you call a job from Unit 18, pairs fire back what that person reads and writes at work — A journalist? — Articles! A lawyer? — Contracts! An architect? — Plans! A point per sensible answer in a full phrase.
  3. Keep the answers in a board corner — half of them are today's new words wearing work clothes.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the unit: Books open, page one hundred and eight. Unit nineteen — The written word. Everything you read that is NOT a book.
📺 On screen: six Unit 18 job words in chalk — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: fire back reading-and-writing matches in pairs. Expect articles and posters to surface by themselves — greet them as old friends when ex.1 confirms them

WHAT WE DO: name the language aim and put up the two working walls of the lesson

WHAT WE DO: About you + ex.1 (track 91): match the ten words to photos A–J

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Think-Pair-Share on the About you questions: Do you like reading? What do you read? Printed magazines or digital — and why? Twenty seconds to think. Tell your partner. I ask two pairs.
  2. ex.1 in pairs, 3 minutes: match advert, article, brochure, e-book, graphic novel, note, notice, paper, poster, sticker to photos A–J — guessing from photo details is allowed and praised.
  3. Play track 91 to confirm. Key: A note · B e-book · C sticker · D paper · E advert · F poster · G graphic novel · H brochure · I article · J notice (track 91; audioscript TB p.278).
  4. Drill the sound traps chorally, three repetitions: advert /ˈædvɜːt/ — stress on AD · article /ˈɑːtɪkl/ · brochure /ˈbrəʊʃə/ — no tch sound · notice /ˈnəʊtɪs/. Fill the THINGS YOU READ wall as words are confirmed.
📺 On screen: track 91 in the whiteboard app (Backup: confirm the matches by voice from the key above — the photos are in every book, the audio only says what you can say yourself)
👦 Students do: discuss, match, justify from photo details (the FRAGILE sticker, the Torquay beach notice). Expect brochure/graphic novel to be guessed last. Typical error: brochure with a /tʃ/ sound

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: the classroom scavenger — the words are in the room

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: the three discussion questions with reasons attached

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frames on the board first: Most of what I read is…, because… · The advantage of printed … is… · I'd rather read … on paper.
  2. Kagan Timed Pair Share, 90 seconds each way: How often do you read each thing from ex.1? Printed or digital? Advantages of each?
  3. Collect two or three positions publicly and push for the reason every time: Digital — WHY?
  4. Mini-poll with hands: printed vs digital vs both — leave the count in the board corner; the writing lesson (book reviews go ONLINE) will call back to it.
📺 On screen: the three sentence frames in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: argue with frames, vote. Expect near-unanimous «digital» — play devil's advocate with the beach: sand, sun, batteries. Typical error: opinion with no reason — the frame catches it

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: what kind of text, where seen; Paula's email as the worked example

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hook, books closed: A sign can say NO in ten polite ways. Today you learn to read the politeness.
  2. ex.1 fast in pairs: texts 1–5 — what kind, where might you see them? Key: 1 notice — in a school · 2 text message — on a phone · 3 note — desk, book or bag · 4 sticker — beach or pool entrance · 5 competition advert — magazine or online.
  3. Pre-teach on the board: CV (curriculum vitae) · qualifications · knock (= hit the door to make a noise) · be accompanied by an adult.
  4. ex.2 together on text 0 (Paula's email): underline where Paula offers to help, who writes the CV, what she discussed. Key: 1 «We should discuss what qualifications and experience you can include» · 2 Jon is going to write his own CV · 3 She discussed what people need to do to apply for the job. Spell out CV = curriculum vitae.
  5. Model the rejection move out loud on the example: Option A says Paula will WRITE the CV. The text says «when YOU write yours». Different — reject.
📺 On screen: the texts are in every book on p.109 — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: orient, read the example, watch the rejection move. Expect «CV» to need the full unpacking — it returns in the Talking point. Typical problem: text 2 (the message to Deb) read as a stranger's text — it is family

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Why: 🎯 Speaking out · my reading map

WHAT WE DO: a two-round interview: what you read, where, printed or digital — with follow-ups

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. One minute solo: everyone lists five things they actually read this week (any language) and marks each P (printed) or D (digital).
  2. Kagan Timed Pair Share, 2 minutes each way: interview your partner about their list — What do you read every day? Where do you see adverts? Do you ever read notices — really? One follow-up per answer, minimum.
  3. Swap partners once (interviewers move one seat right) and repeat faster — this time the interviewer takes two notes for the report-back.
  4. Report-back round: four students report ONE surprising thing — Max reads graphic novels in English!
  5. You walk with a paper: log one-word answers and article/paper slips SILENTLY — they open lesson 2.
📺 On screen: the interview stems in chalk (What do you read…? · Where do you see…? · Printed or digital — why?) — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: interview, follow up, swap, report. Expect round 2 audibly faster — say so. Typical error: I read the paper in my phone — ON my phone; it goes on the error sheet, not into the conversation

WB Unit 19 (p.76–79)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can name the ten things you read, place each where it lives (a sticker on a parcel, a notice on a door), and choose the right reading of a short real-life text, saying why the other two options are wrong.
  • Subsidiary. Listening for which text type speakers mean (tracks 91–92); the printed-vs-digital discussion (SB p.108 ex.5); noticing the reported-speech lines in texts 2–3 for the next lesson.
  • Personal. Do not name the exam part out loud — the signs task is «careful reading». Log who defaults to one-word answers in the discussion stage and feed them the sentence frames.
By the end, students can…
  • name ten things you read and spell the traps (brochure, advert)
  • say where each thing lives — a notice on a door, a sticker on a parcel
  • choose the right reading of a sign and reject the wrong ones out loud
  • argue printed vs digital with one reason attached

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«A note or a notice?» — I write it to my sister and leave it on the table → a note. It hangs on the school door for everyone → a notice.
«A poster or an advert?» — is every poster selling something? → no: a concert poster announces; an advert always wants your money.
«STAFF ONLY — students are not permitted» — can I go in if I knock? → no: knocking calls a teacher OUT, it doesn't let you in.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
note/notice mixed up all lessonAnchor the pair on the two photos (A the yellow «Clean your room!» sticker note, J the beach notice) and point at them silently every time the words swap.
«advert» spelled from the Russian «реклама» sound-imageBoard the family once: advert = advertisement = ad; drill the stress (ADvert) and make pairs spell it back in the vocabulary stage.
The signs task answered by keyword-matching, not readingThe working rule: the right answer says the same thing in different words — the wrong ones reuse the words of the text. Make pairs quote the deciding line for two items before any answer is accepted.
Discussion stage collapses into «digital, because phone»Feed the frame from the board: Most of what I read is…, because… · The advantage of printed… is… One frame used = the answer counts.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
