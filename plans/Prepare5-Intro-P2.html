🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Retrieve last year's vocabulary across six fields — people, city and travel, food, technology, jobs, health — put it back into use in speech within one lesson, and take a fifteen-minute placement slice that shows exactly how much of it survived the summer.
What is genuinely hard
Nothing here is new, which is precisely the problem: students recognise every word and can produce almost none of them under time pressure. Recognition without retrieval feels like knowledge right up to the moment it is needed.
Typical error to expect
«I know this word» said about a word the student cannot produce. Today's whole design is built to make that gap visible and fixable — every station demands production, in writing or aloud, never a tick.
Why the lesson is built this way
Six stations, six fields, four minutes each — the class moves and writes continuously for twenty-four minutes and you say almost nothing. Then Quiz-Quiz-Trade puts the words back into questions, and a team quiz with Numbered Heads Together makes every student accountable for the answer. The placement slice comes at the end, when the language has been woken up — a measurement taken cold in the first twenty minutes of September would tell you nothing except that it is September.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“You learned about a thousand words last year. Some of them are asleep, not gone. Today we wake them up — six stations, six fields, and at the end fifteen minutes that tell me exactly where each of you is standing.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of retrieval, and the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what's the best thing about today so far? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — twelve words, ninety seconds. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs: one pen, one paper, take turns writing any twelve English words you learned last year. Say each one aloud as you write it.
  3. Swap with the pair behind and count. No marking, no correcting — just the number. Take the highest number in the room and put it on the board.
  4. Then the honest line that frames the whole lesson: Twelve in ninety seconds. You learned about a thousand. Today we go looking for the rest.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): set the day up: Six tables, six topics, four minutes each. Then fifteen minutes at the end that tell me where everyone is.
📺 On screen: the highest count written on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write in turns and hear their own number. It is the first honest measurement of the year and it takes ninety seconds

WHAT WE DO: name the aim and the difference between knowing and producing

Why: Six stations · six fields of Prepare 4

WHAT WE DO: twenty-four minutes of continuous production, four minutes per table

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six tables, one field each, four minutes each, groups rotate on your signal: A people and personality · B city, travel and transport · C food and taste · D technology and computers · E jobs and work · F health and the body.
  2. Every sheet has the same four-part shape, so no time is lost explaining: eight definitions → the word · five opposites · one odd-one-out line · three gapped sentences.
  3. Each group MARKS the previous group's sheet in a different colour before starting its own. That is the quality control and it takes thirty seconds.
  4. Floor and ceiling announced before they start: Five words is a pass at every table. If you finish, write two new items for the group coming next.
  5. Your job for twenty-four minutes is the clock and the signal. Do not teach, do not translate, do not sit down.
📺 On screen: six prepared A4 station sheets, printed before the lesson from the Prepare 4 map in this plan — nothing to fail
👦 Students do: every student writes at every table and marks somebody else's work six times. Twenty-four minutes of continuous production
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 26–31 (word formation and vocabulary in context). Six fields of vocabulary retrieved in writing is exactly the bank those items are drawn from.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: add a seventh table with six word-formation items (nation → national, care → careful, friend → unfriendly) and let the rotation absorb it, or give the same six as a five-minute fast-finisher card. Nothing else in the lesson moves.

Why: Quiz-Quiz-Trade · the words back into questions

WHAT WE DO: the harvested words turned into questions and traded around the room

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Every student takes ONE card from the pile you have made from the station sheets: a word on one side, its definition on the other.
  2. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: find a partner, read your definition, they guess the word; swap roles; trade cards and move on.
  3. Four minutes, fast. Announce the target before they start: Six partners minimum. Count them.
  4. Then thirty seconds of Kagan Mix-Pair-Share at the end: Tell your last partner which field was your weakest today. That answer is the one you want to overhear.
📺 On screen: the word cards in their hands — made in advance from the station sheets, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everybody speaks to at least six people and handles at least six different words

Move break · corners
standing, four corners
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that also sorts the class by self-assessment

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four corners of the room labelled: PEOPLE · PLACES · THINGS · WORK AND HEALTH.
  2. Call out fifteen words from today's stations, one at a time. Everyone walks to the corner where the word belongs.
  3. No corrections. After each word, one student in the biggest corner says the word in a sentence. Speed increases.
  4. Freeze and sit: Now the same words, but in teams — and this time you don't get to move.
📺 On screen: four labels in four corners — printed in advance, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone moves fifteen times and hears fifteen sentences. The sort is physical, so hesitation is visible and harmless

44–58′
Word maps + the team quiz
teams of four
Why: Word maps + the team quiz

WHAT WE DO: one map per team, then twenty questions with everyone accountable

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Teams of four. Each team gets ONE of the six fields and three minutes to build a word map on A3: the field in the middle, four branches, at least five words per branch.
  2. Maps go on the wall. Ninety seconds of silent gallery walk — everyone reads every map.
  3. Then the quiz, twenty questions, all built from today's language: definitions, opposites, spelling, one odd-one-out per round.
  4. Kagan Numbered Heads Together: teams confer, you call a number, THAT student answers for the team. It removes the always-the-same-hand problem completely.
  5. Two points for a correct answer, one extra for a correct spelling on the board. Keep the score visible and the pace fast.
📺 On screen: the six word maps on the wall, made by the class — chalk, paper and A3 only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: build a map in fours, read six maps, then answer as a team with individual accountability
A3 sheets ×6 · markers · board for scores

The placement slice
individual, silent
Why: The placement slice

WHAT WE DO: fifteen minutes of official questions that tell you where everybody is

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame it before the papers go out, in three sentences: the Cambridge Placement Test — 60 four-option questions, ten per level (1–10 Prepare 2 · 11–20 Prepare 3 · 21–30 Prepare 4 · 31–40 Prepare 5 · 41–50 Prepare 6 · 51–60 Prepare 7). It is the same serial pack used by every level of the course.
  2. for this group the working slice is questions 21–50, about fifteen minutes: 21–30 is an honest check on last year, 31–40 is the language of THIS year (a low score is normal in September), 41–50 is the ceiling for the strongest. Say the sentence that keeps the mood intact: There are questions on here from a level above yours. You are not supposed to know all of them.
  3. Rules: silence, no dictionaries, and if you don't know, leave it blank — a blank tells me more than a guess.
  4. Fifteen minutes, clock on the board, and you sit down. Do not walk the rows.
  5. Collect face down. Do not mark in class and do not discuss answers: You'll get these back with the answers next lesson.
📺 On screen: the clock and the finish time on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: work silently for fifteen minutes on official material, knowing exactly what it is for

Why: 🎯 Speaking out · the field I own

WHAT WE DO: the productive outcome: last year's vocabulary used for real, out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Each student chooses the field they felt strongest in today and prepares a ninety-second talk: Tell me about your town / your family / what you eat / your phone / a job you'd like. Two minutes of notes.
  2. The rule that makes it a language task, written on the board: At least SIX words from today's stations. Your partner counts them on their fingers.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share, ninety seconds each way, then swap partners and do it again. The second telling is always better.
  4. Three talks to the whole class. The room holds up fingers for the target words as they hear them — silent, instant, and it keeps everybody listening.
  5. Close with the honest line: Nothing today was new. All of it is yours already — it just needed waking up.
📺 On screen: the six-word rule on the board while they talk — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone talks for three minutes in total and listens with a counting job. The vocabulary ends the lesson in speech, not on a sheet

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Stations turn into copyingEach station sheet is a production task (definitions to words, opposites, odd one out, a gapped sentence), never a list to read. The next group marks the previous group's sheet, which makes copying pointless.
The weakest students freeze at the first stationGroups are mixed by design and each station has a floor: Five words is a pass at this table. Nobody sits at a table they cannot start.
The placement slice feels like an exam and the mood dropsIt is framed twice — before and after — as information for the teacher. Say the sentence that actually lands: There are questions on this from levels above yours. You are not supposed to know all of them.
Somebody finishes the slice in five minutesThey have guessed rather than read. The instruction is given before the papers go out: If you don't know, leave it blank. A blank tells me more than a guess.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
