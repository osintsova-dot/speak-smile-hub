🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Consolidate the whole block — social-media verbs, phrases with in, cooking verbs, the passive and modal passives, defining and non-defining relative clauses — and then produce it under test conditions in the two official Cambridge unit tests.
What is genuinely hard
Everything in this block is producible only if it is precise: the third form of the verb, the middle BE of a modal passive, the two commas of a non-defining clause, the exact wording of a phrase with in. A review lesson that only asks students to recognise things gives a false sense of readiness — so today every review task asks them to WRITE or SAY, not to choose.
Typical error to expect
«was took», «were showed», «is broke» — the passive takes the THIRD form. Half the marks in the passive section of the block test are irregular participles, not the rule.
«Pico's Café which is near the football ground is closing» — a non-defining clause lives between two commas, and in the Russian written tasks a missing comma is a punctuation error and therefore a lost accuracy mark.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is the review page and the photocopiable game, in that order: the page checks the language item by item and shows the class where the holes are, and the game — «LIST IT!» — puts the vocabulary under time pressure with points, which is the only way twelve cooking verbs get retrieved rather than recognised. Only the cards and lines that belong to Units 13 and 14 are played; the Units 15–16 cards are physically removed from the pile before the lesson so that nothing is 'skipped' in front of the class. The exam corner of the review page — a Reading Part 5 on chilli peppers whose four options are this block's cooking verbs — is the last thing before the test, and it doubles as a vocabulary check. The test itself is briefed honestly: what is in it, how long, and the two places marks are actually lost.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty minutes of play, then forty minutes of paper. Everything on the test today, you have done in this room. If you doubt a question, I will name the lesson it came from.”

Why: Retrieval against a clock is the only part of a review lesson that predicts test performance — recognition tasks do not.

WHAT WE DO: the photocopiable review game, played on the Units 13–14 cards only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in forty seconds, and they are worth reading out properly: «LIST IT!» — PREPARE_5_Review_game_Units_13-16.pdf, printed and cut along the dotted lines. Teams of two or three, one pile of cards face down. A player picks a card and reads it out — List five foods you can bake. Every team races to write FIVE items and shouts Listed!; the others stop. You check the five are valid — if not, Keep writing! Scoring: one point for the first valid five, one bonus point for every item nobody else wrote, and one more point if the whole list is spelled correctly. Students will argue about what counts; that is the best English in the game, so referee it, do not shut it down.
  2. The pile: play only the nine cards whose answer needs Unit 13 or Unit 14 language — foods you can roast · reasons to block someone online · things people do in advance · foods you usually barbecue · foods you can bake · reasons you boil water · foods you usually fry · foods you can stir · things that taste sweet. The other twelve cards (sports centre facilities, types of performance, famous film directors, places where you can see ruins and the rest) are Units 15–16 and are played in block 8 — take them out of the pile before the lesson rather than skipping them in front of the class
  3. Nine cards, eight minutes, hard stop. Announce the last card when you get there.
  4. Referee actively: when a team argues that cake counts as a food you can barbecue, give them thirty seconds to argue it in English and then decide. That argument is the best language in the game.
  5. Keep the score on the board where everyone can see it. The spelling point is worth naming aloud each round — this block has barbecue and freeze in it, and both get spelled wrong.
  6. Follow-up, one minute: everyone adds two new words from another team's list to their own notebook.
📺 On screen: the running score on the board and the card pile face down — paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone writes in every round, and the whole team argues its own list in English

Form
everything from lessons 1–6 of this block, produced rather than recognised: be + 3rd form · modal + be + 3rd form · who / which / whose / where · two commas or none.
Pron. watch
the eight social-media verbs are easy to say and hard to stress in a sentence: the class will put the accent on the pronoun (I TAGGED her) where English puts it on the new information · the phrases with in run together and the vowel of in reduces to a schwa: /ɪnfækt/, /ɪnəˈvɑːns/, /ɪnpəˈtɪkjələ/ — say them as one word or they sound like a list · in the passive, was and were are weak forms: /wəzˈteɪkən/, /wəˈʃəʊn/, which is why students do not hear the passive in the listening
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«must be used» — how many words? → three, and the middle one is the one people forget.
«My bike, which goes really fast, wasn't expensive.» How many commas? → two. One comma is a mistake, not half an answer.
In the test, if you write take down it instead of take it down — is that a vocabulary mistake or a grammar one? → it is a lost mark either way, which is why we drilled it.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper) and tasks 12–18, since the review page ends with a Reading Part 5 on chilli peppers.

The block test is built the way the exam block is: produce the form and spell it correctly. Two ghosts are named aloud in the briefing because they cost whole marks — the past participle in the passive (was taken, not was took) and the comma in a non-defining relative clause, which in the Russian written tasks is a punctuation mark and therefore an accuracy mark.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
