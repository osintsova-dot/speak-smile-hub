🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use eleven words for nature and wildlife (habitat, population, crops, creatures, endangered, environment, landscape, jungle, rainforest, hunting, humans) and build conditional sentences — zero, first, unless, and then the second — choosing the type by asking how real the situation is.
What is genuinely hard
The tense sequence, in both directions. English refuses will in the if-half of a first conditional and refuses would in the if-half of a second, and Russian does neither of those things. Then unless, which already contains the negative and is therefore doubled by half of any group. And the words themselves are long and misspelled: environment, endangered, population.
Typical error to expect
«If it will rain, we will stay at home» — the if-half takes the present simple. This is the single most reliable conditional error of Russian-speaking candidates and it is on the block test twice.
«Unless you don't hurry, we'll be late» — unless already contains the negative. The double negative is a direct translation of the Russian и is marked wrong every time.
Why the lesson is built this way
Three teenagers, three endangered species, one recording — the words arrive attached to arguments (bees pollinate crops, so food will cost more) rather than to definitions, which is also how they will have to be used in the speaking exam. The reading is a genuine exam task and the hardest reading of the block: a multiple-choice cloze where every option is a real word and only one keeps the right company. The grammar is built in the order the book builds it — zero first, because it is always true and therefore safe; then first, because it is real; then unless as a rewriting of if not; and the second conditional last, because it is the one that needs the others to exist first.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Ninety-five per cent of the world's tigers have gone. A female orangutan has one baby every eight or nine years, and the forest she lives in is being cut down. Today: the words for that, and the grammar for saying what happens if we do nothing — and what would happen if we did.”

Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that pre-teaches the conditional types physically

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands. Three positions in the room: ALWAYS TRUE by the window, REALLY POSSIBLE in the middle, IMAGINARY by the door.
  2. You read a sentence and they walk to the right place: If you drop a stone, it falls. / If we don't stop hunting, tigers will disappear. / If I had a rainforest, I'd protect it.
  3. Nine sentences. Do not explain anything yet — the sorting IS the pre-teaching, and it makes the grammar stage twelve minutes shorter.
  4. Freeze and sit: Page fifty-nine. One orangutan, six gaps, and four possible words in every one.
📺 On screen: the three room positions named aloud and pointed at — no technology, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: sort nine sentences with their feet before any rule is stated

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3 = B1 Reading Part 5: six gaps, four options each, decided by collocation

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1, two minutes: the fact file. Three questions — where do they live, are they endangered, how many species could survive? Answers are on the page and the point is to read it, not to know it.
  2. Background, said in two sentences because it explains the whole text: orangutans are kept as status symbols and taken from the wild; the law has existed since 1931 and does not work; a female has one baby every eight or nine years, and the forest is being burned and cut.
  3. The method on the board, and it is DIFFERENT from the last cloze: Not what it means. What it goes WITH.
  4. ex.2 individually, six minutes, clock visible. Rule: write the PARTNER word in the margin next to every answer.
  5. Pairs compare — partner words first, letters second.
  6. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: 1 C, 2 A, 3 B, 4 D, 5 A, 6 B. For every gap, ask why one of the other three fails; the Teacher's Book explains all of them.
  7. ex.3: the five highlighted words, two minutes in pairs.

Grammar · zero, first, and unless
class → individual → pairs
Why: Grammar · zero, first, and unless

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–4: two types out of the text, then unless as a rewriting of if not

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three columns on the board: IF / WHEN · ACTION · RESULT. Give three situations (you miss the bus, you leave your phone on all night, you don't have breakfast) and let the class fill the columns. The zero conditional appears without being named.
  2. ex.1: the four examples matched to the two rules — 1 and 2 to the zero, 3 and 4 to the first. Write both patterns under the columns.
  3. Then the line that prevents half the block's errors, written once and left up: NO WILL AFTER IF.
  4. ex.2, three minutes: five sentence halves joined — 1 c, 2 a, 3 d, 4 e, 5 b. Two of them are first conditionals and the class says which.
  5. ex.3: two sentences with unless. Elicit the equation and write it next to the no-will line: unless = if … not.
  6. ex.4 individually, two minutes: five items, unless or if. Check by substitution — every unless answer is re-said as if not, and if it produces two negatives the answer is wrong.
📺 On screen: the three columns, the two patterns, the no-will line and the unless equation — all on the board, all staying up
👦 Students do: build the zero conditional from their own situations, then join five halves and test five unless sentences by substitution

Grammar · the second conditional
class → individual
Why: Grammar · the second conditional

WHAT WE DO: ex.5–6: the imaginary half of the room, and the form that goes with it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Point back at the door — the IMAGINARY position from the move break. Two sentences on the board: If I have time, I'll help you. / If I had a boat, I'd sail to the island. One question: which one is likely?
  2. ex.5: the class identifies the second example and states the pattern — if + past simple, would + infinitive. Write it under the other two patterns so all three are visible together.
  3. The warning, said once and clearly: Past tense does NOT mean past time here. «If I had» means «I don't have, now». This is the sentence that unlocks it for Russian speakers.
  4. ex.6 individually, four minutes: seven items across all three types. Check by asking for the TYPE first, then the form — you go, would, you went, you agree, would, I have, we meet.
  5. Two minutes of repair: take the two items the class got wrong most and re-do them on the board with the pattern underneath.
📺 On screen: the three conditional patterns written one under another — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: identify the type before producing the form, seven times

WHAT WE DO: ex.7: three stems finished honestly, then a chain that forces all three types

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the three stems on the board: If I had the chance to see any animal in the wild, … · When I read about animals in danger, … · If I saw an injured animal on the road, …
  2. Three minutes, silent: finish all three in writing, and write the TYPE in the margin next to each.
  3. 🎯 Kagan Timed Pair Share, two minutes each way: read your three and say which type each one is. The listener disagrees if the type is wrong.
  4. Then the chain, five minutes, standing in two lines facing each other: A says a first-conditional sentence about the environment, B answers with a consequence that begins with the end of A's sentence. If we don't recycle, the rubbish will grow. → If the rubbish grows, the rivers will get dirty. → … Longest chain wins.
  5. Take the two longest chains to the floor and count the links aloud.
  6. Talking points if the clock allows: How important is it to protect animals in the wild? What could you personally do? Is enough being done to save the rainforest? — pairs first, then the floor
📺 On screen: the three stems and the three patterns on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write three conditionals with their types, defend them, then build a chain of five or six in a standing line

Structure
If bees disappear, food gets more expensive. · If we don't protect them, they will die out. · Unless we act now, it will be too late. · If I saw an injured animal, I would call someone.
Form
zero conditional: if/when + present simple, present simple · first: if + present simple, will/won't · unless = if … not · second: if + past simple, would + infinitive.
Pron. watch
environment /ɪnˈvaɪrənmənt/ — five syllables, and the middle one disappears in fast speech, which is why it is misspelled · creatures /ˈkriːtʃəz/ · jungle /ˈdʒʌŋɡl/ · endangered /ɪnˈdeɪndʒəd/ · and the at-phrases are said as one word: /ətˈliːst/, /ətˈwʌns/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«If you heat water to 100 degrees, it boils.» Is that sometimes or always? → always. That is the zero conditional.
«If we don't protect them, they will disappear.» Is it possible? → yes, really possible. First conditional.
«If I had a million roubles…» Do I have it? → no. That is why the verb is past and the other half is would.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
will appears in the if-halfOne line on the board and nothing else: NO WILL AFTER IF. Every offence gets the same four words and the sentence is repeated correctly. Do not explain it a second time.
unless is doubled with a negativeWrite unless = if not on the board and make the class say the pair aloud twice. Then every unless sentence is tested by substitution: if the if-not version has two negatives, it is wrong.
The cloze is answered by translationThe partner question goes on the board and stays: What must this word go WITH? In the check, ask for the partner word before the letter.
Fast finishers in the vocabulary setThe Teacher's Book task: write two more definitions in the same style and hand them to another pair. Theirs get used in the check.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
