🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Repair the language of Units 1–5 where this group actually lost marks, using the Reach higher pages (SB p.136–138) — the only pages in the book that make students PRODUCE a unit's language from an empty prompt instead of choosing it from options.
What is genuinely hard
A reserve lesson has no content of its own, so it fails in one of two ways: it becomes a lecture on everything, or it becomes a quiz that entertains and repairs nothing. The language itself is not hard — used to and would, past perfect simple against continuous, future forms, comparatives, past modals — but it is eight months old, and eight-month-old grammar behaves like new grammar. What is genuinely hard is honesty: a seventeen-year-old will happily spend the hour at the station they are already good at.
Typical error to expect
«I will go to the cinema but it rained» inside a past narrative — a plan made in the past is was/were going to, would or was about to. Russian uses the future tense inside past narration quite happily, so the transfer is automatic and invisible.
Why the lesson is built this way
Diagnosis first, and it comes from evidence: the marked Test yourself pages and the Cumulative review they did for homework, not from a show of hands about feelings. Each student names their own two weakest units and goes to those stations. Everything at every station is in their own book, so nothing has to be printed and no station can run dry. Two rotations, a stirrer, two more rotations, and then the part that makes it a lesson rather than a worksheet session: each group teaches the room the one thing it fixed, and you re-teach only the three lines that are still wrong across the whole class. Unit 6 sits on the same page and is deliberately held over to the next lesson, where Units 6–10 are the material.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“This lesson is yours, and it is built from your own papers. You each pick the two units that cost you the most marks this year and you go and fix them — the book has the tasks, I have the answers, and at the end each group teaches everybody else the one thing it repaired. There is no new language today. There is only the language you already met and lost.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, and one honest sentence each about last night's exam pages

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — what is the last thing you read or watched that was NEWS? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.
  2. 2 minutes — yesterday's three habits. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share, standing: Which of the three exam habits from last lesson did you actually use in the Workbook pages — and did it work? Thirty seconds each way, then swap partner and repeat.
  3. 1 minute — two answers to the floor, and one line of feedback from you on the Exam trainer pages: the one thing the class did well and the one thing it did not.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Today has no new language in it at all. Today you repair the units that cost you marks — and you choose which ones.

Frame + self-diagnosis from evidence

Why: A reserve lesson only earns its ninety minutes if it aims at the actual damage. Six minutes of evidence-based diagnosis buys that aim and gives every student a reason to be at their table.

WHAT WE DO: each student names their own two weakest units from their marked papers, and the five stations are set

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Papers out: the marked Test yourself pages and the Cumulative review from Workbook p.93. Nothing here is done from memory.
  2. Three minutes, individually and in writing: On the top of a clean page, write the two units of the year where your own paper says you lost the most. Not the ones you disliked — the ones with the crosses.
  3. Hands up by unit as you read the numbers one to five, and write the count on the board. The class now sees its own shape and so do you.
  4. Set the five stations, one per table: Unit 1 school and study · Unit 2 work · Unit 3 travel · Unit 4 personality · Unit 5 money. Everything is on SB p.136–138 — nothing is printed.
  5. Explain the route in one sentence: You go to your own two stations first. After the break you go to two more, and the last one is free choice. Groups of three or four, made by the counts on the board.
📺 On screen: the five unit names and the hand-count written on the board — that count stays up and is used again at 63′
👦 Students do: identify their two weakest units in writing from marked evidence, then join the matching station

WHAT WE DO: a fast Showdown that wakes up the vocabulary of the whole first half of the year

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Showdown: one mini-whiteboard per student, all boards up on the word.
  2. One minute per unit, two prompts each. Unit 1: DO or MAKE — an exam, an effort, progress, your best. Unit 2: You lose your job because the company does not need it any more — you are …

WHAT WE DO: two eight-minute rotations at the Reach higher tasks of the units each student chose

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Eight minutes per station, and the same three-part routine everywhere so that no instructions are needed after the first round.
  2. Part one — vocabulary from the definitions (2 minutes). The Vocabulary in context task at the top of the unit block: they produce the word or the definition, aloud, round the table.
  3. Part two — the grammar rewrite (4 minutes). The Grammar in context task of the block, written individually, then compared. This is the part that is worth the lesson: the prompt gives no options at all.
  4. Part three — the group sentence (2 minutes). Each student writes ONE sentence of their own using the target form on a mini-whiteboard. All boards up. The group agrees which sentence is the best and copies it onto the station card — that card is what they will teach from later.
  5. You do not teach. You walk, listen, and write down every error you hear on a sheet of paper. You will need it at 63′.
  6. At eight minutes: Stand, take your book, move one table clockwise.
📺 On screen: no display at all — every task is in the students' own book on p.136–138, one unit per table
👦 Students do: produce vocabulary aloud, write a grammar transformation individually, then write and show one sentence of their own

Move break · one sentence, three partners
standing, whole room
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that makes them say the sentence they just wrote to three different people

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share with a rule: you may only say the sentence your group agreed at the last station, and your partner has to say which unit it came from.
  2. Three partners, forty seconds each, music optional and hands in the air to find a partner.
  3. Then one round to the floor: two sentences, and the room names the grammar in each.
  4. Sit down at the NEXT station, not the old one: Two more rotations, then you teach us what you fixed.
📺 On screen: nothing to display — voices and movement only
👦 Students do: say their sentence to three different partners and identify which unit each partner's sentence came from

Why: Stations 3 and 4 · one more weak spot, one free choice

WHAT WE DO: two more eight-minute rotations, the last one chosen freely, with the phrasal-verb or suffix set added

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Same routine, same clock. The only change: at these two stations they also do the Developing vocabulary task of the unit block — the phrasal verbs, the prefixes or the suffixes.
  2. Unit 1 station adds the do/make story · Unit 2 adds keep, get, fill, turn, set · Unit 3 adds the six prefixes · Unit 4 adds the noun suffixes · Unit 5 adds pay back, set aside, cut down on, splash out, get by, chip in.
  3. The Numbered Heads check at the end of each rotation, ninety seconds: you call a number, that student answers for the group on one item you choose. Nobody knows in advance, so everybody keeps up.
  4. The fourth rotation is free choice — say so, and mean it: Last table is yours. Go where you know you are still weak.
  5. Keep collecting errors on your sheet. By now you should have eight or ten, and three of them will be the same three.
📺 On screen: no display — the book, the cards and the mini-whiteboards, exactly as in the first pair of rotations
👦 Students do: complete two more station cycles, add a lexical set at each, and answer for the group when their number is called

WHAT WE DO: Team Stand-N-Share: one minute per group, the repair explained and one example given

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Team Stand-N-Share: the group stands, gives its one repair and sits. One minute each, and you hold the minute.
  2. The shape is fixed and written on the board so that nobody improvises: The unit was … The thing we got wrong was … The rule is … Here is our sentence.
  3. Everybody else writes one line per group in their notebooks. By the end of the stage every student has five lines they did not have at the start — that is their revision sheet for the mock.
  4. You add at most one sentence per group and only if something said was actually wrong. Resist finishing their explanations for them.
  5. If two groups repaired the same thing, say so out loud — it tells the room what to revise.
📺 On screen: the four-line frame written on the board and left up so that every group uses the same shape
👦 Students do: explain one repair to the class in a fixed four-line shape and take one line of notes from every other group

The three lines that are still wrong
whole class
Why: The three lines that are still wrong

WHAT WE DO: twelve minutes of real re-teaching, built entirely from the errors collected during the rotations

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Look at your error sheet and pick THREE. Not five, not everything — three. In a typical group they are: used to used for a state where would was wanted, the past perfect continuous dropped in favour of the simple, and future forms chosen by feeling rather than by meaning.
  2. For each one, the same ninety-second cycle: put the wrong sentence on the board exactly as you heard it, ask the room what is wrong with it, take the repair, and write the rule in ONE line.
  3. Then two production items per line, on mini-whiteboards, all boards up at once. Not discussion — production. When I was ten I ____ play computer games for hours. · Her hands were dirty because she ____ in the garden all morning. · Next year the city ____ a new transport system.
  4. Say where each line came from — this is Unit 2, page 21 — so that revision has an address, not just a feeling.
  5. Close the stage by pointing back at the hand-count from minute eleven: the room's three weakest lines have just been repaired in front of everybody.
📺 On screen: the three wrong sentences written on the board in the students' own words, then repaired next to them — chalk only, nothing prepared
👦 Students do: diagnose three real errors, produce six corrected sentences on mini-whiteboards, and note where each rule lives in the book
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 19–31 (grammar, word formation and lexis). WHAT WE DO: every student writes one revision question, then the cards circulate round the room

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two minutes: each student writes ONE question on a card from any of Units 1–5, with the answer on the back. It must be answerable in one sentence.
  2. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: quiz your partner, get quizzed, coach if needed, swap cards, find a new partner. Five minutes, four or five partners each.
  3. One rule that keeps the quality up: if your partner cannot answer, you do not give the answer — you give a clue and let them get it. That is why the answer is on the back and not read out.
  4. Collect the cards at the end. They are the warm-up for the next lesson and they cost you nothing to prepare.

Structure
the five grammar lines of Units 1–5, produced not recognised: present perfect simple against continuous · used to and would · past perfect simple against continuous · future forms · comparatives · past modals and speculation
Form
no new form is presented. Every station gives a bare prompt and the student supplies the form — that is the difference between Reach higher and a revision exercise, and it is the whole reason this page is worth a lesson.
Pron. watch
eight months of vocabulary come back mispronounced, and two families cause most of it: the -ion nouns move their stress (proMOTE → proMOtion, EDucate → eduCAtion) and the money idioms are said as one unit with one main stress (cost an ARM and a LEG · tighten your BELT). Drill each one the moment it appears at a station, not at the end.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I've been studying French for three years» and «I've studied French» — which one tells you it is still going on? → the continuous. Which one cares about the result? → the simple.
«When I was small, I used to have a skateboard» — can you use would here? → no: would works for repeated actions, not for states. And «we would play for hours»? → yes, that is a repeated action.
«She must arrive on time every morning» in the past → she HAD to arrive. Why not «must have arrived»? → that is a guess about the past, not an obligation in the past.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Students choose the station they are already good atWhich is why the choice is made from the marked papers in writing, not from memory. Say it once and hold it: You go to the two units your own paper says you lost marks in. If your paper says you are fine everywhere, you go to the two you liked least.
The stations turn into copying the answers from the strongest studentEach station ends with a Numbered Heads check where you call the number, so any student may have to answer for the group. And every station has a mini-whiteboard sentence that has to be written by each person individually before the group moves on.
The teacher gets stuck at one station for eight minutesDo not teach at the stations — walk, listen and write down what is wrong. The teaching happens in one block at 63–75, and it is shorter and better because you have collected real evidence for twenty minutes.
A snow day cuts the lesson to half its lengthThen run: diagnosis (6 minutes), one pair of rotations (16), the clinic (12), wrap-up. It works, and it is exactly what a reserve lesson is designed to survive.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 10–18 and tasks 19–31 — but today the exam is genuinely background: the lesson is a language clinic.

Reach higher is the only page of the book that asks students to PRODUCE the language of a unit from an empty prompt — write a sentence with the present perfect continuous, explain a collocation, rewrite with a relative clause. That is the same demand the Russian written paper makes in tasks 19–31, where nothing is chosen from options. Running it as stations lets the strong students go to Units 4 and 5 while the ones who lost marks on the past perfect rebuild it, which is exactly what a reserve lesson is for.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
