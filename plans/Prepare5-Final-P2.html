🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Mark their own mock paper against the official key, classify every loss as a slip, a gap or a misunderstanding, and turn the result into three named, fixable lines to revise from over the summer.
What is genuinely hard
A returned paper teaches almost nothing on its own, because a mark is a verdict and a verdict cannot be acted on. What can be acted on is a classification: this one I knew and wrote badly, this one I have never known, this one I read wrongly. Three different problems with three different cures, and teenagers do not make the distinction unless they are made to.
Typical error to expect
«I was just careless». Sometimes true, usually a way of avoiding the second category. The rule today: a slip is something you can correct in five seconds without looking anything up. If you cannot, it is a gap, and it goes in the log.
Reading the key and moving on. An answer that has been read is not an answer that has been understood. Every wrong item gets a WHY written beside it, in any language.
Why the lesson is built this way
The students mark, because the person holding the pen is the person learning. The paper is returned unmarked with the official key beside it, and the marking is done section by section with a stop after each block for the items that the room got wrong together — those are the only ones worth reteaching. The listening is replayed with the audioscript in hand, which is the single most effective five minutes in any review lesson, because it converts «I didn't hear it» into «I heard it and I did not recognise it». The writing gets its own stage and its own criteria. The lesson ends with the EGE map — the year's exam work finally made explicit, because now there is evidence to attach it to.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“You get your paper back with no marks on it and the official key beside it. You do the marking. And by the end you will have three lines — not a score, three lines — which are the only thing you can actually do something about in August.”

Why: Predicting before marking makes the marking active, and the gap between the prediction and the result is the fastest route to an honest self-assessment.

WHAT WE DO: the mock returned with no marks on it and the key beside it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — the papers back, by name, face down, and with nothing written on them. Say that clearly as you hand them out, because they will look for a number and not find one.
  2. 1 minute — Kagan RallyCoach: in pairs, thirty seconds each way, before anything is opened — which section do you THINK you did best on, and which worst? Write both guesses at the top of the paper.
  3. Those two guesses get checked at the end of the marking, and the gap between the guess and the reality is often the most useful thing in the lesson.
  4. Bridge (30 seconds): No marks. The key is on the desk. You are the examiner today.
📺 On screen: no technology in the opening — the papers and the printed key only, and the papers are theirs
👦 Students do: take back an unmarked paper and commit to two public predictions about their own performance

Why: The classification is the whole method of the lesson and it has to exist before the first item is marked, or everything gets called carelessness.

WHAT WE DO: name the aim and put up the three-way classification

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three headed columns on the board and they run the whole lesson: SLIP (I can fix it in five seconds, unaided) · GAP (I still do not know it) · MISREAD (I knew it and answered the wrong question).
  2. Say the rule that stops «I was just careless» being a hiding place: if you cannot fix it in five seconds without looking anything up, it is not a slip.
  3. Say the shape in 30 seconds: mark it yourself, classify every loss, listen again with the script in your hand, then three lines that you can revise from in August.
  4. Say the honest framing, because somebody needs to hear it: This paper covers six units in ninety minutes with no help. It is built to find holes. Finding eight holes in May is good news.

Why: Marking · vocabulary, sections 1–4

WHAT WE DO: thirty-two items marked, then the ones the ROOM got wrong

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Marking rule on the board: tick, or write the correct answer AND one letter in the margin — S, G or M.
  2. Six minutes, individually and silently, sections 1 to 4. The key is on the desk, one per pair, and it does not move.
  3. Spot-check three papers as you walk, cheerfully and at random. You said you would.
  4. Then the only part worth class time: Hands up for anyone who lost item 6 in section 1. Go through the sections and find the items the ROOM lost, not the items individuals lost. Usually there are three or four.
  5. Reteach those three or four, and nothing else, for four minutes. Use Kagan Numbered Heads Together so the explanation comes from a student who got it right.
  6. Two minutes: everybody counts their G items in sections 1 to 4 and writes the number in a circle at the top of the page.
📺 On screen: the class-wide wrong items listed on the board as they are found — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: mark thirty-two of their own items, classify each loss with a letter, then explain a shared error to the room

WHAT WE DO: thirty-two more items, and the three that always hurt

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six minutes, individually and silently, sections 5 to 8, same marking rule.
  2. Then the three predictable ones, and stop on each for two minutes because they are the year's own themes: articles (section 5 — the zero article is the one nobody believes in), reported speech (section 6 — the backshift and the word order), and the linking words (section 7 — despite against although, which the class met twice this year).
  3. Run each with Kagan Showdown: read the item again, everybody writes the answer, everybody reveals together. It takes forty seconds and it tells you whether the reteaching worked.
  4. Two minutes: everybody adds their G count for sections 5 to 8 to the circle at the top of the page. That circled number, not the score, is what the rest of the lesson works on.
📺 On screen: the three themes written up with one corrected example each — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: mark thirty-two more items, then reveal a corrected answer simultaneously with the whole room three times

Move break · say it correctly, standing
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies — and in an examination week that is truer, not less true.

WHAT WE DO: four minutes between two heavy stages

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands. You read one of the items the class got wrong — the WRONG version.
  2. The whole room says the correct version out loud, together. Then one student says why the wrong one was tempting.
  3. Eight items, eight choruses. Saying a correct form aloud in unison is not a drill for its own sake; it is the only thing in the lesson that puts the right version in the mouth rather than on the page.
  4. Freeze and sit: Now the listening — and this time you have the script.
📺 On screen: no technology in this stage — voices only, and nothing can fail
👦 Students do: everybody produces eight corrected forms aloud, standing

The listening, with the script in your hand
individual → pairs → class
Why: The listening, with the script in your hand

WHAT WE DO: the five most effective minutes in any review lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the audioscript from the answer key, one per pair. Do not let them read it yet.
  2. Play the recording again, whole, with the papers open at section 9. Just listen — no writing.
  3. NOW turn the script over. Three minutes, in pairs: find the exact line that decides each of the five statements and underline it.
  4. The two that matter are items 2 and 4, and they are worth four minutes between them. Item 2 is false because Maria says you can find out a lot online and BEGIN TO FEEL you know them — which is not the same as knowing them, and Ben says so explicitly. Item 4 is false because it is Maria who says celebrities have responsibilities, not Ben.
  5. Say the transferable rule and write it on the board: The statement is a PARAPHRASE. If the words match, suspect it.
  6. Play the two decisive stretches once more, thirty seconds each, and ask what they can hear now that they could not hear yesterday. The honest answer is usually «nothing new — I just wasn't listening for meaning», and that is the lesson.
📺 On screen: PRE-TT-3S_ex.10.mp3 replayed, with the printed audioscript in their hands. Backup: if the audio will not play, the script alone does most of the work — read the two decisive stretches aloud yourself and run the underlining task unchanged
👦 Students do: listen once more without writing, then find and underline the five decisive lines in the script
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 1–9 (the listening section). The statement is always a paraphrase and the option that repeats the spoken words is the trap. Hearing WHY a distractor worked, with the script in hand, transfers to the Russian paper completely.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: after the underlining task, give one three-item Russian-format extract for five minutes. Take the time from the reading stage, which works with three questions instead of five.

The reading, option by option
pairs → class
Why: The reading, option by option

WHAT WE DO: why the wrong answer was attractive

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Section 10, papers open, key on the desk. Two minutes to mark and classify.
  2. Then the real work, and it is not about the right answers: for each of the five questions, the pair has to say what the WRONG option was offering and why somebody would choose it.
  3. Question 1 is the model. The right answer is that Jim's parents wanted to feel less worried and more relaxed; the wrong one says their jobs had changed. Both jobs ARE mentioned in the paragraph — which is exactly why the wrong option works.
  4. Six minutes in pairs on the other four, then take three explanations to the floor with Kagan Numbered Heads Together.
  5. Write the rule beside the listening one: A wrong option is usually TRUE about the text but does not answer the QUESTION. Those two sentences together are most of a reading strategy.
📺 On screen: the two strategy rules — the listening one and the reading one — written up side by side and left for the last two lessons of the year
👦 Students do: mark five items, then explain the attraction of five wrong options in pairs

Why: The writing, against criteria

WHAT WE DO: the section that cannot be marked with a key

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand back their section 11 with four category letters in the margin and no score: C content, O organisation, R range, A accuracy — with a tick or a question mark against each.
  2. Two minutes, silent, reading their own text and their own four letters.
  3. Put the three questions the review should have answered back on the board — what it was, what was good or bad, who should see it — and give three minutes to check their own text against them, marking in the margin where each is answered. Anyone who cannot find one has just diagnosed their own C.
  4. Kagan Timed Pair Share, four minutes, two each way: read your partner's review and say ONE sentence of praise tied to a letter and ONE question tied to a letter.
  5. Four minutes: choose the WEAKEST of your four letters and rewrite just the paragraph it belongs to, above the line.
  6. Say what happens next: the second draft is tonight's homework, and it is the only piece of the week that gets marked.
📺 On screen: the four category letters and the three review questions, both on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: diagnose their own text against three questions, hear a partner's reading of it, then rewrite one paragraph

Why: 🎯 The error log · three lines

WHAT WE DO: the stage the whole lesson exists for

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The format goes on the board and there is no negotiation about it: TOPIC + WHAT I DO WRONG + ONE EXAMPLE FROM MY OWN PAPER.
  2. Do one on the board yourself, from a real anonymous paper: Reported questions: I keep the question word order — I wrote «he asked me where was I».
  3. 🎯 Six minutes, silent, individually: three lines, on a card, with their name on it. Three, not five and not one. Walk the room and reject anything that says «grammar» or «vocabulary».
  4. Kagan RallyCoach, three minutes: read your three to a partner. The partner's job is one question — Could you revise that from what you have written? If the answer is no, it gets rewritten.
  5. Collect the cards and photograph them, then give them straight back. Say what they are for, twice: This goes inside the cover of your notebook. In August you revise from these three lines and nothing else.
  6. Take the two guesses at the top of each paper into account publicly for thirty seconds: hands up, whose best-section guess was right? Whose worst? It is a good-humoured ending to a demanding stage.
📺 On screen: the log format and one real example on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write three specific, revisable lines about their own work and defend each to a partner

Why: The exam has been a footnote all year, on purpose. It is made explicit once, at the point where there is real evidence to attach it to, and then it goes back to being a footnote.

WHAT WE DO: four minutes of the year's exam work, finally made explicit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. This is the one moment in the year when the EGE is named to the class as a whole, and it is named because there is now evidence on the desk to attach it to.
  2. Put the six blocks on the board with their Russian-exam addresses, one line each, and read them out. No detail, no lecture — a map, not a lesson.
  3. Say the honest sentence, exactly as it has been said all year: Not everybody in this room will sit that exam. If you will, the map tells you which parts of today matter most. If you will not, nothing changes — the English is the same.
  4. Point at the two strategy rules still on the board from the listening and reading stages and say that they transfer completely.
  5. Then stop. Four minutes is the correct length for this stage and eight would undo it.

Wrap-up + homework
desks → door
Why: Self-assessment against the aim they heard at the start, so the lesson closes the loop it opened — and in these four lessons the loop is the year, not the hour.

WHAT WE DO: self-check against today's aim, then the homework said once and written on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to the aim: Thumbs up if there are three lines on your card and you could revise from all three of them.
  2. Exit at the door: each student reads me line one of their log. Out loud, once. That is the whole exit ticket.
  3. Homework said once and written up: the review rewritten, about 100 words, with the first version stapled behind it. Twenty minutes, and it is the only marked homework of the week. Plus five minutes copying the three lines into your notebook cover.
  4. Say what is next: Next lesson we revise the year — and the revision comes out of your three lines, not out of my list.

Mock writing, second draft
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can classify their own errors as slips, gaps or misreadings and write a three-line error log that is specific enough to revise from.
  • Subsidiary. Listening again with the script to see what was missed; understanding why a rejected option was wrong; improving a written text against criteria.
  • Personal. You have the papers and you have your own notes from watching them work. Use the notes, not the marks — the student who scored well but never checked anything needs a different sentence from the one who ran out of time.
By the end, students can…
  • mark a paper honestly against an official key
  • say the difference between a slip and a gap in their own work
  • explain why a listening distractor was attractive
  • name three specific things to revise, not «grammar»

Where each section lands in the EGEListening (section 9) EGE tasks 1–9. Five true-or-false statements on a two-person conversation is the same machine as the Russian listening: the statement is a PARAPHRASE, never the speaker's own words, and the candidate who hunts for a repeated word is being led to the wrong answer on purpose. Item 4 is the proof — Ben never says celebrities behave badly, Maria does. · Reading (section 10) EGE tasks 12–18. Two options instead of three or four, so it is an easier version of the same skill: find the place in the text, then read that part carefully. Question 1 asks WHY, which is the item type that decides the Russian reading score. · Vocabulary 1–4 EGE tasks 32–38 (lexical choice) and, for the anagram section, tasks 26–31 (word formation). Same field, same words, different wrapper — a phrasal verb with the wrong preposition scores nothing in either exam. · Grammar 5–8 EGE tasks 19–25. Articles, reported speech, linkers and the mixed cloze are four of the named lines of the Russian grammar codifier. Section 8 in particular — a gapped email with three options per gap — is the Russian task in Cambridge clothes. · Writing (section 11) EGE task 37 (the personal email) and task 38 (the opinion piece). A film review is neither, but it is marked on the same three things: does it answer the task, is it organised, is the language varied and accurate. The Russian paper adds a fixed word count and three questions of your own. · Speaking (section 12) the EGE oral section. The Russian oral has no paired discussion, so the transfer is in the habits rather than the format: answer in two parts (answer, then reason or example), keep going when the first idea runs out, and ask for a repeat rather than answer the wrong question.
Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Somebody marks themselves generouslySpot-check three papers during the stage, at random, cheerfully, and say in advance that you will. The point is not policing; it is that a generous mark is a wasted lesson and they should be told so in those words.
«I got a bad score, so I am bad at English»Head it off in the frame: this paper covers six units in ninety minutes with no support, and it is designed to find holes. Finding eight holes in May is good news; finding them in an exam is not.
The error log says «vocabulary»Reject it and give the format: TOPIC + WHAT I DO WRONG + ONE EXAMPLE. «Phrasal verbs: I use the wrong preposition — I wrote believe to instead of believe in.» Three of those and the summer has a shape.
Time runs shortCut the reading stage, not the error log. The reading review is useful; the log is the only thing that leaves the room with them.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
