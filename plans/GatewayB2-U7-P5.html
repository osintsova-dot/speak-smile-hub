🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Write a review of 140–190 words in five paragraphs — introduce the book, describe the main character, summarise the plot, give a justified opinion, recommend — using a range of adjectives and structures of emphasis: I really DO think… · Not only is it fast-moving, but it's also deep · He is neither violent nor stupid · It's time you picked up this novel.
What is genuinely hard
Three things break a B2 review. First, paragraphing: students write one block of text and lose the organisation mark outright. Second, adjectives: the same four words repeat and the vocabulary mark collapses. Third, the inversion after Not only — English puts the auxiliary before the subject and Russian has no such rule, so it comes out as «Not only it was successful».
Typical error to expect
«Not only the book was successful…» — after Not only at the beginning of a sentence, English inverts: Not only WAS the book successful, but it was also translated…, Not only DOES Ponyboy get good marks, but he ALSO writes well.
«I think it is good. It is interesting.» — an opinion with no example under it is a two-mark answer in both the oral and the essay. Every claim in this unit is followed by For instance… / Just to give you an idea… / You only have to think of…
One paragraph instead of five. The plan is not decoration: it is the criterion.
Why the lesson is built this way
The model is read twice with two different jobs — once for content, once for SHAPE — because the shape is what is being taught. The adjective hunt in the middle is done standing, as a competition, because eleven adjectives in five short paragraphs is a fact students have to discover for themselves before they believe it. The emphasis structures are drilled on the model's own sentences and then transformed five times, so that Not only is automatic before it has to be used under time pressure. Then twenty uninterrupted minutes of writing — real, silent, timed — and a peer check against the printed checklist, not against taste.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“An international website for teenagers is looking for reviews. One question: which book should ALL teenagers read? The best reviews go on the site. Today you write yours — five paragraphs, a hundred and forty to a hundred and ninety words — and then we choose which book this class recommends.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the notice read closely, and the choice defended out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1, three minutes in pairs: read the notice on the page — A book that ALL teenagers should read! Note exactly what it demands: the story, the main characters, and WHY you think it is such a great book for teenagers.
  2. Underline those three requirements on the page. A review that answers only two of them loses the task mark, and that is true of the exam too.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share, ninety seconds each way: tell your partner which book you have chosen and why teenagers specifically should read it. The listener asks one question — usually But what actually HAPPENS in it?
  4. Take four choices to the floor. Where two students choose the same book, say so — they will compare reviews at the end.
  5. The warmer question from the Teacher's Book fits here in thirty seconds: Have you ever read a review online before buying something? Have you ever WRITTEN one? What adjectives did you use?
📺 On screen: the notice on p.93 with its three requirements underlined — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: read the task for its requirements, then defend a personal choice under one question

WHAT WE DO: ex.2: the review of The Outsiders read for content and judged

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the four questions on the board BEFORE they read, exactly as the Teacher's Book does — reading with questions in front of you is a different activity from reading and then answering: When was the book written? Is that a problem? What kind of book is it? Who tells the story? Who are the greasers and the socs? What event changes Ponyboy's life for ever?
  2. ex.2, four minutes, silent: read the model review. It is The Outsiders by S. E. Hinton, written over fifty years ago, narrated by a fourteen-year-old boy called Ponyboy; the greasers and the socs are rival gangs, and everything changes when the greasers are attacked and fight back in self-defence.
  3. Take the answers to the floor in two minutes — briskly, they are comprehension, not the lesson.
  4. Then the real question, four minutes in pairs and then the floor: Would YOU want to read this book after reading this review? Which sentence made you want to — or not? Get them to name the sentence. That sentence is the model of what they are about to write.
  5. Culture note in twenty seconds: S. E. Hinton was seventeen when she wrote it, and Francis Ford Coppola filmed it in 1983.
📺 On screen: the six comprehension questions on the board before reading starts — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: read a model with questions already in front of them, then identify the one sentence that persuaded them

Move break · the adjective hunt
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Sixteen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute competition that proves how many adjectives a good review carries

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands, books open at the model.
  2. Sixty seconds, in pairs, standing: find every adjective in paragraphs 1, 4 and 5 that describes the book or the main character. Count them.
  3. Take the count from each pair aloud before giving the answer. The full list: brilliant, fast-moving, deep, moving, clever, sensitive, likeable, convincing, violent, perceptive, unforgettable — eleven adjectives in three short paragraphs.
  4. Then the question that lands the point: How many different adjectives did YOU use in your last piece of writing? Silence is the correct answer.
  5. Stronger pairs get the extra sort from the Teacher's Book, still standing: which of the eleven describe the STORY and which describe a CHARACTER?
  6. Sit down with the wall in view: Eight of yours. Minimum.
📺 On screen: the eleven adjectives counted and written up beside the wall — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: hunt and count in pairs standing, then compare the number against their own writing

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: the job of each of the five paragraphs, written into the boxes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3, four minutes in pairs: re-read the review and decide what each paragraph is FOR. One sentence per paragraph.
  2. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together and write the agreed answers into the five boxes on the board: 1 introduce the book · 2 give the background and introduce the main characters · 3 summarise the main events · 4 describe what you like about it · 5 recommend it.
  3. Everybody copies the five labels into their own boxes. That page is now their plan.
  4. One diagnostic question, and it is the one that separates a review from a summary: Which paragraph carries the OPINION? Where does the writer stop telling and start judging? Paragraph 4, and paragraph 1 hints at it in the first line.
  5. Then the warning that saves marks: Paragraph three summarises. It does not tell the whole story and it does not spoil the ending. Two or three sentences.
📺 On screen: the five boxes on the board, now labelled, and the same five in every notebook — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: derive the paragraph plan from a real text and copy it into their own page as boxes

WHAT WE DO: ex.4–5: why do/does/did, why the inversion, then five transformations

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4, three minutes in pairs then the floor, on the Writing bank box: Why are do, does and did used in the first examples? What happens to the verb after «Not only» at the beginning of a sentence? Answers: they are there for EMPHASIS; after Not only we use the interrogative form — the auxiliary comes before the subject.
  2. Put the pattern on the board in one line and box it: Not only + AUXILIARY + subject …, but … also …
  3. Say the model sentences aloud, chorally, twice, with the stress in the right place. A structure that has never been spoken is rarely written correctly.
  4. ex.5, five minutes individually: rewrite five pairs of sentences beginning with Not only. Answers: 1 Not only was the book successful in the US, but it was also translated into many other languages · 2 Not only does Ponyboy get good marks at school, but he also writes really well · 3 Not only have I read the book, but I've also seen the film · 4 Not only would I recommend this novel, but I'd also recommend all her other novels · 5 Not only can you understand how he feels, but you can also understand his actions.
  5. Kagan RallyCoach for the check: one writes, one coaches, and the coach's only question is where is the auxiliary?
  6. Point at the rest of the Writing bank in twenty seconds — both … and, neither … nor, still, It's time you picked up this novel. Two of these must appear in what they are about to write.
📺 On screen: the boxed inversion pattern on the board, kept up for the writing stage — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: derive the rule, say it aloud chorally, then produce five transformations in a coaching pair
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE task 38, criteria K2 (organisation) and K3 (vocabulary). WHAT WE DO: ex.6: the chosen book planned paragraph by paragraph, in note form

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.6, eight minutes, silent, individually: fill the five boxes with NOTES about your own book. Not sentences — notes. Say why out loud: a plan written in sentences is a first draft that will be copied, and it wastes ten minutes.
  2. Requirements written on the board while they work, so nobody can claim not to have known: eight different adjectives listed in box 4 before you start writing · two emphasis structures marked where they will go · the ending is not spoiled in box 3.
  3. Circulate and check ONE thing per student — that box 4 (the opinion) has a reason under it, not just an adjective. That is where the marks are.
  4. Exam tip from the page, read aloud once at minute sixty: to make reviews interesting, use a variety of adjectives and adverbs; the advice on making writing more interesting (Unit 5), involving the reader (Unit 4) and expressions of opinion (Unit 3) all applies here.
  5. Two minutes before the end of the stage: Anybody with an empty box, raise your hand now, not in ten minutes.

WHAT WE DO: ex.7a: the review written in full, timed, in silence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 🎯 Twenty minutes. Timer on the board, visible, counting down. Silence is the rule and it is not negotiable — this is the closest thing to exam conditions in the whole unit.
  2. Say the three reminders once, then stop talking: Five paragraphs. A hundred and forty to a hundred and ninety words. Underline every adjective you use.
  3. Do not answer vocabulary questions during the first fifteen minutes — point at the wall instead. In the last five minutes you may answer them.
  4. Circulate silently. Mark nothing. Note two sentences you will want to read aloud later, and note any student who has not started by minute five and deal with that quietly.
  5. At minute fifteen, one instruction on the board, not spoken: Five minutes left. Count your words now. Word count is a habit, and a review of 90 words fails whatever else it does.
  6. At twenty minutes: pens down, genuinely. The check is the next stage and it needs the full four minutes.
📺 On screen: the countdown timer and the three reminders on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everybody writes for twenty uninterrupted minutes and counts their own words before stopping

WHAT WE DO: ex.7b–7c: the Writing checklist on p.141 applied to a partner's review

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Swap reviews with a partner. Open the Writing checklist on SB p.141 — the check is against the printed criteria, not against opinion.
  2. Four questions only, and the checker writes the answers at the bottom of the page: 1. Five paragraphs — yes or no? 2. How many DIFFERENT adjectives? Count them. 3. At least two emphasis structures — find them and circle them. 4. Is the opinion justified, or just stated?
  3. Then one sentence of feedback in the checker's own words: Would you read this book after reading this?
  4. Two students who chose the same book swap with each other — the comparison is instructive and costs nothing.
  5. Collect the reviews. Say what you will mark: task, organisation, range of adjectives, and the emphasis structures. Nothing else, and say so.
📺 On screen: the Writing checklist on SB p.141 — paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: every student checks a real text against four countable criteria and writes a verdict

Structure

Form
emphatic do/does/did + bare infinitive · Not only + AUXILIARY + subject …, but … also … · both … and · neither … nor · still for emphasis · It's time + subject + past tense.
Pron. watch
the emphasis carries in the voice or it does not carry at all: I really DO think · NOT only is it fast-moving · neither VIolent NOR stupid. Read the model sentences aloud before writing them — a structure that has never been said is rarely written correctly
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Not only is it fast-moving…» — why «is it» and not «it is»? → because Not only at the START of a sentence forces the question form. Is it a question? → no. That is the whole trap.
«I really do think» — am I unsure? → no, the opposite. Where is the stress? → on do.
ICQ before writing: How many paragraphs? — Five. How many words? — Between 140 and 190. Do you count them? — Yes, at the end.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«I haven't read a single book»Ask in the opening, not at minute sixty. Anyone with nothing may review a film, a series or a graphic novel — the paragraph plan is identical and the point is the writing, not the reading history.
One block of text, no paragraphsThe five boxes drawn at the top of the page before writing starts. It is impossible to forget a paragraph you have already drawn an empty box for.
The same four adjectives on repeatThe wall is right there and the rule is explicit: at least EIGHT different adjectives, and they are underlined in the final text so both the writer and the checker can count them.
«Not only it is…» in the final draftDrill it in ex.5 until it is automatic, then put it in the peer checklist as a yes/no question. Anything not on the checklist does not get checked.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
