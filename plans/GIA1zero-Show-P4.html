🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The camera scares them more than the stage — we cure that with takes: the rule “a take is a try, not a mistake”. Filming stays in the classroom/the room next door — minimal logistics, maximum support.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Welcome to our classroom! This is our smiley board. Here you can see our season!” “A take is a TRY, not a mistake.”

WHAT WE DO: teams fill in the template: their own words into the notes, phrases shared out (everyone gets at least 1); Kagan · NHT — a phrase gate

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. They fill in the template: what to show in scenes 2–3 (the board? the stand? games?), their own words in the notes.
  2. Sharing out: EVERYONE gets at least 1 phrase; scenes 1 and 4 can be done in chorus as a team.
  3. ▶ Kagan · Numbered Heads Together (a gate): Heads together: how do we START the video? … Number TWOS — say it! How do we END? — Number ONES! — everyone knows the phrases.
  4. My gate: script signed off — the team is cleared for the camera.
📺 On screen: script templates (🐣 asset) · phrase cards
👦 Students do: they put together a script from the notes and clear the phrase gate — nobody freezes on camera. Expected: they pick scene 3 about classroom games. Typical error: one phrase for everyone — I hold the “1 each” rule.

WHAT WE DO: rehearsal without the camera: chorus scene 1 as a whole team, personal lines in turn; pace and a smile

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: filming with support (I stay by the cameras) alongside catch-up stations on weak topics; rotation ×3

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Filming corners: I help at the cameras — sound (1 metre!), notes behind the phone, Take two — no problem!
  2. Catch-up stations: self-check cards on the year's weak topics (the P1–P3 “let's review” corner) + my station for the hardest ones.
  3. Rotation on a signal: filming → to the stations, stations → to the cameras.
  4. I check: everyone got their phrase on camera (a team checklist).
📺 On screen: phones · TAKE signs · station cards with keys
👦 Students do: they film in turns with support, and close personal gaps between shoots — not a minute of downtime, not a minute of stress. Expected: 3–4 scenes out of 4 shot for everyone. Typical error: whispering on camera — “the camera is your friend, talk to IT”.

WHAT WE DO: watching back with support: teams pick the best take of every scene; specific praise; deciding on at most 1 reshoot

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The term: ‘Dailies’ — film studios watch their takes. We're a studio!
  2. Teams watch the takes, mark the best one on the script (the editor's tick).
  3. The praise rule: for every take — 1 specific “what worked” (voice? smile? clean phrase?).
  4. At most 1 scene reshot — now, or at the start of pair 65.

👦 Students do: they watch themselves with a mindset of praise — the camera stops being an enemy. Expected: kind laughter; “I actually sound OK!” is the phrase of the day.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, teams have a 4-scene video script from the template and most scenes shot; everyone has at least 1 phrase on camera.
  • Subsidiary. The guide language uses core phrases (Welcome! · This is… · Here you can see… · Thanks for watching!) — a review of there is/can see put to real use; a catch-up station for weak topics runs alongside (KTP); “lots of takes is normal”.
  • Personal. The camera scares them more than the stage — we cure that with takes: the rule “a take is a try, not a mistake”. Filming stays in the classroom/the room next door — minimal logistics, maximum support.
By the end, students can…
  • say a guide phrase to camera with support
  • follow the 4-scene template
  • work in camera roles with help
  • treat retakes as normal practice

Target language
Lexis
Welcome to…!This is… · Here you can see…Follow me!Action! · Cut! · Take two!This is our classroom. · Here you can see…
Structure
Template scenes: 1) Welcome to our classroom! 2) This is… (board/stand) · Here you can see… 3) We can… here (what we can do in the classroom) 4) That's all — thanks for watching!
Form
a scene = 1–2 lines from the notes; EVERYONE has at least 1 phrase on camera; this is + singular, these are + plural (fixed gently).
Pron. watch
to the camera — slowly and with a smile · Welcome! — energetic · talk to the camera like a friend.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Take 5 — am I bad at this?” → No: professionals sometimes need take 25. A take = a try. Many takes is NORMAL.
“Can I have the phrase on paper behind the camera?” → Yes! The camera operator holds the notes behind the phone — eyes are almost on the camera.
“What if someone doesn't want to be on camera?” → 1 phrase on camera — for everyone (chorus is fine in a group scene); bigger roles — by choice.

Assumptions: Roles from P1 (at their level); 1 phone per team is enough (I have a spare). Filming stays in the classroom → no route approvals needed; the scene template is ready, the core phrases are on cards.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Fear of the camera is bigger than fear of the stageA “camera is a friend” warm-up: first we film a CHORUS scene as a whole team (the fear is shared by everyone), then personal lines.
Giggling ruins the takesThe rule “laugh BEFORE and AFTER a take”: before — fine, once Action! is called — guide faces. If it breaks down — Take two, calmly.
Quiet soundWe speak 1 metre from the phone; a 5-sec test take with playback is mandatory for every team.
Weaker learners don't have enough language for a sceneTheir scene is a chorus or pair one; a personal phrase of just 3 words (This is our board!) is a win too.
The catch-up station sits empty (“everyone's filming”)Filming goes team by team: while 2 teams film in the corners, the rest are on catch-up stations, rotating.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
