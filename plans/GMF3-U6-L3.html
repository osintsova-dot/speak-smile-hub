Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to follow, read and act out the story «The raft race» and use be going to + instructions in context: “I'm going to enter the race. / You must paddle your raft. / Come on, paddle quickly!”
  • Subsidiary. Break Luke's code («…a windsurfing RACE…»); Values — listening to other people's ideas; story skills: predict → listen → read along → Ask & answer → act out; recycle L2's going to; preview L4's adverb quickly (Paddle quickly!).
  • Personal. Let the story breathe — big voices, pauses, prediction; the adverb quickly is NOTICED today, drilled properly in L4 — don't over-correct. Give every child a story role.
By the end, pupils can…
  • retell 2–3 things that happen in the raft race;
  • read their role's lines from the book;
  • answer Yes/No: “No, they don't. They enter a raft race.”

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Do the children enter a KAYAK race or a RAFT race?” → a raft race!
“Paddle quickly — fast or slow?” → fast! (that's HOW they paddle).

Assumptions: going to and the 12 items are active from L1–L2; the alphabet code was learnt earlier (recycled in the pre-reading). Readers follow the text with a finger; act-out routine is established.

28–40′
Act out
carpet, groups of 4

Model a frame. You + 3 pupils play the race frame with big drama: Ready, steady, … GO! Come on, everyone! Paddle quickly! (everyone ‘paddles’).

Groups of 4 (Lily · Josh · Ravi · Dad). Rehearse 2 frames with books as scripts. Rotate roles once.

Showtime. Each group performs ONE frame (30–40 sec — fast carousel, no long waits). Audience task: shout what the children use (rope / wood / plastic bottles) at the end.

💪 Support: shortest lines / the “Paddle quickly!” chorus for the shyest child. ⭐ Extension: add an extra line from the frame.

Raft-race board game
