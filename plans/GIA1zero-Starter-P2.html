Opening · welcome + rewind P1

WHAT WE DO: a high five at the door + How are you?; rewind P1: names + a things blitz; bridge to the days

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: Hello! How are you? — an answer is required, I prompt: I'm fine, thanks!
  2. A rewind blitz: I show objects from the mystery bag — the class names them in chorus: keys! a notebook! Pairs, 60 sec: What's in your bag?
  3. Bridge: “We named the things. But WHEN do we meet? Monday? Thursday? Today — the days of the week and… you'll be commanding the class.”
📺 On screen: mystery bag · the P1 frame
👦 Students do: they greet each other by the ritual, recall the things in chorus and in pairs. Expected: the rituals already run themselves.

10–20′
Discover: the days of the week in portions
Discover · days of the week
standing → desks

WHAT WE DO: introducing the days in portions of 3+2+2 with rhythm and gestures → audio 3 → ex.1 order → the Wednesday trickster with Kagan Showdown on mini-boards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Portion 1 in chorus with claps: Monday! Tuesday! Wednesday! ×3. Portion 2: Thursday! Friday! Portion 3 with a “rest” gesture: Saturday! Sunday!
  2. Audio 3: listen-and-repeat; targets: Wednesday — a silent d! Tuesday /tjuː/ · Thursday /θɜː/ — tongue between your teeth!
  3. Ex.1, pairs: put the days in order (the first school day — Monday); a check in a choral chain.
  4. The Wednesday trickster: we write it in chunks, Wed-nes-day, and cross the silent d out in the air; then Kagan Showdown on mini-boards: Write Wednesday… wait… SHOWDOWN! — boards up all at once (EVERYONE writes, nobody calls it out).
  5. Ex.3 together: dates a–e → days from the calendar (key: Saturday · Wednesday · Thursday · Tuesday · Sunday) — the first 3 together, 2 in pairs.
📺 On screen: SB p.8 · audio 3 · calendar (Backup: I say the days myself, no track)
👦 Students do: they drill in portions standing up with claps, put the days in order, work out the trickster. Expected: the rhythm keeps everyone with it; th gets laughs — great. Typical error: Tuesday/Thursday get mixed up — the “2 fingers/tongue finger” gesture.

Discover 2 · our classroom
standing · TPR

WHAT WE DO: p.8 ex.4: 8 classroom objects — introduced round the REAL classroom with a “run and touch” movement; then matching in the book

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Introducing them round the real classroom: I point + the class echoes: board · desk · chair · door · window · clock · poster · computer.
  2. ▶ TPR Run & Touch: Touch a desk! Point to the clock! Go to the door — quietly! — everyone moves; I speed up.
  3. Ex.4 at the desks, pairs: 8 objects in the picture (key: clock · window · board · door · poster · computer · desk · chair).
  4. Ex.5 a vote: Classroom A or B — which do you like? hands up; 2 answers from the frame: I like B — computers! (I don't demand full reasoning — that's the 🚀 bar).

29–38′
Try it: this/these with gestures
Try it · this/these first touch
desks → standing

WHAT WE DO: this/these — a first touch with gestures round the real classroom (finger/palm); a light ex.8 by Kagan RallyRobin in pairs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Anchor gesture: this — ONE finger at something close: This is my pen. these — a PALM at many close things: These are my books. Choral echo with the gestures ×4.
  2. A show (not a drill): that/those — the same, but the hand points FAR: That's the door!
  3. A light ex.8 by RallyRobin — a strict ping-pong of turns: Your turn — my turn! A points at THEIR OWN (close) thing — B names it: This is a pencil case. Then B points — A names it. 4 objects each, nobody just “sits as an audience”.
  4. I only fix this/these confusion — that/those isn't tested today.
📺 On screen: SB p.9 ex.8 (light) · gestures
👦 Students do: they echo-drill with gestures, point-and-name in pairs. Expected: the finger/palm removes the main confusion. Typical error: this are — the “palm → these” gesture, no theory.

47–58′
Commands through the body: do it with me
Discover · classroom instructions (TPR)
standing · TPR

WHAT WE DO: p.9 Instructions ex.1–2: 8 commands with “do it with me” gestures → audio 4, we do it with our bodies → matching with icons

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Introducing them “do it with me”, one at a time: Look at the board! (hand as a visor) Listen! (ear) Work in pairs! (two fingers together) Open your books! Read! Copy! Don't talk! Don't write! — each with a gesture, choral echo.
  2. Pron: listen — the t is silent; don't /dəʊnt/.
  3. Audio 4 (ex.2): we all do the instructions together with our bodies; the second run — “body only, no gesture prompt from me”.
  4. Ex.1 at the desks, in pairs: 8 icons ↔ commands (key: 1e 2b 3d 4a 5c 6g 7f 8h).
  5. Don't anchor on the board: ✋ Don't = NO/NOT (a red frame).
📺 On screen: SB p.9 · audio 4 · gestures (Backup: I do the actions myself, no track)
👦 Students do: they carry out commands with their bodies (moving for a full 10 minutes), match the icons. Expected: listening comprehension is almost 100% through the gesture. Typical error: they start talking on Don't talk — I point at the red frame, laughter.

58–68′
Game: Simon Says — the wheel goes to the children
Play · Simon Says (teacher → children)
standing · teams

WHAT WE DO: Simon Says: I lead first, then — one brave one from each team, with a support bank; everyone moves

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Classic Simon Says: Simon says: stand up! Simon says: look at the window! Sit down! — aha, Simon didn't say! 3–4 rounds are mine.
  2. I hand over the wheel: a team volunteer leads for 60 sec, a bank of commands on the board — they read from it (that's legal and useful!).
  3. Every leader gets applause by the protocol (+1 to the team for bravery).
  4. A quiet version as the last round: whispered commands — we train “volume control” along the way.
📺 On screen: a bank of commands on the board
👦 Students do: they play and LEAD with the bank as support — a first “commander” experience, safely. Expected: more volunteers to lead than slots — a healthy sign. Typical error: a leader with no Don't commands — I whisper a prompt to add one.

68–78′
Write it: 4 commands for the perfect lesson
Build · «Commands for my home» mini-product
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: a mini-product: a pair writes 4 commands for the “perfect lesson” (2 do + 2 don't) from the bank; we get ready to show them

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Task for the pairs: Write FOUR commands for our perfect lesson: two DO, two DON'T. The bank is on the board — copying is legal.
  2. Model: Listen to music! Don't sleep! — creativity is welcome.
  3. Support: for the weaker ones — build commands from half-cards (Don't + talk). For fast finishers: +2 commands with classroom words (Look at the poster!).
  4. Rehearsal: the pair reads their commands to each other WITH GESTURES.
📺 On screen: command bank · half-cards for support
👦 Students do: they make up 4 commands from the bank, rehearse with gestures. Expected: funny commands (“Don't eat my pencil case!”) — the language is alive. Typical error: Don't + a non-verb — the half-cards fix the structure by themselves.
💪 Support: Don't+verb half-cards. ⭐ Fast finishers: +2 commands with classroom vocabulary.		cards

Show: commanding the class
Share · command the class
stage

WHAT WE DO: the show: pairs command the class by choice (2 commands per pair) — the class obeys; applause for everyone

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pairs in turn (by choice, but I gently call on everyone): they come out, give their 2 commands — THE CLASS OBEYS with their bodies.
  2. I prompt quietly anyone who gets stuck; a gesture is a legal prompt to give yourself.
  3. Applause by the protocol after every pair; +1 to the team for every command the class understands (the class understood = the command works!).
📺 On screen: their command sheets
👦 Students do: they command the class in pairs (not scary in twos!), carry out other pairs' commands. Expected: 80%+ of pairs come out; delight — “the class did WHAT I SAID”. Typical error: a quiet voice — “nobody hears the command = the class doesn't move”, and the point of volume is instantly clear.

Homework

Own homework: 1) A little weekly calendar: label the 7 days in English, circle our school days (TB tip: a capital letter!). 2) Give 2 commands in English at home with gestures (Stand up! Don't talk!) — teach the family to obey. 3) Practise the tricky word: Wed-nes-day, 3 times out loud and 3 times in writing.

notebook · a photo of the board on your phone — that's fine
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can say the days of the week, name 8 classroom objects and follow + give simple commands (Open your books! Don't talk!) with TPR support.
  • Subsidiary. The calendar ritual is launched (What day is it today?); this/these — a first touch with movement and gestures; the habit of reacting to classroom English with the body; the “I CAN” final #2.
  • Personal. A TPR day: almost all the new language goes through the body. From today, the teacher's commands are ONLY in English (a gesture doubles the meaning). Speed doesn't matter, engagement does.
By the end, students can…
  • say the 7 days in order (Wednesday survived!)
  • name 8 classroom objects
  • follow 8 classroom commands with actions
  • give 2–3 commands to a partner

Structure
What day is it today? — It's Tuesday! · This is a chair. These are our desks. · Open your books! · Don't talk, please!
Form
days — with a capital letter; this = one thing close, these = many things close (that/those — a show only, no drill); a command = verb first; Don't + verb = “don't …”.
Pron. watch
Wednesday — the d is silent! · Tuesday /ˈtjuːz/ · Thursday /ˈθɜːz/ · listen — the t is silent · don't /dəʊnt/ (the t is often swallowed: Don'(t) talk)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Wednesday — do we read the d?” → No! /ˈwenzdeɪ/ — the d is silent, a tricky word.
“Don't talk — what do we do?” → we're quiet: don't = “no/not”!
“Days with a small letter?” → No — always with a capital: Monday!

Assumptions: After P1: the hello block and things are active, the rituals (pairs, signals, I CAN) are familiar. They may have partially heard the days; they understand classroom English passively from P1 — today we make it active. Course rule #1 (said out loud EVERY Starter pair): mistakes are how we learn — here nobody laughs at mistakes, here we grow from them. The teacher makes mistakes on purpose and fixes them out loud.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do

Wednesday scares themA tricky word: I show the “silent d” — we cross it out in the air; we write it in chunks, Wed-nes-day.
They mix up commands by earEvery command = a gesture; first “do it with me”, then “do it yourself”, then “command it yourself”.
this/these is too earlyJust a finger/palm + 4 classroom objects; we do NOT torture ourselves with the ex.7 table — it's only touched on in the P4 revision.
They get tired of sittingA TPR day: no seated blocks back to back, we stand up every 8–10 min.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
