Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name 12 countries (from their flags) and ask & answer about travel: “Have you ever been to (Italy)? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.”
  • Subsidiary. Sing «All around the world» (CD4 14) & the vocabulary chant (CD4 13); brainstorm countries (which are in Europe? where do you go on holiday?); read flag/country words and match; play the flag-guessing game (Talk Partners); early literacy — find country words in a word search (AB).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; stirrer→settler rhythm — the guessing game is lively, plan the freezes. Insist on the full question Have you ever been to…? and the short answer (Yes, I have / No, I haven't), not just the country name.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name the 12 countries from their flags;
  • ask “Have you ever been to…?” and answer “Yes, I have / No, I haven't”;
  • read country words and match them to flags.

Structure
Have you ever been to (Italy)? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.  ·  It's got a (blue, white and red) flag. — Is it (France)? — Yes, it is.  ·  Would you like to go to (France)? — Yes, I would.
Form
Have you ever been to + country? Short answers: Yes, I have / No, I haven't. Flag description: It's got a … flag (colours). preview: Has he/she ever been to…? (drilled in L2).
Pron. watch
country names — stress the right syllable: MO-roc-co, ro-MA-ni-a, POR-tu-gal, JA-pan, BRA-zil; weak have you ever /əvjuˈevə/ at speed.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Have you ever been to Italy? — am I asking about NOW or your whole life?” → your whole life — any time, ever.
“I've never been to Brazil — Yes, I have or No, I haven't?” → No, I haven't.

Assumptions: Pupils know some countries from GMF3 and the world map; the 12 flags/names are largely NEW to produce. Have you ever…? is met receptively — production is today's stretch. Readers — flag word cards work alongside pictures. Real travel is limited, so a role-play “travel passport” makes the survey work for everyone.

10–20′
Presentation · 12 countries
carpet → board

Present with flag cards + chant (act. 2, CD4 13). Each flag: What country is this? It's… France! Italy! India! Choral ×3, clapping the stressed beat (MO-roc-co, ro-MA-nia).

Sort by continent. Board columns — Europe (France, Italy, Germany, Russia, Portugal, Romania) · Not in Europe (India, Morocco, China, Japan, Mexico, Brazil). Hold each flag — the class calls the column, then the country.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: Stand up if this country is in EUROPE… Italy! — flash flags fast, everyone reacts every time.

💪 Support: answer with the country word + point to the flag. ⭐ Extension: add the continent: Italy is in Europe.

28–36′
Flag-guessing game
carpet → desks, pairs

▶ Guessing game (act. 4, Talk Partners). Pairs — A picks a flag secretly & describes the colours: It's got a blue, white and red flag. B guesses: Is it France? — Yes, it is! Would you like to go to France? — Yes, I would. Swap. Point per correct guess.

ICQ: Do you SHOW the flag or DESCRIBE it? (→ describe the colours!)

✔ Assess: stand behind one pair — is the colour description clear and the guess a full question?

 flag cards · Pupil's Book p.92
36–44′
Travel-passport mingle
mingle

▶ Class survey mingle (Genki Mingle style). Each child gets a role-play travel passport — 3 countries “stamped”. Music → mingle; off → pair: A asks Have you ever been to (China)? B answers from the passport Yes, I have! / No, I haven't. Find one “Yes” for as many countries as you can. 5–6 exchanges — everyone speaks many times.

Whole-class check: Hands up if you found someone who's been to Japan!

 travel-passport cards · music · flag cards
44–52′
Where I've been · write
desks

Speak. ▶ RallyRobin: Take turns: I'd like to go to… — countries you'd like to visit.

Write (notebooks / AB p.90 format). Board frame: I'd like to go to ___. I've never been to ___. I've been to ___. Each child writes 3 true sentences & draws a flag. Fast finishers add because.

💪 Support: copy the frame + choose from 3 flag word cards. ⭐ Extension: add a reason: I'd like to go to Japan because I love sushi.
