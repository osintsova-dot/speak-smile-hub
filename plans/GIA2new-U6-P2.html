🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): A long text frightens new students, so the way in has four steps: words BEFORE the text → a quiz about extinct animals (it builds context without reading) → reading paragraph by paragraph in crews → tasks with the “finger on the proof line” support. The unit subskill is given straight and simply: read the heading first, then the paragraph. The product is modest: 4 opinion sentences on a frame.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two animals, two stories. The saiga: more than a million, then fifty thousand. The mountain gorilla: five hundred, then two hundred. Both nearly disappeared — and both are still here. Today: six new words first, then we read the article in parts — each crew takes one part — and at the end you write four sentences: what helped these animals, and what can WE do? Your opinion, your words.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the saiga and the mountain gorilla almost disappeared and came back: six words first, then we read the article paragraph by paragraph (one per crew), and at the end four sentences about what helped and what we can do.

Reading paragraph by paragraph
crews

WHAT WE DO: Jigsaw: one paragraph (A–E) per crew — what it is about and which heading fits → put it together on the board → ex.5 saiga or gorilla

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Do the first paragraph aloud yourself as a model: read two lines and think aloud about which heading fits and why. Then Kagan Jigsaw: each crew reads ONLY its own paragraph and becomes the expert on it — the other four it learns from the other crews. Crew one — paragraph A. Crew two — paragraph B… Read your paragraph. Which heading fits? Two minutes.
  2. Collect it on the board, one line per crew. TB key ex.4: 1 E · 2 B · 3 D · 4 A · 5 C.
  3. ex.5 with a hint: Saiga or gorilla? The answer is in your paragraph — point at the line. TB key: 1 S · 2 S · 3 G · 4 S · 5 G.
  4. Mini-summary: five lines on the board = a retelling of the whole article.
📺 On screen: SB p.75 · the table on the board (Backup: hand the article out on paper, cut into paragraphs)
👦 Students do: read a small chunk and do not drown. Expect: every crew becomes the “expert” on its own paragraph — and this is the first time weaker students explain things to stronger ones

WB Unit 6 · the six-word mini-glossary
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can match a heading to a paragraph with support, find facts in an article and point at the proof line, match 6 new words to definitions, and write 4 opinion sentences by frame.
  • Subsidiary. The quiz ex.1–2 (track 37) as context; ex.3 for the general idea; ex.4 headings with a hint; ex.5–6 in pairs with the paragraph named; Word work BEFORE the text; Critical thinking in a simplified form (one action + a plus and a minus).
  • Personal. Cut the text into 5 paragraphs — one per crew, then put it together. Vocabulary BEFORE the reading, not during it. Read the keys out one by one, not as a list. Pre-teach extinct and conservation (the TB asks for this).
By the end, students can…
  • read a heading and find the paragraph it fits
  • find an answer and point at the proof line
  • match 6 new words to definitions
  • write 4 opinion sentences with a frame

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
extinct — does it mean “only a few left”? → no: the species is GONE. In danger — still here, but very few.
hunting — is it “a walk in the forest”? → no: killing an animal for food or sport.
Why read the heading BEFORE the paragraph? → the heading tells you what the paragraph is about, so you already know what to look for.

Assumptions: SB pp.74–75, syllabus pair 47. The text is longer than anything so far in the unit; we cope by splitting it into paragraphs and pre-teaching the words.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
A long text is frighteningSplit it into five paragraphs, one per crew; the whole picture is built on the board out of five lines.
They don't get half the words and give upWord work comes BEFORE the text; a six-word mini-glossary stays on the board all lesson.
Guessing instead of searchingTB routine: name the paragraph the answer is in. The rule: point at the line before you say the answer.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
