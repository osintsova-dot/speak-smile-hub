WHAT WE DO: gate on the subskill: predict 6 key words BEFORE reading → 60 sec of scanning → how many were predicted correctly

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Subskill p.62 out loud, briefly: Before you read again, write the words you EXPECT to see. Then hunt for them.
  2. Task: Five robot photos, no text yet. Write SIX words you expect on that page. Ninety seconds.
  3. Then ex.4: Read fast — sixty seconds only — and tick the words you actually found.
  4. Score in the margin: how many of the 6 hit. The reveal: Three out of six? Predicting is a SKILL, and it's the one that makes you fast readers. That's today's growth zone.
  5. Ex.3 along the way: whose guesses about the robots' abilities matched (key ex.4: Photo A: walk, run, climb · Photo B: cook, clean · Photo C: walk, dance, climb, play the piano · Photo D: walk, carry).
📺 On screen: slide: 5 robot photos with no text · timer 90 sec + 60 sec (Backup: the 5 robot names written on the board)
👦 Students do: they write a prediction solo, then scan the text against the clock, count the hits. Expected: 2–4 out of 6 — the subskill is exposed as a real growth zone. Typical error: they predict general words (robot, good) — “be specific: piano, kitchen, cat”.

🔥 Challenge · true/false with proof

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: ex.6 for speed: 6 statements, but the point is only for the correction + paragraph number; disputed ones — a crew duel

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round rule: True — say TRUE plus the paragraph. False — say FALSE, correct it, plus the paragraph. Half answers get zero. (TB p.72: mark the line.)
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly around the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. Crews, 4 minutes, then a volley around the chain.
  4. Key: 1 True (para 1) · 2 False — it has only got arms (para 2) · 3 False — it cooks AND cleans the kitchen (para 2) · 4 False — Lindsey works in a museum (para 3) · 5 False — most robots cost thousands (para 4) · 6 True (para 4).
  5. Disputed ones — a mini duel: two crews read out their lines, the class votes by hand.
  6. Fast finishers (TB p.72): invent two of their own robot types + what they can do.
📺 On screen: SB p.62 ex.6 · crew score table on the board
👦 Students do: they hunt for proof lines, correct the false statements. Expected: 4–6 out of 6, and most importantly — the habit of naming the paragraph. Typical error: “False, because it feels that way to me” — I don't give the point, they go and find the line.

WHAT WE DO: GMF4 bonus: Present perfect with ever — “this is not in the book”; a class survey about personal experience with tech

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

🏁 PRODUCT · the buyer's pitch
stage

WHAT WE DO: finale: a 60-sec defence of the choice — all crews in turn, the class judges by the rubric; a budget vote

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. All crews present (60 sec each, Speaker talks, the crew can prompt ONE word).
  2. Listeners are never idle: everyone has a judge's card — they score fluency / accuracy / richness 1–3 AND note whether the crew named the paragraph.
  3. After everyone — Vote with the budget: put your token on the robot you'd really buy. You can't vote for your own.
  4. The winner + gold runs go on the leaderboard.
📺 On screen: stopwatch · judges' cards for EVERYONE · voting “coin” tokens
👦 Students do: they present as crews, judge every presenter in writing — nobody is left out. Expected: Nybble and Moley split the votes. Typical error: a defence with no figures — the judges take off richness.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — can with no to and no -s, comparative by the rule, than not then (1–3) · Richness — 2+ adjectives, a comparison, precise tech vocabulary (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		stopwatch · rubric cards · tokens

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can defend a purchase decision in 60 seconds — which of the five robots the school should buy — using specific information found in the web page (SB p.63) as evidence.
  • Subsidiary. The subskill “reading for specific information” is worked as a scanning race (predict key words → scan); Word work (6 words) taken as a game round; Critical thinking Analyse/Evaluate — through sorting and ranking, not a long chat.
  • Personal. 🚀 can read the long text — it's a question of pace and evidence. Not “translate the text” but “find the proof line in 40 seconds”. Every answer = a paragraph number.
By the end, students can…
  • predict key words before reading and use them to scan (subskill)
  • find and quote the line that proves an answer
  • use six Word work items in new sentences
  • argue a choice with two pieces of evidence in 60 seconds

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Nybble costs $200 — is ‘cost’ a thing or an action?” → an action, verb: it costS.
“A guide — a machine or a person's job?” → a job: someone who helps visitors.
“Evidence: can you say ‘I think’ with no line from the text?” → No — the mission requires the line.

Assumptions: 🚀 read an A1+ text with no translation, but tend to answer “by feel”. What's new is the discipline of evidence and scanning speed. They recognise can/can't in the text; the rules come in pair 3.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
