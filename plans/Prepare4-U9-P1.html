🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the arts and entertainment set (audience, biography, director, gallery, novel, painter, poetry, sculpture, studio, writer/author) to talk about who does what in the creative world, survive a one-word-per-gap exam cloze, and report instructions with verb + object + to-infinitive: she told me to wait, he warned us not to touch.
What is genuinely hard
Two different problems in one lesson. The words are near-synonyms that Russian collapses into one (artist / painter, author / writer / poet, studio / gallery). The grammar breaks in three predictable places: say instead of tell, the object dropped altogether, and the negative put after to instead of before it.
Typical error to expect
«My friend said me to calm down» — say cannot take a person plus a to-infinitive. It is told me to calm down, and the unit's own Corpus task hunts exactly this line.
«She persuaded me to not go» — the negative sits BEFORE the to: not to go. Russian word order puts it after, and the book's Corpus set catches it twice.
Why the lesson is built this way
Unit 17 has only TWO lessons in the syllabus, so this one carries the whole first spread plus the grammar — and the shape is built to make that survivable. Vocabulary arrives in three real workplaces and goes straight back out in three news stories (one per group member). The reading sells itself — songwriters, ghostwriters, stunt doubles — and ends in the book's own six-gap exam cloze. Then the grammar walks in physically: the class OBEYS five instructions and reports them before the rule is named. The productive stage puts a student in the director's chair giving real orders while reporters convert them live. One extra verb is taught on purpose today — encourage — because the official unit test asks for it and the Student's Book never prints it.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Sia writes the songs other people sing. Somebody else wrote the Jenner sisters' novel. Damien Hirst has painted five of his own paintings. Today: who really does the work — and how English reports what somebody told you to do.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 as a jigsaw + ex.5 as a definition quiz

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Groups of three, one news story each (the exhibition, the best-selling author, the Hollywood trouble). Three minutes: fill your own gaps, then read your story to the group.
  2. Whole-class check, one group per story. Key: 1 gallery · 2 paintings · 3 sculptures · 4 author · 5 biography · 6 novels · 7 actors · 8 studio · 9 director. Watch item 4 — author, not writer: the sentence needs the person who WRITES BOOKS, and the word writer comes back two lines later.
  3. ex.5 as a lightning quiz, Kagan Numbered Heads Together: five questions, teams confer for 20 seconds, you call a number. Key: 1 a studio is where an artist WORKS, a gallery is where the work is SHOWN · 2 an artist paints, draws or sculpts — a painter only paints · 3 a novel is imaginary, a biography is a true life story written by someone else · 4 an author writes books, articles or plays; a poet writes poetry · 5 a sculpture is made of solid material (stone, metal, wood, clay), a painting is made with paint.
  4. Every answered word goes onto the wall in its column.

Reading · the six-gap cloze
pairs → class
Why: B1 Reading Part 6 in its natural habitat: the last section of the text is gapped, and every missing word is grammar. The method is taught out loud — look left, look right, name the part of speech, then choose.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: one word per gap, the exam method taught out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the format: Six gaps, one word each, and every missing word is grammar, not a fact. You can't guess these from the story — you find them from the words next door.
  2. Gap 1 demonstrated by you, thinking aloud: How does one man produce … many? What can stand before many? So or too. Which one fits the meaning? So many. The method goes on the board in four words: LEFT · RIGHT · WHAT KIND OF WORD · CHOOSE.
  3. Pairs do gaps 2–6, four minutes. Key: the six-gap cloze (B1 Reading Part 6): 1 so (quantifier — so many) · 2 to (infinitive) · 3 too (adverb) · 4 for (preposition — work FOR somebody) · 5 by (preposition — painted BY Rachel) · 6 whose (relative pronoun).
  4. Feedback names the part of speech every time, not just the word: Gap six — whose. What kind of word? A relative pronoun. Whose work? The artist's.
  5. Close the reading with the Talking points, two minutes: Is it fair that famous people get other people to do things for them? Whose book would you most like to ghostwrite? — pairs first, then open the floor; every opinion carries a because
📺 On screen: SB p.99 in every book plus the four-word method in chalk — paper and board are the media, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: watch the method once, then apply it five times. Expect gap 3 (too) to be the hardest — it is an adverb in a position that feels like a verb

Grammar warm-up · the teacher's instructions
standing → desks
Why: The TB's own warmer: the class actually obeys five instructions, then reports what the teacher told them to do. The structure is produced before it is named, which is what makes the rule feel obvious.

WHAT WE DO: the TB warmer: the class obeys five instructions, then reports them

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Five instructions given fast, and the class actually does them: Find a piece of paper and a pen. Write a short message on it. Make a paper aeroplane. Fly it to a friend. Open it and read the message.
  2. Then, on the board, one half-sentence: Our teacher told us to…
  3. Hands up, finish it — five times, once per instruction. Write the five completions on the board as they come. Do not explain anything yet.
  4. Look at what you just built. Told us TO find. Told us TO write. That's today's grammar — you already used it.
📺 On screen: the half-sentence in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: obey, fly the plane, then report what they were told to do. The paper aeroplanes are worth the ninety seconds of chaos

Grammar · reported commands
pairs → class
Why: Guided discovery from four sentences the class met minutes ago in the text, then the two-line rule and the Corpus set that hunts the classic errors: said me to, to not go, tell for help.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2 discovery from the text's own sentences, then ex.3 Corpus

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs: four reported sentences, each matched to the words actually spoken. Key: 1 a · 2 a · 3 b · 4 b — and the reporting verb can sit in any tense: I'm going to tell him not to….
  2. ex.2 — the two-line rule extracted, not announced. Key: a to (reporting verb + object + TO infinitive) · b not (the negative goes BEFORE to). A noun works as well as a pronoun — and say is banned from this structure. Say the ban aloud and write it in red: SAY is not allowed in this structure.
  3. The verb list goes up: ask · tell · order · warn · advise · remind · persuade — plus encourage in a different colour, with the reason said honestly: This one isn't in your book, but it IS in the unit test. My teacher encouraged me to try again.
  4. ex.3, the Corpus set, in pairs — eight real learner errors. Key: 1 ask · 2 not to read · 3 told me · 4 ask · 5 told · 6 to do · 7 reminded · 8 not to print. Grammar reference p.154 open for one minute at the end: students tick the table they have just built.
  5. CCQ round (see the list): instruction or polite request? where does the negative go? why is said me impossible?
📺 On screen: SB p.100 and Grammar reference p.154 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: match, extract, correct, tick the reference. Expect items 3 and 5 of the Corpus set (said me / told us) to be the revelation — let it land

Grammar · six commands
solo → pairs
Why: Controlled practice with a twist — every item starts as direct speech in quotation marks, so the transformation (verb + object + to-infinitive, pronouns adjusted) is visible rather than mechanical.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: direct speech converted, verbs given, pronouns adjusted

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model item 0 aloud with the whole class, pointing at each moving part: «Please help in the kitchen!» → Mum asked THEM to help. Verb, person, to.
  2. Solo, four minutes, then pairs compare. Key: 1 warned me not to touch · 2 ordered us to stop · 3 told him to be · 4 advised her not to worry · 5 persuaded us to go · 6 reminded us not to hand in.
  3. Feedback with one extra question on item 1: Karen said «my things». Why is it HER things now? Because we're not Karen any more.
  4. Anyone who finishes early rewrites two of the six with a different reporting verb and says what changes.
📺 On screen: SB p.100 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: convert, compare, explain the pronoun shift. Expect the negatives (1 and 6) to attract to not — the red rule on the board answers it silently

🎯 Speaking out · the director's chair
class → groups
Why: The productive outcome: one student gives real instructions, the class obeys, and the reporters convert them into reported commands live. Nobody can score without the structure, and the pronoun shifts happen in public.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 plus a live game: one director gives orders, the class obeys, the reporters report

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5 first, four minutes in pairs: six situations reported, pronouns changed. Key: 1 The father told his child not to run · 2 The boy asked his sister to pass (him) the salt · 3 The girl ordered her brother to get out of HER room · 4 The woman warned her daughter not to be late · 5 The taxi driver advised his passenger not to get out at the traffic lights · 6 The boy persuaded his friend to watch the match (with him) — the pronouns move in 3 and 6, which is where the marks are.
  2. Then the game. One student takes the director's chair and gives three real instructions to the class (Stand on one leg! Don't look at me! Give your neighbour a pen!). The class obeys.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share: everyone immediately reports all three to a partner — The director ordered us to stand on one leg and told us not to look at her. Then a new director, new instructions, new partner. Three rounds.
  4. Scoring, if the group needs it: one point per correctly reported instruction, half a point lost for said.
  5. You walk with a paper and log slips SILENTLY — they open lesson 2.
📺 On screen: the director's frame in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: direct, obey, report at speed. Everyone reports every round — the director changes, but nobody watches

WB Unit 17 (p.68–71)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can use the arts and entertainment words accurately (including the pairs Russian merges) and report commands and requests with ask, tell, order, warn, advise, remind, persuade and encourage.
  • Subsidiary. Reading for gist and detail with evidence; a six-gap B1 Reading Part 6 cloze with a stated method; listening to three short conversations (track 82).
  • Personal. Start the block's WORD WALL in two columns — PEOPLE (painter, poet, director) and PLACES/THINGS (gallery, studio, sculpture). Add encourage to the reporting verb list in a different colour and say why out loud. Log errors silently in the last stage; delayed feedback opens lesson 2.
By the end, students can…
  • say the difference between a studio and a gallery, an artist and a painter, a novel and a biography
  • fill a one-word gap by naming the part of speech first
  • report an instruction: she asked me to wait · he told us not to run
  • choose between ask and tell without thinking about it

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«He told me to go» — did he ask politely or give an instruction? → an instruction. Ask is the polite one; tell and order are not.
«She asked me not to shout» — where is the negative? → before TO. Say it as one chunk: not-to-shout.
«My friend said me to wait» — what is wrong? → say never takes a person here. Tell takes a person; say does not.

Actor, artist and writer are known; audience, sculpture, poetry and the author/writer distinction are new. Infinitives of purpose exist from earlier units, which is why the grammar can be discovered rather than taught.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The arts words blur into each otherThe wall is two columns for a reason: PEOPLE on the left, PLACES AND THINGS on the right. Every wrong answer gets the same three-second treatment — walk to the wall, point, say the pair aloud (studio is where you WORK, gallery is where people LOOK).
The cloze becomes guessingThe method is said out loud and stays on the board: look LEFT, look RIGHT, name the part of speech, THEN choose. Do gap 1 together as a demonstration, including a wrong guess eliminated in public.
Reported commands come out as reported speech from Unit 19Not today's job, and it will confuse: if somebody produces «he said that he would…», accept it warmly and park it — That's a different machine. We meet it later. Today: somebody TELLS somebody TO DO something.
Fast finishers in the readingTB's own extension: write definitions for successful, grow up, not mind, reputation and tournament; the class matches them later.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
