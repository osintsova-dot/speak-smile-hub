🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Build and use word families — music / musician / musical, perform / performance / performer, advertise / advertisement — and write an informal email of about a hundred words that answers all four notes, with short forms and informal vocabulary throughout.
What is genuinely hard
Two different difficulties in one lesson. The word families are a spelling and stress problem: -tion, -ment, -ance and -er all sound alike and none of them is predictable, and the stress moves (REcord / reCORD, ADvertise / adVERTisement). The email is a discipline problem: teenagers write a lovely paragraph and answer three notes out of four.
Typical error to expect
«He is a very music person» — the adjective is musical, and the noun cannot do an adjective's job in English the way it can in Russian.
The email that answers three notes. The fourth note is usually the one that asks a question back, and it is the one that disappears. Every mark scheme in both exams punishes it the same way.
Why the lesson is built this way
The families come first and fast, because they are the tools the writing needs: an email about a school concert cannot be written without performance, performer and musical. The model email is read as a piece of evidence — the class hunts for what makes it informal (short forms, loads, mates, scary, cool) rather than being told. Then the exam task is done in real time, fifteen minutes with a visible clock, and marked by another student against a four-item checklist that is on the board before anybody writes. The four notes get ticked off physically, in the margin, which is the habit that saves the communicative mark in both exams.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One word, four shapes: music, musician, musical, musically. Half the marks in a vocabulary exam are decided by whether you can move between them. Then fifteen minutes, one email, four notes — and if you answer three of them, it does not matter how beautiful the English is.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a 60-second repair of yesterday's tense choice, and the bridge into word families

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what have you listened to today? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — yesterday's tense choice, on the board. Two sentences, one of them wrong: I have seen him live last summer. / I've seen him live twice.

  4. Then thirty seconds of the wall: three students, three words each from the three columns.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the page: Page forty-four, the right-hand side. One word, four shapes.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: what kind of word is it, then the seven-row table

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1, ninety seconds: three sentences with help, helpful and help. Elicit the labels — verb, adjective, noun — and write them as column headings.
  2. ex.2 individually, four minutes: the table with seven gaps (musical, perform, advertisement, entertain, entertaining, achievement, record/recording).
  3. Check with Kagan Showdown: everybody writes the answer privately, all reveal on the signal, disagreements settled before moving on.
  4. Drill the stress physically — clap the stressed syllable: ADvertise / adVERTisement · ENtertain / enterTAINment · REcord (noun) / reCORD (verb). Two rounds, everyone claps.
  5. Fast finishers: anagrams of the words from the table, swapped with another fast finisher (the Teacher's Book task).
📺 On screen: the three column headings and then the completed table on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: fill seven gaps alone, reveal simultaneously, then clap the stress twice
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 26–31 (word formation). Word families ARE tasks 26–31: the exam prints the root in capitals and wants the derived form, spelled correctly.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: add a five-minute written round: six sentences with a gap and a root in capitals (MUSIC, PERFORM, ENTERTAIN, ADVERTISE, ACHIEVE, RECORD). It fits inside the table stage without extending it.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer on parts of speech

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands. You say a word from any family; the class shows it: fists together for a NOUN, arms out for a VERB, hands on head for an ADJECTIVE.
  2. Twelve words at speed — musician, perform, entertaining, advertisement, achieve, musical, recording, entertain, performance, advertise, achievement, record.
  3. Then the pair round, ten seconds each: Give me the other two members of the family.
  4. Freeze and sit: Page forty-five. Somebody has written to Sophia, and she has to answer three questions.
📺 On screen: no technology — the class is standing and the words are said aloud, so nothing can fail
👦 Students do: everyone shows a part of speech twelve times; nobody can watch from the side

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3: does she answer everything, and what makes it informal

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1, two minutes silent: read Jacob's questions and Sophia's reply. One question only — Does she answer all three? Answer: yes, and the class should be able to point at each one.
  2. ex.2: the Prepare to write box. Read the four features aloud (informal opening, informal ending, short forms, informal language), then find them in the text: Dear · Write soon · great.
  3. ex.3, ninety seconds in pairs: hunt for the three short forms (can't, I've, I'll). Whoever finds them first reads the whole sentence, not just the form.
  4. ex.5: the five informal words matched to meanings — loads, scary, great, mates, had such a cool time. Put them on the board in a box headed INFORMAL and leave the box up for the writing stage.
  5. Thirty seconds of register contrast, once: Dear Sir / Hi Alex. I am writing to inform you / Guess what. Then rub the formal column out — literally.
📺 On screen: two columns, FORMAL and INFORMAL, and the informal one survives — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: read a model with a job, then harvest the register features themselves

🎯 The exam email, in real time
individual, silent → pairs
Why: 🎯 The exam email, in real time

WHAT WE DO: ex.6 = B1 Writing Part 1: fifteen minutes, four notes, about 100 words

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read Alex's email aloud yourself, once, at speed. Then the four notes, one at a time, and the class says what each one DEMANDS: «Great!» → react · «Yes — say where you went» → name a place · «Explain to Alex» → a reason · «No, but …» → a refusal with an alternative.
  2. Everyone copies the four notes into the margin as a checklist. Walk the room in the first ninety seconds and check every margin — this is the stage where the mark is won or lost.
  3. Fifteen minutes, silent, clock on the board. About 100 words. You sit down; do not answer language questions during it (Write the word you have. We fix it after.).
  4. Announce the time at minute 10 and minute 13, nothing else.
  5. At the end, three minutes: everyone reads their own email once and ticks the four notes in the margin. Anyone with fewer than four ticks adds a sentence now, before it is swapped.
  6. Fast finishers: rewrite the same email in exactly fifty words.
📺 On screen: the four notes and the running clock on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write about 100 words alone against a clock, then audit their own text against four notes

Peer marking against a four-line checklist
pairs → class
Why: Peer marking against a four-line checklist

WHAT WE DO: the emails swapped and marked on exactly four criteria

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The checklist goes on the board and nothing else is marked today: 1 all four notes answered · 2 informal opening and ending · 3 at least three short forms · 4 about 100 words.
  2. Swap papers. Six minutes. The marker writes the four numbers in the margin with a tick or a cross — no other corrections, no crossing out of another student's English.
  3. Then ninety seconds, spoken, in pairs: the marker says ONE thing that worked and ONE thing that is missing. In English.
  4. Take two emails to the floor with the writer's permission and read the good sentences aloud. Read only the good ones; the crosses stay private.
  5. Collect the papers — you mark them properly for next lesson, against the same four criteria plus language.
📺 On screen: the four-line checklist on the board, up during the whole marking stage — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: mark a partner's email on four fixed criteria and say two sentences of feedback in English

Structure

Form
noun / verb / adjective families (-ian, -al, -ance, -ment, -ing) · short forms in informal writing (I've, we'll, won't) · informal register: loads, mates, great, cool.
Pron. watch
the stress MOVES and it is the thing to drill: ADvertise → adVERTisement · ENtertain → entertainMENT is wrong, it is enterTAINment · perFORM → perFORMance (stress stays) · REcord the noun against reCORD the verb
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«He's a music person.» Is that right? → no. The adjective is musical. The noun cannot describe another noun here.
«I have not seen it» in an email to a friend — natural? → no. Informal writing uses short forms: I haven't seen it.
Four notes on the email. How many do I answer? → four. Three is a different mark, in both exams.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The word-family table is filled by guessing endingsEvery answer is said ALOUD before it is written, because the ear catches entertainment and refuses entertainment-with-the-wrong-stress. Wrong stress = wrong answer today.
The email turns into a paragraph with no notes answeredThe four notes are copied into the margin as a checklist BEFORE writing, and ticked off during. No checklist, no start — walk past and check the margins in the first two minutes.
Formal language creeps in (Dear Sir, I would like to inform you)Put the two registers side by side on the board once, then leave only the informal one up. Anything from the formal column that appears in a draft gets circled, not explained.
Fast finishers in the writingThe Teacher's Book task: write the SAME email again in exactly fifty words. Cutting is harder than writing and it teaches more.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
