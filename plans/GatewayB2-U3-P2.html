🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use about forty-five words for transport and travel — from steering wheel and traffic jam to off-peak, fare, ticket inspector and lost property — including the five nouns Russian collapses into two (journey, travel, trip, excursion, voyage); and put removed sentences back into a text by tracking the words that point backwards.
What is genuinely hard
The sentence-insertion task feels like a comprehension task and is not: it is a task about reference. «This problem», «the technology», «in the UK's case» each point at something in the previous sentence, and a student who reads for general meaning will never see them. The vocabulary difficulty is narrower and very Russian: one word (поездка / путешествие) has to be split into five English nouns with different collocations.
Typical error to expect
«I had a good travel», «my travel to Moscow» — travel is uncountable and general; a single occasion is a journey, a trip, an excursion or, by sea and space, a voyage.
Why the lesson is built this way
The vocabulary arrives in two halves and both are practical: the first is the physical world of transport (wheels, gates, seat belts, traffic jams), the second is the language of actually using a public transport system — fares, zones, off-peak, contactless, ticket inspectors — inside a real text about how to get around Greater Manchester. The reading is a genuinely interesting article about what cars will be like in twenty years (electric, then shared, then possibly flying), and the task is the exam's insertion task. The whole second half is built around ONE technique, modelled on the first gap and then required for every answer: name the word that points backwards.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“In a few years' time a Chinese company is going to sell you a car that flies. It carries one passenger, at 100 kilometres an hour, at a maximum height of three thousand metres. Today: everything English says about getting around — and how to put the missing sentences back into an article without understanding every word of it.”

WHAT WE DO: prediction and the words of the title, before a single line is read

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

🎯 Putting the sentences back
desks
Why: This is the exam skill named in the aim, and it becomes a skill only when the evidence is compulsory and stated aloud.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.33 ex.3 (track 12): seven gaps, eight sentences, one technique

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the Exam tip, then reduce it to one instruction on the board: Look at the sentence BEFORE the gap. Find the word in the missing sentence that points back at it.
  2. Model gap (a) in full, exactly as the Teacher's Book does: the sentence before is about countries stopping the sale of petrol cars; sentence 8 says «In the UK's case»; «case» points at that decision. Underline it on the board.
  3. Eight minutes alone. For every answer, underline the word that proves it.
  4. RallyCoach: A explains gaps a–d, B explains e–g. The explanation must name the pointing word.
  5. Whole-class check by evidence, not by letter. Key: a 8 · b 4 · c 1 · d 7 · e 5 · f 2 · g 3 — and sentence 6 is the extra one.
  6. Optional: play track 12 under a final silent reading of the completed text.
  7. Fast finishers: two true sentences and one false one about the text, for the class to judge.
📺 On screen: the one-line technique on the board and the modelled gap (a) under it. Backup: track 12 is optional support for the final reading; if it fails, they simply reread.
👦 Students do: insert seven sentences alone with an underlined proof word, then explain half of them to a partner

Words from context
desks
Why: Guessing before checking is the sub-skill both exams reward; the three questions send them back into the text one last time.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.33 ex.4: eight expressions guessed from the line they are in

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Guess first. Dictionaries after. Read the whole sentence, not the word.
  2. Pairs compare guesses for the norm, source, brake, handle, take your eyes off, misleading, take to the sky, cruising.
  3. Confirm the three that are genuinely hard: handle (be responsible for), misleading (likely to make you believe something false), cruising (travelling at a constant speed).
  4. Three comprehension questions from the Teacher's Book to close the text: what will happen by 2040? why will electric cars have to make a noise? what has to change before self-driving cars can be trusted?
📺 On screen: nothing — deliberately screen-free. Backup: not applicable.
👦 Students do: guess eight expressions, compare in pairs, then answer three questions on the text

🎯 Which of these four would you actually want
desks → open floor
Why: The productive outcome: the reading content and the new vocabulary are used to defend a ranked position, which is harder and more useful than «what do you think».

WHAT WE DO: SB p.33 ex.5 (Critical thinkers): an opinion with a reason and an example

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Board the four options and brainstorm one advantage of each in thirty seconds: fully electric (less pollution) · driverless (you don't have to concentrate) · car sharing (fewer cars, less space for parking) · flying cars (no traffic jams).
  2. The question: How important, useful or necessary is each one? Rank them one to four and be ready to defend number one and number four. Two minutes of notes.
  3. Timed Pair Share, one minute each way, then change partners and repeat once.
  4. Four reports, and the frame is compulsory: I'd put … first because … . For example, … . I'd put … last, although …
📺 On screen: the four options and the sentence frame on the board. Backup: dictate the frame.
👦 Students do: rank four options, defend two of them to two different partners

Why: The transfer is made explicit exactly once, immediately after the skill has been practised — which is when it costs seven minutes instead of a lesson.

WHAT WE DO: seven minutes: the insertion technique transferred, then stopped

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say it once, plainly: The task you have just done is task eleven of the Russian exam. Same skill, different letters — there you get six gaps and seven sentence fragments.
  2. Board one exam-shaped mini-item using the same article: two sentences with a gap between them, and three candidate sentences, one of which begins «This technology …».
  3. Thirty seconds alone, then hands up — and the answer must name the pointing word.
  4. Give them the one habit to keep: Read the sentence before the gap. Then look for the word that points back. Never read the options first.
  5. Then stop. Seven minutes, once per unit — this is a language course, not an exam course.
📺 On screen: the mini-item on the board. Backup: read it aloud twice.
👦 Students do: answer one exam-shaped item in thirty seconds and name the pointing word

Why: Two texts in one lesson is what the split makes possible: the first is taught, the second is measured. The workbook text used to go home unchecked; here it becomes the exam practice.

WHAT WE DO: the second text of the unit, done here rather than at home — with the clock running

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the workbook reading of the unit. Say the format and the time: Same skills, exam conditions. Twelve minutes, alone, no dictionary.
  2. They work silently. You do not answer content questions during it — note them and answer afterwards.
  3. Check in pairs first, then as a class, and every answer is justified with the line that proves it. An answer without a line does not count, even when it is right.

Chunks worth stealing
desks → pairs
Why: Reading pays for itself only when something leaves the page. Chunks, not single words — that is what the writing and the speaking of this unit will need.

WHAT WE DO: the phrases from both texts, harvested for their own speaking and writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to both texts: Find five phrases you could use yourself — not words, phrases. Underline them in the book.
  2. Board the best eight as the class calls them out. Insist on the whole chunk, with its preposition.
  3. RallyRobin: partners take turns making a sentence of their own with a chunk from the board, alternating, until the board is used up.
  4. These eight go on the unit word wall and into the interactive notebook sheet — they are the ones that come back in the test.
📺 On screen: the eight chunks, written up as they are found. Backup: none.
👦 Students do: harvest five phrases each, then use the boarded chunks in their own sentences

Structure
I commute to school by tram. · The 7.44 was delayed, so I missed my connection. · You pay a lower fare if you travel off-peak. · We went on a day trip to …, and the journey took two hours.
Form
travel (uncountable, general) · a journey (getting from A to B) · a trip (a short return journey, often repeated) · an excursion (a short organised trip for pleasure) · a voyage (long, by sea or into space) · verbs that go together: catch / miss a train · get on / off a bus · get in / out of a car · board a plane · take off and land.
Pron. watch
word stress in the long unit words: photograph vs photographic · similarity · difference · between · whereas · the contracted future perfect is three sounds, not five: I'll have /aɪləv/ · I'll have been /aɪləv bɪn/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I had a good travel» — correct? → no. Travel is general and uncountable: I had a good journey / a good trip.
«Off-peak» — is it more expensive or less? → less, because fewer people are travelling.
ICQ before the reading: are the missing sentences in order? → no. How many extra sentences are there? → one — check the number.
Gap (a): the sentence before talks about countries stopping the sale of petrol cars. Sentence 8 starts «In the UK's case». What does «case» refer back to? → that decision. That is the whole technique.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The insertion task is answered by intuitionEvery answer must come with the reference word underlined in the text or in the sentence. No underline, no answer accepted. Model it on gap (a) so nobody can claim they did not understand the requirement.
Two students finish the reading in four minutes and stopThe Teacher's Book task, ready in advance: write two true sentences and one false one about the text, for the class to judge at the end of the stage.
The five journey nouns are learned as a list and then forgottenThey go on the wall in a separate box with one collocation each: a business trip · a long journey · space travel · a school excursion · the maiden voyage. The box is checked in the review lesson, and the students are told so.
Forty-five words is too many for one lessonIt is. Name the fifteen you will actually test (they are the ones in the workbook and on p.42) and let the rest live in the book as recognition vocabulary.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Task 11 (the sentence-insertion task) above all, plus tasks 10 and 12–18. Travel and transport is one of the standing topics of the Russian paper.

The reading task on page 33 is the Russian task 11 in Cambridge clothes: sentences have been removed from a text and must be put back. The technique is identical and it is not «understanding» — it is tracking reference words. «This problem», «the technology», «in the UK's case» all point backwards, and the candidate who learns to look at the sentence BEFORE the gap gets the mark. The vocabulary is exam money too: departure lounge, carbon emissions, traffic jam, off-peak, fare, delay, cancellation.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
