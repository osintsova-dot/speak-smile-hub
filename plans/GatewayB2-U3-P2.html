🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Choose correctly between will, be going to, the present continuous and the present simple for the future — prediction from evidence against prediction from opinion, intention against arrangement, timetable against decision made now — and build words with negative prefixes (dis-, il-, im-, in-, ir-, un-) and meaning prefixes (over-, under-, mis-, re-, pre-, post-, co-, inter-, semi-, sub-, super-).
What is genuinely hard
English has five ways of saying the future and Russian has one, so students do not choose — they default to will. The two decisions that actually cost marks are «evidence or opinion?» (going to or will) and «is this an arrangement?» (present continuous). And there is the rule everybody breaks: no future form after when, before, as soon as, by the time or until.
Typical error to expect
«When I will arrive, I will call you» — after when, before, after, as soon as, by the time and until, English uses the PRESENT: when I arrive, I'll call you. Russian uses the future in both clauses, so this error is universal and it is a mark in every exam.
«Look at those clouds — it will rain» — with visible evidence English takes be going to. Will is for an opinion or a decision made at the moment of speaking; be going to is for evidence and for intentions.
«I will meet my dentist tomorrow at five» for an appointment already made — a fixed arrangement takes the present continuous: I'm meeting the dentist at five.
«unpossible», «disregular», «inlogical» — the negative prefix is fixed by the word, not by logic: impossible, irregular, illogical. English chooses the prefix by the first letter of the adjective more often than by meaning.
Why the lesson is built this way
The grammar is discovered from five sentences and then immediately used on a text about something real: Greta Thunberg crossing the Atlantic in a racing yacht rather than flying, with fourteen underlined verbs that are sometimes right and sometimes wrong. Judging somebody else's sentences is harder than filling a gap and it is what the test asks for. The prefix half is deliberately placed last, because it is word-building rather than decision-making and works well when the class is tired: it lands in the story of Franky Zapata, who crossed the English Channel standing on a jet-powered board.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Greta Thunberg refused to fly to New York, so she crossed the Atlantic in a racing yacht with no toilet and no kitchen. And a French inventor crossed the English Channel standing on a flying board. Today: the five ways English talks about the future — and how one prefix turns a word into its opposite.”

WHAT WE DO: SB p.34 ex.1a–1c: name the form, match the rule, then the present simple

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. If the Flipped classroom video was homework, ask two check questions and start at ex.1a; if not, play it now (three minutes).
  2. Ex.1a, whole class, fast: five sentences, name the form. Key: 1 be going to · 2 will · 3 present simple · 4 be going to · 5 present continuous.
  3. Ex.1b alone, three minutes: match the five rules. Key: a going to (evidence, sentence 4) · b going to (intention, 1) · c will (opinion, 2) · d present continuous (arrangement, 5) · e present simple (after an expression of time, 3).
  4. RallyCoach for the check: the partner must SAY the rule, not point at it.
  5. Ex.1c, and this is the one that earns marks: after when, before, after, by the time, as soon as and until — the present simple. Drill three sentences chorally: When I arrive, I'll call you.
📺 On screen: the five sentences projected while they match. Backup: they are printed on p.34 — run it from the page and use the board for the time-expression rule.
👦 Students do: name five forms as a class, match five rules alone, then explain each to a partner

WHAT WE DO: SB p.34 ex.3: fourteen underlined verbs, some right and some wrong

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Look at the photo: Do you know who this is? What did she do in August 2019? Thirty seconds.
  2. Support: weaker students only decide CORRECT or INCORRECT. Stronger: rewrite the incorrect ones AND say why.
  3. Six minutes, then pairs compare.
  4. Check the six that are wrong, with reasons: c is going to sail (intention) · e will be / is going to (fact or evidence) · f are going to be (evidence) · g arrives (after «when») · h 's going to attend / will attend · k will listen (a hope) · m arrive (after «as soon as»).
  5. Fast finishers: write a paragraph arguing whether Greta's journey really was carbon-zero.
📺 On screen: the photo and the text on p.34 — the page is enough. Backup: not applicable.
👦 Students do: judge fourteen forms, correct the wrong ones, justify in pairs

Prefixes 1 · the negatives

Why: Sorting by prefix rather than by word makes the spelling pattern visible; the speed drill is how a fixed list is actually stored.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.35 ex.1: six prefixes and twenty-nine adjectives

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Warmer, books closed: possible, interesting, correct — make them negative. Elicit im-, un-, in-, then ask which others they know (il-, ir-, dis-).
  2. Ex.1 in pairs with a table drawn in the notebook (prefix / example / meaning), five minutes.
  3. Check by prefix, not by word: all the il- words, all the ir- words, and so on — the pattern is visible that way (il- before l, ir- before r, im- before m and p).
  4. Then stress: board disadvantage, disagree, unbelievable, incapable, illogical, immature, irresponsible, and have pairs underline the stressed syllable before you confirm.
  5. Sixty-second speed drill, whole class: you say the adjective, they shout the negative. Twenty items, no thinking time.
📺 On screen: the six prefixes in a column on the board with words added under them. Backup: not needed.
👦 Students do: build a three-column table in pairs, mark stress, then answer a twenty-item speed drill

Structure
Many countries have decided that they are going to stop the sale of petrol cars. · Experts believe there will be nearly one billion electric vehicles. · When you find yourself in a traffic jam, you'll be able to get up and away. · Next summer a Chinese company is testing a large passenger drone.
Form
be going to = evidence or intention · will = opinion, expectation, or a decision made at the moment of speaking · present continuous = a confirmed arrangement · present simple = a timetable, AND after when / before / after / as soon as / by the time / until · prefixes attach to the word, not to the meaning: impossible, irregular, illogical, unnecessary.
Pron. watch
word stress in the long unit words: photograph vs photographic · similarity · difference · between · whereas · the contracted future perfect is three sounds, not five: I'll have /aɪləv/ · I'll have been /aɪləv bɪn/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Look at those clouds.» Will it rain or is it going to rain? → going to — I can see the evidence.
«I'm meeting the dentist at five.» Have I arranged it? → yes. That is why it is the present continuous.
«When I will arrive, I will call you» — correct? → no. After when, the present: when I arrive.
«Unpossible» — correct? → no: impossible. Is there a rule? → mostly the first letter of the word, and mostly it has to be learned.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Everything is «will»Two questions on the board that they must ask out loud before answering: Can I SEE it? Is it ARRANGED? If yes to the first, going to. If yes to the second, present continuous. Will is what is left.
«When I will finish school …»Board it as a permanent cross the moment it appears: when + will ✘. Then every occurrence is pointed at rather than explained again.
Judging correct/incorrect in ex.3 is harder than expectedIt is meant to be. Support the weaker half as the Teacher's Book suggests: they only decide CORRECT or INCORRECT; the stronger half rewrites and explains.
Prefixes are guessed by logicSay once, honestly, that logic will not help: it is the word that chooses the prefix. Then drill twenty of them at speed, which is how a fixed list is actually learned.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text) and tasks 26–31 (word formation — the prefix half of the lesson is precisely this).

The Russian paper does not test «the future» as a topic; it tests whether a candidate can put the right form into a gap in a narrative. Will, be going to and the present continuous all appear, and the commonest single error is the present simple where English needs a future form — or the reverse, a will after «when». The prefixes are the other half of the mark: dis-, il-, im-, in-, ir-, un- and the meaning prefixes over-, under-, mis-, re-, pre-, post- are exactly what tasks 26–31 ask a candidate to build.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
