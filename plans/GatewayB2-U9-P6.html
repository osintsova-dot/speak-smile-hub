🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Measure the unit honestly across all five skills — the photocopiable Unit 9 Test, 100 points: vocabulary, grammar, use of English, listening, reading, writing and speaking — and leave the room with every student knowing the paper was fair.
What is genuinely hard
Not the content — the format. This is the first time in the unit that seven sections sit on one paper with nothing labelled, that listening is marked, and that speaking is filmed. The second difficulty is the writing: 140–190 words with every bullet answered, produced in sixteen minutes at the end of a demanding lesson.
Typical error to expect
«USB-port», «touchscreen», «drop down menu» — the compound nouns of this unit are spelled in a fixed way and the Test yourself page counts letters. A right word written wrong scores nothing.
«Alexa, that is a smart speaker, was launched in 2014» — that is impossible in a non-defining clause. Only who, which, whose, where and when survive after a comma.
Why the lesson is built this way
The order of the paper is set by physiology, not by the page: listening while the ears are fresh, writing last, and the speaking test run in the corner in pairs while the room writes — the only honest way to fit five skills into ninety minutes. The version is a choice, because a student who picks Higher has already decided to be measured seriously. Every speaking answer is filmed: the mark is for today, the video is for December.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Everything from this unit, on one paper, in ninety minutes. You choose which version measures you. You will get the score by skill, not one number — and your speaking answer will be on video, so in December you will hear for yourself how far you have come.”

Why: A test lesson is won or lost in these five minutes: a room that is already quiet, already spaced and already filmed does not need a single word about discipline for the next eighty-five.

WHAT WE DO: papers, pens and the video corner ready before anyone sits down

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in at the door. One question each, one full sentence back in English. Keep the ritual: it is the last easy thing in the lesson.
  2. 2 minutes — the room. Desks apart, bags on the floor, phones in the box. On the desk: pen, spare pen, water. Nothing else.
  3. 1 minute — the corner. Your phone on its stand in the far corner, two chairs facing away from the room. Check the battery and the free space now, not at minute thirty.
📺 On screen: nothing on screen yet — the board is for the test map. Backup: if your phone has no space, use the second phone or the school tablet and rename the files the same evening.

5–12′
The paper explained · Standard or Higher
board
Why: Anxiety in a test comes from not knowing the shape of the paper. Publishing it costs nothing, and letting them choose the version turns the test from something done to them into something they signed up for.

WHAT WE DO: the whole paper on the board, then each student takes the version they want

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Board the map and leave it up all lesson: VOCABULARY 20 · GRAMMAR 25 · USE OF ENGLISH 8 · LISTENING 12 · READING 15 · WRITING 10 · SPEAKING 10 = 100.
  2. Both versions go on the front desk. Say it plainly: Standard and Higher are the same paper — same skills, same hundred points, same time. Higher asks the same things in harder words. Take the one you want to be measured by. The choice is the student's; if a student cannot choose, you choose for them and say so without ceremony.
  3. Say the order of the day: Listening first, while your ears are fresh. Then vocabulary, grammar and use of English. Then reading. Writing last, and I collect at eighty-six minutes.
  4. Say the speaking rule once, now, so nobody is surprised later: While you write, I take you two at a time to the corner. Four minutes, three questions each. I film the answer — the camera is for me, so that in December we can hear how you sounded today.
  5. ICQ before anything opens: How long is the writing? — 140 to 190 words. What do you do if you finish early? — check the word count and the tenses. Nobody leaves.
📺 On screen: the test map on the board. Backup: dictate it — it is seven lines.
👦 Students do: choose Standard or Higher, write name and date, and keep the paper closed
B2_U9_Test_Standard.pdf and _Higher.pdf, printed for everyone with two spare of each
12–26′
Listening · five people talking about technology (12 points)
desks
Why: Listening goes first because it is the only section that needs the whole room silent at the same moment, and because ears at minute seventy score lower than the student actually is.

WHAT WE DO: one recording, played twice, twelve points

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Play B2_U9_Test.mp3 twice with thirty seconds between the playings. Do not pause mid-track and do not take questions while the audio runs.
  2. Before the first playing: Read the questions now. Thirty seconds. Before the second: Now fill the gaps you left and check the ones you guessed.
  3. Stand at the back with the volume in your hand. If a corridor noise ruins a stretch, note the time and replay that stretch a third time — that is fair, and it is your call, not a favour.
📺 On screen: audio only, nothing on the screen. Backup: if the sound fails, read B2_U9_Test_audioscript.pdf aloud twice at natural speed — do not slow down, and do not explain a single word.
👦 Students do: listen twice and answer twelve points
B2_U9_Test.mp3 · speaker · audioscript as backup
26–54′
Vocabulary, Grammar and Use of English (53 points) · speaking pairs start
desks + the corner
Why: Speaking is ten points of the same paper and cannot be quietly dropped; running it in the corner during the written work is the only honest way to fit five skills into ninety minutes. Video turns a one-off mark into a record: the same student, the same kind of question, three months apart.

WHAT WE DO: fifty-three points in silence, while the first pairs are filmed in the corner

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Announce once and write the finishing time on the board: Fifty-three points, twenty-eight minutes. If you are stuck, move on and come back.
  2. At minute thirty take the first pair to the corner. Four minutes: Student A asks the three questions, Student B answers with a reason and a personal detail. Then swap. The topic today is technology, screens and what you would not give up.
  3. Film every pair — this is not optional. Start recording before the first question and have each student say into the camera: Name, Unit 9, today's date. Without that line the file is worthless in three months.
  4. Mark the pair out of ten the moment they sit down again, while you still remember the interaction. The video is not for marking — it is for comparing with the next one.
  5. Walk back through the room between pairs. A teacher who never leaves the corner is a teacher who is not invigilating.
📺 On screen: nothing on screen; the phone in the corner is recording. Backup: if the recording drops, mark the pair live, write «no video» on the mark sheet and film them at the start of the next lesson.
👦 Students do: work in silence; go to the corner in pairs when called
phone on a stand · the speaking page · mark sheet
54–70′
Reading (15 points) · speaking pairs continue
desks + the corner
Why: Reading in the middle keeps the room busy while the corner works, and it is the section where a tired student can still score if the timing is announced.

WHAT WE DO: sixteen minutes, fifteen points, and the last pairs filmed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say it once and write the second finishing time under the first: Sixteen minutes, fifteen points. The answers are in the text.
  2. Keep calling pairs until everyone has spoken. At minute sixty-eight the corner closes whatever is left — the rest speak at the start of the next lesson, filmed the same way.
  3. At minute sixty-two write 8 minutes left on the board without saying anything.
📺 On screen: the finishing time on the board; the phone still recording in the corner. Backup: a wall clock if the board is needed for anything else.
👦 Students do: read and answer fifteen points; speak in the corner when called
the paper · phone on a stand
70–86′
Writing · a report for a local organisation, 140–190 words (10 points)
desks
Why: Writing goes last because it is the only section where thinking time cannot be shared with anything else, and because a student who has already spoken and read has the language of the unit warm.

WHAT WE DO: one task, sixteen minutes, every bullet point answered

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say the shape of the task in one sentence: A report, not an essay: headings, facts, and a recommendation at the end. Write what you would actually tell them.
  2. Name the two things that cost marks here and nowhere else: Every bullet point answered, and the word count. Under 140 or over 190 loses marks even if the English is beautiful.
  3. No more pairs now — you are in the room. Walk the room twice, then sit down and stop moving.
  4. At minute eighty-four: Count your words. Write the number at the bottom of the page.
📺 On screen: the word limit and the finishing time on the board. Backup: write them on paper and put it on the front desk if the board is full.
👦 Students do: write 140–190 words and count them

86–90′
Papers in · what happens next
desks → door
Why: The promise of a score by section is what makes a test formative instead of final, and the video filing rule is the difference between a record of progress and a phone full of unnamed clips.

WHAT WE DO: collected in silence, and the marking promise made out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect at eighty-six exactly, in one pile, Standard and Higher separated.
  2. Say what happens next: I mark this by the next lesson. You get the paper back with the score by section — seven numbers, not one — so you can see which skill to work on.
  3. Homework aloud and on the board — and say why it is selective: The Language checkpoint, but only the parts where you lost points. Doing the parts you already know is not homework, it is decoration.
  4. File the videos the same evening: one folder per group, file name Gateway_B2_U9_Test_Surname_date.
📺 On screen: homework on the board. Backup: dictate it — it is two lines.
👦 Students do: hand in the paper and write the homework down

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
A student refuses to be filmedMark the answer live, write «no video» on the mark sheet and move on. Never argue about the camera during a test — the mark is the same either way.
The room goes noisy when a pair leaves for the cornerTake the first pair only when everyone is already writing, use the corner furthest from the door, and put the two chairs facing away from the room.
A student finishes the whole paper at minute seventyThe rule was announced at minute five: nobody leaves. Word count first, then the tenses, then the spelling in the vocabulary section.
Not every pair has spoken by minute sixty-eightThe corner closes anyway — the written sections cannot be cut. The rest speak at the start of the next lesson, filmed the same way; write their names on the mark sheet before you forget.
Two students take different versions and compare afterwardsExpected, and harmless: the sections and the totals are identical. Say so once and do not let it become a conversation about fairness.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar, word formation and lexis — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The Test yourself page is a production paper: no options in the second half, the word has to come out of the student's own head and be spelled correctly. That is the shape of tasks 26–31, where a correct word in the wrong form and a correct form spelled wrong score exactly the same — nothing. Naming that aloud before the papers turn over is worth more than another ten minutes of revision.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
