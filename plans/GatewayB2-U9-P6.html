🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Retrieve and repair the whole unit — the technology lexis, the compound nouns, the phrasal verbs, relative clauses and the gerund–infinitive contrast — and then produce it under test conditions on the book's own Test yourself page, forty points.
What is genuinely hard
The second half is production, not recognition. Exercise 2 of the Grammar test asks for seven relative clauses written from nothing; the whole Vocabulary test is spelled, not chosen. A student who has «recognised» the unit for five lessons discovers here that recognising is not knowing — which is exactly what the test is for, and why it comes before the exam block rather than after it.
Typical error to expect
«USB-port», «touchscreen», «drop down menu» — the compound nouns of this unit are spelled in a fixed way and the Test yourself page counts letters. A right word written wrong scores nothing.
«Alexa, that is a smart speaker, was launched in 2014» — that is impossible in a non-defining clause. Only who, which, whose, where and when survive after a comma.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first forty-five minutes are retrieval, not re-teaching: everything is timed, everything is written or said by students, and the teacher holds the clock. The Grammar reference on p.120 is used as a source to be MINED (find the rule that explains this error) rather than read aloud. The vocabulary half is played, because the unit's word wall is five lessons old and needs shaking. Then the room is reset in two minutes and the paper is taken in silence, with the two decisive traps named aloud before the pens move — the comma after a non-defining clause, and the spelling of the compound nouns.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty-five minutes to get all of it back, then forty-five to show it. Forty points. Two warnings before we start: in the vocabulary test nothing is multiple choice — you spell it or you lose it. And in grammar exercise two you write the clauses yourself, so a beautiful sentence that defines nothing scores nothing.”

WHAT WE DO: reports returned individually, then a ninety-second spelling race across the whole unit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — what is the last thing you used your phone for today? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.
  2. 2 minutes — the reports come back. Hand them over at the door, by name, with one spoken line each. Not a lecture — one line: Your headings were the best in the group. · Two contractions, that's all.
  3. 1 minute — the unit in twelve words. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs: one pen, ninety seconds, twelve words from Unit 9, taking turns, spelling each one aloud as it is written. Swap with the pair behind and mark — one point per word, minus one for a wrong space or hyphen.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Page one hundred and twenty. Everything you need for the next forty-five minutes is on this page — and after that you get to prove it.

WHAT WE DO: p.120: the reference used as a source to explain errors, with the class doing the reading

WHAT WE DO: the p.120 vocabulary lists shaken out through two fast games

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, four minutes, Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: every student gets one word from p.120 on a card with its definition on the back. Find a partner, quiz, swap cards, move. Three partners minimum.
  2. Round 2, four minutes, Kagan Showdown in fours: you read a definition, everyone writes the word privately, all four boards go up together. Eight rounds, taken from the four lists — Technology and IT, adjectives, words connected with technology, phrasal verbs.
  3. Keep score by table, but keep it light — the point is the eightfold retrieval, not the winner.
  4. Two minutes, seated and written: the eight words that caused trouble go into their notebooks, spelled correctly, with the part of speech marked.
📺 On screen: word cards written before the lesson from p.120; mini-whiteboards for the Showdown
👦 Students do: quiz three partners standing, then produce eight words simultaneously on boards, then write the difficult ones down

Move break · the spelling relay

Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: four minutes of running spelling on exactly what the test marks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two teams, two columns on the board, one marker each. You say a compound noun or phrasal verb; the runner writes it and passes the marker on.
  2. Ten items, chosen to hurt in the right way: touch screen · clickbait · operating system · drop-down menu · battery charger · landline · USB port · back up · log on · run out of.
  3. One point for the word, one extra for correct spacing or hyphenation. The second point is the one they fight over, and that argument IS the revision.
  4. Sit down straight into the traps stage — the board is already full of the right words.
📺 On screen: two board markers and the board itself — nothing else
👦 Students do: ten students write under time pressure while the rest of the team checks spacing out loud

individual → class
Why: The five traps, one last time

WHAT WE DO: a fast mixed drill on precisely what the paper will punish

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ten mixed items dictated by you, written by everyone, checked immediately. Five relative clauses and five gerund–infinitive decisions, mixed on purpose so nobody switches to autopilot.
  2. Sample items: The man ___ phone was stolen called the police. · That's the café ___ we met. · I'll never forget ___ (see) the sea for the first time. · Don't forget ___ (lock) the door tonight. · She's the girl ___ won the competition.
  3. After each item, one student says the answer and one says the REASON. The reason is the mark.
  4. Last two minutes: everybody writes their own personal trap on a sticky note and puts it on the top corner of their desk. It stays there during the test. It is not cheating — it is metacognition, and it works.
📺 On screen: the ten items are dictated, not shown — closest to what the paper will feel like
👦 Students do: write ten mixed answers, justify half of them aloud, then name their own personal trap in writing

WHAT WE DO: three minutes to turn a workshop into an exam room

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Desks apart, bags on the floor, phones in the box by the door. Books closed and stacked at the front — the Test yourself page is the only page open.
  2. Rub the board clean, including the two model sentences. Say it as you do it: Everything you needed is now in your head or in your margin.
  3. Repeat the two traps one final time, and the finishing rule: check spelling, check that every clause defines, do not hand in early.
  4. Water bottles allowed, questions not. Start together.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Revision turns into the teacher talkingEverything in the first half is timed and produced by students. Your job is to hold the clock and to answer nothing that page 120 answers. If a question can be solved by the Grammar reference, point at the page and walk away.
The compound-noun section is lost to spelling, not to meaningNinety seconds of written drill in the relay, with the words COUNTED letter by letter. Say aloud that this is a spelling section pretending to be a vocabulary section.
Someone finishes the paper in twelve minutesSaid before the papers turn over: If you finish, check every spelling in the vocabulary sections, then check that every clause in exercise two actually defines something. You do not hand in early.
A weak student panics at the open writing taskTwo model answers are on the board during the revision half and are rubbed off before the test: Brazil is a country where they speak Portuguese. · Milk is a drink which comes from cows. Seeing the shape twice is enough.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar, word formation and lexis — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The Test yourself page is a production paper: no options in the second half, the word has to come out of the student's own head and be spelled correctly. That is the shape of tasks 26–31, where a correct word in the wrong form and a correct form spelled wrong score exactly the same — nothing. Naming that aloud before the papers turn over is worth more than another ten minutes of revision.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
