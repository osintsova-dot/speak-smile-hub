🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): A project for new students has to be doable, so we work strictly to a blank of five blocks (description · habitat · population then and now · why it is in danger · how to help) and to the model poster on p.83 — it shows both the amount and the level of language. The Super skill (listening to someone else's opinion) is drilled through ready-made phrases. The unit revision is built into the middle as stations — without the word “test”.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Poster day. Two endangered animals, one poster. And you're not inventing anything: page 83 shows exactly what a good poster looks like — five blocks of information, short sentences, numbers, photos. You fill in the same five blocks for your animals. Then the whole crew presents together, with your notes in your hands, and the class votes. Before that, four quick stations to check what we know — so nothing surprises us later.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: we make a poster about two endangered animals strictly on the model from p.83: five blocks, short sentences, numbers, photos. The whole crew presents it with their notes. Before building it — four revision stations.

WHAT WE DO: STEP 2 PLAN: fill in the five-block blank for two animals

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the blank: 1 What is it? 2 Where does it live? 3 How many were there? How many are there now? 4 Why is it in danger? 5 How can we help?
  2. Sources: the printed fact files on 8 species, the article p.75, the model poster p.83, phones — optional.
  3. The rule: Notes in English. Short. Numbers matter.
  4. Fill in block 1 on the board together with the class as a model. Then Kagan RallyCoach: one student fills in a block from the fact file and says aloud what they are writing, the other is the coach: holds the fact file, checks the figure, helps with a word; on the next block they swap roles. That way both blanks get filled and nobody stays silent.
  5. Go round and check block 3 (the numbers) with every pair — it is the most important and the most often skipped.
📺 On screen: the five-block blanks · the printed fact files · timer
👦 Students do: fill in the blank. Expect: they find the numbers quickly, “why it is in danger” needs help

WHAT WE DO: 4 stations of 4 minutes each on Quick review SB p.81 + a self-check key at every one

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four stations, four minutes each. Each station has a key — check yourselves.
  2. Station 1: animals by class + body parts + adjectives (tracks 41–42). Station 2: the eight taking-action verbs (track 43). Station 3: was/were and there was/were + short answers. Station 4: -ed (spelling and the three sounds) + didn't.
  3. A personal power list: Write down what YOU missed. Twenty minutes at home on THAT beats two hours on everything.

WB Unit 6 · the check is next lesson (announced gently)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can fill in five information blocks about an endangered animal and present a poster together, using the language of the unit; the whole unit is revised for the P8 check.
  • Subsidiary. The model presentation on video (ex.1); taking the model poster apart (ex.2–3); choosing a class and researching on the blank (ex.4–5); the Super skills phrases (ex.6–7); building it (ex.8); presenting and peer review (ex.9–10); Quick review p.81 as stations; Final reflection p.83.
  • Personal. Hand out the five-block blank and printed fact files on 8 species — the internet is not required. The WHOLE crew presents, everyone has one line of their own (the TB requires both to speak). Call the revision “a check before we print”.
By the end, students can…
  • fill in five blocks of information about an animal
  • use Super skills phrases to agree and decide
  • present one section of a poster with a linking phrase
  • pass all Quick-review stations of Unit 6

Structure
Red pandas live in the forests of China and Nepal. · They've got red fur and a white striped face. · Fifty years ago, there were about 20,000. Now there are less than 10,000. · They are in danger because their habitat is disappearing. · You can join a wildlife organisation.
Form
three tenses on one poster (a TB observation): present simple — what the animal is; past simple — how many there were; can / need to — what to do.
Pron. watch
clear numbers: thirteen /ˌθɜːˈtiːn/ vs thirty /ˈθɜːti/ · linking phrases with a pause: Now let's look at its habitat. ↘
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
A poster about a cat — does that work? → no, only about animals IN DANGER.
How many blocks are compulsory? → five: description · habitat · population then and now · why it is in danger · how to help.
Are the stations a test? → no, they are a check before we print: find your own gaps today.

Assumptions: SB pp.81–83, syllabus pair 52. All the unit material has been covered; what is new is gathering information and working together. Keep a pack of printed fact files on 8 species.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They don't know where to get the informationPrinted fact files on 8 species + the model poster p.83 + the article p.75. The internet is optional, not necessary.
One writes, the other says nothingThe blank is physically split: each student fills in their own blocks. In the presentation everyone has their own line (the TB requires it).
The project eats the revisionThe stations sit IN THE MIDDLE of the lesson and run on a timer; building the poster only starts after them.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
