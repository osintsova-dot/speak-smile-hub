🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Describe clothes with the twelve Unit 2 adjectives (six opposite pairs) and use the past simple — regular and irregular, negatives and questions — to talk about what people wore and did.
What is genuinely hard
The tense is old; the FORMS are the fight: irregular verbs produced at speed (chose, wore, tore) and the spelling traps (planned, not planed). Plus six adjective pairs that look alike on the page (loose-fitting / skinny, smart / casual) and need anchoring to real clothes.
Typical error to expect
choosed · heared · weared · taked — a regular ending glued onto an irregular verb; and planed for planned [high priority, A2/B1]
Why you like it? / What you wore yesterday? — the missing auxiliary do/does/did [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The four celebrity photos open the lesson because clothes vocabulary without pictures is a list; with pictures it is an argument. The adjectives are learnt as OPPOSITE PAIRS — half the memorising for the same twelve words. The reading then wears the vocabulary (sixty years of youth fashion) and quietly delivers every past simple form the grammar stage will need — so when discovery starts, the examples are already familiar. Practice ends in questions, not statements, because the productive stage is an interview about last weekend: questions are what students actually need. Talking points sit before the speaking as a warm-up of opinions, not after it as an afterthought.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Your grandparents were cooler than you think. Mini-skirts that shocked the world, punks who tore their own clothes on purpose. Today: twelve words to judge any outfit — and the tense that lets you ask anybody what they wore, bought and did. Last weekend included.”

Why: The adjectives arrive from a real interview recording, and the opposites task turns six words into twelve pairs — instantly usable in the reading that follows.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2–3 (track 05): the interview, then ex.4 — the six opposite pairs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Gist first, ex.2: Three teenagers, one question. What is the interview about — a, b or c? Play track 05. Key: c — what they like wearing.
  2. ex.3 — who says it: Ashley, Molly or Luke? Second listening. Key: 1 A (Ashley) · 2 L (Luke) · 3 M (Molly) · 4 A · 5 L · 6 M.
  3. ex.4 in pairs — match the opposites; the six «vs» slots on the board get filled. Key: badly-dressed–well-dressed · casual–smart · loose-fitting–skinny · second-hand–brand new · uncomfortable–comfortable · unfashionable–trendy.
  4. Drill the three pronunciation traps: dressed /drest/ (one syllable!) · casual /ˈkæʒjuəl/ · uncomfortable /ʌnˈkʌmftəbl/.
  5. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the adjective cards: Quiz your partner with the opposite. Coach if they're wrong. Trade. New partner! 3 minutes, five trades each.
📺 On screen: track 05 in the whiteboard app (Backup: read the interview from the audioscript, TB p.261 — two voices, slightly hammed up, works even better)
👦 Students do: listen twice with different depth, pair the opposites, then drill in a mingle. Expect loose-fitting/skinny to be the freshest pair. Typical error: dressed as two syllables — catch it in the mingle

WHAT WE DO: sixty years of youth culture: gist scan, six A/B choices, the word hunt

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Grammar · past simple discovery
pairs → class
Why: The forms are old friends; what is new is producing irregular forms at speed and spelling them right (chose, tore, planned). Discovery from the article keeps it meaningful; the patterns work (sing–sang, ring–rang) makes it stick.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: three examples from the article → three rules; ex.2: the irregular gauntlet

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed, two columns on the board (TB technique): REGULAR · IRREGULAR. From the article, what did teenagers DO in the fifties? They listened… they didn't understand… What did they wear? — write the three examples up.
  2. Pairs complete the rules from the box (did / didn't / -d or -ed): regular verbs end in…, negative = …, questions = … Key: rules a -d or -ed · b didn't · c did.
  3. ex.2 — choose the correct form, 8 items, solo then Kagan Showdown per item. Key: 1 chose · 2 heard · 3 enjoyed · 4 get · 5 read, sang · 6 meet · 7 go · 8 planned.
  4. Pattern moment: sing–sang, ring–rang. choose–chose, speak–spoke. Irregulars come in families — collect them, don't cram them. Start the family list in the corner of the board.
  5. Spelling flash: planned, stopped, shopped — short vowel, double the letter.
📺 On screen: the ex.2 items one at a time on the screen (Backup: write the eight pairs on the board before the lesson and uncover them one by one)
👦 Students do: derive the three rules, then survive eight choose-the-form items. Expect read/read and sang/sung to spark arguments — good, the pattern talk lands there. Typical error: choosed — straight into the watch-out column
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit2_Grammar_Past_Simple_A5
A5 grammar card — PAST SIMPLE: FINISHED IN THE PAST: the four blocks — BE (was/were · wasn't/weren't · Was/Were…?), REGULAR VERBS +-ed with the spelling notes (live–lived · study–studied · stop–stopped), IRREGULAR VERBS (wear–wore · go–went · buy–bought · see–saw · have–had · get–got) and NEGATIVES + QUESTIONS (didn't + BASE VERB · Did + subject + BASE VERB?) with the crossed-out Did you bought it? and the finished-time markers yesterday · last week · in 2024 · two days ago
  • Glue the card in and highlight the base-verb rule after did / didn't.
  • Learn the six irregular pairs by heart — they are the ones needed here.
  • Write three sentences about last weekend: affirmative, negative, question.
  • Retell the sheet's chain: I went shopping, found a jacket and bought it.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: true sentences about yourself · ex.4: the UTme dialogue · ex.5: build the questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: the ex.5 questions asked for real: a past-simple interview about last weekend

Form

Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I choosed a T-shirt» — what's wrong? → choose is irregular: chose.
«She planed a concert» — spelling? → doubled n: planned.
«Did you went shopping?» — fix it. → did + infinitive: Did you go shopping?

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
choosed / heared / weared produced under time pressureThat is the plan working — the error surfaces where it is cheap. Collect them in a «watch out» column on the board; the column stays up into lesson 5's review.
dressed said as two syllables (/ˈdresɪd/)Drill by ear: dressed /drest/ (one syllable!) · casual /ˈkæʒjuəl/ · uncomfortable /ʌnˈkʌmftəbl/. One syllable, /drest/ — three choral repetitions, then catch it every time without fuss.
The reading's six A/B items answered from memory, not textThe evidence rule again: No line from the text — no point. Finger on the line. RallyCoach pairs enforce it cheaply.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
