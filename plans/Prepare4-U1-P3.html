Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners are better able to describe clothes and personal style using 12 clothes adjectives (in opposite pairs) and to talk about finished past events using the Past Simple (positive, negative, questions), in the context of fashion & music.
  • Subsidiary. Reading for gist then detail (article “Fashion and Music”); vocabulary from context (highlighted words); listening for detail (interview, SB ex 2–3); fluency in a personalised speaking task; exam sub-skill — multiple-choice reading & word-meaning matching (ОГЭ / B1 Preliminary).
  • Personal. Keep TTT low; guided discovery for grammar (elicit the rule, don't present it); give an ICQ before every task; run one clear brain-break at the midpoint; delay error correction to a board round.
By the end, learners can…
  • describe an outfit/style with 6 opposite adjective pairs;
  • form Past Simple affirmatives, negatives & questions (regular + 6 key irregulars);
  • read an article for gist and specific detail and infer word meaning from context;
  • ask & answer about last weekend / a past event fluently.

Grammar
Past Simple — regular + -ed (listened) · key irregulars wore · went · bought · saw · had · got · negative didn't + base verb · question Did + subj + base verb?
Chunks / frames
I prefer comfortable, loose-fitting clothes. · For special occasions I wear smart clothes. · Last weekend I went shopping and bought… · What did you do…?
Form
Time markers: yesterday · last week · in 2024 · two days ago. After did / didn't → base verb (not past).
Pron. watch
-ed endings /t/ (watched) · /d/ (played) · /ɪd/ (wanted); weak “did you” /dɪdʒə/; stress on the content word, not on did.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Past Simple: “Is the action finished?” → yes · “Now or before?” → before · “Do we know exactly when?” → yes/often.
“She didn't buy — is it bought or buy after didn't?” → buy (base verb).
Lexis: “Second-hand — new or used?” → used · “Skinny jeans — tight or loose?” → tight.

Assumptions: learners already met Present Simple/Continuous & describing-people adjectives last week (Unit 1); most can produce simple past of common verbs but are shaky on irregulars and on word order in questions/negatives. Grouping: Quiz-Quiz-Trade & mingles need the whole class standing; pairs of 2 for RallyCoach, teams of 4 for the reading race.

Lead-in

Hook. Project the SB p.14 photos (Jaden, Taylor, Ed, Rihanna). Look at these four people. What are they wearing? Whose style do you like — and why?

▶ ABOUT YOU (SB ex, p.14) — Think–Pair–Share: 30 sec think, then to a partner — What are you wearing today? What fashions do you like? Which colours look good on you? Take 2–3 open feedback lines. Set the topic: Today — clothes, fashion through time, and how we talk about the past.

Vocabulary · presentation
whole class → notebook

Meaning & form. Elicit/teach the 12 adjectives in opposite pairs against the photos: comfortable ↔ uncomfortable · loose-fitting ↔ skinny · casual ↔ smart · brand new ↔ second-hand · trendy ↔ unfashionable · well-dressed ↔ badly-dressed. CCQ each tricky one (see CCQ box). Choral + individual drill for stress: com·FOR·table, un·FASH·ion·able.

📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit2_Vocabulary1_Clothes_Adjectives (A5 glue-in). Learners glue in the sheet, mark the opposite pairs with arrows, then tick what they are wearing today and cross out what they never wear. Frame at the bottom: I prefer comfortable, loose-fitting clothes. For special occasions, I wear smart clothes.

ICQ: Do you write a definition or an example? — Tick what YOU wear today.

Vocabulary · practice
carpet/space, standing

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (Kagan): each learner gets a card — one side an adjective, other side a definition/opposite. Stand up, hand up, pair up! Partner A quizzes (What's the opposite of ‘skinny’?), B answers, coach if needed → swap → trade cards → new partner. 4–5 rounds — everyone speaks every round.

▶ SB p.14 ex 5 — About you (Timed Pair Share, 30 sec each): What do you like wearing? What do you never wear? Do you prefer smart or casual clothes? How important is it to wear trendy clothes? Frame support on board: I generally prefer… because… / I never wear… because…

30–34′
Reading · gist
desks → teams

Pre-task. Title only: “Fashion and Music.” How can music and clothes be connected? Predict two ideas with your partner.

▶ Reading race (SB p.15 ex 1) — teams of 4: Read the article QUICKLY. How many bands/singers from the last century can you name? Strict 2-minute limit. ICQ: read every word, or read fast for names? (→ fast). First team with a correct list wins the round.

 SB p.15 article · timer
34–44′
Reading · detail + lexis
desks, pairs

Detail (SB p.15 ex 2). Read again; choose the correct answers (1–6, A/B) individually, then compare in pairs before whole-class check. Push for evidence: Which line tells you that?

Vocabulary from context (SB p.15 ex 3). Match highlighted words to meanings: trends → fashions/styles · tore → pulled to break · messy → untidy/dirty · youth → young people in general · look → someone's appearance · clothing → what people wear in general. Elicit the strategy: Cover the word — what MUST it mean from the sentence around it?

▶ Talking Points (SB p.15) — RallyRobin: Why do young people dress differently from their parents? Why do people copy music stars?

 SB p.15 ex 2–3 + Talking Points
44–47′
Brain-break
standing

▶ “Past tense stand-up.” Call a verb; if you say the correct Past Simple form they clap once and sit fast; if you say a wrong form (e.g. buyed, goed, didn't went) they freeze. Quick, energising, and it primes the grammar focus.

 — (voice only)
47–60′
Grammar · guided discovery
whole class → notebook

Discovery (SB p.16 ex 1). Learners read the three examples from the article (They listened… · They didn't understand… · What did they wear?) and complete the rules with did · didn't · -d/-ed. Elicit, don't tell. Board the concept: We use the Past Simple for finished past actions and states. Run the CCQs (finished? before? base verb after did/didn't?).

Form focus. Highlight regular + -ed vs the 6 key irregulars wear→wore, go→went, buy→bought, see→saw, have→had, get→got; board the crossed-out Did you bought it? → base verb.

📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit2_Grammar_Past_Simple (A5 card). Glue in; highlight the base-verb rule after did/didn't; learn the 6 irregular pairs by heart; then write 3 sentences about last weekend — one affirmative, one negative, one question. Chant the model chain: I went shopping, found a jacket and bought it.

Grammar · controlled practice
desks, pairs

I do → we do → you do. Model item 1 aloud (think-aloud), do item 2 together, then release.

SB p.16 ex 2 (choose correct) & ex 3 (personalise, positive/negative). Individually, then ▶ RallyCoach in pairs: A solves & says why, B coaches & praises → swap each item.

SB p.16 ex 4 (complete the conversation). Pairs complete, then read the dialogue aloud (act it) — watch -ed pronunciation & weak “did you”.

ICQ: In the gap after ‘didn't’ — past form or base form? (→ base).

Freer production · speaking
mingle, pairs

Build the questions (SB p.16 ex 5). Pairs turn prompts into questions: What did you do last weekend? Did you go shopping? Which friends did you meet? Did you play any sport? What did you watch on TV? Did you go to bed late?

▶ SB p.16 ex 6 — “Find someone who…” mingle: learners circulate and ask their questions, noting names. Tiered frames on board (choose your level): Did you…? → What did you…? → How was… and why? Recycle 2–3 clothes adjectives: Did you buy anything new? What was it like?

Report back: Tell us one thing your partner did — and what they wore/bought.
