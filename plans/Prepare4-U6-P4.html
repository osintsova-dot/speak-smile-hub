🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Grade certainty with the adverbs of probability (definitely · probably · perhaps/maybe · definitely not) and describe a photo in connected sentences: what I can see, where it is, what is probably happening.
What is genuinely hard
Two hard points: word order (perhaps opens the sentence, probably/definitely live INSIDE it — Russian puts «наверное» anywhere), and the present continuous under pressure — the auxiliary drops exactly when a student is busy thinking what to say next.
Typical error to expect
It is perhaps a bird · Definitely it is a fox — perhaps/maybe live at the START of the sentence; probably/definitely sit inside it like frequency adverbs (It's definitely…, It definitely isn't…) [A2/B1]
He wearing a red jacket · They looking for someone — the auxiliary dropped out of the present continuous exactly where photo description needs it [national exam report, speaking task 3]
Why the lesson is built this way
The adverbs are pinned to a NO→YES scale and immediately spent on the book's mystery macro photos and the true/false pairwork — grading certainty about each other is the most motivating subject available. The Animals-at-work listening runs the block's three-pass discipline and feeds the fairness discussion, where opinions must carry reasons. The speaking half is the unit's crown and the book flags it as an exam part — we run it nameless: a recorded model first (see the bar before reaching for it), the frames drilled, then round 1 on the rescue photo, a clinic for the dropped auxiliaries, and round 2 on fresh p.124 photos with two minutes of silent thinking — the exact think-then-talk shape that produces connected description instead of a word list.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One photo. Two minutes of thinking. Then you talk for a minute straight — in English, about anything they show you. That's a superpower, and today you build it: first the little words that say HOW sure you are, then a rat with a job clearing bombs… and then the photos. You'll leave describing pictures like a sports commentator.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a deduction hot round in pairs, and the bridge into probability

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — hot round: deduction dice. Kagan RallyRobin in pairs: A names any object in the room plus one fact (It's small and it buzzes), B answers through the frames (It can't be the projector… it must be a fly!). Three each, swap.
  3. One choral repair of the week's trap for the road: Это не может быть — it…? CAN'T be!
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the lesson: Today the animals get JOBS — and you get the examiner's favourite superpower: describing any photo for a minute straight.
📺 On screen: objects and facts by voice — the voice is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: fire deduction chains at speed. Expect the modals to hold from Tuesday; one silent card-point per mustn't

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the scale + the word-order rule; ex.2: the mystery photos

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs, 3 minutes: place the bold adverbs on the NO→YES diagram. Key: the NO→YES scale: 1 definitely not · (probably not) · 2 perhaps/maybe · 3 probably · 4 definitely. Word order: perhaps/maybe open the sentence; probably/definitely sit inside it like frequency adverbs
  2. The slots rule, drilled chorally with claps: PERHAPS it's a bird (clap at the front) · it's PROBably an insect (clap in the middle).
  3. Concept checks from the plan header — percentages and the broken definitely-sentence, cold-called.
  4. ex.2 — the two mystery photos, pairs, one adverb per guess, then check on p.123: the two mystery macro photos: A the eye of a goat · B a horse's leg (answers printed on SB p.123).
📺 On screen: the scale card is already on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; the mystery photos are on SB p.70 in every book
👦 Students do: place, clap the slots, guess the photos. Expect the goat's eye to be called a snake's — definitely, loudly; the reveal teaches humility and the adverb in one move. Typical error: it definitely is — slot drill, one repeat

Why: The listening's own follow-up run as a real exchange of opinions — with the reason compulsory, because an opinion without a reason scores as nothing everywhere that matters.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: Riley and Donna, then the fairness question — reasons compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Groups of three: 1 — how has Riley changed Donna's life? 2 — what OTHER jobs could animals do? 3 — is it FAIR to use animals in dangerous work?

  4. Close with the scale: Will robots take these animal jobs one day? Definitely? Probably? Perhaps? — thirty seconds of choral opinion.

👦 Students do: discuss, hold every member ready, deliver with reasons. Expect real disagreement on fairness — gold; keep it in English with the frame. Typical error: opinion full stop — the frame is the gatekeeper

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3: the rescue photo, the recorded model, the ladder

WHAT WE DO: delayed correction between the rounds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three lines from your round-1 log on the board, anonymous: a dropped is, a definitely in the wrong slot, a bare list.
  2. Kagan RallyRobin in pairs: fix all three, hands up, repair together fast.

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions

«He wearing a red jacket» — what fell out? → is. The photo is happening NOW: is/are + -ing, every time.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
perhaps/probably scattered anywhere in the sentenceTwo slots, said as a rule of the road: perhaps gets on the bus FIRST; probably sits in the MIDDLE seat. The scale card shows both slots with arrows.
«He wearing…» — auxiliaries drop in round 1Never interrupt mid-description. Log, then the clinic repairs three anonymous lines and round 2 exists to use the repair. The delta is the lesson.
Descriptions collapse into word lists (Snow. Dog. Man.)The frames are compulsory and ordered: I can see… → On the left… → It's probably… → They might be… A list answers none of them.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
