🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): A grammar lesson, but the topic is NOT 'past simple of be' — the topic is 'the extinction museum'. Every crew gets an exhibit, writes the label for it and then answers visitors' questions. The form becomes the price of admission: a wrong was/were or a wrong word order in a question and the exhibit comes off the wall. The word-order rule (ex.3) is worked out by the students themselves from the examples, you only confirm it. Draw the TB timeline on the board — that technique will be useful for the tenses that come next.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“The Extinction Museum opens in this room in seventy minutes. Each crew gets one species that is gone: the dodo, the mammoth, Steller's sea cow, the Tasmanian tiger, the baiji dolphin. You write the exhibit label — what it WAS, where it lived, how many there WERE. Then visitors come and ask questions, and you answer live. The museum has one rule: one wrong word on the label and the exhibit comes down. Labels — start writing!”
The gist, if you need to restate it: we open a museum of extinct species: every crew gets an exhibit and a label (what it was, where it lived, how many there were), then the visitors ask questions. One mistake on the label and the exhibit comes down.

WHAT WE DO: the exhibit label (4 lines) + a live tour: the class asks 4 questions, the crew answers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Writing 6 min: Four lines on the label: what it WAS, where it lived, how many there WERE, and one thing that WASN'T true about it.
  2. A peer air-check through Kagan RallyCoach: in a pair the first student reads their line out loud and says WHY it is was or were, the second is the coach with a checklist (was or were? there was or there were? no -s after was?), catches and praises; on the next line they swap roles. All of this BEFORE the tour.
  3. The tour: Visitors, four questions per exhibit — two Yes/No, two Wh-. Guides: short answers only.
  4. Points: +2 for a clean label · +1 for every clean answer · −1 an exhibit taken down for an error (the judges record it).

👦 Students do: write, check each other and run the tour. Expect: the peer check catches most of the errors before the stage — and it shows in the score

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can use was/wasn't, were/weren't and there was/were in statements and questions at speed, and run a live museum tour answering visitors' Yes/No and Wh- questions without a broken form.
  • Subsidiary. The tables in ex.1 are completed with the examples from the box; ex.2 four pairs of sentences; the word-order rule in ex.3 is worked out by them; ex.4 building questions; ex.5 matching answers; ex.6 Fun Facts Forum; Brain teaser ex.7; the error clinic on P1–P2.
  • Personal. The first grammar lesson of the unit: do NOT explain the rule — the students complete the tables and formulate the word order themselves (guided discovery). Draw the timeline on the board (TB technique). The error feedback is anonymous.
By the end, students can…
  • complete a label with was/wasn't, were/weren't
  • use there was and there were with singular and plural
  • build Yes/No and Wh- questions about the past
  • answer a visitor's question with a short answer (Yes, it was. / No, there weren't.)

Structure
Hunting was a serious problem. · The Tasmanian tiger wasn't a tiger. · Steller's sea cows were very big sea mammals. · There was less forest for the gorilla. · There were more than a million saigas. · There weren't any laws. · Were they dangerous? — No, they weren't. · Was the dodo a bird? — Yes, it was. · Were there any baiji dolphins in Australia? — No, there weren't. · What was the problem? · Why was there a big campaign? · How many saigas were there in 1990?
Form
in a Yes/No question the verb comes FIRST: Was the dodo a bird? (TB key ex.3: first) · in a Wh- question the verb comes AFTER the question word: What was the problem? (TB key: after) · in questions with there was/were the verb comes BEFORE there: Were there rhinos in Cameroon? (TB key: before) · there was + singular/uncountable · there were + plural
Pron. watch
the weak form was /wəz/ in a statement and the strong /wɒz/ in a short answer: It was a bird /wəz/ — Yes, it WAS /wɒz/; weren't /wɜː(r)nt/ is said as one chunk, not 'we-rent'
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«There were a big campaign» — what is wrong? → one campaign: There WAS a big campaign.
«Was they dangerous?» — correct it. → Were they dangerous? — they always takes were.
«There was any laws?» — and here? → Were there any laws? — in a question the verb comes BEFORE there.

Assumptions: SB p.76, syllabus pair 48 (both halves are grammar). The students know present simple be confidently; what is new is the second form, there was/were and the word order in a question under the pressure of a game.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
'was/were again, we know this'Don't argue: a gate on writing speed shows the real ceiling in 60 seconds. After that it is the thrill of the museum that works, not the rule.
There were a big campaign — the classicA separate there was/were station: physically two boxes on the table (singular / plural), you put the card in a box and only then speak.
They keep statement word order in questions (Why there was…?)The museum rule: a broken visitor's question gets no answer — the guide stays silent and points at the card with the rule. The burnt questions get sorted out in the clinic in the same lesson.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
