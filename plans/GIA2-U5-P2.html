🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The guidebook is read not “because it was set” but as a food scout's dossier: the crew picks ONE dish worth opening in our town, and has to back every claim with a line. The unit's subskill (identifying key words) is baked into the rule: first underline the key words in the task and decide which words to look for, then scan — otherwise the answer doesn't count. The Critical thinking section from the book (“why try other people's food at all”) becomes the finale of the mission, not an add-on.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“New file: five dishes, five countries. Chips with cheese and meat sauce in Canada. A cake with no chocolate in Portugal. Street chicken in Jamaica. Purple ice cream in the Philippines. Noodles the government invented in Thailand. Your crew is a food scout: read the guidebook, fill the dossier — dish, country, ingredients — and choose ONE dish to open in our town. Sixty seconds to defend it. Every claim needs a LINE from the text. No line, no point.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: a dossier on five dishes from five countries: we read the guidebook, fill in the “dish — country — ingredients” table and choose one dish worth opening in our town. Every claim is backed by a line from the text.

🔥 Challenge · dish dossier

WHAT WE DO: ex.6 (track 31): five false statements — fix them and back each one with a line, 8 sec per turn

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Five statements. All five are WRONG. Fix them — and show me the line.
  2. TB key for ex.6: 1 it is a winter dish · 2 there is NO chocolate in pastel de nata (a pastry of eggs and butter) · 3 jerk chicken is eaten with rice and peas, not with chips · 4 halo-halo is BETTER than ice cream (“there is something better”) · 5 beef and chicken are an optional extra, not compulsory ingredients.
  3. The TB tip, said out loud: Look at HOW the text says it — 'there isn't any chocolate', 'there aren't any chips'. The little words carry the answer.
  4. Constraint: Eight seconds per fix. No line on the page — the point burns.
  5. Handing in through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: the crew agrees first and everyone puts a finger on the right line, then I call a number — Number fours: fix it and read me the line! It isn't the fastest who answers but the one I name — so everyone hunts for the line.
📺 On screen: SB pp.62–63 ex.6 · audio track 31 (backup — I read the guidebook text out myself while they follow the line with a finger) · 8-second timer
👦 Students do: they repair the statements with the line in hand. Expect: no.4 and no.5 are the hardest — they are about meaning, not fact; we go over them out loud

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second verdict: which dish we open here + two facts from the text + one argument from Critical thinking

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. Critical thinking 4 — the bank of arguments (sample TB answer): a varied diet is healthier · you discover new tastes · you understand other cultures better · the same food all the time is boring.
  3. Sixty seconds per crew. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness.
  4. A challenge from the floor: Winter in Norilsk lasts eight months. Does that change your choice?

👦 Students do: they defend their choice with evidence. Expect: poutine wins on the climate — and that is an excellent argument

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can find specific information in a guidebook by key words (underline → decide what to look for → scan), use 6 new words in context, and deliver a 60-second verdict on which dish their town needs.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 track 30 (five food descriptions); ex.3 the flags and countries; ex.5 the dossier table; ex.6 correcting five statements (there isn't/aren't any hide inside them); Word work (delicious · mix · smell · takeaway · add · food stalls); Critical thinking 1–4.
  • Personal. The reading speed of the strong ones drowns out the weaker ones — I hold the rule “an answer only counts with a line of evidence”. I collect the there is/are errors silently for P3.
By the end, students can…
  • underline key words in a question before reading
  • scan a guidebook and quote the line that proves an answer
  • use six new words (delicious, mix, smell, takeaway, add, food stalls)
  • give a 60-second verdict with two pieces of evidence

Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I think poutine is a summer dish” — does that count? → no: only with a line from the text (“cold and hungry in winter”).
is a takeaway a restaurant? → no: food you buy and carry off to eat somewhere else.
Why underline the words in the task BEFORE reading? → to decide WHAT to look for and scan, instead of reading everything.

Assumptions: SB pp.62–63, KTP pair 39. They read confidently, but “key words first” is a new discipline. All 6 Word work words are in the official Wordlist.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
