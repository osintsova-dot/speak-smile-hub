🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Work the book's own Review 5 spread (Units 17–20): the arts and jobs lexis, word building, the reported family (statements AND commands), the second conditional and the passive — mixed, the way May tests them.
What is genuinely hard
Choosing WHICH rule before the rule itself: transformations that flip between reported, conditional and passive in consecutive lines. Units 17–18 are six weeks old — retrieval, not recognition, is the work.
Typical error to expect
He said me he made films — say glued to an object where English wants tell; the pair error is she told that… with the object dropped [high priority, A2/B1]
This house it's built in the 16th century · we were allow to eat — the participle or the auxiliary lost on the way to the passive [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
An honest split runs through the lesson: Units 19–20 are the core (tested only last lesson — today they stretch), Units 17–18 are revisit (reviewed and tested in the block 9 lesson — today they get retrieval with support: the reporting-verbs box, the suffix wall). The bridge is grammar ex.1: reported COMMANDS (advised/warned/persuaded + object + to-infinitive) join lesson 61's reported statements into one family — the spread teaches the connection itself. The exam-style cloze is run as careful reading, and the full Crazy Sentences! deck — the publisher's own Units 17–20 game — is the productive finale the review earned.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Four units, one spread, forty crazy sentences. Everything since March fits on two pages — prove you still own it, then we let the deck of cards ruin it beautifully.”

WHAT WE DO: the flip drill: reported / conditional / passive line to line

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3 in pairs, ≤3 words per gap, system named BEFORE each answer: ex.3 (core) transformations ≤3 words: 1 The Mona Lisa was PAINTED BY Leonardo da Vinci · 2 Salvator Mundi WAS SOLD for US$400 million · 3 Nina said SHE COULDN'T IMAGINE life without her phone · 4 If I were you, I WOULDN'T PAINT your bedroom black · 5 «I'M GOING TO the exhibition later,» said Joe (reported → direct)
  2. ex.4–5 (Corpus) as a hunt — find, fix, justify: ex.4 (Corpus, choose): 1 asked us (not «asked to us») · 2 wanted (second conditional) · 3 tell you · 4 was filmed — and ex.5 (Corpus, correct): 1 warns → warned · 2 will be → would be · 3 tells → told · 4 was tasted → tasted
  3. Kagan RallyCoach throughout: A drives odds, B coaches; swap for evens.
  4. Board the day's best save — a student who named the system first and got the line right because of it.

Why: 🎯 Crazy Sentences! · the full deck

WHAT WE DO: the publisher's Units 17–20 game as the productive finale

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The game, from the sheet: Groups of three or more, one cut set of sentence cards per group, a blank sheet per player. A card is read aloud up to its first blank («The man with the colourful ___»); everyone completes the blank IN SECRET at the top of their sheet, folds the paper back and passes it left. Three blanks per card, three folds — then the sheets are unfolded and the crazy sentences are read out. The group votes for the funniest; the teacher may award points for originality, humour, vocabulary, spelling or grammar.
  2. Today the WHOLE deck is legal: On Review 5 day the WHOLE pack is legal — the cards deliberately mix all four units (firefighters + reported warnings, the vet with a second-conditional thought, the lawyer who advised her to…, the novelist who «would write stories if…»). Run the full game as the productive finale.
  3. Twelve minutes on the visible timer, three cards per group. Then the public round: one crazy sentence per group, read with full drama.
  4. Points from the teacher's notes menu — grammar that survived, vocabulary, spelling, humour. The class votes the champion sentence of the spring.
  5. You log nothing today — this stage is the reward.
📺 On screen: the cut card sets on desks; visible timer (Backup: no timer — call the minutes aloud; the game needs only paper and the cards)
👦 Students do: write in secret, fold, pass, perform. Expect the vet's second-conditional thought and the lady who WAS ASKED to leave to produce the loudest results — four units of grammar laughing at itself

Form

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«The police warned us not to open the door» — their words? → «Don't open the door!»: command → verb + object + not to.
«ordered» or «asked»? — was it polite? → orders don't say please: the verb carries the tone.
«Salvator Mundi was sold for $400 million» — who sold it? → not said, not needed: the price is the news.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
U17–18 material feels «deleted» after six weeksSupport, don't lecture: the reporting-verbs box and the suffix wall go up BEFORE the tasks that need them; retrieval with a ladder is still retrieval.
Reported commands crossed with reported statementsOne board contrast, boxed: said/told + shifted CLAUSE vs verb + object + TO-infinitive. Point at it; the auction prices the difference.
The cloze answered by feelThe words around the gap vote — pairs underline the deciding words for two items before any letter is accepted.
The game inflates past its slotThe deck is dealt (three cards per group), the timer is visible, one sentence per group goes public. The game ends the lesson, so overrun eats only the wrap-up — protect five minutes for it.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 20–34 across four units at once — the exam never tests one unit at a time, and neither does today's Review 5 spread.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
