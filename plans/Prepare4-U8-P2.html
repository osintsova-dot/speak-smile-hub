🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Form and use the present simple passive — be + past participle, by-agent only when it matters — to talk about the digital world, and correct the classic learner errors (my friend is call…, this computer it's used…).
What is genuinely hard
Russian marks the passive with -ся or word order, so the English two-word verb feels optional — students drop BE («it used by everyone») or drop the participle («is call»). And two verbs from the Corpus set (cost, delete) must STAY active.
Typical error to expect
«My best friend is call Sean» — the passive without its past participle (is callED) is one of the most-flagged lines in Russian candidates' scripts; the Corpus task of this very unit hunts it.
«This computer it's used by everyone» — the doubled subject (noun + it) migrates straight from Russian word order; the book's Corpus set catches it too.
Why the lesson is built this way
Guided discovery from yesterday's app texts: the four example sentences were all met in context, so the rule is extracted, not announced. The TB's participle noughts-and-crosses warms up the raw material first. Controlled practice runs on Instagram facts (a text teenagers actually care about), the big-numbers quiz turns passive questions into a guessing game with real answers on p.124, and the Corpus clinic fixes real learner errors. The productive stage has teams write their own passive quiz questions and fire them at each other — production disguised as a quiz show. Truth-or-lie closes the loop with personal passive facts.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Who owns Instagram? Where was your phone made? You don't know WHO did it — and English has a tense exactly for that. Today: two words instead of one, and your own quiz show at the end.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, four-error clinic in pairs, and the bridge into the passive

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — delayed feedback. Four anonymous sentences from lesson 1's error log on the board (dropped collocations, third-person slips). Kagan RallyRobin: pairs fix all four, fastest pair performs the corrections.
  3. Praise precision: You caught every one. Today we add a tense that Russian doesn't have — and by the end you'll own it.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): name the destination: Instagram is owned by Facebook. Your phone was made in China. Who did it? Doesn't matter. THAT's today's grammar.
📺 On screen: four sentences in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: fix yesterday's errors without knowing whose they were. Expect faster correction than acknowledgement — that's fine, the point is the fix

WHAT WE DO: the TB warmer: a 3×3 grid of infinitives, participles win cells, spelling compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Grid on the board: make · own · share · call · give · use · download · add · know. Two teams.
  2. To claim a cell: say the past participle AND spell it. made — M-A-D-E. Yours. Three in a row wins; play twice if the first game is quick.
  3. Leave the grid in the corner — it is the lesson's participle dictionary.
📺 On screen: the grid in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: recall, spell, compete. Expect known → knew confusion — the grid corner holds known for the rest of the lesson

Why: Guided discovery, not presentation: the rules are extracted from sentences the class met in the apps text. Rules they extract are rules they keep.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: extract the rules from yesterday's app sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four examples read aloud — all met yesterday: The app is given awesome reviews. New questions are added every month. The clips aren't downloaded. What is the app called? Pairs complete the rules. Key: b be + past participle · c by — and the four examples all come from yesterday's app texts.
  2. Actives for contrast, said not written: People don't download the clips → the clips aren't downloaded. Same fact. Different hero.
  3. CCQ round (see the list): who does it? do we know? do we care?
  4. Grammar reference p.152 open for one minute — students tick the table they have just built themselves.
📺 On screen: SB p.88 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: extract, contrast, tick the reference. Expect «why not just say people share photos?» — answer with the hero metaphor: the passive puts the interesting thing first

26–36′
Grammar · Instagram facts
solo → pairs
Why: Controlled practice on a text students actually care about — six passives carry six real facts, and every answer is justified out loud: who does the action? do we know? do we care?

WHAT WE DO: ex.2: six passives carry six real facts; justify every choice

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Solo first, 4 minutes: the Instagram text, six gaps, verbs in brackets. Then pairs compare. Key: 1 is owned · 2 is used · 3 are shared · 4 is added · 5 are advertised · 6 are given.
  2. Check with justification: is owned — WHO owns it? Facebook. Known and interesting → by Facebook stays. are shared — by whom? Everyone. So no by.
  3. The two-hands gesture on every answer: BE + participle, clap.
  4. Fact harvest: Which fact surprised you? Full sentence, passive included.
📺 On screen: the text is on SB p.88 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: fill, compare, justify with the deciding question. Expect is added (one 'tag' is added…) to wobble — singular subject, plural feel; catch it with What is added?

Move break

Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds of active/passive statues

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up. I say a sentence — if it's passive, freeze like a statue; if it's active, keep moving. My phone was made in China! I love my phone! Photos are shared every second! I share everything!
  2. Speed up, then: Sit down. Quiz time — big numbers, and nobody knows who did any of it.
📺 On screen: sentences by voice, nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: freeze on passives, move on actives — the form is now a reflex, not a rule

Why: The book's own question stage run as a guessing game: passive questions, real answers on p.124 — form practice that feels like a quiz show, not a worksheet.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: passive questions, guessed answers, real facts from p.124

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pairs build the five questions of ex.3 (passive forms first!) and WRITE their guesses. Key forms + facts: 1 is downloaded (anti-virus software) · 2 is used (123456!) · 3 are searched (2017: Meghan Markle, Gal Gadot) · 4 is spent (over $200 billion) · 5 are, made (China, South Korea, India) — the facts wait on p.124.
  2. Run it as a quiz show: question read aloud, guesses collected, p.124 opened for the truth. Points for the closest guess — but ONLY if the question was formed correctly.
  3. Note on №5, said once: Which countries are most smartphones made in? — the question word is the place, the subject is the phones.
📺 On screen: answers live on SB p.124 — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: form, guess, check, score. Expect delight at 123456 being the most-used password — milk it: Is YOUR password on this list? Don't answer.

Corpus clinic
pairs
Why: Real learner errors — the exact ones Russian students make — get fixed by the class, not by the teacher. Finding someone else's mistake is the fastest way to stop making it yourself.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: six real learner errors, two of them traps that must stay active

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame it honestly: Six sentences by real students. Every one has a problem — and two of them are NOT passive problems. Hunt.
  2. Pairs fix all six, Kagan RallyCoach, one pen. Key: 1 is called · 2 This computer IS used (drop it) · 3 are needed · 4 is called FIFA and costs £50 (cost stays active!) · 5 is recommended · 6 the virus DELETED the files — active, no change to the verb form itself.
  3. Whole-class check with the deciding question on the traps: The game costs £50 — who does the costing? The game itself. Active stays. The virus deleted files — the virus DID it. Active stays.
  4. The two trap verbs go up on the wall in red: cost · delete (as doer).
📺 On screen: SB p.88 ex.4 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hunt, fix, defend. Expect at least one pair to passivise the virus sentence — that error is the whole point of the stage; celebrate the pair that resists

🎯 Speaking out · fact factory
teams of 3–4
Why: The productive outcome: teams write their own passive quiz questions about the digital world and fire them at each other. Nobody can build a question without building the form.

WHAT WE DO: teams write passive quiz questions about the digital world and fire them at each other

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Teams of three or four, 6 minutes: write FOUR passive quiz questions — real or invented-but-plausible. Skeletons on the board: Where is/are … made? · What is … called? · How many … are … every day? · Is … owned by …?
  2. Kagan Numbered Heads Together inside teams: everyone must be able to read any question — you call the number.
  3. Inter-team battle: teams fire questions, answering teams get a point for a full-sentence answer IN THE PASSIVE, asking teams lose a point for a broken form. You referee forms only.
  4. You log form slips silently — they feed lesson 3's opening.
📺 On screen: question skeletons in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write, check each other, battle. Expect invented facts (Is TikTok owned by our school?) — allow them, the form is the point, the laugh is free fuel

74–86′
Truth or lie round
pairs → class
Why: Three passive facts, one of them false — nobody can play without producing the target form, and nobody listens harder than a lie-detector.

WHAT WE DO: three personal passive facts, one false — detectors listen for form AND sense

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Solo, 3 minutes: everyone writes three sentences about their own things in the passive — My trainers were made in Vietnam. My name is spelled with two Ns. Our flat is cleaned by my brother. One is a lie.
  2. Kagan Timed Pair Share: read to your partner, partner interrogates (By whom? How often?) and calls the lie.
  3. Two or three best lies performed for the class; the class votes.
  4. Micro-summary: You just used the passive about your OWN life. That's the tense, owned.
📺 On screen: sentences live in notebooks — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write, perform, interrogate, vote. Expect the brother-cleans-the-flat class of lies — the interrogation questions are where the real fluency happens

  • Main aim. By the end, students can form the present simple passive (affirmative, negative, questions), decide when by is needed, and use it in their own quiz questions about the digital world.
  • Subsidiary. Past participles reactivated as a game; reading a short fact text for detail; justifying active-vs-passive choices out loud.

  • build is/are + past participle without dropping either half
  • choose active or passive and say why

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Instagram is owned by Facebook» — who does the owning? → Facebook. Why passive then? → because the sentence is ABOUT Instagram.
«95 million photos are shared every day» — do we know who shares them? → everyone and no one — that's exactly why there's no by.
«My phone cost £200» — can I say is costed? → no: cost stays active, always.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
BE evaporates: «the app used by millions»Two-hands gesture, every time: left hand = BE, right hand = participle, clap them together. One missing hand = no passive. The gesture is faster than any explanation.
Participle reverts to base form: «is call», «is recommend»That is the warmer's job — nine participles with spelling checked BEFORE the grammar starts. When it happens later, point at the grid corner where called still stands.
Everything becomes passive, including cost and deleteThe Corpus clinic plants both traps on purpose. The deciding question on the wall: WHO does it? If the subject does it — active stays active.
The by-agent appears everywhereOne-line rule after ex.2: by only when the doer is known AND interesting. «Photos are shared by people» — by whom else? Delete it.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
