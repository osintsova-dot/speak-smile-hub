Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to follow, read and act out the story «The Burfi cake disaster» and talk about steps in a recipe: “What do we need to do now? — Put the butter in the pan and mix it.”
  • Subsidiary. Value — paying attention (Ravi's mistake with the salt); break Luke's code (→ NEW YORK CITY — a bridge to the Culture lesson); recycle quantifiers & How much/How many from L2; story skills — predict → listen → read along → Read & answer → act out; prepare the cooking verbs for L4.
  • Personal. Let the story breathe — big voices, pauses, prediction; the “need to do” idea is NOTICED today, drilled properly in L4 — don't over-correct. Give every child a story role.
By the end, pupils can…
  • retell 2–3 things that happen in the story;
  • read their role's lines from the book;
  • answer comprehension questions: “Josh chops the nuts.”

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Ravi gives Josh the SALT not the sugar — is the cake sweet or salty?” → salty — a disaster!
“Why does it go wrong? Was Ravi paying attention?” → no — he was playing a game.

Assumptions: Quantifiers & How much/How many are active from L2; the alphabet-number code was learnt in the Starter (recycled in act 5). First full-unit story of the year — the act-out routine was modelled in the Starter. Readers follow the text with a finger.

28–40′
Act out
carpet, groups of 4

Model a frame. You + 3 pupils play the salty-cake-tasting frame with big drama: Mmm! Let's try a piece. — Ugh! It's salty!

Groups of 4 (Luke · Lily · Josh · Ravi). Rehearse 2 frames with books as scripts. Rotate roles once.

Showtime. Each group performs ONE frame (30–40 sec — fast carousel, no long waits). Audience task: shout what goes wrong (“Salt, not sugar!”) at the end.

💪 Support: shortest lines / the “Ugh!” reaction for the shyest child. ⭐ Extension: add an extra line from the frame.
