Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name 12 health problems and ask & answer about not feeling well: “Have you got a (headache)? — No, I haven't. / Do you feel (dizzy)? — Yes, I do.”
  • Subsidiary. Sing «I'm not very well» (CD1 48) & the vocabulary chant (CD1 47); brainstorm ways to answer “How are you?” (Learning to learn); notice the two patterns — Have you got…? for a thing you HAVE, Do you feel…? for how you FEEL (fully drilled in L2); early literacy — read the health words and solve the AB puzzle.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; stirrer→settler rhythm — the mime game is high-energy, plan the freezes. Insist on the article: a headache, a cold, a cough (but flu / stomach ache without a when they say “I've got…”).
By the end, pupils can…
  • name the 12 health problems from the pictures;
  • ask & answer “Have you got…? / Do you feel…?”;
  • read the health words and match word ↔ picture.

Structure
Have you got a (headache)? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.  ·  Do you feel (dizzy)? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't.  ·  I've got (a cough) and (stomach ache), too.
Form
Two patterns: Have you got + a + problem? (a thing)  ·  Do you feel + adjective? (dizzy / sick). Watch a before headache/cold/cough; NO a before flu / stomach ache / earache when saying “I've got…”.
Pron. watch
stomach /ˈstʌmək/ (ch = /k/!); headache /ˈhedeɪk/ (ache = /eɪk/); cough /kɒf/ (gh = /f/); sore throat /θ/ — tongue between teeth; dizzy /ˈdɪzi/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I've got a headache — is it a problem I HAVE, so Have you got…? or how I FEEL, so Do you feel…?” → Have you got.
“I feel dizzy — Have you got dizzy, or Do you feel dizzy?” → Do you feel dizzy!

Assumptions: Unit 2 routines (chores + adverbs of frequency) are active; pupils know How are you? — I'm fine and have got from GMF2. The 12 health problems are largely NEW; the Have you got…? / Do you feel…? split is met receptively today, drilled in L2. Readers — word cards work alongside pictures.

10–20′
Presentation · 12 health problems
carpet → board

Present with flashcards + chant (act. 2, CD1 47). Each card: What's the matter? He's got… a headache! Choral ×3 + one gesture (headache = hold head, cough = cover mouth, broken leg = limp, feel dizzy = wobble).

Sort HAVE vs FEEL. Two board columns: Have you got…? (headache · cold · cough · sore throat · temperature · stomach ache · earache · cut · broken leg · flu)  ·  Do you feel…? (dizzy · sick). Hold each card — class points to the right column BEFORE you place it.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: Stand up if you've got THIS today… a headache! — flash cards fast, everyone moves every time.

💪 Support: answer with the gesture + word. ⭐ Extension: full sentence — I've got a bad headache today.

28–36′
Word ↔ picture · Memory
carpet, pairs

▶ Memory · What's the matter? — in pairs. Each pair: 6 picture cards + 6 WORD cards (readers!) face-down in two rows. Turn one of each; a match is claimed only with a sentence: He's got a cough! or She feels sick! Wrong pattern → cards go back. Most pairs wins.

ICQ: For “dizzy” — do you say He's GOT dizzy or He FEELS dizzy? (→ He feels dizzy!)

✔ Assess: stand behind one pair at a time — is the HAVE/FEEL split automatic yet?

 6+6 cards per pair
36–44′
Mime & guess
carpet, 2 teams

▶ Slow-motion doctor mime. One player mimes a health problem; their team guesses together with the QUESTION: Have you got a headache? Do you feel sick? Mimer answers Yes, I have! / No, I haven't. Point per correct guess; rotate the mimer every round — everyone mimes once.

Whole team asks together — no solo shouting: One voice, one team!

44–52′
At the doctor's · speak & write
desks

Speak. ▶ RallyRobin: Take turns: What's the matter? — I've got… / I feel… — about a card you pick.

Write (notebooks / AB p.26 format). Board frame: I've got a ___. I feel ___. Each child writes 3 “not well” sentences, copying the health words. Fast finishers add and: I've got a cough and a sore throat.

💪 Support: copy the frame + choose from 3 word cards. ⭐ Extension: add advice: I must go to bed.
