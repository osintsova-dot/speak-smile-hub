🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Choose correctly between present simple, present continuous and will for present habits, and between present perfect simple and present perfect continuous — result versus process, finished versus still going on; then use do and make collocations in the field of study.
What is genuinely hard
Russian has one present tense, so all three English options feel like decoration. The genuinely difficult point is not the form but the meaning of the continuous perfect: Russian says «я учу английский пять лет» in the present, and the student writes «I study English for five years» for years unless the structure is drilled as a reflex. Add the stative verbs, which look like ordinary verbs and refuse the -ing.
Typical error to expect
«I am studying English for five years» — Russian uses the present for an action that started in the past and is still going on; English uses the present perfect continuous: I have been studying English for five years. This is the single most common B2 error in Russian written work.
«I am knowing», «I am thinking that it is good» — stative verbs (know, believe, understand, like, sound, belong) do not take the continuous when they mean a state. The Student's Book plants the trap in the pyjama text.
«since three years», «for last summer» — for takes a period (for three years), since takes a point (since last summer). One preposition, one mark.
«make homework», «make a mistake in the exam» for «do badly» — Russian has one verb (делать) where English has two, and the split is not logical: you DO a course, an assignment, an exam, your best; you MAKE a decision, a mistake, progress, an effort. It is learned as pairs, not as a rule.
Why the lesson is built this way
The grammar is not explained, it is discovered: six sentences from the unit's own topic, and the students name the tense and match it to a use before any rule is spoken. The controlled practice is a text about a girl who goes to university in animal pyjamas, which is worth reading, and it hides four stative-verb traps. The present perfect half is built on a contrast pair — I've been doing homework all evening / I've won two certificates — because the distinction is a meaning, not a form. The do-and-make block closes the lesson because it is a memory job and works best when the brain is warm and standing up.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“There is a society at a British university whose members go to lectures in animal pyjamas. Today you will read about one of them. And you will learn the one sentence Russian speakers get wrong more often than any other in English: how long you have been doing something.”

WHAT WE DO: SB p.8 ex.4a–4c: the meaning first, the form second

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the contrast pair on the board and say nothing for ten seconds: I've been doing homework all evening, but I've still got more to do. / I've won two certificates.
  2. Ask two questions only: Which sentence tells me HOW LONG? Which tells me HOW MANY? → fill the two columns: PROCESS · RESULT.
  3. Ex.4a alone: match the four sentences to the four uses. Key: 1 a · 2 b · 3 d · 4 c.
  4. Ex.4b — form, on the board with gaps: present perfect continuous = have/has + ____ + ____ · present perfect simple = have/has + ____. Elicit, do not tell.
  5. Ex.4c: which tense gives more importance to completion, to process and duration, to quantity, to something temporary. Key: 1 simple · 2 continuous · 3 simple · 4 continuous.
  6. Drill the weak form five times, chorally: I've been /aɪvbɪn/ studying. Not «I have been».
📺 On screen: the two-column board (RESULT | PROCESS) filling up as they answer. Backup: none needed — this stage is deliberately board-only.
👦 Students do: answer two questions about the contrast pair, then do 4a–4c and say the two forms aloud

Adverbs and error clinic
desks
Why: Showdown is the fix for «two students answer, ten watch»: everyone commits to an answer before anyone hears the correct one.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.8 ex.5–6: for, since, ever, never, just, already, yet — and twelve broken sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.5 in pairs, three minutes: which of the seven words go with both perfects? Then confirm on the board — all of them go with both EXCEPT ever, never, just, already and yet, which belong to the simple.
  2. Nail the two that cost marks: for + a period (for three hours). since + a point (since Monday, since I was born).
  3. Ex.6 alone, six minutes: twelve sentences, correct or improve. Warn them, as the Teacher's Book does: Sometimes the mistake is the adverb, sometimes the tense, sometimes the word order.
  4. Showdown in fours: the leader reads item 1, everyone shows their corrected version at the same moment, the team agrees. Then item 2, and so on.
  5. Take only the three items the room disagreed on.
📺 On screen: for + PERIOD / since + POINT written large. Backup: not needed.
👦 Students do: sort seven adverbs in pairs, correct twelve sentences alone, then show answers simultaneously in fours

Structure
I train three times a week. · We're organising the next event at this very minute. · My friends are constantly doing essays. · I've won two prizes. · I've been participating in conferences for years. · How long have you been learning English?
Form
present simple = routine · present continuous = now / around now · will + always/constantly = a repeated habit, often someone else's and often annoying · present perfect simple = have + past participle, the result · present perfect continuous = have been + -ing, the process and its duration · for + a period, since + a point.
Pron. watch
the ending -ture is /tʃə(r)/, never /tuːr/: lecture, culture, future · word stress moves and changes the word: undergraduate vs undergraduate is wrong — undergraduate · qualifications · education · weak forms in the perfect: I've been /aɪvbɪn/, not /aɪ hæv biːn/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I've been writing essays all evening.» Are the essays finished? → we don't know, and it doesn't matter — the point is the process.
«I've written three essays.» Do we care how long it took? → no, we care about the result: three.
«He's always losing his keys.» Once, or many times? → many times, and I'm not happy about it.
«I'm knowing the answer» — possible? → no. Know is a state, and states do not take -ing.
ICQ before ex.2: do you write the tense or the verb? → the verb, in the right form.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«I study English for five years» keeps coming backDo not correct it as a mistake — reformulate it as a question every single time: How long HAVE you BEEN studying? and wait for the repeat. It is a reflex, and reflexes are built by repetition, not by explanation.
The class cannot see why the two perfects are differentTwo columns on the board: RESULT (how many, what is finished) and PROCESS (how long, still going on). Every disputed sentence gets put in a column by the student who wrote it.
Stative verbs get an -ing in the pyjama textBefore ex.2, elicit the list and write it on the board: have (possess), know, think (believe), believe, sound, like, mean. Then the trap is a game rather than a failure.
do / make is treated as guessableIt is not guessable and you should say so: it is a memory list. The Last-person-standing game at the start of the block is the learning, not the fun.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text) — the present perfect simple against the present perfect continuous is one of the standing lines of the codifier.

The Russian paper never asks «what tense is this?». It gives a gap in a narrative and expects the candidate to feel the difference between a result (I have written three essays) and a process (I have been writing essays all evening). That decision is exactly today's grammar. The do / make half is quieter but just as expensive: a collocation error costs a mark in the lexical section and in the writing criteria.
