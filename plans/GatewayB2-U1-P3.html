🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Listen to a two-speaker interview and say who said what (Poppy, Harry or both), and choose the gerund or the infinitive after nine groups of verbs — suggest doing, manage to do, avoid doing, the first to do, good at doing.
What is genuinely hard
The listening is hard for one structural reason: the two speakers agree about half the time, so a student who listens for content instead of for the speaker gets «both» wrong in either direction. The grammar is hard for a different reason — there is nothing to understand. The pattern belongs to the verb and Russian gives no clue whatever, so it is memory work dressed as grammar, and the lesson has to be honest about that.
Typical error to expect
«I suggest to go», «I managed finishing», «I avoid to eat» — the pattern belongs to the verb, not to the meaning, and Russian gives no clue at all: suggest + -ing, manage + to, avoid + -ing, decide + to.
Also watch the two prepositions: «interested in to study» and «good at to speak» — after a preposition the verb is always -ing, with no exceptions at all.
Why the lesson is built this way
The listening comes first and the grammar comes out of it: the nine rules in Grammar in context 2 are matched to sentences the students have just heard Poppy and Harry say, which is why the exercise is set the way it is on the page. Between them sits a Critical thinkers stage — is a year abroad worth it? — where the two students argue with real content from the recording instead of inventing opinions. The advice text at the end («New kid at school? Advice from past students») puts both halves together: it is about the listening topic and it is fourteen gerund-or-infinitive decisions.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Poppy spent a year in a French school. Harry spent a year in Germany. One of them says it was hard to make friends; the other says it was the best year of their life. Your job: who said what — and then the grammar those two people used without thinking about it.”

Why: Every student says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls the previous lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, then yesterday's present perfect continuous used once each before the new material starts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — what is the best thing that has happened this week? Any content counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — grammar recall, not vocabulary. On the board: How long ___ you ___ (study) at this school? Everyone writes the full question. Two students read theirs aloud.
  3. 1 minute — answer it. RallyRobin: partners take turns, one sentence each, until you stop them. Every answer must contain «have been».
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two people in today's recording spent a whole year at school in another country. Not a holiday — a school year, with exams, in a language they did not speak.
📺 On screen: one gapped question on the board. Backup: dictate it if the board is busy.
👦 Students do: write the question, then answer it in turns with a partner

WHAT WE DO: SB p.12 ex.2 (track 03): which of OUR ideas do they mention?

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. Play track 03 straight through. No pausing.
  3. Pairs compare ticks for one minute. Then take three ideas from the floor.
  4. Say the thing that matters: They will not use your words. It is the ideas that count, not the words.

24–36′
Listening for detail · Poppy, Harry or both
desks
Why: Numbered Heads makes the whole team responsible for all nine answers, which is the opposite of the usual «the strongest one answers» pattern.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.12 ex.3: nine statements, second play, then the proof

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the nine statements aloud first, so unknown words are not the obstacle. Then: In pairs, before you hear it again — who do you THINK said each one? Pencil.
  2. Warn them: Only four of the nine are «both». If your paper says «both» six times, you are guessing.
  3. Play track 03 again. Students confirm or change.
  4. Numbered Heads Together in fours: the team agrees on all nine, then you call a number and that student answers for the team. Key: 1 Both · 2 Harry · 3 Poppy · 4 Both · 5 Poppy · 6 Poppy · 7 Poppy · 8 Poppy · 9 Harry.
  5. Play the two disputed items a third time, ten seconds each, and let them hear themselves being right.
📺 On screen: track 03, second play. Backup: the same script read aloud; for the third listening of a disputed item, simply reread the two lines.
👦 Students do: predict in pencil, listen, agree in a team of four, then answer for the team when your number is called

36–39′
Move break · gerund or infinitive, on your feet
open floor
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Sixteen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: three minutes of physical sorting that also pre-teaches the grammar

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Left wall = -ING. Right wall = TO. Read a verb, students walk.
  2. Ten verbs, fast: suggest (-ing) · manage (to) · avoid (-ing) · decide (to) · risk (-ing) · promise (to) · consider (-ing) · expect (to) · recommend (-ing) · hope (to).
  3. No explanations while they walk. The wrong wall is information, not a failure.
📺 On screen: two wall labels. Backup: left hand / right hand raised instead of walking.
👦 Students do: walk to a wall for each of ten verbs

39–47′
🎯 Is a year abroad actually worth it
desks
Why: The listening content is reused as argument material, so the recording stays useful for eight minutes longer, and the concession structure is the B2 upgrade of a B1 opinion.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.12 ex.4 (Critical thinkers): two sides and a conclusion

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three headings on the board, and insist on notes before speaking: ADVANTAGES · DISADVANTAGES · MY CONCLUSION. Two minutes of silence.
  2. Tell them where the content comes from: Use what Poppy and Harry actually said, not only your own ideas.
  3. Think-Pair-Share, then four students give a conclusion in one sentence each — and every conclusion has to start with a concession: Although … , I think …
  4. This is B2 speaking: not two lists, but a position that admits the other side.
📺 On screen: the three headings on the board. Backup: dictate them.
👦 Students do: make notes in three columns, talk to a partner, then state a conclusion that begins with «although»
board · SB p.12
47–60′
Gerunds and infinitives · nine rules from what they heard
desks
Why: The grammar is derived from language the students have already processed for meaning, which is what makes an arbitrary pattern stick.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.12 ex.1 (Grammar in context 2): match the nine rules to sentences from exercise 3

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Do item a together, aloud, from the page: «Studying abroad is expensive» — the gerund as the subject.
  2. Pairs, six minutes, for b–i. Weak students are supported by the pair, as the Teacher's Book suggests. Key: 2 g · 3 d · 4 f · 5 c · 6 b · 7 h · 8 e · 9 i.
  3. Check by RULE, not by number: a student reads the rule, then the sentence from the listening that proves it.
  4. Then the two rules that carry the marks, boarded and drilled: after a preposition — always -ing and after the first / the last / the only — always to. Three chorus repetitions of a model sentence for each.
  5. Fast finishers: write two sentences that break a rule, for the class to correct later.
📺 On screen: the nine rules projected if you have the board app, so hands stay free. Backup: the rules are printed on p.12 — work from the page and use the board only for the two key rules.
👦 Students do: match nine rules to nine heard sentences in pairs, then say the rule aloud with its proof sentence

60–70′
Error correction · ten sentences, six of them broken
desks
Why: Simultaneous showing means nobody copies the first speaker; the error log turns a corrected exercise into a personal record that is checked in later lessons.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.12 ex.2: rewrite what is wrong, and say which rule was broken

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it precisely: Four of the ten are already correct. For every sentence you change, write the LETTER of the rule you used.
  2. Five minutes alone. Then Showdown in fours: everyone shows their version of item 1 at the same moment, then item 2, and so on.
  3. Whole-class confirmation, key items only: 2 seems to find · 3 avoid doing · 5 the first British student to study · 6 expect to pass · 7 suggested going · 8 risk feeling · 9 considered living · 10 promised to help.
  4. Every student now writes ONE line in the error log at the back of the notebook: their own mistake, the correction, the rule.
📺 On screen: the numbers 1–10 on the board with a tick or a cross added as the class agrees. Backup: not needed.
👦 Students do: correct ten sentences alone, show answers simultaneously in fours, then write one line in the personal error log

The advice text · both halves in one place
desks
Why: One text that is simultaneously the topic of the listening and fourteen grammar decisions — the most economical practice on the spread.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.13 ex.4a–4b: fourteen decisions inside advice about starting a new school

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Brainstorm for thirty seconds: What is hard about starting at a new school? Board three ideas.
  2. Ex.4a alone, six minutes: complete the five pieces of advice with the gerund or the infinitive. Key: 1 making, to remember · 2 getting, to think · 3 to pay, making · 4 joining, To meet, doing, Making · 5 speaking, to talk, sharing.
  3. RallyCoach: A explains items 1–3 to B, B explains 4–5 to A. The explanation is the point, not the answer.
  4. Ex.4b in pairs: which of the five pieces of advice is actually useful, and what would you add for a student arriving in YOUR school? Two ideas taken from the floor.
📺 On screen: nothing new on the screen — the text is on p.13. Backup: not applicable.
👦 Students do: complete fourteen gaps alone, then teach half of them to a partner and add advice of their own

Structure
I suggest reading a bit about the country beforehand. · I managed to learn Portuguese in six months. · I avoid doing the housework. · I was the first British student to study in the school. · You risk feeling lonely if you can't speak the language.
Form
-ing after: prepositions · verbs of liking and disliking · admit, avoid, consider, involve, recommend, risk, suggest · as subject or object · with go + physical activity. to + infinitive after: adjectives · too, enough, the first, the last · agree, arrange, decide, expect, hope, learn, manage, need, promise, seem, want · to explain why.
Pron. watch
the -ing ending is /ɪŋ/, not /ɪn/ and not /ɪŋk/ · unstressed to in «managed to learn» is /tə/, never /tuː/ · word stress: experience · opportunity · independent
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I suggest to go» — correct? → no: suggest + -ing. Say it three times: I suggest going, I suggest going, I suggest going.
«I'm interested in …» — what form comes next? → -ing, because in is a preposition. Always.
«He was the first person to arrive.» Why to? → after the first, the last, the only — always the infinitive.
ICQ before the listening: how many times will you hear it? → twice. What do you do the first time? → get the general idea and nothing else.

The class have met gerunds and infinitives at B1 as isolated pairs (like doing / want to do); what is new is the systematic nine-rule set and the preposition rule. Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Everything is answered «Both» in the listeningBefore the second play, say: Only four of the nine are «both». If you have six, you are guessing. The number itself does the correcting.
The audio will not playRead the script from the Teacher's Book p.123 yourself, in two voices, at natural speed — sit on a desk at the front and change your position for each speaker so the class can see who is talking.
The nine rules are copied and not usedThe rules are matched to sentences from the listening they have just heard, and the check is oral: the student says the rule, then says a NEW sentence that follows it.
Fast finishers in the grammar blockGive the Teacher's Book extra: five sentences to complete (promised to speak · Reading books helped · considered moving · the last to finish · interested in taking up). Then they write two of their own to trap the class with.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 3–9 (listening: a long interview, seven three-option questions) and tasks 19–25.

Poppy and Harry are a radio interview with two speakers — precisely the shape of the second half of the Russian listening paper, where the candidate has to say who said what and hears it twice. The gerund and infinitive patterns are the other half of the mark: verbs like suggest, avoid, manage, decide and expect appear in the grammar section and every one of them takes a fixed form.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
