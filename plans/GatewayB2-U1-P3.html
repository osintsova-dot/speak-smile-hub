🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Choose correctly between present simple, present continuous and the two «present habit» forms (will + adverb of frequency; always/constantly + -ing), and between present perfect simple and present perfect continuous — result against process — to talk about their own studies; and use the do / make collocations of school and university (do an assignment, do a course, make a decision, make progress, make an effort) as whole chunks.
What is genuinely hard
Russian has one word — «сейчас», one perfective past — and no continuous aspect at all. So «I live here for three years» comes out for I've been living here for three years; «I have lost my phone yesterday» mixes the perfect with a finished-time word; and do / make is a lottery, because Russian «делать» covers both. None of this is guessable — it has to be met, sorted and re-used.
Typical error to expect
«I am knowing the answer» (a stative verb in the continuous), «I live here since 2020» (present simple where the perfect is needed), «I have finished it yesterday» (perfect + finished time), «make homework / make a course», «do a decision». Aspect and collocation, carried straight across from Russian.
Why the lesson is built this way
Grammar is discovered, not lectured: the students name the tense and match the rule themselves (guided discovery, I-do / we-do / you-do), because a rule they reconstruct is a rule they keep. Each grammar point is closed with a hinge question on mini-whiteboards — a diagnostic checkpoint that decides whether the class moves on or the point is re-taught, before ten minutes of practice are wasted on a class that has not understood. Every stage ends in the students' own sentences about their own studies, because the present and the present perfect are exactly the tenses they need to describe the life they are living right now. do / make comes last, as the vocabulary the grammar sentences were already using.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“In Russian there is one word for «now». In English there are three, and choosing the wrong one is the fastest way to sound like a textbook. Today: the three presents and the perfect that never quite lets go of the past — and by the end you will describe your own school year with all of them, without a single Russian «сейчас».”

Why: One English sentence from every student at the door, then a retrieval task that turns last lesson's reading straight into today's grammar — the new point lands on live material, not cold.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, one sentence each, and yesterday's clubs pulled back as three tenses on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

8–20′
Three ways of saying «now» · discovery
desks
Why: Present simple and continuous are B1 revision, so they are discovered fast, in pairs, not taught. The genuinely new members here are the two present-habit forms — will for your own routine, always + -ing for someone else's irritating one.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.8 ex.1a–1b: name the tense in six sentences, then match the rule to each

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books open, p.8. I do (item 1). Read aloud: «I train three times a week.» What tense? Present simple. Why? Three times a week — a routine. Model the thinking, not just the answer.
  2. We do (items 2–3), whole class. Take the tense AND the reason for each. Ex.1a key: 1 present simple (routine) · 2 present continuous (happening now) · 3 present simple (a state — takes your mind off) · 4 present continuous + constantly (an annoying habit) · 5 will (a present habit) · 6 present simple (a state — sounds).
  3. You do — ex.1b, in pairs. Match the six rules (a–f) to the six sentences. Key: a1 · b3 · c6 · d2 · e4 · f5. RallyCoach: A justifies a match, B agrees or challenges, then swap.
  4. Fill the DECISION board together from their answers: routine / general truth / state → simple · happening now → continuous · MY habit → will + usually · SOMEONE's annoying habit → always/constantly + -ing.
  5. Pin down the two traps out loud: State verbs — know, believe, sound, think — do NOT take the continuous. And «always + -ing» is not «now» — it means it happens too often and it annoys me.
📺 On screen: the Flipped classroom «Grammar Presentation» video is the pre-teach — if it was NOT set for homework, play it here (3 min) before the exercises. Backup: the whole discovery runs from the page — the video is support, not a requirement.
👦 Students do: name six tenses with reasons, then match and coach a partner through the six rules

20–30′
Present forms · in a text and in your life
desks → pairs
Why: One controlled task to check the discovery held (the gapped text), then straight into their own sentences — because a rule only becomes usable when the student has produced it about something true.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.8 ex.2 gapped text, then ex.3a–3b: your habits with will, their annoying habits with always + -ing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.2 — «Wearing pyjamas in class», individually, 4 minutes. Present simple or continuous. Watch the state verbs. Then pairs check. Key: a have · b get / will get · c 's always wearing · d 's starting · e sounds · f Do … know · g describes · h believe · i think · j 's meeting · k hope · l isn't wearing.
  2. Flag item c aloud: «She's always wearing her pyjamas» — is that happening right now? No. It happens too often and it drives the family mad. That is the annoyance form.
  3. Ex.3a — produce. Everyone writes three sentences with will + an adverb of frequency about their OWN study habits (I'll usually revise the night before.) and three with present continuous + always / constantly / forever about someone ELSE's annoying habit (My brother is always borrowing my charger.).
  4. Ex.3b — compare. RallyRobin: partners read one sentence each in turn and find one habit they share. Take three of the funniest annoying-habit sentences from the floor.
📺 On screen: the gapped text projected while they check, so eyes stay on the page. Backup: the text is in every book — run the check from p.8.
👦 Students do: gap-fill alone, check in pairs, then write six true sentences and compare

30–44′
🎯 Present perfect · simple vs continuous
desks
Why: This is the new grammar of the lesson and the one Russian gives no help with. It is discovered from four model sentences, closed with a hinge question that diagnoses the whole class in ninety seconds, and only then does the signal-word work begin.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.8 ex.4a–4c + p.9 ex.5: result against process, and the signal words — with a hinge check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4a — match meaning. Four sentences (has existed · have done · 've just arrived · 've won) to four uses (a–d). Pairs first, then check. Key: 1 a · 2 b · 3 d · 4 c.
  2. Ex.4b — the form, from two sentences: I've been participating… vs I've joined…. Elicit onto the DECISION board: continuous = have/has + been + -ing · simple = have/has + past participle.
  3. Ex.4c — the meaning split, the heart of it. Which tense stresses: 1 completion & result → simple · 2 process & duration → continuous · 3 quantity (how many times / how many) → simple · 4 temporary, incomplete, just-finished → continuous. Board it as one line: DONE & how many → simple. STILL / how long → continuous.
  4. Ex.5 (p.9) — the signal words. Sort for · since · ever · never · just · already · yet: for + a length of time, since + a point in time, ever/never/just/already/yet only with the perfect. Give the CCQs below as you go.
  5. Fast finishers: which of the seven words can go with either tense? (for / since) Which only with the simple? (ever, never, just, already, yet)
✋ Hinge question (mini-whiteboards, 90 sec — before any practice): Choose A or B. «I ___ here for three years.» A: have lived · B: have been living. Both are possible — but which one puts the accent on the DURATION? → B (process/duration). Diagnostic: a class that all writes A has heard «for + time» and defaulted to the simple — re-teach ex.4c with the DONE/STILL line before moving on. Mostly B → go straight to practice. Follow up with the killer: «I ___ my phone.» (lose) — I don't have it now. → simple, «I've lost» (result in the present, one completed event — not continuous).
📺 On screen: the four model sentences (ex.4a) and the DONE/STILL line. Backup: write the four sentences on the board — the discovery does not need a screen.
👦 Students do: match meaning and form in pairs, then answer two hinge questions on whiteboards — everyone at once, nobody hides

47–57′
Present perfect · fix it & build it
desks → pairs
Why: Error correction is the most exam-honest practice there is — it isolates exactly the mistakes Russian speakers make. Then the students build the questions they will actually ask, so the form leaves the page as speech.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.9 ex.6 twelve sentences to correct, then ex.7 build seven real questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.6, individually, 5 minutes. Warn them: The mistake is sometimes the adverb, sometimes the tense, sometimes the word order. Key highlights: 1 I have never tried… (word order) · 4 I've been waiting… for half an hour · 5 We've already won… / won… already · 7 I've been at this school for two years (for, not since) · 9 …haven't finished yet · 12 I haven't finished my essay yet.
  2. Showdown to check: pairs write each corrected sentence, on Showdown! both flip whiteboards at once and compare. Every disagreement gets read out and settled against the DECISION card.
  3. Ex.7 — build seven questions in the present perfect simple or continuous (How long have you…? / Have you ever…? / How many times have you…?). They write; you circulate and fix aspect on the spot.
📺 On screen: the answer key line by line during the Showdown check. Backup: read the corrected sentences aloud; the class marks their own.
👦 Students do: correct twelve sentences alone, Showdown-check in pairs, then write seven of their own questions

57–64′
🎯 Interview · how long have you…?
open floor → desks
Why: The productive outcome of the grammar half. Reporting the partner makes listening compulsory, and the second telling is always tighter than the first.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.9 ex.8 (Use it … don't lose it): ask a partner your own questions, then report one thing to the class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it precisely: Interview your partner with YOUR seven questions. Full answers, not «yes» — a reason and an example. Model one exchange with a strong student first.
  2. Timed Pair Share: 60 seconds each way, listener silent. Then change partner and interview once more — faster, better.
  3. Four reports from the floor, and the rule is: Tell me about your PARTNER, not yourself. — Marina has been learning the piano for eight years and she has just passed grade five.
  4. Any error you hear in the reports goes on the board as a fix-it and the class corrects it — no names.
📺 On screen: a visible timer. Backup: phone timer held up, or count it on your fingers.
👦 Students do: interview two partners under a timer, then report a partner's answers in the third person

do & make · two lists and a text
desks → pairs
Why: do / make is not a rule you can reason to — it is memory. So it is met as two physical lists, sorted with cards, given a working rule of thumb, and then fixed by a text where every gap is a collocation.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.9 ex.1 sort the collocations, ex.2 the rule, ex.3 the «getting older» blog text

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 — sort into two lists. Run it as Quiz-Quiz-Trade with collocation cards: each student gets a card (homework · a decision · progress · an assignment · a favour · an effort · a course · a mistake…), mingles, quizzes a partner do or make?, then they trade cards and find someone new. After 3 minutes, board the two lists from their cards. Key (do): a course, a degree, a favour, an activity, an assignment, an essay, an exam, business, chores, homework, sport, the shopping, the washing, well, your best, your hair. (make): a cake, a choice, a decision, a mistake, a noise, a phone call, a plan, a promise, a suggestion, an appointment, an effort, an excuse, an offer, friends, money, progress, the dinner.
  2. Ex.2 — the rule of thumb. Elicit: do = work & activities in general (1 do school/uni work · 2 do work around the house · 4 do activities in general) · make = producing / creating something new (3 make things we produce or construct). Warn them it is only a guide — make progress, make an effort and do your hair just have to be learnt.
  3. Ex.3 — the text «Studying as you get older», individually then pair-check. Key: a do · b make · c do · d do · e do · f make · g make · h do · i do · j do · k make · l do · m make · n make.
📺 On screen: the two-column list (DO | MAKE) built live as cards are read out. Backup: write the two columns on the board — the cards do the work, not the screen.
👦 Students do: quiz each other on cards, build two lists, agree the rule, then gap-fill the text alone

Structure
I'll usually revise the night before. · My brother is always borrowing my charger. · The club has existed for ten years. · I've been studying English since I was seven. · Have you made any decisions about your future? · I've made a lot of progress this term.
Form
continuous perfect = have/has + been + -ing · simple perfect = have/has + past participle · for + a length of time · since + a point in time · ever/never/just/already/yet only with the perfect · state verbs (know, believe, sound, think, want) take no continuous.
Pron. watch
weak forms in the perfect: I've been /aɪv bɪn/, has /həz/, never /aɪ hæv biːn/ · contractions 've / 's / 'll swallowed, not stressed · linking the -ing: been_organising · sentence stress lands on the content word, not the auxiliary.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«She's always wearing pyjamas» — is she wearing them at this second? → not the point; it happens too often and it annoys me.
«I've been living here for three years» — do I still live here? → yes. Do we stress the length of time? → yes → continuous.
«I've lost my phone» — do I have it now? → no. One finished event, result now → simple, not continuous.
«I've finished it yesterday» — right or wrong? → wrong. Yesterday is finished time → past simple, not perfect.
ICQ before ex.6: is the mistake always the verb? → no — sometimes the adverb, sometimes the word order.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«For + time» triggers an automatic present perfect simpleRun the hinge question before any practice. If most write A, re-teach ex.4c with the DONE/STILL line — for tells you it is the perfect, but the -ing is what stresses duration.
Finished-time words with the perfect: «I've finished it yesterday»Put yesterday · last week · in 2020 on the board as a red-light list: these can never sit with the perfect. Drill the swap to past simple aloud.
State verbs pushed into the continuous: «I'm knowing / I'm believing»Keep a visible list — know, believe, sound, think, want, like. When one appears in the continuous, freeze and repeat the sentence in the simple; they echo.
do / make guessed from Russian «делать»Never accept a bare word — accept the whole collocation. The card and the Quiz-Quiz-Trade fix them as chunks; the rule of thumb is a guide, not a law (make progress, do your hair are just learnt).
Fast finishers idle during the grammar sortEx.5 extension: which signal words go with either tense (for/since), which only with the simple (ever/never/just/already/yet); write two of their own trap sentences for the class.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
“Showdown!” — pairs flip mini-whiteboards at the same instant — everyone commits an answer, nobody copies first.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the mingle.
English-first — an idea may start in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: tasks 19–25 (grammar — the open cloze, where the present and present perfect are chosen from context), tasks 26–31 (word formation), and the lexical items in every paper. School and study is the single most frequent topic in the Russian exam.

The simple-vs-continuous perfect choice is exactly what tasks 19–25 test: a gap, a context, and no options — the student supplies the form. Today's DONE/STILL rule and the finished-time red-light list are the two habits that fix the most common losses. do / make prints in the lexical section and is needed the moment the speaking card says «education».

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer line in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
