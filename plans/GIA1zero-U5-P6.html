WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin: ex.1, the model video (Long Arm Luke), 2 passes with tasks → ex.2–3: how to show the problem and the solution

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed, a question before the video (TB p.81): What problem does the robot solve? Pass 1.
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we alternate one answer each — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!
  3. Check: 1 David's grandmother, Elena — grandma Elena can't reach things on high shelves.
  4. Pass 2 with tasks on the board: What's the name of the robot? (Long Arm Luke) · How long can the arm be? (2 metres) · What's at the end of the arm? (a hand that opens and closes).
  5. Ex.2 together: we sort the phrases into the THE PROBLEM columns (Who / What / Where / When). Key: 1 David's grandmother, Elena · 2 she's short and can't get things from high places · 3 in her kitchen · 4 when she wants to cook a meal.
  6. Ex.3 in pairs (key: 1 b · 2 d · 3 a · 4 c); I point out: ‘smaller and lighter THAN other robots’ — that's your comparative from last lesson.
  7. Model p.71: Long Arm Luke: arm extends up to 2 metres · hand opens and closes · wheels to move easily · plastic body = light · long body for stability · friendly face · sound — I show how arrow-labels replace text.
  8. No video → we work from Model project p.71 + the TB p.141 script.
📺 On screen: project video (backup — Model project p.71) · SB pp.70–71
👦 Students do: watch with tasks, analyse the model together. Expected: the “drawing + arrows” format is understood right away. Typical error: they want to copy Luke — «your problem, your robot».

teams of 3

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share: ex.4–5: choosing a problem from the bank of ideas, filling in 4 columns, drilling the Creativity Key phrases

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Trios (TB p.81 — the project is in groups of three). Role cards: Problem-owner · Robot-designer · Presenter — but all three speak.
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A talks for their time on the timer, B is the active listener (counts pauses on their fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. Ex.4: Think of a problem. Only the problem — no robot yet. Four minutes. Reinforcement (TB p.81) — a bank of problems on the board: litter in the street · cleaning your room · homework · losing your phone or keys · learning English · doing sport (TB p.81 Reinforcement).
  4. We fill in THE PROBLEM using the 4 questions (Who / What / Where / When) — following the model of ex.2.
  5. Ex.5 — Key phrases Creativity: What do you think? · Let's listen to (Jon). · Why don't we (+ verb)? · Don't forget … · That's a good idea. · That's great!. Chorus with intonation, then each trio says a phrase.
  6. Working rule (Super skills box): Listen to everyone. Use your imagination. Your idea must SOLVE the problem.
📺 On screen: role cards · bank of problems on the board · SB p.70 Super skills box · timer
👦 Students do: choose a problem in trios, repeat the Key phrases. Expected: everyday problems (cleaning, lost keys). Typical error: a fantastical problem — I bring them back to «real problem in YOUR life».

teams of 3

WHAT WE DO: drawing the robot and putting together 4 slides using How to p.120; language targets on the board; a timer per slide

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.6: Design a robot that solves YOUR problem. Six minutes for the drawing — then we write.
  2. Ex.7 — four slides (SB p.70): Slide 1: explain the problem · Slide 2: introduce your robot · Slide 3: more information — the robot's abilities, comparing it with other solutions · Slide 4: say how it solves the problem. 2 minutes per slide, I announce the transitions out loud.
  3. Language targets on the board (points are given for them): 3 sentences with ‘can’ · 1 comparison with than · 1 phrase “It helps because…”.
  4. I remind them (TB p.82): Notes on the slides, not sentences. You SAY the details.
  5. We divide up who speaks on which slide, and do one run-through in the trio.
  6. Support: a template slide with ready-made phrase starters.
📺 On screen: A3 / sheets for the slides · How to p.120 · language targets on the board · timer (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: draw, label with arrows, rehearse. Expected: 4 slides from every trio in 13 minutes. Typical error: text in paragraphs — I point at the model on p.71.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can present a robot they designed in 4 slides — problem → robot → abilities → solution — using can/can't and one comparative, and sit the Unit 5 test having met every format in class.
  • Subsidiary. Super skill Creativity through Key phrases in trios with roles; Peer review with 3 questions; the test, calmly, model-first; a gentle error clinic; a WDYT? re-vote closes the unit.
  • Personal. The project and the test in one pair — we keep the timeline on the board so nobody worries. The project is a celebration, the test is routine, the debrief is about growth, not about grades.
By the end, students can…
  • work in a three with a role and use Creativity key phrases
  • present four slides about their robot with unit language
  • sit a 30-minute test calmly using familiar formats
  • name two things to work on next

Unit 5 test — specification (DRAFT until the official Macmillan A1+ pack)
PartContents & marking
Part 1 · Vocabulary (14 pts)A) 6 items: ability collocations — ___ a ball (catch), ___ a plane (fly), ___ a language (speak), ___ a bike (repair), ___ numbers (add), ___ stories (tell). B) 4 items: label the tech nouns from a picture (touchpad · charger · screen · speaker — traps: keyboard/touchpad). C) 4 items: adjective opposites (cheap ↔ ___ · heavy ↔ ___ · difficult ↔ ___ · fast ↔ ___).
Part 2 · Grammar (18 pts)A) 6 gaps: can / can't affirmative & negative (Robots ___ get tired. · He ___ sing, but he ___ play the piano.). B) 4 items: order the words → a Can-question and a Wh-question + a true short answer (you / fly / can / a plane ?). C) 5 items: comparative forms across all five rules (big → ___ · easy → ___ · expensive → ___ · good → ___ · light → ___) + a than trap. D) 3 items: possessive pronouns (Is this your tablet? — No, it isn't ___ .).
Part 3 · Listening (8 pts)A short talk about two gadgets (in the style of SB p.65 ex.6–7; teacher-read twice if no track): 4 items — complete a table “You can …” with ticks + 4 items — true/false about price and size. Mirrors Subskill: completing a table.
Part 4 · Writing (10 pts)An advert (30+ words) for an object you want to sell. Marked for: can/can't + what you can do with it (3) · at least two comparatives with than (3) · adjectives + price/condition (2) · spelling of unit words (2) — mirrors SB p.69 ex.8 Skills boost and Subskill: Spelling.

Total 50 pts · pass focus: not the number but the personal error log. Built strictly from Quick review SB p.69 — every format was met in class during the unit. The two lines: 🚀 higher (Wh-questions with can in writing, all five comparative rules, more spelling traps) · 🐣 standard (Yes/No questions, comparatives by rules 1, 3 and 4, only familiar traps).

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
