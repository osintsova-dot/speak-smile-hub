🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Both productive skills of the unit in one lesson: the speaking task rehearsed in pairs until it holds, and the written genre taken apart down to a plan of paragraphs — the clean copy written at home.
What is genuinely hard
Not the language — the behaviour. Russian classroom habit is to answer the teacher and stop; a discussion needs a student to notice that the other person has lost them and to say so, out loud, in English. The second difficulty is prosody: these expressions are questions and they need rising intonation, and said flat they sound aggressive rather than cooperative.
Typical error to expect
«I don't think the website's good» inside a formal report — contractions are the single fastest way to lose the register mark, and they are the hardest habit to break because spoken practice trains them in.
Why the lesson is built this way
Speaking first, while the room is awake and the model is fresh; writing second, because a plan survives the walk home and a first draft does not. Nothing is cut from either half except the part that works better alone: the twenty minutes of silent writing, which happen at home where there is time to think.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two things today, and both of them are what the exam actually asks for: speak with a reason, and write a text that answers every point. We build both here — you write the clean copy where nobody is rushing you.”

WHAT WE DO: ninety seconds of gerund–infinitive recycling, then straight into the photograph

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — what is the last thing you used your phone for today? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.
  2. 2 minutes — yesterday's forms, out loud. Kagan RallyRobin in pairs, taking turns: six true sentences in ninety seconds using stop, remember, forget or like, with the gerund or the infinitive chosen on purpose.
  3. 1 minute — one pair performs, the class judges the meaning. Say the sentence, the room says what it means. That is the whole check and it takes sixty seconds.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Page one hundred and eighteen. Look at the photograph — that arena is full, and every one of those people came to watch somebody else play a video game.
📺 On screen: nothing to project — this is a speaking lesson from minute one
👦 Students do: produce six meaning-carrying sentences each, then judge two aloud

The two families + the intonation drill
class → pairs
Why: The two families + the intonation drill

WHAT WE DO: ex.3b: sorting the bank by function, then making the questions sound like questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3b, two minutes in pairs: complete the two headings. Answers — 1 b (checking someone has understood you), 2 a (asking someone to clarify).
  2. Elicit the meaning of the four that are opaque: Are you following me? / Are you with me? (do you understand), Could you go over that again? (explain it again), I don't understand what you're getting at (what are you trying to say).
  3. The intonation drill, four minutes, and it is the most valuable four minutes of the lesson. Backchain each Yes/No check with a clear rise: … what I MEAN? ↗ · … what I'm SAYing? ↗ · Are you WITH me? ↗
  4. Then the same expressions said with a FALL, deliberately, so the class hears how rude they become. That contrast is what makes the rise stick.
  5. Pairs, ninety seconds: one says any sentence about video games, the other tags a checking expression onto it with a rise. Swap.
📺 On screen: the two families written up in two columns on the board — this is the board the discussion stage will run on
👦 Students do: sort fourteen expressions by function, then drill five of them for rising intonation in pairs

groups of three
Why: 🎯 Practice makes perfect · the discussion

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: three real discussions, with observers counting the target language

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Groups of three. Roles: two speakers, one observer with a tally slip. The observer counts checking expressions and clarification requests, by name.
  2. The target is written on the board and it is not negotiable: Everybody uses at least TWO checking expressions and ONE clarification request.
  3. Round 1, four minutes, question 1. Then rotate the observer role and change the question. Round 2, four minutes, question 2. Round 3, four minutes, question 3 — by now every student has observed once and spoken twice.
  4. 🎯 Between rounds, thirty seconds only: the observer reads the tally aloud. No advice, just numbers. The numbers do the coaching.
  5. Last three minutes: one group performs the most disputed question in front of the class, and the class tallies. Applause, not correction.
📺 On screen: the target sentence on the board; tally slips are quarter sheets of paper. Backup: if the room is too small for three groups to be audible, run two rounds in the corridor half of the room and swap
👦 Students do: speak in two rounds and observe in one, hitting a named target of expressions each time
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains Task 4 of the oral paper (the monologue with an argument) and the interaction marks throughout. The Russian oral rewards a candidate who keeps going and repairs misunderstanding; today's two families of expressions are exactly the repair kit, and they transfer word for word.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: in the final round, give one group the Russian-format task instead: two minutes on «Some people say video games are a waste of time» with a plan on the board. Costs nothing — it replaces one of the three rounds.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that also builds the argument bank for later

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Corners: four corners of the room labelled SPORT · NOT SPORT · DEPENDS · NO IDEA. Everyone goes to a corner and finds one reason to say to the person next to them.
  2. Two more rounds with two more statements from the article: Watching other people play is as good as playing. · Prize money makes it professional.
  3. After each round, one voice from each corner. One sentence, no speeches.
  4. Sit down straight into the recording: Two people are about to have exactly this argument. One of them will change how you talk.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: the model read for shape first, then for the ideas it borrowed from stage one

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. First read, three minutes, silent: the model «Improvements to the school website». One job — what is each of the four sections DOING? (Introduction · The current website · Suggested improvements · Conclusion.)

Planning the six paragraphs
individual
Why: Planning the six paragraphs

WHAT WE DO: ex.5a: the task read, the four questions listed, the skeleton written before a word of prose

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the task on the page with the class and put the Teacher's Book questions on the board: who is the report for, and what FOUR things do they want to know? (Which technologies you use in and outside class · how you use them · students' attitude to using technology for studying · what the ideal use would be.)
  2. Elicit the paragraph plan and write it up: paragraph 1 introduction · paragraphs 2–5 the four areas · paragraph 6 conclusion with a recommendation. Six paragraphs, each with a heading.
  3. Four minutes, silent: everyone writes the skeleton only — a title, six headings, and two or three words of content under each. No sentences yet.
  4. Circulate and check ONE thing: does every heading say what its paragraph will do? A heading called «Technology» is refused.
  5. Remind them where the content is: the ideas from the discussion on p.118 and from the reflection work of this unit are legal and encouraged.
📺 On screen: the six-paragraph plan and the four company questions on the board, next to the five language families
👦 Students do: write a title, six headings and a content note under each, in silence

WHAT WE DO: the writing task set properly, so the clean copy is worth marking

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the task aloud from the book and have one student say it back in their own words. If they cannot, it will not be written correctly either.

  3. Board the four criteria they will be marked against: every point answered · paragraphs · the register of the genre · linkers that actually link. Same four as the unit test.

Structure
Do you see what I mean? · Do you get what I'm saying? · Are you with me? · What I mean is … · That's not quite what I meant. · Are you saying that …? · I'm not sure what you mean by … · Could you go over that again? · Sorry, I'm not with you.
Form
checking = a Yes/No question with rising intonation, said AFTER your point · clarifying = a question or a hedge said BEFORE you answer, so that you answer the right thing · reformulating = What I mean is + a simpler version, never a louder one.
Pron. watch
rising tone on all five Yes/No checks — Do you see what I MEAN? ↗ · Are you WITH me? ↗ · falling tone on What I mean is … ↘ · the whole of «Do you get what I'm saying» reduces to about four beats in natural speech, which is why it is drilled and not just read
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Are you with me?» — am I asking where you are? → no, I am asking if you follow. And does my voice go up or down? → up.
«That's not quite what I meant.» Am I saying you are wrong? → no, I am saying I explained it badly. Which is politer, and which is safer.
«Our school's website isn't very good» — allowed in a report? → no. Why? → a contraction and a personal judgement. Give me the formal version. → The current website is not sufficiently attractive.
«Students think the site is boring.» How many students? All of them? → say it: nearly all the students we spoke to. Now it is a finding, not a rumour.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Two students carry the whole discussionGroup size is three, never four, and every group has a rotating OBSERVER with a tally sheet. An observer who has counted nobody's expressions for two minutes is told to hand the role over and join in.
The expressions are understood but never usedThey are required, not offered: each group is given a target — every member uses at least two checking expressions and one clarification request. The observer's tally is read out at the end.
Contractions everywhere after a speaking lessonThe register sort at minute forty is physical and public, and after it every contraction found in a draft costs the writer nothing except the word CONTRACTION written in their margin by a partner. Peer marking fixes this faster than teacher marking.
Students write an essay with headings on topThe four functions are elicited from the model and written on the board as questions: what is this report FOR? what is happening now? what should change? what is the overall picture? A paragraph that answers none of the four is cut.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Task 38 (the written piece, all five criteria) and, for the register work, task 37.

A report is not a Russian exam genre, but every criterion that decides task 38 is trained by it: a stated aim in the first paragraph (К1), paragraphs with visible headings and linkers (К2), formal register with no contractions (К3), determiners and quantifiers that make a claim measurable — nearly all, a large number of, none (К4). Students who can write «The aim of this report is to outline…» stop starting their exam essays with «I want to tell about…», which costs a mark every time.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
