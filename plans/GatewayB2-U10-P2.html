🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use six structures for the future in the past (was going to · was about to · was to · would · past continuous · was in for), build mixed conditionals in both directions, and use eighteen collocations connected with the news (make the headlines · hot off the press · take out a subscription · breaking news).
What is genuinely hard
Mixed conditionals are the hardest grammar of the year for a Russian speaker, and the reason is structural, not lexical: Russian has ONE conditional form for every unreal situation, and it marks no time at all. So «If I had studied medicine I would be a doctor now» and «If I were a doctor I would have helped you» look identical from the inside. Future in the past is easier but invisible — students go on using will inside past narratives because Russian does exactly that.
Typical error to expect
«If I studied medicine, I would be a doctor now» when the studying was in the past — the two halves of a mixed conditional live at different times, and Russian marks time nowhere in «если бы… я бы…». If + past perfect → would + infinitive (past cause, present result); if + past simple → would have + past participle (present cause, past result).
Why the lesson is built this way
Both grammar points are discovered from texts, not from rules. The future in the past arrives through a timeline drawn on the board and a true story — a message left in a bottle in a cathedral roof in 1930 and found eighty-eight years later — where every gap is a different structure. Mixed conditionals are discovered by asking which of the two halves is present and which is past, sentence by sentence, before any formula is written. The vocabulary half then lands on two short news texts about The Happy Newspaper, a real paper that prints only good news — which is also the argument bank for the speaking lesson two days later.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“In 1930 four builders repairing a cathedral roof in Germany wrote a note and hid it in a bottle. They had no idea who would find it. In 2018 a builder opened it — and one of the workers on the roof that day was his own grandfather. Today: how English talks about a future that is already in the past.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a ninety-second diagnostic of the second and third conditionals, and the bridge into today

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the six ways named from the sentences on the page, with the timeline behind them

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write on the board the sentence the Teacher's Book suggests: A spaceship launched from Florida on 22nd February … Ask Did it happen? When? (yes, in the past). Then read sentence 1 — «The spacecraft was going to land a time capsule on the Moon» — and ask Did THAT happen? (no: it was the plan).
  2. Put «land time capsule» on the timeline after the dot. Ask them to imagine today is 22 February: has it happened yet? It has not. That is a future event inside the past.
  3. ex.1, five minutes individually: find the six structures in sentences 1–6. Answers: 1 was/were going to · 2 was/were about to · 3 was/were to · 4 would · 5 past continuous · 6 was/were in for.
  4. Add the shade of meaning to each as you confirm it, because the forms are not interchangeable: was to = arranged, and formal · was about to = a second before it happened · past continuous = a confirmed plan · was in for = something surprising or unpleasant was coming.
  5. Sixty seconds in pairs: each partner says one true sentence about last week using two of the six.
📺 On screen: the six structures written up under the timeline as they are found — chalk only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: find six structures in six sentences, then produce two true sentences each about their own last week

WHAT WE DO: ex.8: eight mixed conditionals written, checked and then made personal

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.8 individually, six minutes, clock visible. Eight items, and the instruction on the board is one word: TIME. Decide which half is past and which is present BEFORE writing anything.
  2. Pairs compare for two minutes. Rule: if you disagree, say the time of each half aloud — it settles the argument without you.
  3. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together, taking only the four that split the room. Full key in the answer table below; the two worth slowing down on are 5 (hadn't spent … wouldn't be a doctor now) and 8 (had remembered … wouldn't have to do it now).
  4. 🎯 Then three minutes of production, silent: everyone writes TWO true mixed conditionals about themselves — one past cause with a present result, one present cause with a past result.
  5. Four read out to the floor. Correct only the time relationship; leave everything else.
📺 On screen: the answer key stays hidden until the pairs have compared — write the four contested items on the board only
👦 Students do: write eight items alone, argue them in pairs by naming the time of each half, then produce two true sentences about themselves

Structure
He was going to … but … · They were about to … when … · The meeting was to take place … · She knew it would … · They were in for a surprise · If I'd known, I'd have … · If I didn't like it, I wouldn't have bought it
Form
future in the past = a past form of a future structure: was/were going to + inf · was/were about to + inf · was/were to + inf (formal, arranged) · would + inf · past continuous (fixed plan) · was/were in for (a surprise, usually unpleasant). Mixed conditionals: if + past perfect → would + inf (past cause, present result) · if + past simple → would have + past participle (present cause, past result). news is UNCOUNTABLE: an item of news, a piece of news, the news IS at six — but a news bulletin and a newsflash are countable.
Pron. watch
the headline words are short and stressed, and the group will swallow them: axe /æks/ with a clean final cluster · plea /pliː/ not «plee-a» · probe /prəʊb/ with a diphthong · blaze /bleɪz/ against blast /blɑːst/ · wed /wed/ · PM said as two letters, pee-EM, stress on the second · the collocations take a compound stress on the FIRST element (FRONT-page news · NEWS bulletin · PRESS conference · CHIEF editor) · and the question tag carries the whole meaning in its intonation: falling = I am sure, rising = I am asking. Drill the two contours physically, with a hand
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Ella was going to have breakfast» — is breakfast in the past or in the future? → in the future, but seen from a moment in the past. Did she have it? → we do not know, and that is the point.
«If I hadn't spent years writing the book, it wouldn't be great» — when did the writing happen? → in the past. When is the book great? → now. So the two halves live in different times.
«I heard three interesting news» — what is wrong? → news is uncountable: three interesting ITEMS of news. Say the correct version twice.

«Would» used in both halves of a mixed conditionalThe board carries one line all lesson: WOULD NEVER LIVES IN THE IF-HALF. Point at it, do not repeat it. It is the single most common error and it is worth five points on the Test yourself page.
«a news», «news are»Correct it on the spot every single time, with the fixed phrase rather than the rule: an item of news. Write the four repairs on the wall and point at them wordlessly when it happens again.
Fast finishers in the controlled practiceThe Teacher's Book mixed-ability stems: If I had won the match yesterday, … · If I didn't like chocolate, … · I wouldn't be angry with you now if … · I wouldn't have seen this film three times if … — more confident students complete all four as mixed conditionals, alone.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text) — conditionals and past forms are the two heaviest lines of that section.

Mixed conditionals are the single most profitable grammar point of the year for a Russian candidate. The paper gives a verb in capitals and a sentence whose two halves point at different times; the student who only knows «second conditional» writes would be where would have been belongs and loses the mark. Russian has one conditional form for everything (если бы…, я бы…), so the distinction has to be taught as TIME, not as a formula. Future in the past is quieter but it prints in reported narratives: was going to, would, was about to.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
