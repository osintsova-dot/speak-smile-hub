🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Measure the whole year, Units 1–10 honestly across all five skills — 100 points: vocabulary, grammar, use of English, listening, reading, writing and speaking — and leave the room with every student knowing what the paper said about them.
What is genuinely hard
Range, not difficulty. Nothing on this paper is harder than the unit tests were; what is harder is that five units sit on one page with nothing labelled, and the student has to decide for themselves which grammar the sentence is asking for.
Typical error to expect
An essay that answers the two given notes and forgets the third point. Part 1 gives two notes and a blank line marked «your own idea», and the blank line is worth as much as the other two. It is the single most common way to lose Content marks in this paper.
Why the lesson is built this way
Identical in shape to every unit test the group has already sat, and deliberately so: by now the format costs them nothing, so the paper measures the language. Listening while the ears are fresh, writing last, speaking in the corner in pairs while the room writes — and filmed, so that the year can be heard next to the first recording of the year.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Same paper you have sat five times, one bigger subject: everything. You choose the version. And your speaking answer goes next to the one from September — that comparison is the real report card.”

5–12′
The paper explained · Standard or Higher
board
Why: Anxiety in a test comes from not knowing the shape of the paper. Publishing it costs nothing, and letting them choose the version turns the test from something done to them into something they signed up for.

WHAT WE DO: the whole paper on the board, then each student takes the version they want

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Board the map and leave it up all lesson: VOCABULARY 20 · GRAMMAR 25 · USE OF ENGLISH 8 · LISTENING 12 · READING 15 · WRITING 10 · SPEAKING 10 = 100.
  2. Both versions go on the front desk. Say it plainly: Standard and Higher are the same paper — same skills, same hundred points, same time. Higher asks the same things in harder words. Take the one you want to be measured by. The choice is the student's; if a student cannot choose, you choose for them and say so without ceremony.
  3. Say the order of the day: Listening first, while your ears are fresh. Then vocabulary, grammar and use of English. Then reading. Writing last, and I collect at eighty-six minutes.
  4. Say the speaking rule once, now, so nobody is surprised later: While you write, I take you two at a time to the corner. Four minutes, three questions each. I film the answer — the camera is for me, so that in December we can hear how you sounded today.
  5. ICQ before anything opens: How long is the writing? — 140 to 190 words. What do you do if you finish early? — check the word count and the tenses. Nobody leaves.
📺 On screen: the test map on the board. Backup: dictate it — it is seven lines.
👦 Students do: choose Standard or Higher, write name and date, and keep the paper closed

Why: Listening goes first because it is the only section that needs the whole room silent at the same moment, and because ears at minute seventy score lower than the student actually is.

WHAT WE DO: one recording, played twice, twelve points

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Play B2_End_of_Year_Test.mp3 twice with thirty seconds between the playings. Do not pause mid-track and do not take questions while the audio runs.
  2. Before the first playing: Read the questions now. Thirty seconds. Before the second: Now fill the gaps you left and check the ones you guessed.
  3. Stand at the back with the volume in your hand. If a corridor noise ruins a stretch, note the time and replay that stretch a third time — that is fair, and it is your call, not a favour.
📺 On screen: audio only, nothing on the screen. Backup: if the sound fails, read B2_End_of_Year_Test_audioscript.pdf aloud twice at natural speed — do not slow down, and do not explain a single word.

26–54′
Vocabulary, Grammar and Use of English (53 points) · speaking pairs start
desks + the corner
Why: Speaking is ten points of the same paper and cannot be quietly dropped; running it in the corner during the written work is the only honest way to fit five skills into ninety minutes. Video turns a one-off mark into a record: the same student, the same kind of question, three months apart.

WHAT WE DO: fifty-three points in silence, while the first pairs are filmed in the corner

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Announce once and write the finishing time on the board: Fifty-three points, twenty-eight minutes. If you are stuck, move on and come back.
  2. At minute thirty take the first pair to the corner. Four minutes: Student A asks the three questions, Student B answers with a reason and a personal detail. Then swap. The topic today is the topics of the year — the examiner questions are on the card.
  3. Film every pair — this is not optional. Start recording before the first question and have each student say into the camera: Name, End of Year, today's date. Without that line the file is worthless in three months.
  4. Mark the pair out of ten the moment they sit down again, while you still remember the interaction. The video is not for marking — it is for comparing with the next one.
  5. Walk back through the room between pairs. A teacher who never leaves the corner is a teacher who is not invigilating.
📺 On screen: nothing on screen; the phone in the corner is recording. Backup: if the recording drops, mark the pair live, write «no video» on the mark sheet and film them at the start of the next lesson.
👦 Students do: work in silence; go to the corner in pairs when called

Reading (15 points) · speaking pairs continue
desks + the corner
Why: Reading in the middle keeps the room busy while the corner works, and it is the section where a tired student can still score if the timing is announced.

WHAT WE DO: sixteen minutes, fifteen points, and the last pairs filmed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say it once and write the second finishing time under the first: Sixteen minutes, fifteen points. The answers are in the text.
  2. Keep calling pairs until everyone has spoken. At minute sixty-eight the corner closes whatever is left — the rest speak at the start of the next lesson, filmed the same way.
  3. At minute sixty-two write 8 minutes left on the board without saying anything.
📺 On screen: the finishing time on the board; the phone still recording in the corner. Backup: a wall clock if the board is needed for anything else.
👦 Students do: read and answer fifteen points; speak in the corner when called

70–86′
Writing · the task printed on the paper — 140–190 words, 140–190 words (10 points)
desks
Why: Writing goes last because it is the only section where thinking time cannot be shared with anything else, and because a student who has already spoken and read has the language of the unit warm.

WHAT WE DO: one task, sixteen minutes, every bullet point answered

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say the shape of the task in one sentence: One task, printed on the paper. Read the bullet points twice and plan before you write: the plan is what stops you losing the last two points.
  2. Name the two things that cost marks here and nowhere else: Every bullet point answered, and the word count. Under 140 or over 190 loses marks even if the English is beautiful.
  3. No more pairs now — you are in the room. Walk the room twice, then sit down and stop moving.
  4. At minute eighty-four: Count your words. Write the number at the bottom of the page.
📺 On screen: the word limit and the finishing time on the board. Backup: write them on paper and put it on the front desk if the board is full.
👦 Students do: write 140–190 words and count them

WHAT WE DO: collected in silence, and the marking promise made out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect at eighty-six exactly, in one pile, Standard and Higher separated.
  2. Say what happens next: I mark this by the next lesson. You get the paper back with the score by section — seven numbers, not one — so you can see which skill to work on.
  3. Homework aloud and on the board — and say why it is selective: The Language checkpoint, but only the parts where you lost points. Doing the parts you already know is not homework, it is decoration.
  4. File the videos the same evening: one folder per group, file name Gateway_B2_End_of_Year_Test_Surname_date.

Structure
the seven grammar lines the paper tests, revised as meaning in the first ten minutes: relative clauses · the conditionals, including the mixed one · the passive and the impersonal passive · reported speech · wish and if only · indirect questions and question tags · verb patterns
Form
revision only; nothing new is presented on the last day of a course. The full list of what the paper asks for is in the specification table of this plan — read it before the lesson and aim the ten minutes of review at the three lines your group is weakest on.
Pron. watch
assessed once, in the speaking section, and only at the level of intelligibility — the printed band scale says «pronunciation mostly clear». Do not correct pronunciation during the assessed speaking; note it and let it go.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
The essay is worth ten marks out of a hundred. How many minutes does it need? → about fifteen. How many do most people leave for it? → four, and that is the mistake this briefing exists to stop.
Exercise 5 gives seven sentences and says four contain a mistake. What do you do with the other three? → tick them. A blank line scores nothing even if the sentence really is correct.
In the speaking task you have to give arguments for AND against before your own opinion. Can you skip the side you disagree with? → no: the band scale rewards a full contribution, and one side is half a contribution.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
A student freezes at the sheer size of the paperSay the map again, quietly, at their desk: seven sections, and they have done every one of them five times. Start them on the section they are best at — the order of the middle sections is not fixed.
The corner runs late and the last pair is not filmedThe corner closes anyway; the written sections cannot be cut. The rest speak at the start of the next lesson, filmed the same way.
«Why is there no Cambridge mock this year?»Answer it in one line, without apology: the course measures B2 across five skills, seven times a year, on paper that comes with the coursebook. That is more measurement than a single mock, and it is spread through the year where it can still change something.
Two students take different versions and compare afterwardsExpected and harmless: same sections, same hundred points. Say so once and do not let it become a conversation about fairness.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The final paper is the course's own summary rather than an exam rehearsal — that job was done in lesson 69 with the Cambridge paper. Whatever a student scores today is a fair starting number for September.
