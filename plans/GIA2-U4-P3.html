🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A grammar pair, but the topic is NOT 'comparative and superlative adjectives' — the topic is 'the record bureau'. A record claim is not accepted if the form is broken: 'more hotter' and 'the hotest' are rejected on the spot, and the cost of the error works better than a rule. The students build the rule themselves out of the four sentences about Siberia and India (ex.1–2), take the spelling from Spelling rules p.57, and the world-records quiz (track 26) and the text about Dubai are the final calibration.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Welcome to the Record Bureau. Every claim on this desk has to be perfect. 'Norilsk is more colder than Moscow' — rejected. 'The coldest' with one t — rejected. Today each crew files five records about our region and our country, and the bureau accepts only clean forms. Sixty seconds to defend your file at the end. Calibration test in three minutes. Forms out!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The record bureau: a claim is only accepted if the form is perfect. The crew files five records about their own region and country and defends the file in 60 seconds. A broken form is rejected on the spot.

⚡ Base sprint · the form table
crews · mini-boards

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 p.52 (which sentences compare two, which compare three or more) → ex.2, they work out the rule themselves → ex.3, the table of forms with Spelling rules p.57 as support → Showdown on 6 flips

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four sentences. Two of them compare TWO things, two compare three or more. Sort them — two minutes. (TB key ex.1: two — Siberia is colder than India, India is wetter than Siberia; three or more — This is the coldest town in the world, May and June are the wettest months).
  2. Now finish the rules yourselves. (TB key ex.2: 1 than · 2 the). They formulate the rule, I confirm it and bring in the gesture: two fingers → than, open hand → the …est.
  3. ex.3 with Spelling rules p.57 as support (the poster goes on the wall): TB key 1 coldest · 2 wetter · 3 heavier · 4 most difficult · 5 worst.
  4. Kagan Showdown: I give an adjective — everyone writes both forms — Boards up! 6 rounds: big · dry · good · beautiful · hot · bad.
📺 On screen: SB p.52 ex.1–3 · Spelling rules p.57 · poster · mini-boards · timer
👦 Students do: they build the rule out of the examples. Expect: by the third flip they chant the doubling of the consonant (hot → hotter) in chorus
📓 Interactive notebook — Get_Involved_A2_Unit4_Grammar1_Comparative_And_Superlative_Adjectives_Visual_Takeaway_A5
A5 grammar card — COMPARE THE EXTREMES: comparative (two things: -er + than / more + than) against superlative (three or more: the -est / the most) with the spelling routes (cold → colder → the coldest · hot → hotter → the hottest · sunny → sunnier → the sunniest · beautiful → more → the most) and the irregulars good → better → the best, bad → worse → the worst
  • Glue the card in; draw a line between the two routes — short words and long ones.
  • Compare two towns, then name the most-of-all out of three.
  • The rule in one line: two = -er + than, three or more = the -est.
  • The card stays open through the comparison scale.
When: ~5 min at the start of the “Sprint: the table of forms and the spelling” stagePrep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Scissors, glue sticks, pencils.

🔥 Challenge · the record duel
crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 (six sentences) against the clock + ex.5 (Brazil and Ecuador) + a duel of teams, 8 seconds per turn, repeating an adjective = the turn burns

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 as a race, 3 minutes. TB key: 1 hotter · 2 windier · 3 colder · 4 better · 5 more dangerous · 6 warmer. I write the comparatives on the board — spelling matters more than speed (TB tip).
  2. ex.5 in pairs: TB key 1 Brazil is bigger than Ecuador · 2 Quito is higher than Brasilia · 3 Summers in Brasilia are hotter than in Quito · 4 Winters in Quito are cooler than in Brasilia · 5 Quito is wetter than Brasilia.
  3. A duel of crews: My region vs yours. One comparison each, eight seconds: Our winter is longer than yours. Your turn!

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can build comparative and superlative forms correctly (including spelling and irregulars) and file and defend five record claims about their own region at speed.
  • Subsidiary. The rule is derived from the examples in the P2 text (ex.1–2); the table of forms ex.3 with Spelling rules p.57 as support; ex.4–5 written rounds against the clock; the quiz ex.6–7 (track 26); the text about Dubai ex.8; Brain teaser ex.9; the error clinic on P1–P2.
  • Personal. The first grammar pair of the unit: I do NOT explain the rule — the students formulate it themselves (guided discovery), I only confirm it and put up the spelling poster. Error feedback is anonymous.
By the end, students can…
  • choose between comparative + than and the + superlative
  • spell the forms correctly (hotter, driest, the wettest)
  • use the irregulars good/bad
  • file five clean record claims and defend them

Structure
Siberia is colder than India. · India is wetter than Siberia. · This is the coldest town in the world. · May and June are the wettest months.
Form
comparing TWO things → comparative adjective + than: colder than, wetter than · comparing THREE or more → the + superlative adjective: the coldest, the wettest · good → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst
Pron. watch
than is unstressed in connected speech, it sounds like /ðən/: colder than /ˈkəʊldə ðən/ · the -est ending is short: the coldest /ˈkəʊldɪst/, not 'cold-EST'
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'India is more hot than the UK' — what's wrong? → hot is short: hotter than.
'the hotest place' — and here? → hot doubles the consonant: the hottest.
'Siberia is colder that India' — correct it. → colder THAN India.

Assumptions: SB p.52 + Spelling rules p.57, KTP pair 32 (grammar in both halves). The comparative is patchily familiar from A1+; the load is in the spelling, the speed and the choice between 'two things or three'.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
'Comparatives again, we know this'I don't argue: the gate on writing speed shows the real ceiling in 60 seconds. After that it is the thrill of the bureau that works, not the rule.
more hotter / the hotest — the classicsThe Spelling rules p.57 poster stays up all lesson; the bureau's rule: a claim with a double form is rejected on the spot and rewritten.
They confuse 'two things' with 'three or more'A physical gesture: two fingers → than, an open hand → the …est. The gesture stays with us to the end of the unit.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
