🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Choose between as and like — the job you do against what something resembles or an example — and write a 100-word informal email that answers every question in the prompt.
What is genuinely hard
Russian «как» is both English words, so «he works like a teacher» feels correct and says the opposite of what was meant. The writing difficulty is not language at all: it is completeness. Writing Part 1 gives four notes and a candidate who answers three of them cannot score well however good the English is — and the model on the page is there precisely to show a good writer missing one.
Typical error to expect
«He works like a teacher» — that says he is not one. As + a job is what you ARE; like is what something RESEMBLES or an EXAMPLE. Russian «как» is both, so this one is produced by every group and it changes the meaning, not just the style.
Answering three notes out of four — the model reply in exercise 2 does exactly this, on purpose, and finding the missing answer is the task. It is the commonest cause of a low mark in Writing Part 1 and it has nothing to do with English.
Why the lesson is built this way
as and like takes fifteen minutes because it is one decision, and it is placed first so that it can be used inside the writing. The writing half is run in the order the examiner reads it: what does the prompt ASK (the notes, counted), what does a good answer look like (the model, with its one missing answer found by the class), what language does it need (the Prepare to write box and the linkers from yesterday), then plan, then write in class in silence with a word count. The peer check is a note-counting check, not a language one, because that is the mark that gets lost.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“The reply on this page was written by somebody with good English. It still would not get full marks, and there is exactly one reason why. Find it, and you will never lose that mark yourself. Also today: two two-letter words that change what a sentence means.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3: the decision named, found in a text, then made nine times

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 as a class, thirty seconds: which of the two introduces an example? Answer: like.
  2. ex.2, and this is the stage. Read the short work-experience text and ask first whether they would want that placement — thirty seconds of real reaction before the grammar.
  3. Then the method the Teacher's Book asks for: look at what FOLLOWS each bold word, because that is what decides. Mixed-ability pairs, four minutes. Answers: 1 b (like = similar to), 2 c (as + verb = the job), 3 a (like = an example), 4 d (known AS = called), 5 a (like = an example).
  4. As each is confirmed, the student writes the example into one of the two boxes on the board.
  5. ex.3 individually, three minutes: three sentences, four gaps. Answers: 1 as a waiter, like dirty plates and glasses · 2 like castles in the sky · 3 as their favourite character.
  6. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together and demand the box each time — not «as» but «AS, because it is the job he does».
📺 On screen: the two boxes filling with real examples, written by students — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: read a short text for real reaction, then make nine as-or-like decisions and justify each by box

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: counting the notes before reading a single word of the reply

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read Daisy's email aloud, slowly, once, while they follow. Then close the book and ask: How many things does she want from you? Let them guess. The answer is three, plus the fourth note that says «Great!».
  2. ex.1, two minutes: tick the three things Daisy wants. Answers: suggest which of the two jobs she should take · tell her what to do with the money she will earn · tell her what work experience you are going to do.
  3. Now fill the four tick boxes on the board with those notes, and say the rule that runs the rest of the lesson: one note, one answer, one tick. Nothing else in this task earns marks as reliably.
  4. Make the point about the little notes in the margin, because candidates ignore them: Great! is also an instruction — it means react to the news before you do anything else.
  5. Two minutes, pairs: what would YOU tell Daisy to do with the money? Two ideas each, and they are needed in twenty minutes.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5–6 = the exam task: about 100 words, all four notes, informal

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the task from the page: Ben's email about future jobs, with four notes in the margin — react to his class discussion, ask why he wants to be a firefighter, explain what your perfect job would be, and tell him how you could find out more about that work.
  2. Read it aloud once, then ask the class to say the four notes back to you. Write them into the four tick boxes on the board, replacing Daisy's.
  3. ex.5, five minutes: plan only. One note beside each number, in their notebooks. Walk the room and check for four notes, not for English. Anyone with three gets sent back.
  4. Pairs compare plans for two minutes and help each other with the thinnest box — that is the Teacher's Book instruction and it rescues the weakest writers before they start.
  5. 🎯 Twelve minutes, silent, individually, visible timer. Rules on the board: about 100 words · all four notes answered · at least two linkers from yesterday · at least one as or like · informal opening and closing.
  6. Announce the last two minutes: Two minutes. Count your words, write the number, and tick the four boxes on your own paper.
  7. Do not circulate correcting. Circulate counting ticks.

WHAT WE DO: the check that finds the mark candidates actually lose

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Swap papers. The checking job is deliberately narrow and it is written on the board: find the four answers. Number them in the margin of your partner's paper — 1, 2, 3, 4.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach, five minutes: if you cannot find one, say so out loud and let the writer show you where it is. Half the time it is there and unclear, which is a different problem with the same cure.
  3. Second pass, two minutes, register only: circle one short form, one phrasal verb and the opening and closing phrases. If any of those is missing, say which.
  4. Three minutes back on their own text: make two changes, written above the line so the first version stays visible.
  5. Hand out the photocopied Teacher's Book model for two minutes of silent comparison. Do not analyse it — the comparison is enough at this point in the lesson.
  6. Collect. Tell them what happens next: These come back marked on the four notes first and the English second, because that is the order the examiner uses.

Structure
I was working as a receptionist on the front desk. · We were known as the Terrible Twins. · She could wear things like jeans and t-shirts. · If I were you, I'd go for the restaurant.
Form
as + a job or a name (work AS a vet, known AS the Twins) · like + a comparison or an example (things LIKE jeans, looks LIKE a castle) · informal email: short forms, phrasal verbs, an informal opening and closing.
Pron. watch
calculate /ˈkælkjuleɪt/ and organise /ˈɔːɡənaɪz/ both take the stress on the FIRST syllable and the class will put it on the second · deal with runs together as /ˈdiːlwɪð/ in speech · although /ɔːlˈðəʊ/ has no /g/ sound in it at all, whatever the spelling promises · and in as and like the difference is heard in the stress: he works AS a teacher, he works LIKE a machine
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«He works like a teacher.» Is he a teacher? → no. He behaves in a similar way. «He works AS a teacher» — is he? → yes.
«Bring some food, like sandwiches» — is that a comparison? → no, an example. Like does both jobs.
The prompt has four notes. How many answers does your email need? → four. Count them before you write and tick them when you have.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«She works like a nurse»The two boxes on the board answer it without a word being said. Point, wait three seconds, let them repair it themselves.
Three notes answered, one forgottenThe four numbered tick boxes go on the board and into their notebooks BEFORE the planning. Nothing is written until there is a note beside each number, and the peer check counts boxes, not commas.
The email is too formalThe register work is done on the model, not in the abstract: find the phrasal verb, find two short forms, find the exclamation mark. Then the rule writes itself.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

Writing Part 1 and EGE task 37 are the same task in two accents: read a friend's message, answer every question in it, keep the register informal, land near a hundred words. Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
