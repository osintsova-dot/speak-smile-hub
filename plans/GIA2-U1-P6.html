🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A pair made of two broadcasts: an oral one (Key phrases for checking answers as a team — a real skill for exams and for life) and a written one (the first blog post of the unit). The 'newsroom' storyline: first the reporters check the facts (the dialogue), then they publish the post. Capital letters come as 'the house style of the newsroom', not as a rule from the coursebook.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Tonight we go live. First — the newsroom check: two reporters, one geography quiz, and the phrases real teams use: What have you got for number three? Are you sure? Then — the launch: your first blog post about YOUR school, published on the class wall. Editors reject posts with broken capitals. Newsroom — to work!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: A newsroom: first we check the quiz answers with the phrases real teams use, then we publish the first blog post about our own school. A post with broken capitals gets sent back by the editor.

solo → pairs

WHAT WE DO: the geography quiz individually for 5 min → checking in pairs with ONLY the Key phrases → preparing the dialogue; the frame is Think-Pair-Share, the phrases are held in place by Talking Chips

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: Five minutes, five questions, no phones, no partners. Your answers only.
  2. Kagan Think-Pair-Share: first their own sheet in silence (THINK), then the check in the pair (PAIR) and only then on air to the class (SHARE). Everyone arrives in the pair with THEIR OWN answer — which is what makes the check real.
  3. PREPARE: Now check together — but ONLY with the Key phrases. Russian check = minus point.
  4. Kagan Talking Chips: 5 chips each, a chip goes down for EVERY Key phrase said out loud; out of chips means you listen. That way both of them rack up phrases, not just the chatty one.
  5. Each pair prepares its own checking dialogue (on the model, with their own answers).
📺 On screen: SB p.19 quiz · Key phrases on screen (Backup: the same written on the board before the lesson)
👦 Students do: they check with the phrases for real (the answers genuinely differ). Expected: Are you sure? comes out with real bite

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They read the dialogue instead of playing itThe quiz is real (THINK is genuinely individual) — so the check becomes real too: the answers really do differ.
They copy the post off AltinThe newsroom rule: Altin's structure — yes, his facts — no. The 9 questions on p.20 give them their own material.
The teacher fixes the capitalsCapitals patrol: they swap posts and the editor works through a 6-point checklist.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
