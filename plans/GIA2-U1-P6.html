Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to check answers with a partner in English using the unit Key phrases, and to write a short three-paragraph blog post about their school in the present simple with correct capital letters.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle Unit 1 lexis (daily routines & school subjects) and present simple; develop the writing subskill capital letters; listening for detail from the model video (Carmel & Lukas); interaction skills — agree / disagree politely; peer review.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; model before every product (I do → we do → you do); give an ICQ before each stage; run one clear brain-break at the midpoint; hold the “phrases only” rule during the speaking task.
By the end, pupils can…
  • check answers with a partner using the Key phrases (ask · answer · agree · disagree · “I don't know”);
  • use I think it's… / Do you think it's…? to give and query an answer;
  • write a 3-paragraph blog post about their school with correct capital letters.

Key phrases
Ask: What about number…? · What have you got for number…? · Do you think it's…?  Answer: I've got… · I think it's…  Agree: Yes, me too. · You're right.  Disagree: Really? I've got… · Are you sure?  “Don't know”: I don't know.
Real-world grammar
I think it's ‘34 million’. · Do you think it's ‘rugby’?
Writing genre
Blog post (3 paragraphs): general description → subjects → school day & free time; present simple throughout; model = Altin, Tirana (SB p.20).
Form / literacy
Subskill — capital letters: not only the first word of a sentence → I · days (Monday) · months (July) · nationalities/languages (French) · countries & cities (Tirana) · names & surnames (Mr Flynn) · people's titles.
Pron. watch
rising, polite intonation on checks — Are you SURE? · Do you think it's…?; word stress on number + the digit.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
You disagree with your partner — do you say You're wrong! or Really? Are you sure? → the polite one.
Is the goal to be right, or to use the phrases? → use the phrases (checking language is the point).
i live in tirana — how many capital letters are missing? → two: I and Tirana.
French (the language) — capital? → yes. maths? → no (not a proper noun).

Assumptions: daily routines, school subjects & present simple (aff/neg, Yes/No, object pronouns) were met in Pairs 1–5; pupils can write simple present-simple sentences and work in stable pairs. Pairs: keep A/B roles; odd number → one trio (two answerers).

Hook. Two minutes — how much of Unit 1 is still in your head? Each pupil gets one card (a daily routine or a school subject).

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (Kagan): stand up, hand up, pair up. A quizzes B — What time do you start school? / Which subject is this? — then swap, then trade cards and find a new partner. 3–4 rounds.

This warms up exactly the language they'll need to write about later. Settle: Class? — Yes! Pens down, eyes here.

THINK → WRITE — SB p.21 ex 9. From the PLAN, write your own All About My School entry — three paragraphs, headings from ex 3, present simple, model covered. Use tiered frames on the board for support:

§1 My school is in […]. It's a (big/small) school with about […] students. The teachers are… §2 My favourite subjects are […]. We also study… §3 We start school at […] and finish at […]. In the evening I…

Monitor: nudge for present simple 3rd-person -s and capitals; whisper-correct, don't stop the flow. Wrapping up in 5…4…3…2…1.
