🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The food vocabulary is not introduced as a list but as a chef's working tool: in 60 seconds a crew has to explain what their dish is made of — the way a “how to” clip does. Mon's video with the Oreo cheesecake gives them a live model of the genre and the formula “you need … / add some … / mix”. some/any and there is/are come up in every line, but they are NOT announced as the topic — that is P3's job. The unit's WDYT question (“what makes a town a good place”) is opened here and kept alive until the project.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Chefs, cameras on. Mon just posted a picnic video: Oreo cheesecake and mango lemonade — eighteen cookies, some sugar, some cream cheese. Two minutes of video, one million views. Now it's your channel. Each crew picks a dish — yours, your grandmother's, the one your town is famous for — and films the pitch: what it is, what's in it, why we must try it. Sixty seconds. Every ingredient in English or the shot doesn't count. Kitchen — open!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Mon filmed a video about an Oreo cheesecake and a lemonade — now it's your channel. Each crew takes a dish and films a 60-second pitch: what it is, what is in it, why you must try it. All the ingredients in English, or the take does not count.

WHAT WE DO: the level for our students: the chef's language — quantity and precision (with no grammar announced)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Weak pitch: 'It has cheese.' Useless — a chef says HOW MUCH and WHAT KIND.
  2. Upgrade frames: You need some cheese and two eggs. · There's a lot of sauce in it. · There isn't any meat — it's for vegetarians. · It's a mix of rice, chicken and spices.
  3. Two upgrade lines in your pitch = richness points.
  4. A mini pronunciation drill (TB): chips /tʃɪps/ · grapes /ɡreɪps/ · noodles /ˈnuːd(ə)ls/ · onions /ˈʌnjəns/ · yoghurt /ˈjɒɡə(r)t/.

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second pitch: the dish + the ingredients + one “how it's made” step + “why it's a must try”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes to prepare: Keywords only, no scripts. Everyone owns one block: name and country · ingredients · one step · the 'must try' line.
  2. The rule of the channel: Every ingredient in English. A Russian word = a cut in the take.
  3. Sixty seconds per crew. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness — cards up!
  4. A challenge from the floor after each one: You said 'a lot of spices'. How much is a lot — one spoon or five?

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can name 24 food and drink words, sort them into seven food groups and pitch a dish in 60 seconds (name + ingredients + why you must try it).
  • Subsidiary. The gate exposes the real vocabulary ceiling; the table on p.60 ex.2 and Fun food facts p.61 ex.3–4 are closed by a race; Video skills (“Picnic snack ideas”) is watched like producers watch it; the first use of some/any and there is happens in context, with no topic announced.
  • Personal. I do NOT announce countable/uncountable as the topic: for now it is simply “how chefs talk”. Errors like “a milk” and “there is three eggs” I collect silently — that is the material for the clinic in P3.
By the end, students can…
  • write 15+ food and drink words in 90 seconds
  • sort food into seven groups (meat/fish, carbs, dairy, fruit, vegetables, desserts, other)
  • list the ingredients of a dish
  • pitch a dish in 60 seconds and say why it's worth trying

Form
recipe chunks: you need + the food · cut / crush / mix / add / blend · a kilo of, 300 ml of, two tablespoons of — the quantity comes before the food.
Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I'd like a milk” — can we say that? → no: some milk or a glass of milk.
“herbs” — we say /hɜː(r)bz/, is the h heard? → in British English yes, in American no; we take the British version.
“You know food in English. But can you write 15 words in 90 seconds?” → gate.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“We know food already” from the doorwayI don't argue — I put up the gate: not recognition but WRITING against 90 seconds, spelling included. The result is private, the rematch is at the end of the pair.
The pitch turns into a shopping listThe rule of the channel: three compulsory blocks — what it is · what's in it · why it's a must try. The judges take a point off for “just a list”.
The strong ones take over the airtimeRoles inside the pitch: name and country · ingredients · the “how it's made” step · the closing line. Everyone speaks out loud at least once.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
