🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Read eight words with two meanings from context (coach, match, train, fit, point, exercise, trainer, work out), follow a sports programme for gist and detail, and tell a past sports event — past simple for the events, past continuous for the background, an opinion with a because.
What is genuinely hard
The double words fight Russian head-on: «тренер» wants trainer (shoes!), «матч» hides that match also means «go together». And telling a past event well is a structure problem: without the model, stories arrive as a list of nouns with no tenses and no opinion.
Typical error to expect
He is my trainer for a person who trains a team — English wants coach; trainers are shoes. Russian «тренер» is the false friend [A2/B1]
An opinion with no reason attached — It was good full stop — scores as no opinion at all in the speaking [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The vocabulary opens the lesson because it is light and winnable — energy for the listening half. The sports programme gets three passes (photo prediction → the winning photo → order and detail) because that is how ears actually work; the beach-ball goal is a story worth hearing, which is the point: the class then hears Max and Rachel TELL such stories, sorts their tenses by ear, and steals their phrases. The speaking is built as two rounds with an error clinic in between — the improvement between rounds is audible within one lesson, which is the cheapest motivation there is. Phrases and becauses are compulsory: a bare «It was good» scores zero in this room.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“A football hits a beach ball, the ball goes in — goal or no goal? A real referee had to decide. Today you hear the whole story, learn eight English words that secretly have two jobs — and then tell YOUR best match story twice, better the second time.”

Vocabulary · one word, two meanings
pairs → class
Why: Eight short words, sixteen meanings: the concept (English recycles its words) costs one minute on «watch», and then the pairs do the deciding — context votes, not translation.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: choose the meaning from context; ex.2: produce the right form

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs, 4 minutes: eight sentences, two candidate meanings each — context decides, translation forbidden. Key: 1 b · 2 a · 3 b · 4 a · 5 b · 6 b · 7 a · 8 b.
  2. Debrief the two traps out loud: a coach to the match — did it teach anyone? No: it drove. · extra maths exercise — did anyone sweat? No: it was written.
  3. The false-friend wall: TRAINER = the shoe. COACH = the person. Russian тренер is lying to you. Draw the shoe.
  4. ex.2 solo, 4 minutes, pair check — the words change form. Key: 0 matches (colour) · 1 fit, exercise · 2 coach, work out (or train) · 3 point · 4 trainers, fit · 5 exercise · 6 points, matches · 7 coach, trains · 8 work out. Watch item 4: trainers that don't FIT — both double words in one sentence.
📺 On screen: the false-friend wall (a drawn shoe + COACH the person) in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: decide from context, then produce the forms. Expect work out (= find the answer) to be the genuinely new meaning. Typical error: he is my trainer — the shoe on the wall answers it

WHAT WE DO: three passes: photos → the winning photo + what happened → order and cities

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Photo prediction, pairs, 90 seconds: Three photos — basketball, women's football, and… what IS that beach ball doing? Guess the story.
  2. Pass 1, track 18 — ex.1: Which photo is the photo of the week — and what happened next? Key: Photo C. What happened next: the football hit the beach ball and then went into the net.
  3. Pass 2, same track — ex.2: order the six events a–f solo, pair check. Key: order a–f: a 5 · b 3 · c 1 · d 2 · e 6 · f 4.
  4. Pass 3 — ex.3: the cities from the box (Liverpool ×3, Manchester, Sunderland ×2). Key: 1 (studio in) Manchester · 2 (a) Liverpool (fan threw the beach ball) · 3 Sunderland (won 1–0) · 4 (the) Liverpool (players weren't playing well) · 5 Sunderland played better than Liverpool — warn them: the answer is in the words, not in football logic.
  5. Colour with the background if they ask: TB background: Manchester has United and City, Liverpool has Liverpool and Everton; Sunderland is in the north-east — last big title the 1973 FA Cup.

Speaking model · Max and Rachel
solo → pairs
Why: A recorded model shows the bar before students have to reach it: who watched, who took part, which tense carries the events and which the background — sorted by ear, then the phrases drilled with attitude.

WHAT WE DO: track 19: who watched, who took part — tenses and phrases by ear

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame: Two people, two events. One WATCHED, one TOOK PART. Which is which? Play track 19 — ex.2. Key: Max watched; Rachel took part; the sports are football and athletics.
  2. ex.3: the four gapped sentences — past simple or continuous? Solo, pair check. Key: past tenses: 1 were winning · 2 scored · 3 were watching · 4 came (third in one race). Point at the menu boxes: were winning = the film, scored = the event.
  3. ex.4: which opinion phrases did they actually use? Key: the recording uses «It was the best … ever» — tick it; mark the other opinion phrases against the tape as you listen (the box lists five).
  4. ex.5: the reasons. Key: reasons: 1 I was very happy because I support Real Madrid · 2 I really enjoyed taking part because it was my first time. Fill the OPINION and BECAUSE boxes from the Prepare to speak list: Prepare to speak — past simple for the main things · past continuous for actions in progress · opinion: It was an amazing… · It was really exciting · It was the best… ever · It was so cool · I really enjoyed… · reasons: because…
  5. Drill the opinion line with attitude, choral then rows: It was the BEST match EVer!
📺 On screen: track 19 in the board app (Backup: read Max and Rachel from the audioscript, TB p.263–264, in two voices; the sorting works identically)
👦 Students do: sort by ear: who did what, which tense does which job, which phrases fire. Expect the tense sort to go fast after lesson 3 — praise it as proof. Typical error: opinion phrases mumbled flat — the drill wants drama

solo
Why: Five questions stop the blank-mind freeze: nobody speaks without knowing the event, the result and the reason they enjoyed it. Planning is where weak speakers are made average.

WHAT WE DO: ex.6: five questions, five lines — the story before the telling

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Brainstorm 90 seconds, class-wide: sports events they have watched or played in — school matches, a relay, a home victory on TV. Six ideas on the board.
  2. Five planning questions on the board (the book's ex.6): ex.6 plan: which event and when · did you watch or take part · what happened · the final result · why you enjoyed it. Five answers, one line each, 4 minutes, silence.
  3. No event? Legal to borrow: the beach-ball goal, retold as if you were in the stadium.
  4. Walk and unstick with menus, not answers: a goal? a fall? rain? your team losing — then winning?
📺 On screen: the five planning questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: plan the story in five lines. Expect two students with «no event» — the borrow rule saves them without shame

🎯 Speaking out · round 1
pairs
Why: The productive outcome: a real past-event interview with the phrases compulsory. Round 1 is the diagnosis — you log errors and phrase-dodging for the clinic.

WHAT WE DO: ex.7: tell the story, interview-style — phrases compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Timed Pair Share, 2 minutes each way: A tells the story from the plan, B is the journalist — one What happened next? and one Why did you enjoy it? minimum. Swap.
  2. Requirements visible on the menu: four sentences minimum, one continuous background line, one opinion phrase, one because.
  3. You walk and log SILENTLY: missing becauses, flat «it was good», tense collapses, trainer-for-coach.
  4. Close: Freeze. Round two is coming — after the clinic.
📺 On screen: the story menu and requirements stay on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: tell, stretch, swap. Expect stiffness and thin backgrounds — normal for round 1; the clinic and round 2 exist because of it. Typical error: the whole story in past continuous — logged for the clinic

Error clinic
class
Why: Delayed correction between the rounds: anonymous sentences from your sheet, repaired by the class — feedback lands while the story is warm and there is still a round 2 to use it in.

WHAT WE DO: delayed correction between the rounds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four sentences from your round-1 log on the board, anonymous: one tense collapse, one missing because, one double-word slip, one agreement slip.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs: fix all four, then hands up; repair together fast.
  3. One drama replay: the class delivers the opinion line chorally, properly: It was the BEST match EVer — because we WON!
  4. Announce the round-2 delta: Same story, new partner — and I listen only for the background line and the because.
📺 On screen: four faulty sentences in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: fix, drill the line once, reset. Expect recognition laughs at «it was good» — anonymity keeps it safe

🎯 Speaking out · round 2
new pairs
Why: Same task, new partner, higher bar: the improvement is audible within one lesson, which is the cheapest motivation there is.

WHAT WE DO: same story, new listener, audible improvement

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: stand, mix, new partner, sit.
  2. Round 2 — 90 seconds each way, same story, better: the background line and the because are non-negotiable.
  3. Two volunteers perform their best 30 seconds; the class reacts ONLY with opinion phrases: So cool! · The best ever!
  4. Praise the delta by name: Round one: «We win. Good.» Round two: «We were losing two-nil when Dima scored…» — THAT is a story.
📺 On screen: the menu stays up; performing pairs stand where everyone sees them — the room is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: re-tell the story better, react with phrases. Expect audibly longer stories — say so out loud. Typical leftover: because-dropping under speed; one reminder, not a correction

Structure
We got a coach to the volleyball match. · Our coach trains us on Fridays. · Manchester City were winning for most of the game — then Real Madrid scored twice. · It was the best match ever. · I really enjoyed it because it was my first time.
Form
one spelling, two meanings — context decides (a noun after a/the vs a verb after a subject); past event shape: past simple = the events, past continuous = the background, opinion phrase + because = the ending.
Pron. watch
coach /kəʊtʃ/ · trained /treɪnd/ one syllable · the stress of the opinion phrases: it was the BEST match EVer · athletics /æθˈletɪks/ (three syllables, th!)
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I got a coach to the match» — a person or a bus? → a bus: you GET (take) a coach. «Our coach trains us» — person or bus? → a person: it does something.
«These trainers don't fit» — are they unhealthy or the wrong size? → the wrong size; fit the verb is about size, fit the adjective is about health.
«City were winning… then Real scored» — which tense carries the main event? → past simple (scored); the continuous is the film running behind it.

Assumptions: SB p.26 (bottom)–27, syllabus lesson 12. The past continuous and its questions are one lesson old — the opening clinic recycles them. Of the eight double words the class owns one meaning each at best (match = матч, trainer = кроссовки maybe); the second meanings and the speaking phrases are effectively new. Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«He is my trainer» for the person who trains the teamThe false-friend wall: TRAINER = the shoe, COACH = the person (and the bus!). Draw the shoe. Every slip — point at the shoe, silently.
The story arrives with no tenses («Match. We win. Good.»)The five-question plan is compulsory, and the menu is on the board: events in past simple, background in continuous, one opinion phrase, one because. Hold up two fingers for the missing because.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
