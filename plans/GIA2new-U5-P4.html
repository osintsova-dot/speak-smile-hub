🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The place vocabulary is given in groups by function (where you eat / where you do sport / where the arts are) — twelve words go in more easily that way than as a list. For newcomers the listening is broken into three runs with a different task each time, and the compulsory step is predicting the words BEFORE the recording (that is the subskill of the unit, and it genuinely saves them). The product is six lines about their own town from a frame, with the sheet in hand.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Mia is moving to Buenos Aires for a year. She's never been. She has one phone call with her cousin Ethan to find out if the city is any good. We'll listen to that call — but first we learn the twelve places she asks about, and then we do the trick that makes listening easy: we write down the words we EXPECT to hear. At the end, each of you describes our town in six lines. First, the map on page 65…”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Mia is moving to Buenos Aires and rings her cousin to find out whether it is a good city. First we learn the 12 places, then we use the trick that saves you in a listening: we write down the words we expect to hear. At the end — six lines about our own town.

WHAT WE DO: 12 places in three portions by function (ex.2) → then the whole map (ex.1)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols: every place gets its own gesture. I show the gesture, the class repeats it chorally with the word, then I show only the gesture — they say the word.
  2. Portion 1 — where you eat: fast-food restaurant — burger! · juice bar — shake it! · department store — many floors!
  3. Portion 2 — where you do sport: skate park — roll! · sports centre — jump! · cycle lane — pedal!
  4. Portion 3 — where the arts are: library — shh! · museum — look! · music venue — loud! And transport: bus stop · underground station · bike station.
  5. ex.1 on the map (TB key: 1 bike station · 2 skate park · 3 library · 4 cycle lane · 5 fast-food restaurant · 6 underground station · 7 music venue · 8 sports centre · 9 bus stop · 10 juice bar · 11 department store · 12 museum).
  6. ex.2 for consolidation (TB key: 1 fast-food restaurant, juice bar, (department store) · 2 skate park, sports centre, (cycle lane) · 3 library, music venue, museum). TB tip: some places fit into more than one group.
📺 On screen: SB p.65 ex.1–2 · the word bank on the screen (backup — the word bank in big letters on the board, not to be wiped until the end of the pair)

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 the two word-builders with the poster → ex.4 we describe a place and the partner guesses

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

First we write the definition, then Kagan RallyRobin: in the pair, strictly in turn — one reads their definition, the other guesses the place and reads theirs straight away. PRODUCT · my town (6 lines)
solo → crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: the ladder: notes on the four topics → 6 lines from the frame → reading in the crew → the stage with the sheet

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1 (ex.9): four topics — places to do sports · transport · places to eat · places to go out. Notes, single words.
  2. Step 2 — the frame: In our town there's a … . There are two … . There aren't any … . The best way to get around is by … . I like … because it's … .
  3. Step 3 — Kagan RoundRobin: round the crew clockwise, everyone reads their six lines off the sheet; the crew helps with spelling only once the circle is closed.
  4. Step 4: the crew comes out together, the speaker holds the sheet. The audience: Thumbs up if it's true for you too!
  5. I praise concretely: Clear numbers! Nice place! — no error correction at the board.
📺 On screen: the frame · word bank · timer
👦 Students do: they write and then speak — the fear is gone. Expect: everyone has 6 lines

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 12 places in a town, build compound names (… station / … centre) and describe their own town in 6 lines; they can predict vocabulary before a listening.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the map with the 12 places, with a prompt sheet; ex.2 three groups by function; ex.3 two word-builders; ex.4 guess the place from the description; ex.5 word prediction; track 33 in three runs (ex.6, 7, 8); ex.9 the pros and cons of their own town.
  • Personal. I give the words in groups, not as a list. I only press play once the word prediction is written. Every answer goes down in writing first, then out loud.
By the end, students can…
  • name 12 places in a town
  • build compound names with station and centre
  • write down words I expect to hear before listening
  • describe my town in 6 sentences

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
are bus stop and bus station the same thing? → no (TB note): a stop is the little one, a station is the big one.
Where do you go if you are ill? → medical centre. To fill up the car? → petrol station.
Why write the words down BEFORE listening? → so you recognise them in the flow.

Assumptions: SB p.65, KTP pair 41. Museum and library are familiar; new ones — juice bar, music venue, cycle lane, bike station, department store, underground station.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
12 words at once will not fitI give them in three portions by function (food / sport / the arts), with a gesture for each, and only then the whole map.
“I understood nothing in the listening”The word prediction in ex.5 is a compulsory step; without it I do not press play. Plus three runs with a different task each, and pauses.
The compound names turn to mushThe builder poster: six words on the left, STATION on the right; below, five words and CENTRE. It stays up until the end of the unit.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
