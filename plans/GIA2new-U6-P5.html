🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The topic is named openly: today we learn to say what we have already done. The rule is simple (+ -ed / didn't + infinitive), but with new students the spelling and the sound of the ending both break down, so the two layers are drilled separately and physically: four spelling gates and three sound baskets. The product is minimal and “about me”: five lines about their own last month, on a frame.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today: how to say what you DID. One ending — -ed — and the past is yours. Two rules: add -ed for what you did, and didn't plus the plain verb for what you didn't. Careful with spelling: stop becomes stopped with two p's, study becomes studied. And listen — the same -ed has three different sounds. At the end you write five lines about YOUR last month: three things you did, two you didn't.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the topic straight out: how to say what you did. One ending, -ed, and didn't for the negative. We drill the spelling and the three sounds of the ending separately. At the end — five lines about your own last month.

WHAT WE DO: the p.81 rules as four gates → ex.2, eight verbs → a team spelling game (TB routine)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four gates on the board: + -ed (visit → visited) · + -d (receive → received) · consonant + y → -ied (carry → carried) · double the consonant (stop → stopped, travel → travelled).
  2. Every verb “goes through” a gate with a finger before it is written down.
  3. ex.2 in writing, then check it (TB key: finished, liked, organised, played, studied, travelled, visited, watched).
  4. The TB game through Kagan Showdown: do the first card aloud yourself — show which gate the verb goes through and write the form on your own board. Then hold up a card with the infinitive — everyone writes the form silently on their own mini-whiteboard and on Showdown! they all show together; if the crew matches, a point, if not, the turn and the point go to the next crew.
📺 On screen: SB p.78 ex.2 + Spelling rules p.81 · verb cards · mini-whiteboards
👦 Students do: sort the verbs by gate. Expect: travel → travelled needs a separate explanation (the British form)

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can form the past simple of regular verbs (spelling included), make negatives with didn't + infinitive, and write 5 sentences about their own last month with time expressions.
  • Subsidiary. The ex.1 rules are given straight; the p.81 spelling as four gates; ex.2 in writing with a self-check; ex.3 affirmative/negative; ex.4 their own sentences; the Irregular verbs box; Pronunciation p.117 (tracks 72–75); Grammar round-up ex.5 in part (the first four items).
  • Personal. Name the grammar as the topic. Show the didn't + bare verb rule with the TB routine — cross the -ed out in chalk. The mini-test with self-check comes before the product, so the mistake is found privately.
By the end, students can…
  • add -ed correctly (visit, receive, carry, stop)
  • use didn't + plain verb for the negative
  • hear the difference between /d/, /t/ and /ɪd/
  • write 5 sentences about my last month with dates

Structure
She started to sell the drink. · She visited the White House. · She didn't like bees. · She didn't stop there. · yesterday · last night, last Thursday, last weekend · in August, in 2017
Form
affirmative form: verb + -ed (TB key ex.1: -ed) · negative form: didn't + infinitive WITHOUT -ed (TB key ex.1: didn't) · most verbs + -ed: visit ➞ visited · verbs in -e ➞ + -d: receive ➞ received · consonant + -y ➞ y + -ied: carry ➞ carried · consonant-vowel-consonant ➞ double the last consonant + -ed: stop ➞ stopped, travel ➞ travelled · irregular: go ➞ went · see ➞ saw · have ➞ had · meet ➞ met (Irregular verbs box, SB p.78)
Pron. watch
SB p.117: three ways to say -ed — /d/ (played, listened, received, travelled) · /t/ (watched, finished, helped, stopped) · /ɪd/ (visited, decided, needed, started). TB key for ex.3: /ɪd/ comes after the letters t and d.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«She didn't visited the park» — what is one word too many? → -ed: didn't VISIT (didn't already shows the past).
«He stoped» — how many p? → stopped: consonant-vowel-consonant → we double it.
«I studyed» — correct it. → studied: consonant + y → -ied.

Assumptions: SB p.78 + Pronunciation p.117, syllabus pair 50. The form, the spelling and the sound are all new — hence three separate layers and a minimum of volume.

Likely problemWhat you do
didn't visited — the commonest mistakeTB routine: write “She didn't visited” on the board and cross the -ed out in chalk. Say the rule chorally. After that, a stop-gesture every time it happens.
Spelling (stoped, studyed, traveled)Four gates on the board: + -ed · + -d · y → -ied · double the consonant. Every verb goes through a gate with a finger before it is written down.
Every ending is read as /ɪd/Three physical baskets /d/ /t/ /ɪd/ on the desks; the rule is discovered from the TB key — /ɪd/ only after t and d.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
