🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Split Russian «смотреть» into look (at), see and watch, follow a six-question podcast interview with exam discipline, and react with the surprise-and-disbelief phrases (You're joking! · No way! · I find that hard to believe).
What is genuinely hard
look/see/watch is a concept split with no home-language anchor — the decision (effort? movement? duration?) has to run before every verb. And in the listening, the six options are all MENTIONED — the discipline is matching meaning, not spotting words.
Typical error to expect
I watched a famous actor in the street / let's see TV — look (at), see and watch swapped; Russian «смотреть» covers all three [A2/B1]
An opinion with no reason attached — I think e-books are better full stop — scores as no opinion at all in the speaking [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The verb trio opens the lesson because the podcast and the speaking both feed on it. The p.125 mime pairwork makes the distinction physical — you can SEE a mime, WATCH a mime, or LOOK AT a frozen one, and the guessing forces the exact verb. The podcast runs three passes (predict → gist → six choices), and the plan is honest about its one gap: the answer letters are not in the course notes, so the teacher checks the printed TB before class. The speaking phrases are drilled WITH ATTITUDE — flat disbelief is no disbelief — and the No Way! quiz pays everything off: five facts too strange to believe, phrases compulsory, answers verified in the students' own books on p.127.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“In 1911 the Mona Lisa was stolen — and the police arrested a poet. True. Today you get the English for exactly that feeling: You're JOKING. No WAY. And a university magician who won't tell anyone how the tricks work.”

WHAT WE DO: name the aim and board the three-question test

WHAT WE DO: write two sentences per stem, mime one, partner guesses EXACTLY

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set up from the book: SB p.125 pairwork: write two sentences for each of «You're looking at… / You've just seen… / You're watching…» from the box (a famous actor in the street · someone steal a car · a fly in your meal · a concert poster · pictures in a recipe book · a soap opera · an exciting football match · an eagle in the sky · a clip from your favourite show · a school notice about exam results). Then mime one — the partner guesses the sentence EXACTLY, verb and all.
  2. Writing phase, 4 minutes, pairs pick from the box — six sentences per pair across the three stems.
  3. Mime phase, Kagan RallyCoach energy: one mimes, one guesses — and the guess must be the EXACT sentence, verb included. «You're watching an exciting football match!» — not «football».
  4. Two best mimes replayed for the class; the class guesses chorally.
📺 On screen: the pairwork box is in every book on p.125 — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write, mime, guess verb-exactly. Expect the fly-in-your-meal mime to become legend. Typical error: guessing «you see a match» for a long two-handed WATCH mime — the test settles it

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3: the photo, the Mark/Lucy dialogue, the phrase box drilled hot

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The photo first: The speaking photo: a man apparently levitating against a fence — How would you feel if you saw this? Collect reactions in any English — then upgrade them with the box.
  2. Play track 97 (ex.2–3): who doesn't believe whom — check against your pencilled margin note; then tick which box phrases Mark and Lucy actually use.
  3. Drill the seven phrases WITH ATTITUDE, chorally then in pairs: Prepare to speak (Expressing surprise and disbelief): You won't believe this, but… · I find that hard to believe. · You're joking! · Are you serious? · No way! · I can't/couldn't believe my eyes. · That just isn't possible. — eyebrows up, pitch jump, the WAY stressed.

👦 Students do: react, tick, drill hot. Expect self-consciousness for one round, then delight — attitude is permission to be loud legally. Typical error: Are you serious. — falling intonation; bounce it

  • Glue the map in — here the intonation matters as much as the words.

pairs → class
Why: 🎯 The No Way! quiz

WHAT WE DO: ex.4–5: five impossible facts, phrases compulsory, answers on p.127

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the game: The No Way! quiz (ex.4): 1 which two events happened in the same year (Eiffel Tower finished / contact lenses invented / Nintendo started)? · 2 which law is true in parts of Australia? · 3 who did the police suspect when the Mona Lisa was stolen in 1911? · 4 what would paper folded 42 times reach? · 5 who was born in the same year? Page 127 stays CLOSED until the word.
  2. Pairs argue each item with the phrases compulsory — a listener's finger goes up per phrase heard: A pet rabbit only if you're a MAGICIAN? That just isn't possible! 8 minutes, all five items, commit answers on paper.
  3. Class vote item by item, THEN the reveal: books open to p.127. The answers: quiz answers (SB p.127 — in the students' own books): 1 A and C — 1889: the Eiffel Tower finished AND Nintendo started (as a card-game company); contact lenses were sketched by da Vinci ~1508 and produced in Germany in 1888 · 2 A — in Queensland you may keep a pet rabbit only if you are a magician (B is true in parts of the USA, C was true in Switzerland until 2017) · 3 A and C — the poet Guillaume Apollinaire was arrested first, and Picasso was questioned too · 4 C — 42 folds would in theory reach the moon (nobody can fold past about seven) · 5 A and B — Marilyn Monroe and Queen Elizabeth II, both 1926 (Pelé is 1940)
  4. Debrief the feeling in English: Which answer got the loudest NO WAY out of you? Say it with a brick: it never crossed my mind that…
  5. You log quietly: flat reactions and look/see/watch slips for the review lesson.
📺 On screen: the quiz and its answers are in every book (p.115 + p.127) — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: argue, commit, react at the reveal. Expect the paper-folding fact to be tested at desks with actual paper — allow ninety seconds of science. Typical error: reactions in Russian at the reveal — bounce them into the phrases, that's the game

Target language
Lexis
look (at) — pay attention, short time, not movingsee — notice with your eyeswatch — pay attention for longer, something moving
Structure
Look at this picture. · I haven't seen your brother for ages. · The final was watched by over a billion people. · You won't believe this, but… · I couldn't believe my eyes. · That just isn't possible.
Form
SEE = it enters your eyes (no effort) · LOOK AT = you point your eyes at something still · WATCH = you follow something that moves or lasts. Russian «смотреть» covers all three — that is the whole problem.
Pron. watch
believe /bɪˈliːv/ · You're JOKing — stress the JOK · No WAY — both words hit · seriously /ˈsɪəriəsli/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I saw a spider» — did I try to find it? → no: it entered my eyes. «I was watching a spider» — effort? → yes, and it moves.
«Look at the board» — for an hour? → no: short, and the board doesn't move.
«The final was watched by a billion people» — who is the hero of the sentence, the people or the final? → the final: yesterday's passive, still working.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
look/see/watch chosen by translationThe three-question test on the board all lesson: Effort? Moving? Long? — pointed at silently, never re-explained.
The listening options all sound «mentioned»Name the trap before play 1: all three options are IN the recording. The question asks which one ANSWERS it. Justify two items after play 2.
Phrases delivered flat («no way.»)Drill with attitude explicitly: eyebrows, pitch jump, the WAY stressed. Flat delivery gets a theatrical «I don't believe your disbelief» and a second try.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
