👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: we analyse the model paragraph by paragraph and build the map we'll use to write our own story

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.3 p.104 together, paragraph by paragraph: Paragraph 1 — introduction. Paragraph 2 — what happened at home and the first part of the journey. Paragraph 3 — the journey to the airport. Paragraph 4 — what happened at the airport. (key TB p.118).
  2. I draw the MAP on the board with four boxes — it stays up until the end of the pair and will support their own story.
  3. Useful language from the model on the board (TB p.118): My worst journey was when we went to … · We had our first problem … · Unfortunately, … · When we arrived, …
  4. Drama devices: Look — an exclamation mark! And here — someone SPEAKS: ‘We are sorry to inform you…’ That's what makes a story exciting.
📺 On screen: SB p.104 · the board: the MAP of 4 paragraphs + a phrase bank (stay up)
👦 Students do: they analyse the model paragraph by paragraph, see the structure. Expected: the map removes the fear of “what to write about”. Typical error: they mix up paragraphs 2 and 3 — I tie it to place (home → the road → the airport).

WHAT WE DO: the subskill step by step: we draw a time arrow, place events, work out the comma rule; ex.5–6

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ A living timeline: four students come up with event cards and STAND in a line in order; the class prompts who moves where. Only then do I draw the same arrow on the board (TB p.119): ← past … present →.
  2. Example 1: Before we arrived at the gate, we heard a message. I put two events on the arrow together with the class — the message comes earlier. Ex.5 key: 1 b.
  3. Example 2: After we arrived at the station, my dad noticed… → they arrived first. Ex.5 key: 2 a.
  4. The rule in big letters: BEFORE + event = it's SECOND. AFTER + event = it's FIRST. And: a COMMA at the start of the sentence.
  5. Ex.6 in pairs: find one more example each in the text and place it on the arrow.
📺 On screen: the board: the timeline + the rule + the comma rule (stay up) · SB pp.104–105
👦 Students do: they place events on the arrow together with the teacher, then look for examples in the text. Expected: the visualisation solves it — sharply fewer errors. Typical error: they read word order as event order — I bring them back to the arrow.

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: writing in a portion: notes on 4 events from the map → we write paragraph 1 (and 2, if there's time) → a Quick review blitz

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK, 2 min, from the bank of problems: A real journey or invent one. FOUR events. Notes only — words, not sentences.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYBODY writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift them all together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. PREPARE, 1 min: we lay the 4 events out on the paragraph map on the board.
  4. WRITE, 4 min: Paragraph one only: My best/worst journey was when we went to … Two or three sentences. Support: a card with ready-made starters for every paragraph.
  5. For fast finishers — start paragraph 2. Paragraphs three and four are homework — nobody writes 150 words in five minutes.
  6. ▶ Quick review p.105 in a blitz, 3 rounds on mini-boards: negative (In 1910, children ___ to school by bus) · a question from scrambled words (did / how / you / come ?) · superlative (good → ?, big → ?).
📺 On screen: the board: the paragraph map + phrase bank · mini-boards · timer
👦 Students do: they make notes and write the first paragraph from the support, then close three targets in one volley. Expected: 3 sentences from everybody. Typical error: they write in full sentences at the notes stage — I bring them back to words.

WHAT WE DO: STEP 1–2 step by step: we analyse the model survey → threes get roles and a template with two ready-made questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.2 from the model p.107: How many questions? Three. Is there space for answers? Yes. Past and present? Yes.

WHAT WE DO: Find Someone Who: everybody personally surveys 4 people from their own sheet — everybody moves and talks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rule (SB STEP 2): Each person asks FOUR other students. Write every answer.
  2. This is Kagan Find Someone Who: a mingle round the class with a sheet: A DIFFERENT classmate for every line — full question, full answer, then they sign! Finished — sit down; we check 2–3 finds aloud.
  3. ▶ Mingle survey, 6 min to music; Reinforcement (TB p.120): for the weaker ones — ask ONLY one question, but of all four.
  4. Stop signal: Freeze! How many answers? — we count aloud.
  5. Back into threes: 2 min to combine the numbers into one table (the Data collector role).
📺 On screen: a survey sheet for everybody · music · timer
👦 Students do: they walk around and survey their classmates. Expected: 12 answers per three. Typical error: they only ask their neighbour — the rule “4 different people”.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can order events with before and after and have written the plan and the first paragraphs of a story about a journey, and have run and presented a class transport survey in threes.
  • Subsidiary. A soft cumulative rewind of U5–U8 before Unit 9; the model story is analysed paragraph by paragraph; Quick review p.105 in a blitz; the project step by step STEP 1–4 with roles and a survey template; Peer review of 2 questions; Final reflection with emojis.
  • Personal. The pair is packed — keep to the timers and don't chase volume. In class — a plan + 1–2 paragraphs, the rest at home. In the project everybody has their own role and their own sheet, or two watch while one writes.
By the end, students can…
  • say which event happened first in a before/after sentence
  • put a comma after a before/after clause at the start
  • write the plan and paragraphs 1–2 of a journey story
  • ask 1–3 survey questions and present a chart with a simple conclusion

Structure
Before we arrived at the gate, we heard a message. · After we arrived at the station, my dad noticed his passport. · How do you come to school? · How did you travel on holiday last year? · Most students come by bus.
Form
before/after + event; at the start of the sentence — a COMMA; in the middle — none. The story is past simple. The survey's conclusion — the most popular / more … than …
Pron. watch
expressive reading: exclamations in the story · a presentation — we speak to the whole class.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Before we arrived, we heard a message — what happened first?” → the message.
“After I had breakfast, I went out — do we need a comma?” → Yes, after comes first.
“How many people does everybody survey?” → Four.

Assumptions: Past simple (all forms) — from Pairs 2–3, still needs support. Superlatives — from yesterday's pair. The “story” genre is new, but the model in the book is strong and detailed.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
