Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to ask & answer about quantities of ingredients — How many (nuts) do they need? — They need a few nuts. / How much (sugar) does he need? — He needs a lot of sugar. — choosing quantifiers by countable / uncountable.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle Unit 1 ingredients (retrieval starter); sort countable vs uncountable nouns; listen for detail (dialogue → which ingredients); collaborative speaking (Talk Partners, mingle, team relay); handwrite the two question forms.
  • Personal. Run I Do → We Do → You Do with one clear think-aloud; graduate the practice so the scaffold fades game by game; ask a CCQ before each task; keep teacher-talk low.
By the end, pupils can…
  • ask How many…? for things they can count and How much…? for things they can't;
  • pick a few for countable and a little for uncountable nouns;
  • answer with a lot of / some / a few / a little + ingredient.
Target language
Lexis (revised)
Count it: nutsbiscuitschocolate chipsraisins · Measure it: flouroilsugarsaltjambuttercoconutcream
Structure
How many (nuts) do they need? — They need a few (nuts).  ·  How much (sugar) does he need? — He needs a lot of (sugar).
Form
How many + plural countable + do/does + subj + need?  ·  How much + uncountable + do/does…?  ·  a few + count-plural / a little + uncount / a lot of · some + both. need (I/you/we/they) → needs (he/she).
Pron. watch
weak /ə/: a lot of /ə ˈlɒt əv/, a few /ə ˈfjuː/, a little /ə ˈlɪtl/; stress on HOW + the noun; the -s in needs.
Phonics tie-in
Light only (full i_e–ie set comes in L5): keep the weak /ə/ of the quantifiers honest in every drill — “a lot OF sugar”, not “a lot OFF”.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Can I count nuts? One nut, two nuts…” → yes → How many / a few.
“Can I count sugar? One sugar, two sugars?” → no → How much / a little.
“Is a few a lot or a little?” → not much / small amount.

Assumptions: the 12 ingredients were met in Lesson 1; some / a lot of and Is there any…? are familiar from the L1 song; pupils read short sentences; Talk Partners & team routines are known. Группы: команда — максимум 4; при другой численности делим по 3–4 (12=3×4 · 10=3+3+4 · 9=3×3 · 8=2×4 · 7=3+4).

0–4′
Opening · retrieval
carpet, teams standing

Do-now retrieval (Rosenshine daily review). Greet, split into 3 teams. Flash the 12 ingredient cards fast: Team 1 — name it! Team 2 — next! Quick-fire round the teams, ~15 sec each miss = pass.

Prime the concept — everyone moves. Hold up a card: Can you count it? Nuts — hands UP! Sugar — hands DOWN! Run 8 cards fast — every child shows count (up) vs measure (down) every time. This is today's grammar, already in their bodies.

6–12′
Model · dialogue
board / desks

I DO — present in context. PB p.9, ex 1 — Listen and read. Listen and repeat. Act out. (CD1 13) Luke & Lily plan the Burfi cake. Play once, books open.

Think-aloud (mark the model). Stop on two lines: Lily asks “How MUCH sugar…” — sugar, I can't count it, so “How much”. Then “How MANY nuts…” — nuts, I can count them, so “How many”. Underline both on the board.

Check meaning — PB ex 2. Read again. Which ingredients do Luke and Lily need? (→ sugar, nuts, milk, coconut — not salt, oil). Choral repeat + 2–3 pairs act out 4 lines.

12–18′
Clarify · grammar box
board → carpet sort

WE DO — build the rule together. PB p.9, ex 3 Quantifiers table + Grammar clue: How many = things we CAN count. How much = things we CAN'T count. Choral drill the table (I/you/we/they need · he/she needs).

CCQs. Can I count biscuits? … So — how many or how much? · Can I count flour? … So — a few or a little?

▶ Board sort — COUNT IT / MEASURE IT. Two columns on the board. Hold up an ingredient card: whole class points L or R and calls the pair — Biscuits — COUNT — a few! / Butter — MEASURE — a little! A rotating child sticks it in the right column. All 12, fast.

18–23′
📓 Interactive notebook
desks, pairs

📓 Interactive notebook — “COUNT IT OR MEASURE IT?” card (A5). Two columns: COUNT IT → HOW MANY? (a lot of · some · a few) with model How many nuts do they need? — They need a few nuts. · MEASURE IT → HOW MUCH? (a lot of · some · a little) with How much sugar does he need? — He needs a lot of sugar.

Glue the card in; pupils trace HOW MANY and HOW MUCH in two colours. Sort 5 ingredient cards from Lesson 1 into COUNT / MEASURE on the card. Then say the two model sentences with a partner, swapping the ingredient each time.

ICQ: Do we write “a few” or “a little” with nuts? (→ a few). The card stays open — it's the reference for the games.

23–31′
Controlled · game
carpet, teams

YOU DO (supported). ▶ Tic-Tac-Toe · quantity — the L2 signature game. 9 A4 ingredient cards laid 3×3 on the carpet (each card = one cell); teams play ✕ vs ⭕ with big chips on top.

Rule (Oksana's format): you claim a cell only by saying a full correct sentence about that ingredient — We need a few chocolate chips. or a question How much flour do we need? A single word or a wrong quantifier = no cell, other team's turn.

Notebook card stays open as reference. First team to 3-in-a-row wins the round; re-deal & play best of 3. Full sentence, please! = your two-finger signal.

 9 A4 ingredient cards · ✕/⭕ chips · notebook card (reference)
31–39′
Freer · mingle
open space, all standing

YOU DO (scaffold fades — card, no notebook). ▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (Kagan). Every pupil holds one ingredient card. Stand up, hand up, pair up!

In each pair: A quizzes — How much / How many [coconut] do you need? B answers with a quantifier — I need a little coconut. Swap roles, praise, trade cards, find a new partner. Everyone talks at once — no one waits.

Monitor for the two errors (a few/a little · How much/How many); note names for quick whole-class correction after.

 1 ingredient card per pupil · music (start/stop)
39–49′
Production · relay
carpet, teams in lines

YOU DO (production, no card). ▶ Boat Race — “The recipe relay”. Teams in lines; a spread of ingredient mini-cards in front of each line; a “recipe list” sheet on the board per team.

Runner 1 dashes, grabs a card and must produce a full exchange to add it: How much butter do we need? — We need some butter! Correct → card goes on the team list, tag next runner. Wrong quantifier → card back, try again next turn. First team with a complete 6-ingredient cake list wins.

Settle with Freeze! then a quick whole-class fix of the errors you noted in the mingle.
