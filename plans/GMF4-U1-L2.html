Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to ask about quantity: “How much (sugar) do we need? / How many (nuts) do we need?” and answer with quantifiers: “We need a lot of / some / a few (nuts). / a lot of / some / a little (sugar).”
  • Subsidiary. Understand Luke & Lily's dialogue about the Burfi cake (CD1 13); discover the rule — How many for things we can count, How much for things we can't; use a few (countable) vs a little (uncountable); recycle the 12 ingredients from L1; write questions & answers about a recipe (AB).
  • Personal. Drill the How much / How many contrast until it's automatic; keep a few (count) vs a little (uncount) visible; model the full answer, accept short answers and recast.
By the end, pupils can…
  • choose How much (uncount) vs How many (count) correctly;
  • answer with a lot of / some / a few / a little;
  • ask & answer about the ingredients we need for a cake.

Structure
How much (sugar) do we need? — We need a lot of sugar.  ·  How many (nuts) do we need? — We need a few nuts.  ·  He needs a little (cream).
Form
How many + plural countable? · How much + uncountable? Quantifiers: countable → a lot of / some / a few · uncountable → a lot of / some / a little. I/you/we/they need · he/she needs.
Pron. watch
weak do we /djəwi/ at speed; a few /əˈfjuː/ vs a little /əˈlɪtl/; rising-then-falling How-question ↘.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“How ___ sugar? — can I count sugar?” → no → How MUCH.
“a few or a little nuts — which one?” → a FEW (I can count nuts).

Assumptions: 12 ingredients are fresh from L1 (checked in warm-up), sorted countable/uncountable. How much/many met before, but choosing correctly + a few/a little is the new focus. Pupils can read the dialogue speech bubbles & the quantifiers table.

19–27′
Tic-Tac-Toe · quantity
desks, 2 teams

▶ Noughts & Crosses — ingredients. 3×3 grid of ingredient pictures. To claim a cell, the team ASKS the other team the right question — How many raisins do you need? — and the other team answers with a quantifier: We need a few raisins! Both sides speak every turn. Rotate the asker.

🎮 Projector/board option: draw the 3×3 grid on the whiteboard; a rotating child taps the winning cell as the WHOLE team says the question aloud.

ICQ: For nuts — a few or a little? (→ a few!)

27–37′
Recipe info-gap
desks, pairs

Model first. Show two half-recipes (A knows the sugar & nuts, B knows the flour & raisins). Model publicly: How much flour do we need? — We need a lot of flour.

▶ Talk Partners info-gap (act. 4-style). Pairs ask & answer to complete the missing amounts — How many nuts? How much cream? Both write. 5–6 exchanges.

✔ Assess: circulate with the class list — who chooses much/many + a few/a little correctly? Recast, note names.

💪 Support: question stems printed on the half-sheet. ⭐ Extension: add: Do we need any biscuits? — No, we don't.

How much / How many chain
carpet, circle

▶ Numbered Heads Q&A. Teams numbered 1–4. You (then a pupil) draw an ingredient card: How much/many … do we need? Teams huddle 10 sec; you call a number — that child answers the full sentence with a quantifier. Point per correct answer. The number is random — everyone must be ready.

ICQ: Do you shout the answer or wait for your number? (→ wait!)
