🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): For new students the mission is simple and personal: learn to name types of holiday, places to stay and facilities — and describe your own dream holiday. That gives a safe product (I talk about myself, from a frame, with notes in my hand) and brings in the three lexical sets of the unit. The video “Cool summer holidays” is not a task but a model: you can see how simple words add up to a whole story about a holiday.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at page 96. Seven photos. Ten nights in August — ‘Have fun in the sun’. Two nights in April — ‘The world's best rides’. Somebody wrote those words to sell a holiday. Today two things: we learn to name every kind of holiday, every place you stay and everything a place can offer — and each of you tells us about YOUR dream holiday, six lines. At the end you speak with your notes in your hand. This is not a test. Let's start with MY dream holiday…” (and you show the model straight away)
The gist, if you need to restate it: p.96 has seven holiday photos. We learn to name types of holiday, places to stay and facilities, and then everyone describes their own dream holiday — six lines. At the end we speak with our notes in our hands.

Warm-up · seven photos from a model
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: model before the task: my holiday on the board → ex.1 seven types with gestures → three columns

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. MODEL: tell them about YOUR holiday: I'd like to go to the beach. I usually stay in an apartment. There's a swimming pool. I like it because it's quiet.
  2. Seven types with gestures: go camping — a tent! · go skiing — down the hill! · go to the beach — sun! · go on a city break — a short trip! · go on a walking holiday — boots! · go to a theme park resort — the rides! · go on a water sports holiday — splash!
  3. The captions on the photos help: 'Sleep under the stars' — which one? 'Put your boots on' — which one? (TB key ex.1: 1 go to the beach · 2 go skiing · 3 go camping · 4 go on a city break · 5 go on a walking holiday · 6 go to a theme park resort · 7 go on a water sports holiday).
  4. Fix the gestures with Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols: first you do the gesture and say the phrase yourself, the class repeats after you chorally twice round — and only then you drop your voice and leave just the gesture. You show a tent — the crew says go camping. The word is held by the body, not by memory.
  5. Three columns on the screen for good: go · go ON · go TO. Every phrase goes into a column out loud.
  6. Kagan RallyRobin: taking turns in pairs — one type of holiday and one word why.
📺 On screen: SB p.96 ex.1 · word bank and the three columns on screen (Backup: the word bank large on the board — do not rub it off before the end of the lesson)
👦 Students do: repeat the model, sort, take turns speaking. Expect: the gestures pull them in. Problem: “go to camping” — point at the column, don't explain in words
📓 Interactive notebook — Get_Involved_A2_Unit8_Vocabulary1_Holidays_Visual_Takeaway_A5
A5 glue-in sheet — BUILD YOUR DREAM HOLIDAY in three steps: 1 CHOOSE THE HOLIDAY (go camping · go on a city break · go on a walking holiday · go on a water sports holiday · go skiing · go to a theme park · go to the beach) · 2 WHERE TO STAY (an apartment · a B&B · a campsite · a country cottage · a hotel · a youth hostel) · 3 FACILITIES (bike hire · car park · games room · garden · picnic area · swimming pool), by the formula TYPE + PLACE + FACILITIES
  • Glue the sheet in — it is a holiday builder.
  • Assemble your own version along the three steps.
  • Say it aloud in one sentence, as in the model.
  • Mark which facilities are non-negotiable for you.
When: ~5 min at the start of the “Warm-up: seven photos from a model” stagePrep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Scissors, glue sticks, pencils.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 7 types of holiday, 6 places to stay and 7 facilities and describe a dream holiday in 6 lines (type + place + facilities + why).
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 seven photos (vocabulary track 52) with support; ex.3 six riddles about places to stay (vocabulary track 53); ex.4 which place suits which holiday; ex.6 seven icons (vocabulary track 54); ex.7 four descriptions; ex.8 the “My dream holiday” form; Video skills with a task set before watching.
  • Personal. Never call on anyone who has no draft. Every speaking task comes AFTER writing it down. The word bank and the three preposition columns stay on the screen all lesson.
By the end, students can…
  • say and write seven types of holiday
  • name six places to stay and say what each one is
  • read the facility icons and name seven facilities
  • describe my dream holiday in 6 sentences

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“B & B” — how is it said? → /ˌbiː ən ˈbiː/, and it's somebody's home with breakfast, not a hotel.
“go on the beach” or “go to the beach”? → go TO the beach.
“youth hostel” — how is it spelt? → y-o-u-t-h, one s in hostel.

Assumptions: SB pp.96–97, syllabus pair 62. beach, hotel, camping are familiar; new are B & B, youth hostel, country cottage, bike hire, picnic area, games room, theme park resort.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Afraid to speak in front of people they don't knowThe rule of the lesson: write first, then read it to your partner, and come to the board with your notes in your hand.
Twenty words at once don't fitGive them in three portions: types of holiday → places to stay → facilities, with a gesture for each; recognition first (point at the photo), naming after.
Prepositions in the holiday types get mixed upThe three columns go / go on / go to stay on the screen all lesson; students put each new phrase into a column themselves.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
