🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Choose correctly between say, speak, talk and tell — four English verbs for one Russian «сказать/говорить» — and write a ~100-word online book review with a title, an author, a character, an opinion and NO spoilers.
What is genuinely hard
The verb set is a concept problem, not a memory problem: each verb owns a grammar pattern (speak a LANGUAGE, say WORDS, tell a PERSON, talk ABOUT/TO). And in the writing, the hard part is restraint — students retell the whole plot; a review needs a taste, not the meal.
Typical error to expect
Can you say me the way? / He talked me the truth — one Russian verb spread over say, speak, talk and tell, each with its own grammar [A2/B1]
brochure · advert(isement) · imagination · concentration · believe — the block's misspelling traps; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
Why the lesson is built this way
The verb work comes first and is loud (Showdown + pairwork interview), because the writing half needs quiet — the lesson spends its energy budget early. Sachin's model review is read as a reader first (would you read this book?), then mined as a writer: the tick-list of what he includes IS the checklist students write against. The no-spoilers rule gets its own minute because it is the genre's one law. Planning is five questions on one line each — no plan, no pen. Peer review against the same checklist closes the loop, and the printed-vs-digital poll from lesson 1 gets its payoff: reviews live ONLINE.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“You choose books by reviews — admit it. Two lines from a stranger and you buy or you don't. Today you learn to BE that stranger: a hundred words, no spoilers, and somebody somewhere picks up a book because of you.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a three-slip repair clinic from the gossip chain, and the bridge into the four-verb problem

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — error clinic from the gossip chain. Three anonymous finds from your lesson-2 log (an unshifted tense, a «said me», a lost pronoun). Kagan RallyRobin in pairs: fix all three, quote the rule.
  3. Celebrate the chain: Most reports survived two mouths intact. Journalists get paid for that.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): bridge: «He said me» is dead. But WHY said — why not «he talked me»? English has FOUR verbs where Russian has one. Page one hundred and ten, bottom half.
📺 On screen: three faulty sentences in chalk — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hunt, fix, quote the ladder. Expect «said me» to be found first — it is the loudest trap

pairs → class
Why: Vocabulary · say, speak, talk, tell

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 patterns + ex.2 choices, committed on whiteboards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: match the four verbs to their uses via the book's examples. Key: 1 speak — d a language · 2 say — c something · 3 tell — a someone something · 4 talk — b about something or to someone. Board the four formulas.
  2. Concept checks from the plan header — the tell-me one and the speak one, cold-called.
  3. ex.2 solo, 3 minutes, pair check — every answer must QUOTE its formula. Key: 1 say · 2 speak · 3 told · 4 talking · 5 said · 6 talk · 7 speak · 8 tell (verify against your printed TB).
  4. Kagan Showdown, six fired prompts: ___ me your name! · Can you ___ French? · We ___ about football. · Nobody ___ anything. Boards up on three.
  5. Bridge to yesterday: said and told from the ladder are the same two verbs — the patterns don't change when the speech is reported.
📺 On screen: the four formulas in chalk; Showdown prompts by voice — board and voice are the media, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: match, choose, commit. Expect item 8 (tell us the truth) to be solid and item 5 (nobody said anything) to wobble — say/words settles it. Typical error: speak me
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit19_Vocabulary2_Say_Speak_Talk_Tell_A5
A5 glue-in sheet — SAY · SPEAK · TALK · TELL: four verbs, four patterns — say something / say that… (She said hello.) · speak to someone, use a language (He speaks Spanish.) · talk to or with someone, talk about something (We talked about the book.) · tell someone something, tell someone that… (Tell me the answer.) — with the memory line SAY something BUT TELL someone and two mini contrasts (She said that she was tired. / She told me that she was tired.), plus the crossed-out «Say me» and «tell to me»
  • Glue the sheet in and highlight the line SAY something, TELL someone.
  • Mark the prepositions: speak TO, talk TO, talk ABOUT — tell takes none.
  • Say four sentences about today, one per verb.
  • Fix the two crossed-out forms out loud before you write anything.
When: ~5 min at the start of the “Vocabulary · say, speak, talk, tell” stagePrep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Scissors, glue sticks, pencils.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: read the model as readers, then tick what it includes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Gist hook before reading: Read Sachin's review and answer ONE question: do you want the book now — yes or no? 2 minutes silent reading, then a hands vote.
  2. Kagan Think-Pair-Share on WHY: what exactly made you want it (or not)? Collect: the danger, the spy, the «I won't write more».
  3. ex.2: tick the eight items — what does Sachin include? Key: Sachin includes: 1 title ✓ · 2 author's name ✓ · 4 main character ✓ · 6 part of the story ✓ · 8 his own opinion ✓ — NOT 3 (author info), 5 (whole story), 7 (the ending).
  4. Land the law on his own sentence: «I won't write more in case you read the book» — THAT is the whole genre. He sells the beginning and locks the end.
📺 On screen: Sachin's review is in every book on p.111 — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read for the yes/no, argue why, tick the list. Expect the vote to be mostly yes — the cliffhanger works, name the trick. Typical problem: «he doesn't say the story!» — exactly: he says ENOUGH

WHAT WE DO: ex.3–4: the Prepare-to-write box and the Artemis Fowl rebuild; ex.5: five planning questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3, the Prepare to write box read aloud by a student: Prepare to write (an online book review): include the title and the author · the name of the main character(s) · give your opinion · some details of the story — but avoid spoilers: never say how the book ends.
  2. ex.4 in pairs: rebuild the Artemis Fowl review with the box words. Key: Artemis Fowl review: 1 written · 2 author · 3 character · 4 incredible · 5 thought (rating 4/5; verify against your printed TB).
  3. ex.5 solo, silence, 5 minutes: choose YOUR book (any language — the review is in English) and answer the five questions one line each: title? author? main character? what happens (a taste)? your opinion?
  4. Walk and unstick: anyone blank gets a menu, not an answer — the last book you finished? a graphic novel? the book of a film you love?
  5. Plan check with the partner, 60 seconds: one question or one idea, no more.
📺 On screen: the five planning questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: rebuild, choose, plan in five lines. Expect two or three «I don't read» protests — the menu covers them (films have books; graphic novels count). Typical error: the plan already contains the ending — cross it out theatrically

WHAT WE DO: ex.6: write the review — about 100 words, checklist on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Eighteen minutes. About 100 words — 80 is the floor. Checklist on the board. Sachin stays open on the desk. Go.
  2. Visible timer on. First five minutes: whispered questions allowed. After that — silent mode, palm down.
  3. Walk with a pencil: underline (don't fix) one spelling and one verb-pattern slip per draft as you pass.
  4. Minute ten, quiet announcement: Check your opinion line — does it have a BECAUSE in it?
  5. Fast finishers: a one-line clickbait review of the same book — Five words that sell it.
📺 On screen: visible timer counting down 18 minutes (Backup: write the end time in the corner of the board and update every five minutes by hand)
👦 Students do: write, hit the floor, attach the because. Expect the middle to bulge with plot around minute eight — point silently at the NO ENDING line. Typical error: the author tell about… — the pencil catches it

Structure
Can you speak Turkish? · I said, 'Hello'. · I've told you the answer. · We talked about blogs. · I've just read… · The main character is… · I think it's a very exciting story, but I won't write more in case you read the book!
Form
Four verbs, four jobs: SPEAK a language · SAY words («I said, 'Hello'») · TELL a person something (tell needs a listener!) · TALK about a topic or to a person. The trap pair is say/tell: say + words, tell + person. Review shape: title + author → character → a taste of the story → opinion — never the ending.
Pron. watch
says /sez/ — not /seɪz/ · said /sed/ · character /ˈkærəktə/ — stress on the first syllable · author /ˈɔːθə/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Tell me the truth» — can I delete «me»? → no: tell needs a person. «Say me the truth» — broken for the same reason, the other way round.
«She speaks three languages» — is she saying anything right now? → not necessarily: speak = the ability or the act, languages are its object.
«I won't write more in case you read the book» — why does Sachin stop? → spoilers: a review sells the beginning, never the end.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
say/tell chosen by sound, not by patternThe object test again, now for four verbs: put the four patterns on the board as formulas (speak + language · say + words · tell + person · talk + about/to) and require the formula to be QUOTED with every answer in ex.2.
Reviews retell the whole plotThe one law, said twice and boarded: a review sells the beginning, never the end. Peer reviewers get «spoiler hunt» as their first job.
Weak writers freeze at 100 wordsThe floor is 80 words and Sachin's model stays open on the desk: copying the SHAPE is allowed and said out loud to be allowed.
Fast finishers at minute ten of writingThey write a second, one-line review of the same book — clickbait style — and we compare which sells better.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
