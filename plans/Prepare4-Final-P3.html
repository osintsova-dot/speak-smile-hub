🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Keep the whole year warm and spoken one lesson before the final paper: a topic carousel across the twenty units, the official Speaking bank Part 3 discussion task with its agreement language, and a team quiz over the year.
What is genuinely hard
Retrieval at speed across ten months of language — and discussion discipline: making suggestions, disagreeing politely and actually REACHING agreement instead of talking in circles.
Typical error to expect
A one-word reply does not count as an answer at all: an answer needs a verb in it [national exam report]
I've known her since a long time — for and since swapped after the right tense is finally chosen [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The syllabus marks this lesson revision-plus-reserve, and the design honours both: the first half is retrieval (words, then grammar volleys built from the final paper's own task shapes — nothing decorative), the middle is the carousel that says every topic of the year out loud once, and the late block is the official Part 3 discussion task — chosen because it is the exam's closest cousin to the final paper's own paired task, so it warms up next lesson's finale without showing anyone the paper. The quiz ends the lesson as a group. Fifteen homework sentences from the error logs are the entry ticket to the grammar volleys.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Everything you can say now, you say today. Five partners, one official Cambridge discussion, ten questions over twenty units — and then there is nothing left between you and the paper except a good night's sleep.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls back the one thing that is needed today rather than everything that was ever taught.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, the fifteen sentences checked in pairs, and the promise that today covers tomorrow

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — fifteen-sentence check, Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade style in pairs: partners swap notebooks and read three of each other's homework sentences aloud; one compliment, one question.
  3. Collect the count by hands only: fifteen done? ten? Honest hands. No names, no scolding — the stations absorb the difference.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): frame the day: today the year comes back out loud. Tomorrow's paper contains nothing we do not say today.
📺 On screen: notebooks in hands — paper is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: swap, read, compliment. Expect most to have twelve-plus — the log format made the homework concrete

WHAT WE DO: Showdown volleys built from the paper's own task shapes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Showdown, four volleys of four, all in the final paper's shapes: (1) transformations — «Sit down!» said the teacher → the teacher ordered…? (2) the passive — people watch YouTube everywhere → YouTube…? (3) small systems — who/which, for/since, already/yet, as/than · (4) unless and the conditional.
  2. Boards up on three every volley; pairs coach between volleys; the fifteen homework sentences are open on desks as each student's personal cheat sheet.
  3. Anything that breaks class-wide goes on the corner of the board — it opens tomorrow's briefing.
📺 On screen: prompts by voice, whiteboards in hands — no backup needed
👦 Students do: commit, coach, feed the briefing corner. Expect unless to wobble again — ninety seconds of repair, then move

two circles
Why: Revision that sounds like conversation: every topic of the year comes back once, spoken rather than read — fluency kept warm one week before the paper.

WHAT WE DO: the year's topics, 90 seconds a face, five rotations

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Timed Pair Share in rotation, one prompt per turn: one prompt per rotation, all from the year: describing a friend (U1–2) · the best/worst job for you (U17–18) · a crime story in four sentences (U5) · my perfect place to live (U6) · have you ever…? (U9–10) · a book worth reading, no spoilers (U19) · a fact that made you say NO WAY (U20) · if you could change one school rule… (U18)
  2. 90 seconds each way, then the outer circle steps left; the prompt changes with the step.
  3. You orbit and enjoy; log ONLY task-7 shapes that break (the shift, the passive) — everything else is fluency and fluency is winning.
  4. Close the carousel with a public stat: five partners, ten minutes of your English each. That is the year, spoken.
📺 On screen: prompts called by voice between rotations — no backup needed
👦 Students do: talk to five faces, survive, warm up thoroughly. Expect the NO WAY prompt to be the loudest — Unit 20 is one week old

WHAT WE DO: the official Speaking bank discussion task, model first

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the task from the bank sheet: Speaking bank Part 3 — the paired discussion task («your friend's birthday is soon; talk about the presents and agree on the most suitable»): a picture sheet of options and the key language box (What about…? · I'm not sure · I don't agree because… · It's time to decide · We'll go for that one, then). Model dialogue on audio 07.
  2. Play the model (audio 07) once: do they talk about ALL the presents? What do they agree on? Tick the key-language box phrases as they land.
  3. Drill the six agreement chunks hot, with attitude.
  4. Pairs run the task in full, 3 minutes on the visible timer: all options touched, a decision said out loud. Then swap partners and run it again, faster, banning the phrase each pair leaned on hardest.
  5. Why this task today, for your own notes: Part 3 is the exam's closest cousin to the final test's own paired discussion (task 13), so today's practice quietly warms up next lesson's finale without showing anybody the paper.
📺 On screen: audio 07 in the whiteboard app (Backup: skip the model — read the two-line exchange yourself in two voices from the bank's key-language box and set the task from the printed sheet; the workshop stands on paper)
👦 Students do: listen for the phrases, drill, decide out loud twice. Expect round 2 to be sharply better — the ban forces range. Typical slip: deciding without discussing — the all-options rule catches it

The year in ten questions
teams
Why: The whole class back together for the finale, so the lesson ends as a group. Ten questions across twenty units is the fastest possible tour of what the year contained.

WHAT WE DO: the last quiz: ten questions across twenty units

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Teams of four, Kagan Numbered Heads Together: ten questions sweeping the year — a crime word from its definition · a food adjective mimed · a reported shift · a passive fact · a for/since call · a collocation half · a jobs suffix · a No Way fact · a say/tell trap · one free brag: tell us something you can say in English that is TRUE and surprising.
  2. Heads together twenty seconds, then the called number answers for the team.
  3. Score lightly, celebrate loudly; the last question's brags are the real prize — collect three of them.
📺 On screen: questions by voice, score in chalk — no backup needed
👦 Students do: confer, answer, brag once. Expect the brags to be the moment of the lesson — write the best one down for September

Form

Pron. watch
the agreement phrases with attitude — a flat «I don't agree» sounds rude; the because saves it
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I don't agree with you because…» — what makes this polite? → the because: disagreement with a reason is discussion, without one it is just no.
«It's time to decide» — who says it and when? → anyone, when two minutes are nearly gone: reaching agreement is PART of the task, not a defeat.
«We'll go for that one, then» — is the discussion over? → yes, and saying so out loud is what the examiner is listening for.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Carousel answers shrink to one word by partner threeThe rescue frames stay on the board (Well, let me think… · Actually,…) and the rule holds: an answer needs a verb and a detail. Partners raise a finger per detail heard.
Part 3 pairs agree instantly to be done («the book. OK.»)The task's own rule from the bank: talk about ALL the options before deciding. A pair that decides in thirty seconds gets the follow-up: convince me — why not the concert tickets?
Grammar volleys expose a class-wide gap the day before the paperGood — that is the point of running them today: ninety seconds of repair on the spot, and the shape goes into tomorrow's briefing as the first named trap.
Energy spikes into chaos in the carouselThe bell (or a clap pattern) rotates the circle; the prompts change every rotation so momentum belongs to the structure, not to the noise.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the OGE — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Speaking tasks 1–3 of the OGE: read aloud (2 marks), six interview questions (6 marks), a monologue from a plan (7 marks).

Today's carousel and the B1-format paired discussion train the OGE's real bottleneck — keeping English coming for two minutes at a time. Note the difference for the two or three who will sit it: the OGE speaking has no partner and no live examiner; the questions come from a recording. Say it to them quietly, not to the class.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
