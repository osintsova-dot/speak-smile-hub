Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /w/ sound (Why is the white whale wearing a watch? — “wh says w”), talk about activities in Hawaii, and read & plan a magazine article — three mini-blocks, one “amazing sea” thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that w and wh spell the same /w/ sound (recognise → say → sort); culture — activities in Hawaii (watch the whales · surf · snorkel · sandcastle) + Channel 21 video, 21C digital skills: using key words to find information online; literacy — text type a magazine article (Amazing Animals), and to add information, plan an article.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (chant/wave bridges); the whale twister is play; cap each block hard so the literacy plan actually gets written.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the “white whale” /w/ twister and sort w / wh words;
  • name 2–3 Hawaii activities and compare with ours;
  • find an amazing fact in an article and plan one with a partner.

Structure
Why is the white whale wearing a watch?  ·  In Hawaii you can (learn to surf / watch the whales).  ·  article: A blue whale is about 30 metres long. They eat (krill).
Form
/w/ = w (water, window, watch) AND wh (why, white, whale — the h is silent!). Article: use and to join facts — They're different sizes and different colours.
Pron. watch
/w/ round lips; whale /weɪl/, why /waɪ/ — silent h in wh; watch /wɒtʃ/; contrast with /v/ (not a Russian В!).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“whale and water — same first SOUND?” → yes, /w/! (wh and w).
“In why — do we say the h?” → no, it's silent — /waɪ/.
“A magazine article — for scientists, babies or children?” → children — it's fun & amazing!

Assumptions: Sea animals, comparatives & the 6 adjectives are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from earlier phonics is enough for the w / wh noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short article texts with support.

Block 1 · Phonics /w/
carpet → desks

Speak and spell (act. 1, CD2 46). PB p.55: “wh” says “w”. Why is the white whale wearing a watch? Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort w / wh. Two hoops/columns: w (water, window, watch, weather) · wh (why, white, whale, what). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card (same sound, different spelling!).

Notice the rule. Listen — whale, water… same /w/ sound! But in “why”, do we hear the h? No — it's silent! (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more /w/ word to a hoop (e.g. wet, wheel, week).

✔ Assess: who says /v/ for /w/? Note them — extra lip-rounding drill next unit.

39–52′
Block 3 · Literacy · a magazine article
carpet → desks, pairs

Before you read (act. 1–3, CD2 48). PB p.56 «Amazing Animals» (blue whale · seahorse · spider crab): Look at the photos. Find an amazing fact for each animal. Read/listen.

▶ Amazing-fact scavenger hunt. Race in pairs to find on the article: Which animal is 30 metres long? Which hasn't got a stomach? Which has got a hard shell? First hands up read the answer aloud — everyone scans.

Investigate a text (PB p.57, CD3 01). «Super starfish»: which questions does it answer (How big? What does it eat? Why is it amazing?). Writing tip: Use “and” to join facts — They're different sizes AND different colours!

▶ Plan a magazine article (Cooperative learning, AB p.54 format). Pairs choose a sea animal and make notes: Animal name · How big? · What does it look like? · What does it eat? · Why is it amazing? 2 pairs share one amazing fact aloud.

💪 Support: fill the plan template with word support. ⭐ Extension: write the full article using and + an amazing fact: Do you know that…?

📓 Interactive notebook — GMF3_Unit5_Lesson5_My_Amazing_Sea_Animal_A5
A5 glue-in writing template — a 5-question PLAN + WORD HELP banks (body & looks · size & colour · food) + an article frame with sentence starters + a DRAW YOUR ANIMAL box + a CHECK list
  • Glue the template in; pupils answer the 5 plan questions in single words.
  • Point at the writing tip: use and to join similar facts — every article needs at least one.
  • They write the article on the frame lines and draw the animal in the box.
  • The title must be exciting and end with a hook question: Do you know that starfish haven't got a brain?
  • Read the CHECK list aloud; pupils tick their own boxes, then two pupils read theirs to the class.
