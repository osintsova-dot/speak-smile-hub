🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Describe a home with eleven words and choose between past simple and past continuous inside one story — the background action and the thing that cut across it.
What is genuinely hard
Not the form: was + ‑ing is easy to build. The hard part is deciding which of the two actions is the background, at the speed of telling a story. And Russian has no separate form for it, so there is nothing to translate from.
Typical error to expect
While I cleaning the kitchen — the dropped was.
The question was in the past, the answer comes back in the present (— Where did you go? — I go to my grandmother.). In the speaking paper that scores zero for the whole item [exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The grammar is discovered from three sentences that are already in the reading, so the tense arrives attached to a story about a family who moved into a lorry rather than as a table. Vocabulary comes first because the eleven words are what the story is made of, and they are spent immediately on describing the students' own homes — the shortest possible route from meeting a word to owning it. Pronunciation sits between the reading and the grammar as a change of gear, and it is not decoration: live and leave are one sound apart and both are in this unit. The three timelines replace the explanation; TB p.46 is right that a drawing settles in ten seconds what a paragraph cannot. The productive stage is a written story of 35 words because the choice of tense is visible on paper in a way it never is in speech — and because it is short enough to finish and read out inside ten minutes.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One picture, two tenses. Something was going on — and then something happened and cut across it. Today you learn to say both halves in one sentence, and by the end you write a story of thirty-five words that a stranger can follow.”

Why: Two sounds Russian does not distinguish, taught by feel and by minimal pairs rather than by explanation. Ninety seconds, then straight back into use.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5–6 p.25: /iː/ and /ɪ/, taught by feel and by minimal pairs (tracks 27–28)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB p.46: give the minimal pair beat / bit first, in case the symbols mean nothing to them. For /iː/ in ceiling the lips are wide, the tongue is high and the sound is LONG; for /ɪ/ in sink the lips are loose, the tongue is central and the sound is short. Grammar · discovery · two past tenses
pairs → class
Why: The rule is short but the choice is not: it is about which action is the background. Three timelines on the board do what a paragraph of explanation cannot.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3 p.25: three sentences from the article, three timelines, one rule

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the three sentences from ex.1 on the board (they are already in the article they have just read): a Gary was building everything for their new home, while Paula was working. · b They were looking at homes on the internet one day, when Tim, Gary's dad, had an idea. · c Finally, Gary finished the work on the lorry and they moved in.
  2. ex.1 — underline every verb and label it simple or continuous, in pairs, 4 minutes. Key: Past simple: had, finished, moved · Past continuous: was building, was working, were looking
  3. Now the drawings, TB p.46 — do not explain, draw: TB p.46, three quick timelines on the board instead of an explanation: two parallel arrows (was building / was working) = at the same time · a long arrow with a cross through it (were looking ✕ had an idea) = one interrupts the other · two arrows one after the other (finished → moved in) = one follows the other.
  4. ex.2 — match the three descriptions to the three sentences. Key: i c · ii a · iii b
  5. Pre-teach interrupt by doing it: ask a student to count to ten and cut in at six (TB p.46).
  6. ex.3 — complete the rule. Key: To form the past continuous we use the past simple of the verb be and the present participle.
  7. Put the two joining words up in a box: while = the long one · when = the short one.
📺 On screen: the three sentences and the three timelines on the screen, drawn live as you talk (Backup: the same three timelines in chalk on the board — they must stay up until the end of the lesson either way)
👦 Students do: underline, match and formulate. Expect the timelines to land instantly and the labels to lag behind. Typical error: had an idea filed as continuous because it sits next to two continuous verbs

🎯 Story out · three pictures
solo → pairs
Why: The productive outcome in writing: a story of at least 35 words that has to contain both tenses. Short enough to finish in class, long enough to show whether the choice is under control.

WHAT WE DO: ex.6 p.25: the three-picture story in at least 35 words, with both tenses

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the task exactly as the book does: SB p.25 ex.6: three pictures — a woman with a mug of coffee imagining a cake; the same woman opening an empty cupboard; the woman in a supermarket with a cake in her hand. Write the story with the past simple and the past continuous, 35 words or more.
  2. Two conditions on the board: 1 One «while». 2 One «when». Thirty-five words minimum.
  3. Six minutes to write, silently. Students who need support (TB p.46) get the model on the board as a gap-fill; everybody else writes from the pictures.
  4. Then pairs swap notebooks and do one job only: count the words and find the two joining words. Not a correction — a check.
  5. Read two stories out loud, then show the model: Model answer, TB p.46: While Jane was drinking some coffee, she felt hungry. She wanted to have a cake but when she opened her cupboard there was no food. So Jane went to the supermarket and bought one.
  6. Log the tense errors on your correction sheet; the clinic is at the start of the next lesson.
📺 On screen: the three pictures on the screen, large enough to see the cake in the third one (Backup: the same three pictures are printed in the book on p.25 — everybody works from the book and the two conditions go on the board)
👦 Students do: write 35 words, then check a partner's for the two joining words. Expect most stories to reach 40–50 words once the pictures are clear. Typical error: three past simple sentences with no background at all — the word count is what exposes it

Structure
Gary was building everything, while Paula was working. · They were looking at homes when Tim had an idea. · Gary finished the work and they moved in. · While I was cleaning the kitchen, I saw the broken window.
Form
past continuous = was/were + verb‑ing. While introduces the long background action; when introduces the short one that cuts across it.
Pron. watch
/iː/ ceiling, leave, seat, he's — long, lips wide. /ɪ/ sink, live, sit, his — short, lips loose. And the p is silent in cupboard but not in lamp (TB p.45).
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I was leaving the house just after you called me» — is that right? → no: one action follows the other, so it is I left the house just after you called me (TB p.46).
«While I cleaning the kitchen …» — what is missing? → the verb be: While I was cleaning …
Which one interrupts which: They were looking at homes when Tim had an idea? → the idea interrupts the looking. The long action takes the continuous.

Assumptions: SB p.24–25, syllabus lesson 12. The past simple was done in the last two lessons and is warm. The past continuous is formally familiar from the present continuous, so the form is not the lesson — the choice is. Of the eleven home words they usually know five (sofa, garage, lamp, ceiling, balcony); cooker, cupboard, entrance, sink and the two floors are new.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
ground floor and first floor mean different things in Russian buildingsDo not treat it as vocabulary, treat it as a fact about Britain: draw a two-storey house on the board, label the bottom ground floor and the one above it first floor, and leave the drawing up.
They build the past continuous everywhere, including where it does not belongSend them back to the timeline every time: is this the long thing or the short thing? The drawing answers it faster than you can.
Someone writes the 35-word story in two sentences and stopsThe condition is in the task, not in your voice: the story must contain one while and one when. Two sentences cannot hold both.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
