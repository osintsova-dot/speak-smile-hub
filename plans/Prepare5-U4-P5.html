🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Retrieve and repair everything from Units 7 and 8 — the music words, word families, the perfect against the past with its three adverbs, the building adjectives, the comparatives and the strong adjectives — and then produce it under test conditions in the two official Cambridge unit tests.
What is genuinely hard
Two sections of the papers are quietly harder than they look. Unit 7 Grammar 4 marks the POSITION of the adverb, so a student who knows yet perfectly can still score nothing by putting it in the middle. Unit 8 Grammar 3 is a spelling test with a grammar label: bigger, cosier, thinner and the furthest all change their letters.
Typical error to expect
«Yesterday I have bought a new album» — a finished time takes the past simple, always. Russian has one past tense for both, so the perfect leaks into sentences that name a day, and it is the single commonest grammar error in the Russian exam's written work.
«more taller», «the most best» — one comparison per adjective. Russian marks the comparison twice without sounding wrong, so this survives into B2 unless it is named.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is retrieval, not re-teaching, and it uses the book's own Review 2 spread, which covers Units 5–8 — so only the sections that belong to our two units are played, and the class is told that in one honest sentence. The Workbook listening that was deliberately left blank last lesson is done here, with the recording, because a listening is the one thing homework cannot do. Then the official review game, filtered to Units 7–8 (block 3 played the same sheets filtered to Units 5–6). The second half is the real paper, briefed honestly: sections named, minutes named, and the two quiet traps named BEFORE the papers turn over.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty-five minutes to get it all back, then forty-five to show it. Two warnings before we start: on the Unit seven adverbs, WHERE you put the word is the mark. On the Unit eight table, the spelling is the mark. Nothing else today is a surprise.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of retrieval with a spelling penalty, and the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what have you listened to today? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.

  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): name the day honestly: First half: get everything back. Second half: show it. Nothing new today.

WHAT WE DO: the official Cambridge review game, filtered to Units 7–8

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it up in ninety seconds: «WHAT'S THE WORD?» — PREPARE_5_Review_game_Units_05-08.pdf. Two teams, A and B, one sheet each, and the sheets must not be visible to the other team. Team A reads out a sentence from their sheet, pausing at the gap (they do NOT say the word printed in capitals — that is the answer). Team B chooses the word from the box at the bottom of their own sheet. Three points if they get it first time, two at the second attempt, one at the third. One word in each box is never used.
  2. Announce the filter and why: the same sheets were used in block 3, filtered to Units 5–6. Today the filter flips: play only the lines whose answer belongs to Units 7 and 8 — gig · concert hall · studio · lyrics · musician · celebrity · perform · live · cosy · spectacular · unusual · traditional · brand new · busier · wider · narrower · modern. The Unit 5–6 lines (customise, design, yawn, exhausted, freezing, don't have to) were played last block — mark them on both sheets before the lesson and say aloud why they are skipped. The follow-up printed in the rules is worth two minutes at the end: find FIVE words that could fill the same gap
  3. Play with the scoring exactly as printed — three, two, one — because the descending points are what keep the losing team thinking.
  4. Two minutes before the end, stop and run the follow-up printed in the rules on one sentence: find FIVE words that could fill the same gap.
📺 On screen: one A and one B sheet per pair of teams, cut along the dotted line and pre-marked. Backup: if you have only one copy, run it as a whole-class game with you reading team A's sheet
👦 Students do: teams listen to each other and choose from a word box; the team decides together before anyone answers

Likely problemWhat you do

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The block test is built the way the exam block is: produce the form and spell it right. musician written with a c, or a superlative without its the, scores nothing at all — which is why the briefing names both ghosts aloud before the papers go face down.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
