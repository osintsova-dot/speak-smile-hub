🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Tell apart and produce the zero conditional (always true: if you heat water, it boils) and the first conditional (possible future: if it rains, we'll stay in) — and use unless as if not without a double negative.
What is genuinely hard
Russian happily puts the future after если — so if you'll press is the single most stubborn conditional error. Unless adds the double-negative trap: unless you don't fill.
Typical error to expect
«If you'll press the button…» — will inside the if-clause is THE conditional error of Russian learners; the exam tests exactly this slot.
«Unless you don't fill the bottle…» — a double negative: unless already means if not, so the clause after it stays positive.
Why the lesson is built this way
The zero conditional is discovered inside yesterday's Ig Nobel facts (if a cow has a name, it produces more milk) — familiar sentences give up their grammar without a fight. The first conditional arrives by contrast, and the deciding question (always true? or future possibility?) is said aloud at every choice. The TB's unless-cards method has EVERYONE vote on every sentence simultaneously — no hiding, no waiting. The Corpus clinic kills will-after-if in public. The productive stage is chain reactions: conditional chains built live around the room, each result becoming the next condition — pure form practice that sounds like a party game.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“If you heat water, it boils — always, boring, true. If you don't do the homework tonight… what WILL happen? Same little word if, two different machines. Today you learn to drive both — and unless, the if with a minus sign.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.3–4: same shape, different job

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The contrast pair on the board: If you heat water, it boils. / If you take off your shoes, mosquitoes WILL bite your feet. — Which is always? Which is a future possibility?

Grammar · unless cards
solo, all at once
Why: The TB's own method: everyone holds IF and UNLESS cards and votes on every sentence at the same moment — all answer, nobody hides, and the double-negative trap dies in public.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5–6 the TB way: IF and UNLESS cards, everyone votes on every sentence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. unless introduced through ex.5: Life will end unless we stop climate change = if we DON'T stop. Unless = if not. The not is already inside.
  2. Everyone makes two scrap-paper cards: IF · UNLESS.
  3. You read the eight ex.6 gaps aloud, pausing at each space; on three-two-one — show! every card goes up at once (Showdown logic). Key: 1 If, unless · 2 unless, If · 3 unless, if · 4 if, Unless.
  4. Any split vote → the sentence is read BOTH ways aloud; the double negative sounds wrong and dies in public.
  5. Micro-summary on the wall: unless + POSITIVE verb.
📺 On screen: sentences read by voice, cards in every hand — the cards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: vote simultaneously on every item — nobody hides, nobody waits. Expect item 3 (cover the picnic) to split the room — reading both ways settles it

Corpus clinic
pairs
Why: Real learner errors — the exact ones Russian students make — get fixed by the class, not by the teacher. Finding someone else's mistake is the fastest way to stop making it yourself.

WHAT WE DO: ex.7: five real learner errors, will-after-if dies here

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame: Five sentences by real students. Find the broken part, fix it, name the rule. Pairs, one pen. Key: 1 IF you want to start a fire… · 2 …unless YOU text him later (no will!) · 3 IF it's OK with you… · 4 Unless WE meet before 8 pm… · 5 …if/when you have a fever.
  2. Check with rule-naming: unless you'll text — which side is you'll on? The if-side. Present only. Unless you text.
  3. The two wall rules get their final tap: will never on the if-side · unless already means not.
📺 On screen: SB p.92 ex.7 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hunt, fix, name the rule. Expect item 3 (When it's OK with you → If) to feel subtle — one minute on when = certain, if = maybe

🎯 Speaking out · chain reactions
circle → groups
Why: The productive outcome: conditional chains built live around the circle — each result becomes the next condition. Nobody can add a link without producing the form, and the chains get gloriously silly.

WHAT WE DO: conditional chains: each result becomes the next condition

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model one chain with the class, machine MAYBE: If I miss the bus, I'll be late for school. → If I'm late for school, the teacher will call my mum. → If my mum finds out… Five links, each result becomes the next if.
  2. Kagan RoundRobin in groups of four: chains of six links minimum, one link per person in turn, started from a card you deal (if it rains on Saturday · if I win a million · if our wi-fi dies · if school starts at 6 am).
  3. Machine check mid-chain: any link may be challenged with always or maybe? — a wrong machine breaks the chain and it restarts.
  4. Best chain performed for the class, one link per member, applause.
  5. You log if-side slips silently — they feed lesson 6's opening.
📺 On screen: starter cards dealt by hand — the cards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: build, challenge, perform. Expect chains to escalate gloriously (…we'll move to Mars) — the form holds as long as the challenge rule is alive

WHAT WE DO: personalised production: real rules for this room with if and unless

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The task: Write the three TRUE rules of this classroom — one zero (always), one first (maybe-future), one with unless. Model: If you forget your book, you share with a neighbour. Unless you charge your phone, it dies before the break.
  2. Pairs write, 5 minutes; Kagan Timed Pair Share with a second pair: read, listen, steal the best rule.
  3. Six or seven rules collected on the board; the class votes the OFFICIAL RULE OF THE WEEK — it stays in the corner until the test.
  4. Micro-summary: Two machines, one minus-sign word. You just legislated with them.
📺 On screen: rules in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write, share, steal, vote. Expect phone-and-wifi rules to dominate — they are perfect zero conditionals and everyone means them

Structure
If you heat water to 100°C, it boils. · When a player scores, the fans go crazy. · What will happen if we don't stop climate change? · The experiment won't work unless you fill the bottle.
Form
zero: if/when + present simple, present simple — both clauses; first: if + present simple, will + infinitive; unless = if not, verb stays POSITIVE; comma when the if-clause comes first.
Pron. watch
conditional contractions: I'll /aɪl/, won't /wəʊnt/ (not want!), the if-clause said as one breath group with a rise before the comma
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«If you heat water, it boils» — sometimes? usually? → ALWAYS. That's why both verbs stay present.
«If it rains tomorrow, we'll stay in» — certain? → no, possible. Future result → will, but only in the RESULT half.
«Unless you fill the bottle» — fill or don't fill? → the sentence means IF YOU DON'T fill. Unless already carries the not.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
will invades the if-clauseThe wall rule in red: IF-side = present. Every violation gets the same silent treatment — point at the wall, wait, student self-corrects. Consistency beats explanation.
unless collects a second notThe minus-sign metaphor: unless already IS the not. The cards stage catches it live: any UNLESS card raised over a negative verb → freeze the room, read the double negative aloud, laugh it away.
zero and first blur in productionThe deciding question said before every chain link: ALWAYS or MAYBE? Two seconds of naming saves the form.
Chain reactions collapse into RussianThe chain only advances on an English link — the English-first signal applies; a Russian idea gets reformulated with your scaffold and THEN counts.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
