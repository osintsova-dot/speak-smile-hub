🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Write a for-and-against essay of 140–190 words in four paragraphs on «We should always try to avoid any type of stress in our lives» — introduction, arguments in favour, arguments against, conclusion with the writer's own opinion — in formal register and with a controlled range of linkers from six functional families.
What is genuinely hard
Two things are genuinely difficult and neither of them is vocabulary. The first is the discipline of arguing a side you do not hold: the class will write two paragraphs agreeing with themselves and call it a for-and-against essay. The second is register. Speaking practice trains contractions in, and «don't», «it's» and «a lot of» arrive in the essay automatically — each one costs a mark, and they are invisible to the writer.
Typical error to expect
«I don't think sports stars deserve it» inside a for-and-against essay — contractions are the fastest way to lose the register mark, and the hardest habit to break because speaking practice trains them in.
Why the lesson is built this way
The model comes before the rules, and the rules come out of the model. Students first argue a live question — do footballers deserve more than doctors, with Neymar, Osaka and Hamilton on one side of the page and a surgical team on the other — and make notes in BOTH columns whatever they privately think. Only then do they read the model essay and discover the four-paragraph architecture themselves. The linkers are harvested from the model rather than issued as a list, so every expression arrives with a sentence around it. Planning is a separate, timed, board-visible stage — the single biggest predictor of a passing script — and twenty-two minutes are protected for uninterrupted writing.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Neymar earns in a week what a surgeon earns in a decade. Now argue the other way — properly, in writing, even if you agree with me. That is what a for-and-against essay is, and it is the one piece of writing where your own opinion is banned until the last paragraph.”

Why: Everyone speaks English inside the first two minutes, and the rewind pulls the previous lesson's language back into working memory so that today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of ranking and reasoning, and a disagreement that opens the essay topic

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — how did you sleep last night, and how do you feel about it? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.
  2. 2 minutes — who deserves the money? The Teacher's Book warmer, in groups of three, Kagan RallyRobin: if you could decide what every job pays, which three jobs would be the best paid, and why? One sentence each, in turn, ninety seconds.
  3. 1 minute — the awkward second question. Is there any job where you think the person should NOT be paid much? Take two answers and let the disagreement start — it is exactly the disagreement the lesson needs.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): point at the two names still on the board: You have just given me one side. Today you write both — and your own opinion has to wait until the last paragraph. Page seventy-nine.
📺 On screen: nothing to project in the first five minutes — voices and the board only
👦 Students do: produce three ranked jobs each with reasons, then argue about a job that should be paid less

Lesson frame

Why: The skeleton is referred to at every stage — planning, writing and the peer check all point back at the same four boxes.

WHAT WE DO: name the aim, draw the four-paragraph skeleton, set the bar

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Draw the skeleton on the board and leave it there for ninety minutes: 1 introduction · 2 arguments FOR · 3 arguments AGAINST · 4 conclusion + your opinion.
  2. Say the shape of the lesson in thirty seconds: argue a question aloud, take a model essay apart, harvest its linkers, plan four paragraphs, write for twenty minutes, check each other.
  3. Set the bar and mean it: One hundred and forty to one hundred and ninety words. No contractions. Your opinion in paragraph four and nowhere else.
  4. Write the three Despite patterns underneath the skeleton now, so they are on the board before anybody needs them: Despite + noun · Despite + -ing · Despite the fact that + clause.
📺 On screen: the four-paragraph skeleton and the three Despite patterns, written on the board for the whole lesson — chalk only

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: the four photographs, then a two-column table that must not stay half empty

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 as a whole class, two minutes: who are the people in the photographs? Neymar, Naomi Osaka, Lewis Hamilton — and a surgical team. Do they deserve to earn a lot? Take two answers for and two against, deliberately.
  2. Write the statement on the board: Doctors should be paid more than top sports stars. Underneath, two columns — REASONS FOR and REASONS AGAINST.
  3. ex.2, four minutes, individually: copy the table and fill BOTH columns. Say the rule that makes this a for-and-against lesson: Fill both columns whatever you personally think. A column with nothing in it means you have not done the task.
  4. Then Kagan RallyRobin in pairs, two minutes: read alternate items, one from your FOR column, one from your AGAINST column, and steal anything good.
  5. Circulate with one question only, and ask it of the students who have written five things in one column and one in the other: Give me one more for the other side.
📺 On screen: the statement and the two empty columns drawn on the board and filled by the class — chalk only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: make notes in both columns of a table, then trade arguments alternately with a partner

WHAT WE DO: ex.3–4: read for agreement first, then discover the architecture

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3, three minutes, silent: read the model essay quickly and tick any idea that is already in your own table. That is the only job on the first read.
  2. Take two of the essay's arguments to the floor and ask for agreement or disagreement — Top sports stars help society by acting as inspirational role models. Ninety seconds, no more; the point is that they engage with the content before analysing it.
  3. ex.4 as a whole class, four minutes. Question 1 — what is each paragraph for? Answers: 1 introduces the topic · 2 arguments AGAINST the statement · 3 arguments FOR it · 4 summary plus the writer's opinion. Note out loud that the order of 2 and 3 is the writer's choice, not a rule.
  4. Question 2 — how is this different from an opinion essay? Answer: both sides are given, and the writer's own view appears only in the conclusion. Write that sentence on the board next to the skeleton.
  5. Point at one paragraph and ask the class to find the topic sentence. Then ask what would happen if it were deleted. Thirty seconds, and it teaches paragraph structure faster than a diagram.
📺 On screen: the model essay is in the book — nothing needs projecting. The board carries only the paragraph functions as the class names them
👦 Students do: read a model for content, then name the function of each of its four paragraphs

Move break · linker auction
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that sorts the six families before they are written down

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six corners or six points around the room, each with one family name on a sheet: SEQUENCING · ADDING · CONTRASTING · CONSEQUENCE · OPINION · CONCLUDING.
  2. You call a linker — Nevertheless · Furthermore · As a result · To sum up · As far as I'm concerned · Secondly — and everybody walks to the family they think it belongs to.
  3. One person from the biggest group justifies the choice in a full sentence using the linker. If the room split, let the two groups argue for fifteen seconds before you rule.
  4. Eight calls, eight walks, and by the end the six families exist as places in the room rather than as rows in a table.
  5. Freeze and sit: Now find all of those in the essay you have just read.
📺 On screen: six sheets of paper on the walls — that is the entire preparation, and nothing can fail
👦 Students do: physically sort eight linkers into six functional families and justify one in a full sentence

WHAT WE DO: ex.5a–5b: the underlined linkers sorted into the bank, then extended from memory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5a, four minutes individually: put the underlined words from the model into the right section of the Writing bank. Answers: 1 Firstly, Lastly · 2 What is more, Furthermore, In addition · 3 However, On the other hand, Nevertheless · 4 Therefore · 5 to my mind, I believe · 6 All in all.
  2. ex.5b, three minutes in pairs: add anything else you know to each section. Collect on the board and add the book's own extras — not only … but also, In contrast, Despite / In spite of, and so, as a result, In my opinion, Personally, As far as I'm concerned, In conclusion, To sum up.
  3. Now spend three minutes on the one pattern that will be wrong tomorrow: Despite / In spite of. Put the three forms on the board and drill two transformations from the Teacher's Book — Despite their wealth, rich people aren't always happy → Despite being wealthy / Despite the fact that they are wealthy.
  4. Kagan Showdown to check, two minutes: you give a sentence with «although», pairs rewrite it with Despite and hold it up on the signal. Four rounds, everyone answers every round.

Planning the four paragraphs

Why: Planning the four paragraphs

WHAT WE DO: ex.6: the new topic, the same table, and a plan that is checked before anyone writes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the new task aloud: We should always try to avoid any type of stress in our lives. Then draw the same two-column table on the board — REASONS FOR, REASONS AGAINST.
  2. Four minutes, silent and individual: notes in both columns. Feed in content only if a table is stalling — some stress is useful before an exam; long-term stress makes you ill; a life with no pressure teaches you nothing; deadlines get work finished; the health cost is real.
  3. Then, and this is the step students skip: decide WHICH paragraph each note goes in and write 2 or 3 next to it. Two minutes.
  4. Kagan Think-Pair-Share, two minutes: show your plan to a partner and ask one question — Which of my arguments is weakest? Cross it out and replace it.
  5. Read the Exam tip on p.79 with them, and put the four non-negotiables on the board where they stay for the writing: four paragraphs · both sides · opinion only in the conclusion · formal, no contractions · 140–190 words.
  6. Last thirty seconds: everyone writes their first sentence — the introduction — and reads it to their partner. Starting is the hardest part and it is now done.
📺 On screen: the empty two-column table and the four non-negotiables written on the board — chalk only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: make notes on both sides, assign every note to a paragraph, have the plan challenged by a partner and write the first sentence

WHAT WE DO: ex.7a: the full essay written in class, on paper, with two checkpoints and no interruptions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 🎯 Announce the conditions and then keep them: Twenty-two minutes. Silent. I will not answer any questions about ideas — only about words.
  2. Palm down: this is the silent-mode signal and it stays down for the whole stage.
  3. Circulate slowly with one question and one only, asked at the desk in a whisper: Which paragraph is this sentence in? It repairs structure without giving content.
  4. Checkpoint at 15 minutes: everybody stops, counts their words and writes the number in the margin. Under 120 means one more argument with an example. Over 200 means cut the introduction.
  5. Checkpoint at 20 minutes: read your own last paragraph silently and check one thing — is your opinion in it, and is it ONLY in it?
  6. Do not correct anything in red while circulating. The checklist does that in four minutes' time, and a page of your handwriting stops them reading their own.
📺 On screen: the timer and the four non-negotiables are the only things visible. Backup: if there is nothing to project, the board carries both and you call the two checkpoints aloud
👦 Students do: write a complete 140–190-word essay in four paragraphs, in silence, with two self-checks built into the time

Peer check against the checklist
pairs
Why: Peer check against the checklist

WHAT WE DO: ex.7b: the Writing checklist on p.141 used on somebody else's essay, out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Swap essays with a partner who was NOT their planning partner — a fresh reader catches what a co-author cannot.
  2. First job, sixty seconds, and it is the register test: read the essay aloud in a whisper and circle every contraction you HEAR. Contractions are invisible on the page and obvious in the mouth.
  3. Second job, four minutes: work down the Writing checklist on p.141 and tick or cross each line. Every cross needs a line number in the margin — no vague comments.
  4. Third job, ninety seconds, Kagan Timed Pair Share: give the writer two specific things that worked and one thing to change. In that order, and «it was good» is not accepted.
  5. Two minutes for the writer to make the corrections in a different colour, on the same sheet, before handing it in. The correction is where the learning happens, not the mark.
  6. Collect the essays. Tell them plainly what you will mark: structure, both sides, register and the linkers — in that order.
📺 On screen: the Writing checklist is on p.141 of the book — nothing needs projecting for this stage
👦 Students do: read a partner's essay aloud for register, audit it against a printed checklist and give two praises and one change

Structure
Firstly, … · What is more, … · On the other hand, … · Despite / In spite of + noun / gerund / the fact that … · Therefore, … · To my mind, … · All in all, …
Form
the six functional families: 1 introducing and sequencing (Firstly, Secondly, Lastly) · 2 adding (What is more, Furthermore, In addition, not only … but also) · 3 contrasting (However, On the other hand, Nevertheless, In contrast, Despite/In spite of) · 4 consequences (Therefore, and so, as a result) · 5 opinion (In my opinion, Personally, To my mind, As far as I'm concerned) · 6 concluding (All in all, In conclusion, To sum up) · after Despite and In spite of comes a NOUN, a GERUND or «the fact that», never a clause on its own.
Pron. watch
this is a writing lesson, so the pronunciation work is reading aloud for register: contractions are audible when you read your own paragraph out — don't, it's, can't jump out of a formal text the moment it is spoken, which is why the peer check begins by reading the essay aloud in a whisper.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«This essay says top sports stars deserve their money AND that doctors deserve more. Is the writer confused?» → no — those are paragraphs two and three, and both sides are required.
«Where is the writer's own opinion?» → the last paragraph, and nowhere else. Which two words tell you it is an opinion? → «I believe», «to my mind».
«I don't think it's fair.» — is that formal? → no, two contractions. Rewrite it. → «I do not believe it is fair.» How many marks did that just save? → one, every time.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Both body paragraphs argue the same sideThe notes table is built BEFORE the model and checked at the desk: if a column is empty, the student does not start writing. The Teacher's Book insists on this and it is the whole difference between a for-and-against essay and an opinion essay.
Contractions everywhereMake them audible rather than explaining them. In the peer check the reader reads the essay aloud in a whisper and circles every contraction they HEAR. Ten seconds per essay, and the class starts self-editing next time.
The essay is 90 words or 260Word count is done at 15 minutes, not at the end: everybody stops, counts, and writes the number in the margin. Too short means one more argument with an example; too long means cutting the introduction, which is always the fattest paragraph.
«Despite of» and «In spite the fact»Put the three patterns on the board before the writing starts and leave them: Despite + noun · Despite + -ing · Despite the fact that + clause. The Teacher's Book fast-finisher task rehearses exactly these and takes two minutes.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
