Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name 6 animals from the past and talk about past ability: “A dodo could run, but it couldn't fly. Could dodos swim? — No, they couldn't.”
  • Subsidiary. Sing «A long, long time ago» (CD2 04); discover could / couldn't for ability in the past (Grammar clue); meet extinct · tusks · shell; a true-or-false ability game (Talk Partners); build could/couldn't sentences from a chart (AB follow-up).
  • Personal. Insist on could / couldn't + base verb; model each animal & ability with a big TPR action; keep the game moving — short turns, everyone mimes.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name 6 animals from the past;
  • say what an animal could / couldn't do;
  • ask & answer “Could …? — Yes, it could / No, it couldn't.”

Structure
It could (walk), but it couldn't (jump).  ·  Could dodos (fly)? — No, they couldn't.  ·  Some of them are now extinct.
Form
could / couldn't + base verb (ability in the past) — same for I/you/he/she/it/we/they. Question: Could + subject + base verb? — Yes, … could / No, … couldn't.
Pron. watch
couldn't /ˈkʊdnt/ (silent l); sabre-toothed /ˈseɪbə tuːθt/; mammoth /ˈmæməθ/; extinct /ɪkˈstɪŋkt/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“It could swim — is that now or a long time ago?” → a long time ago (past ability).
“couldn't — can it do it or NOT?” → NOT (couldn't = could not).

Assumptions: Story language & past questions are active (L2–L3); the 6 animals are new but very picturable and some were named in the story (mammoth, sabre-toothed tiger). The switch to could / couldn't + base verb is the key challenge. (Book teaches 6 animals: sabre-toothed tiger · giant ground sloth · prehistoric armadillo · dodo · sabre-toothed squirrel · woolly mammoth — note КТП listed a different set.)

Presentation · 6 animals
carpet → board

Listen, point and say (act. 1, CD2 03). Animal cards: A dodo! A woolly mammoth! A sabre-toothed tiger! Choral ×3 + a big TPR shape each (mammoth = tusks, sloth = slow, armadillo = curl up).

💪 Support: action carries the meaning. ⭐ Extension: read the animal word without the picture.

Song + grammar box

Sing (act. 2, CD2 04). PB p.32 «A long, long time ago». First run listen & do the animal actions; second sing with could/couldn't gestures (tick = could, cross = couldn't).

Grammar box (act. 3, CD2 05). Board: I/You/He/She/It/We/They + could / couldn't + walk · fly · swim · jump · run · climb. Grammar clue: We use “could” to talk about ability in the past. Sabre-toothed squirrels couldn't fly. Circle could / couldn't.

27–37′
True or false · ability game
desks, pairs → 2 teams

Model first. Say some true & false facts: A dodo could fly. — That's false! A mammoth could walk. — That's true!

▶ Talk Partners (act. 4). Pairs play true/false: A says An armadillo could climb trees. B replies That's false! It couldn't climb trees. Swap. 6+ turns each.

▶ Fact-file relay (team). Using the PB p.33 animal fact cards, you say an ability — teams race to hold up the matching animal card & say the full sentence A sabre-toothed tiger could climb trees! Point per correct.

✔ Assess: circulate — is it could / couldn't + base verb, right animal? Recast, note names.

💪 Support: animal cards have the ability icons. ⭐ Extension: add a Could…? question: Could a dodo swim? — No, it couldn't.

 animal cards · PB p.33 fact cards · ▶ Talk Partners
