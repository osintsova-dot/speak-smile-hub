Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to follow, read and act out the story «The important discovery» and talk about the past: “What did they find? — They found a spear in a cave. Did prehistoric people use spears? — Yes, they did.”
  • Subsidiary. Value — don't drop litter (the children follow a dropped bottle); break Luke's code (→ KAYAKING); recycle past-simple questions from L2 (What did they find/wear/drop?); meet Did they…? Yes, they did / No, they didn't; story skills — predict → listen → read along → Read & answer → act out; feed the animals/could of L4 (mammoths · sabre-toothed tigers).
  • Personal. Let the story breathe — big voices, pauses, prediction; give every child a role; the value discussion is short & personal, not a lecture. Notice Did they…? — it's drilled through the acting, not over-corrected.
By the end, pupils can…
  • retell 2–3 things that happen in the story;
  • read their role's lines from the book;
  • answer questions: “They found a spear and an axe.”

Structure

Form
Did (they) + base verb…? — Yes, they did / No, they didn't; past answers (found · dropped · used · hunted); could preview (Sabre-toothed tigers could run fast — full focus in L4).

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“The children follow a dropped BOTTLE — is dropping litter good or bad?” → bad — but it leads to a discovery!
“Did they find gold? Yes or no?” → no — they found prehistoric TOOLS (a spear & an axe).

Assumptions: Past-simple questions are active from L2; the number-code was learnt in the Starter (recycled in act 1). Second unit story of the year — the act-out routine is established. Readers follow the text with a finger; Did they…? is receptively known.

Listen 2 — read along (books open pp.30–31). Finger under the text. Pause per frame: Oh no! I dropped it. … Wow! There's a cave here. … It's a spear from prehistoric times. … Did prehistoric people use spears? Yes, they did. They used them for hunting.

Values (act. 4, whole class — don't drop litter). Why did the children follow the bottle? How do you feel when you see litter in the countryside? Why is it important to put litter in the bin? 3 hands each, recast key words.

Read & answer · comprehension
carpet → desks

Read and answer (act. 3, Talk Partners). Questions: What did the children do today? What did they wear? What did Lily drop? Where did they find it? What did they find in the cave? Pairs answer in full: They went kayaking. Every pair speaks.

True or false? (Showdown). Mini-whiteboards: you read a fact — “The children found a hammer.” True or false? — all write T/F, Show me! 5 rounds.

✔ Assess: the boards are your check — who follows the story? The rest sit with you in the board game.

💪 Support: point to the right frame in the book. ⭐ Extension: correct the false ones aloud: No — they found a spear and an axe.

28–40′
Act out
carpet, groups of 4

Model a frame. You + 3 pupils play the cave-discovery frame with big drama: Look! What's that? — It's a spear from prehistoric times!

Groups of 4 (Josh · Lily · Ravi · reporter). Rehearse 2 frames with books as scripts. Rotate roles once.

Showtime. Each group performs ONE frame (30–40 sec — fast carousel, no long waits). Audience task: shout what the children found (“A spear and an axe!”) at the end.

💪 Support: shortest lines / the “Wow!” reaction for the shyest child. ⭐ Extension: add an extra line from the frame.

▶ Mistake-hunt teams (RallyCoach). In pairs, partner A reads a line, B spots & corrects the wrong word, then swap. Each pair fixes 4 mistakes and reads the correct sentence aloud.

🎮 Projector option: show the blog text on screen; a rotating child circles the wrong word as the class calls the correction.

ICQ: Did they go water-skiing or kayaking? (→ kayaking!)
