WHAT WE DO: Inside-Outside Circle to revise: I meet them at the door; rewind U3 in the new topic: breakfast+food (there is/some); a bridge to the unit question

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: Hi! One word — how are you? Fingers for mood, 1–5.
  2. I build Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: two circles facing each other, a question → both answer → Outside circle, rotate two steps! — new partner, new question; 3 rotations, everybody speaks.
  3. Rewind U3 → bridge into U4: Breakfast check! Tell your partner: I have … for breakfast. Is there milk? juice? — 60 sec in pairs, from the frame.
  4. 2 answers aloud (+1 for the future teams).
  5. Bridge: You told me WHAT you eat. But WHEN? Seven? Eight? Today we learn to say the time like the British do.
📺 On screen: board: the frame “I have … for breakfast. / Is there …?” · U3 leaderboard → a clean U4 leaderboard
👦 Students do: they greet with one word, interview their partner from the U3 frame. Expected: a calm, warm start. Typical error: they answer WHEN with Russian numbers — I smile: “after the lesson you’ll say it in English.”

WHAT WE DO: step-by-step input: o’clock → half past → quarter past/to → five/ten/twenty past/to; choral drill with pronunciation

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I move the hands (cardboard clock): Three o'clock. Say it! — chorus ×2.
  2. Step by step: half past three → quarter past → quarter to → twenty past → five to. After each — chorus + a gesture at the clock half.
  3. Pronunciation drill: half — /hɑːf/, no L! · quarter · pas(t) two · ten TO ten.
  4. Ex.1 p.48: teams sort 5 phrases onto the sides of the clock (60 sec).
  5. Language note: twelve o'clock = midday or midnight.
📺 On screen: cardboard clock with hands (or I draw one) · SB p.48 ex.1 · anchor clock
👦 Students do: they name the times in chorus with gestures, sort the phrases onto the sides. Expected: the system sinks in through the anchor clock. Typical error: “half to” — gesture at the right half: half only lives with PAST.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can tell the time with past/to (It's twenty past ten · quarter to six) and say their daily routine with 8+ routine phrases (get up · have breakfast · go to school…).
  • Subsidiary. WDYT? unit question opened; TO/PAST system anchored; have/do/go collocations noticed; Video skills (sloth) watched with visual tasks; first present simple phrases FROM A MODEL (I get up at… — no theory).
  • Personal. Time is a concrete, “grown-up” topic — a real skill, pride guaranteed. Keep the choral drills lively, keep the supports on the board till the end of the lesson, don’t rush the weaker students on past/to.
By the end, students can…
  • say clock times with past/to using the anchor clock
  • name 12 daily routine phrases
  • say 4–5 sentences about their day from a frame
  • react to the sloth video in English

Structure
What time is it? — It's half past eight. · I get up at seven o'clock. · I have breakfast at… · Language note: twelve o'clock = midday / midnight.
Form
minutes 1–30 = past, after 30 = to; half past · quarter past/to; have + breakfast/lunch/dinner/lessons · do + homework · go + to school / home (no to before home!).
Pron. watch
half /hɑːf/ — the l is silent! · quarter /ˈkwɔːtə/ · pas(t) two — the t is swallowed · to = /tə/ (TB p.57 ABCD).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“8:20 — past or to?” → past (before the half hour).
“8:40 — which hour are we talking about?” → the NEXT one: twenty to NINE.
“I go to home?” → No — go home, no to.

Assumptions: After U1–U3: be, have got, there is/are, numbers are active. They can already read the time in Russian; the English past/to system and the routine collocations are new. We do NOT explain present simple today — we only hear it and repeat it in frames.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
past/to get mixed upThe anchor clock stays on the board all lesson: the right half PAST (in yellow), the left TO; a circular hand gesture on every answer.
Do they read half past six as 6:30 or 5:30?Anchor rule: half past = 30 minutes AFTER the named hour; a CCQ check on 2–3 examples in chorus.
‘go to home’A yellow card go home ✂ to on the board; the class fixes every slip in chorus.
The video is fast for beginnersA visual task (what you SEE), not a verbal one; 2 viewings; success = noticing, not understanding everything.
They get tired of clocks by the middleChange of pace: after the input — stand up, “living clocks” with your hands; no seated block longer than 10 min.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
