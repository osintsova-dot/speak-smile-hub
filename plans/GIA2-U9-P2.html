🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The last lesson of the course. It cannot be run like an ordinary one: if today is «one more revision lesson», the year ends in nothing — and it has been a big year, and they should walk out feeling «I did this», not «right, you can go». So the lesson is built as a season finale: three skill arenas (you listen · you speak · you write), the Review game board game as the last crew battle, and the defence of the best product of the year on the classroom wall. The second purpose is purely practical: SB p.112 and p.114 give the format that is closest to the final test (a–c questions on a long listening, a 120-word piece of writing), and p.113 covers the speaking part. So the finale is both a celebration and the last rehearsal before the FINAL block. Say that honestly — teenagers hate being entertained behind their backs.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Season finale. Three arenas and one game. Arena one: you listen — cold, no warm-up, exam questions. Arena two: you speak — a real situation, judged. Arena three: you write — 120 words, timer running. Then the board game: twenty-one squares, every square is a unit from this year, and the winner takes the season. And on the wall behind me: your best work of the year. You made it, you defend it, sixty seconds each. This is the last lesson. Make it count.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the season finale: three arenas (you listen — you speak — you write), then the 21-square Review game board game covering the whole year, and the defence of the best product of the year on the classroom wall. Plus, honestly: the formats of today's arenas are the formats of the final test.

PRODUCT-1 · live dialogue
pairs → stage

WHAT WE DO: p.113 ex.5–6: their own dialogue on one of three situations (THINK–PREPARE–PRACTISE–PERFORM), 90 seconds on stage, judges working to the rubric

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the situations yourself so that all three are covered (TB advice): A — make arrangements for a weekend together · B — buy a present for a friend · C — talk about a new hobby or course.
  2. THINK/PREPARE, 4 minutes: Write the dialogue. At least FOUR Key phrases. Underline them — the judges count them. Send them to the right page of the year: A → SB p.103 · B → SB p.55 · C → SB p.91.
  3. PRACTISE, 2 minutes: Practise. Then close the paper. On stage you may hold ONE card with four words on it — nothing else.
  4. PERFORM: 90 seconds per pair, four or five pairs on stage (the rest are on the judges' sheets — everyone marks). Judging cards 1–2–3 to the rubric.
  5. ex.6 Peer review straight after each pair, to the formula: Which situation? Which Key phrases did you hear? One thing that was better than ours. — the third point is compulsory, otherwise peer review turns into criticism.
  6. Read the Exam tip before the stage: Take a deep breath before you start. In a speaking exam, calm beats fast.
📺 On screen: SB p.113 ex.5–6 + Skills boost · judging cards · 90-second timer (Backup: the judges show 1–2–3 on their fingers instead of cards; the «stage» is simply two chairs in front of the board)
👦 Students do: students write, rehearse and come out in pairs. Expected: the pairs who got situation B (shopping) perform most confidently — that model was drilled in Unit 3 more than the others. Error: they read off the page — the judges take fluency marks off, and that works better than any comment from you

Writing sprint
solo

WHAT WE DO: p.114 ex.1–4: five text types → the six language questions of the year → repairing five sentences → 120 words against the clock

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in 90 seconds: five texts ↔ five types. Key: 1 C a blog · 2 D an invitation · 3 A a letter · 4 B instructions · 5 E a forum post.
  2. ex.2 through Kagan RallyCoach, 4 minutes: the first one answers the question aloud and explains the rule, the second is the coach with the book and confirms or repairs it; they swap roles on every question. Six questions, each on one unit of the year: the order of events (U6) · capital letters (U1) · and/but/or (U2) · adjectives of place (U5) · also/too (U4) · because/so (U3). If you have to look it up — put a dot next to it. Those dots go on your year card.
  3. ex.3 as a race, 2 minutes: five sentences with an error. Key: also after the verb · Mondays with a capital letter · interesting with no -s · quiet OR busy · a small town in Spain last August.
  4. The sprint (ex.4): 2 minutes to plan (three paragraphs — what goes in each), 12 minutes to write, 120 words. A choice of task: A a blog about the last holiday · B a letter to a friend about plans for the end of the year and the summer · C a reply to a post «where should I go, tell me about your town».
  5. Timer on the board. Two minutes to plan, twelve to write. Last two minutes: CHECK — tenses, capital letters, linking words, punctuation. Do not skip the check.
  6. Peer review (ex.5) is NOT done now but at the end of the lesson, when they swap — otherwise it eats the board game.
📺 On screen: SB p.114 ex.1–4 + Skills boost · a timer on the board (Backup: write the three CHECK points — tenses · linkers · capital letters — in marker and do not rub them out; the clock on the wall instead of an on-screen timer)
👦 Students do: students sort the text types, repair the sentences and write 120 words against the clock. Expected: most of them choose task B (the summer is the easiest thing to write about); the tenses drift where «what was» meets «what will be». Error: they write one solid block with no paragraphs — the three-paragraph plan at the start catches that, if you really give it 2 minutes

Final arena: the Review game board game
🏁 REVIEW GAME · arena
groups of 3–5

WHAT WE DO: p.115: the 21-square board game — the last crew battle of the year across all the units

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the rules TOGETHER before the dice go out (a TB requirement): roll → move → do the task on the square. Check understanding of the blue «Miss a turn» and green «Go back to START» squares separately.
  2. The Key phrases of the game go on the board, and this is a condition, not a suggestion: You start. · Whose turn is it? · What's the question? · It's your/my turn. Russian at the table = your turn is skipped. That's the game rule, not my rule.
  3. The one-minute squares (3 · 10 · 14 · 18 · 20) are timed by the group's timekeeper — Speak for one minute. Silence longer than five seconds ends your turn.
  4. Walk round and listen, correcting nothing: write down three phrases that sounded good — they go into the last minute of the lesson.
  5. The finish: First to FINISH takes the square. Then the group answers: which square was the hardest? — the question comes from the TB, and it honestly shows where to look before the final test.
📺 On screen: SB p.115 · a dice per group · counters (coins or rubbers) · a way of timing a minute (Backup with no dice: a dice app on the captain's phone, or 6 slips of paper in a cup; the board is in the book itself, it is printed there)
👦 Students do: students play in groups of 3–5 and speak English. Expected: the one-minute squares («My last birthday», «My plans for the holidays») go best — there is something to say; the listing squares sag (how many school subjects can you name). Error: they slide into Russian when it gets hard — the missed-turn rule holds that without you stepping in

PRODUCT-2 · Best of the Year
wall → stage

WHAT WE DO: defending the best product of the year: 60 seconds at the wall, judges to the rubric, swapping the written work

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The wall. Your year. Sixty seconds each — what it is, why you chose THIS one, and one thing you'd do better now. The third point is compulsory: it is what turns a show-and-tell into reflection.
  2. Not everyone comes out one after another — one per crew plus anyone who wants to (in the time available, usually 6–8 students). The judging cards are in use and the points go to the crew.
  3. In parallel, while the defences run — peer review of the writing (ex.5): swap, three questions in the margin (which task? are the tenses and linkers right? what did you like most?). Written, not discussed aloud.
  4. Read out the three good phrases you heard during the board game, naming the author: Heard at table three. Say it again for everyone.
  5. A gold run (9/9 on the rubric) — +5 and your signature on the product itself.
📺 On screen: the wall of products of the year · judging cards · 60-second timer (Backup: if there are few products, they defend from a photo on the phone, projected or simply shown down the rows)
👦 Students do: students defend their work at the wall and check each other's writing in parallel. Expected: the most common choices are the Unit 6 poster and the Unit 3 mini-book — they are the most hand-made. Error: they describe WHAT is on the poster instead of saying why they chose that one — send it back with one question, «Why THIS one?»

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, agents can handle all four exam-format skills in one lesson: answer multiple-choice questions on a 2-minute podcast, perform a real-world dialogue with at least four Key phrases, and write 120 words with correct tenses and linking words.
  • Subsidiary. Listening SB p.112 (track 57 — five extracts, track 58 — the e-sports podcast, 5 a–c questions). Speaking SB p.113: the six situations of the year, Key phrases sorted into categories, their own dialogue through THINK–PREPARE–PRACTISE–PERFORM. Writing SB p.114: five text types, the six language questions of the year (the order of events · capital letters · and/but/or · adjectives of place · also/too · because/so), 120 words against the clock. Put the wall of products of the year up before the lesson, not in the first 10 minutes of it.

Structure
spoken: the six situations of the year (information · clothes · suggestions · directions · opinion · arrangement) · written: a blog / a letter / a forum reply, 120 words, three paragraphs
Form
the tenses in the writing are chosen consciously: the past (last holiday) → past simple; summer plans → going to / present continuous; general advice → present simple. Linkers: and (a similar idea) · but (a different one) · or (an alternative) · because (a reason) · so (a result) · also (after be, before other verbs) · too (at the end of the sentence)
Pron. watch
question intonation in a dialogue (voice up on Yes/No, down on Wh-) and the ’ll contraction in sentences about the future (SB p.117): I’ll · you’ll · we’ll — on stage you either hear them or you don't, and the judges notice
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Take time to read the questions before you listen» — what for? → so that you know what to listen out for: otherwise you listen to everything and hear nothing.
«also» and «too» — what is the difference in position? → also goes after be and before other verbs; too goes at the end of the sentence.
«because» or «so»? → because gives the reason (I like it because it's cheap), so gives the result (It's cheap, so I like it).
«120 words» — is that about quantity or about content? → about content: three paragraphs, every point of the task. A point you did not write is a guaranteed loss of marks.

Assumptions: SB pp.112–115, syllabus pair 71 (lessons 139–140, May) — the LAST lesson of the course. All the Real-world speaking pages of Units 1–8 are covered, all the Writing pages are written. Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Last lesson = «let's just play something»There IS a game today and it is announced from the start — but it comes at the end, after the three arenas, and it is competitive. Announce the order in the brief: arenas first, then the finale. No «if we have time» — that kills the motivation instantly.
Somebody hasn't brought their product of the yearHave the fallback ready in advance: photos of the projects from earlier lessons on your phone or printed out. Anyone who hasn't brought one defends it from a photo, or defends their crew's project. Excluding them from the defence is not an option: this is the last lesson of the year.
The board game falls apart: they argue about the rules and about whose turn it isRead the rules TOGETHER before the dice go out (the TB requires exactly this) and check understanding of the blue «Miss a turn» and green «Go back» squares separately. The Key phrases of the game (You start. · Whose turn is it? · It's your turn.) stay on the board — arguing in Russian is not allowed, and that is a rule of the game, not a demand of yours.
120 words in 14 minutes — they don't finishThe bar is honest and announced: 2 minutes to plan (three paragraphs — what goes in each), 12 to write. Anyone who doesn't finish hands in what they have, and that is fine: the point of the sprint is to feel the pace of the exam before the exam, not to write a masterpiece.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
