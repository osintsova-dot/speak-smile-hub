🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Name the fourteen body parts and the illness words (sore throat, fever, flu, stomach ache…) and choose between will and be going to by asking the deciding question: planned already? evidence now? or decided this second?
What is genuinely hard
Two silent letters (knee, thumb) and one lying digraph (cough) in the vocabulary; and in the grammar — Russian has ONE future where English splits four uses across two forms, so «I will go to the dentist tomorrow» (already booked!) writes itself.
Typical error to expect
I will to visit my grandma · I going to see the doctor — an extra to after will, and be dropped out of be going to; plus will used for settled plans where English wants going to («Я собираюсь…») [high priority, A2/B1]
stomach ache (not stomachache with the letters scrambled) · cough (the gh says f!) · toothache and earache are ONE word · mosquito · a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
Why the lesson is built this way
⚠️ SB p.66 is missing from the scanned PDF — the students' printed books have it; every grammar key below is rebuilt from the TB facsimile (p.128–129) and complete. This is the first lesson after the winter holidays, so the opening is a holiday round in the perfect-vs-simple frame — December's grammar pulled back across the gap and pointed at today's topic. The body words arrive pinned to a photo, the illness words arrive inside three recorded stories, and the reading about living to 1,000 does double duty: careful reading AND the quarry for will/going to — the discovery stage extracts its rules from sentences the class has just argued about. The dense spread is honestly triaged: of the reading's nine detail questions, five run in class and four go to fast finishers. The productive stage is a future interview at 64′, after both forms have been met three ways.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“A scientist in Cambridge says the first person who will live to ONE THOUSAND is already alive. Maybe it's you. Today you read his argument — and by the end you can talk about your own future two ways: what you PREDICT, and what you've already DECIDED. English uses two different verbs for that. Russian doesn't. That's the whole game today.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a holiday round with two partners, and the bridge into the unit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the holiday round. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share, one question, two partners: Tell me ONE thing you did in the holidays — and one thing you've never done but want to. Thirty seconds each way, swap once — December's two tenses wake up by themselves.
  3. Collect two answers and reformulate them into the new topic: Interesting — and will you do it this year? Are you going to? No grammar talk yet; just let the forms be heard.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the unit: Books open, page sixty-four. Unit eleven — A healthy future. First: your body, in English. Then: a man who says you might live to a THOUSAND.
📺 On screen: questions by voice — the voice is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: mingle, answer, hear the future forms land unnamed. Expect rust and Russian fragments — accept, reformulate, move; the point is warm mouths, not accuracy

Vocabulary · the body map
pairs → class
Why: Fourteen body parts arrive pinned to a photograph and sorted into four zones — a map, not a list. The two silent letters (knee, thumb) are drilled by ear before the eye can install the wrong sound.

WHAT WE DO: About you + ex.1 (track 52): pin fourteen words to the dancer photo; ex.2: sort into head/body/leg/arm

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Think-Pair-Share on two About-you questions: About you (video 08): Are you generally quite healthy? · Have you ever broken an arm or a leg? What happened? · Do you think most people would like to live to be 1,000 years old? Why / why not? — twenty seconds to think, tell your partner, two pairs report.
  2. ex.1 in pairs, 3 minutes: match the fourteen words to letters A–N on the photo — guessing allowed. Play track 52 to confirm. Key: track 52 — body parts on the dancer photo: A forehead · B shoulder · C elbow · D finger · E thumb · F back · G toe · H ankle · I knee · J chest · K neck · L throat · M chin · N cheek.
  3. ex.2, fast sort into four zones: head: cheek, chin, forehead · body: back, chest, neck, throat · leg: ankle, knee, toe · arm: elbow, finger, shoulder, thumb — then add any other visible parts
  4. Drill the sound traps by ear, three choral repetitions: knee /niː/ — the k is SILENT · thumb /θʌm/ — the b is SILENT · cough /kɒf/ — the gh says f · ankle /ˈæŋkl/ · elbow /ˈelbəʊ/ · throat /θrəʊt/ · stomach ache /ˈstʌmək eɪk/. The silent k and b get a finger on the lips.
  5. Words go into the BODY column as they are confirmed.
📺 On screen: track 52 in the whiteboard app (Backup: say the words yourself and point at your own body — the matching works identically by ear and gesture)
👦 Students do: guess, match, sort, drill. Expect back, finger, knee to be known by sight; chin/cheek to swap places; knee and thumb to be said with the letters sounded until the drill. Typical problem: answers in Russian — English-first rule

Why: The illness words come out of three recorded conversations about real people, so every word lands attached to a story — and the class hears the difference between an ache you describe and an illness you catch.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 (track 53): three conversations, who is who; ex.4–5: the illness words into the gaps

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the scene: Three people, three problems. Sam did sport, Kelly had an accident, Josh might be ill. Listen — who is who? Play track 53. Key: track 53 — three conversations: a Josh (might be ill) · b Kelly (accident, injured) · c Sam (sore legs and arms after sport). Audioscript: TB p.270.
  2. ex.4 books closed, mini-groups, 2 minutes: list every illness word you know; collect onto the ILLNESSES column. Ask which two words are adjectives (broken, sore).
  3. ex.5 in pairs — complete the ten gaps from the box, then replay track 53 to check. Kagan RallyCoach, one pen. Key: track 53 again — 1 Sam: a aches · 2 Kelly: b cut, c sore, d toothache, e broken · 3 Josh: f fever, g cold, h cough, i earache, j flu — stomach ache is the word that is NOT needed.
  4. Frame drill, choral: I've GOT a sore throat. · My back ACHES. · She's BROKEN her thumb. — the frames matter more than the list.
📺 On screen: track 53 in the whiteboard app (Backup: read the three conversations from the audioscript, TB p.270, in two voices — the who-is-who and the gaps work identically by ear)
👦 Students do: listen for who is who, harvest words, complete, drill the frames. Expect flu/cold to blur — one line fixes it (flu is worse and comes with a fever). Typical error: my leg is ache — the frame drill exists for it

Reading · live to 1,000?
solo → pairs
Why: Gist first, then the word harvest, then selected detail questions — the CELTA order. De Grey's article is also the quarry: every will and going to the next stage names is already sitting in a text the class has just argued about.

WHAT WE DO: de Grey's article: gist → the five words → five of the nine detail questions → a fast vote

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hook, books closed: A scientist says the first person to live to ONE THOUSAND is alive right now. Believe it? Ten seconds of noise, then books open.
  2. Gist race, 3 minutes — ex.1: Three readers comment at the end. Who AGREES with him? Key: gist: Damian from London is the reader who agrees with Dr de Grey.
  3. ex.2, pairs, 3 minutes — the five highlighted words. Key: highlighted words: 1 drugs (medicines) · 2 disease (an illness) · 3 damaged (broken, hurt) · 4 alive (opposite of dead) · 5 predict (say something will happen). Drugs = medicines here — flag it, the false friend is real.
  4. ex.3 triaged: items 1, 3, 4, 6, 8 — solo first, then Kagan RallyCoach; the partner must HEAR the line that decides. Key: detail (nine questions; run 1, 3, 4, 6 and 8 in class, rest for fast finishers): 1 people used to die at ~50, now many live past 90 · 2 de Grey believes people will soon live to 1,000 · 3 old age — the diseases of old age · 4 we'll look after and repair damaged bodies the way we service cars · 5 the population would grow quickly — he says health is the priority · 6 they will still die in accidents · 7 both were scientists who thought they knew how to live for ever · 8 Simone thinks living so long would be boring · 9 Damian would like a cure for cancer first
  5. Close with a 60-second vote: What do you think of de Grey's ideas? · Do you agree with Olshansky? · Would you like to live to be 1,000? — TB shape: two reasons FOR, two AGAINST, then a class vote
📺 On screen: track 54 is the recording of the article — optional (Backup: skip the audio entirely; the stage is built on silent reading)
👦 Students do: read for the agreeing reader, mine the words, argue the detail from the line. Expect the car-service comparison to need one gesture; expect the vote to run hot — cap it at a minute and promise the Talking points return. Typical problem: answering from opinion, not the text — point at the line

Grammar · discovery
class → pairs
Why: Guided discovery, not presentation: the rules are extracted from sentences the class met in the article ten minutes ago. Rules they extract are rules they keep.

WHAT WE DO: will and be going to extracted from the article (SB p.66 ex.1 — the page is in their printed books)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB warmer first, books closed: TB grammar warmer: books closed, recall de Grey's article; you reformulate their answers through the target forms — People will live to be 1,000 · Drugs are going to help us stay young — and the class hears the grammar before it is named.
  2. Books open on p.66 — ex.1: match the four example sentences (all from the article!) to rules a–d. Pairs, 3 minutes. Key: match examples to rules: 1 a (predict generally) · 2 d (predict from evidence) · 3 c (already decided) · 4 b (deciding while speaking).
  3. Board card, said as questions: Decided BEFORE? → going to. · Can you SEE it coming? → going to. · Just predicting? → will. · Deciding RIGHT NOW? → will.
  4. Concept checks from the plan header — popcorn, tickets and clouds, cold-called by name.
📺 On screen: the four-question card in chalk — the board is the medium and it stays up into lesson 2, so no backup is needed; ⚠️ SB p.66 is in the students' printed books (not in the scanned PDF) — your keys are in this plan
👦 Students do: match, extract, answer the CCQs. Expect «why not just will everywhere?» — the popcorn CCQ settles it. Typical error: I will to… — one choral fix, will takes the bare verb
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit11_Grammar_Will_Going_To_A5
A5 grammar card — WILL OR GOING TO?: WILL + BASE VERB for an instant decision, an offer or promise, and a prediction based on opinion (I'll help you. · I think you'll feel better soon.) against BE GOING TO + BASE VERB for a plan or intention and a prediction from evidence (I'm going to see the doctor. · Look out! You're going to fall.), with the negatives, the questions (Will you help? · Are you going to rest?) and the DECISION CLUE — decide NOW or I THINK → will · planned BEFORE or look at the evidence → going to
  • Glue the card in and mark the two triggers: a decision now, or a plan before.
  • Colour the two kinds of prediction — opinion against evidence.
  • Write four sentences: an offer, a promise, a plan and an evidence prediction.
  • The clue question goes first, the form second.
When: ~5 min at the start of the “Grammar · discovery” stagePrep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Scissors, glue sticks, pencils.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 Corpus choices + ex.3 completion — with the reason said every time

🎯 Speaking out · the future interview
pairs, then swap
Why: The productive outcome: the two future forms in real questions about real plans and predictions, under time pressure. Errors are logged silently — correcting now would kill fluency.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 personal questions + two of their own — both forms, reasons on demand

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frames on the board — the book's five questions: ex.4 personal questions: 1 Is it going to rain later? · 2 What are you going to do at the weekend? · 3 Do you think you'll go to university? · 4 What kind of job do you think you'll do? · 5 Where will you live when you're older? — TB: elicit the form and WHY for each before pairs answer
  2. One minute solo: everyone notes real answers — two plans (going to) and two predictions (will).
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share, 2 minutes each way: A interviews B with the five questions plus ONE follow-up per answer; swap.
  4. Swap partners once (interviewers move one seat right) and repeat faster — add one health question: What are you going to do to stay healthy this year?
  5. You walk with a paper: log will/going to swaps and dropped be SILENTLY — they open lesson 2.
  6. Close the loop: two interviewers report the best answer — She's going to be a doctor. She decided in Year 5!
📺 On screen: the five interview questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: interview, answer with details, follow up, swap. Expect fluent going-to plans and shakier will predictions — feed the prediction question again in round 2. Typical error: I will to study… — logged, not corrected

Predictions round
groups → class
Why: The TB's own cooler grown into a stage: predictions about 100 years from now force will, plans for the weekend force going to — and the vote gives every sentence an audience.

WHAT WE DO: the TB cooler grown up: predictions for 100 years from now, voted on

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Prompts on the board: TB cooler — prediction prompts: cars · houses · students · mobile phones · food × in 100 years / in 1,000 years; mini-groups predict (In 1,000 years, cars will be able to fly!)

WB Unit 11 (p.44–47)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can use the body and illness vocabulary in context and choose will or be going to with the reason named (prediction · evidence · plan · on-the-spot decision).
  • Subsidiary. Reading a news article for gist and detail (SB p.65); listening to three conversations for who-has-what (track 53); the About-you exchange.
  • Personal. Start the block's HEALTH WALL (body | illnesses — two columns, up all block). Log errors silently in the interview stage; delayed feedback opens lesson 2. Keep TB p.128–129 printed or open — SB p.66 is not in the scanned PDF.
By the end, students can…
  • point to and name the fourteen body parts — with the silent letters silent
  • say what hurts with the right frame (I've got a sore throat · my back aches)
  • choose will or be going to and say WHY in four words
  • ask and answer questions about plans and predictions

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
