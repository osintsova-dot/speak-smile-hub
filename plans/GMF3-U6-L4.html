Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to say HOW they do an action with adverbs of manner: “I (swim) (quickly). / He (plays chess) (badly). / She (rollerblades) (well).”
  • Subsidiary. Sing «Warm up!» (CD3 14); consolidate the adverb quickly noticed in the story (L3) — now drilled properly; discover the rule — usually add -ly, but good→well & fast→fast (Grammar clue); a real how-do-you survey (AB follow-up).
  • Personal. Model each adverb with a big speed/quality gesture (quickly = zoom, slowly = drag, well = thumbs-up, badly = wobble); insist the adverb comes AFTER the verb; keep the mime game moving.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name 8 adverbs of manner (quickly · slowly · badly · well · carefully · loudly · quietly · happily);
  • make sentences: “I swim quickly. / He dances badly.”;
  • use the irregulars well & fast correctly.

Structure
I / You / We / They (swim) (quietly / slowly / well).  ·  He / She (dances / plays chess) (badly / quickly).
Form
verb + adverb. Rule: add -ly (bad→badly, quiet→quietly, slow→slowly). Irregular: good→well, fast→fast (no -ly!).
Pron. watch
quickly /ˈkwɪkli/; badly /ˈbædli/; well /wel/ (not ‘good-ly’!); happily /ˈhæpɪli/ (y→i + ly).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He plays chess badly — is he GOOD or BAD at chess?” → bad!
“good — what's the adverb?” → well (NOT good-ly!).

Assumptions: quickly was met in the story (L3); sports verbs (swim, ride, play, dance, cycle) are known. The other adverbs + the good→well / fast→fast irregulars are the new focus. Readers can read short adverb words.

27–37′
How do you…? survey
mingle → desks

Model first. Ask 2 pupils publicly: How do you sing? — I sing loudly! How do you swim? — I swim slowly.

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade survey. Each child holds an action card + a Me/Friend grid. Music → mingle; off → pair: A How do you dance? B answers truthfully I dance happily. Tick the grid, TRADE cards, new partner. 5–6 exchanges.

Harvest. Who sings loudly? Hands up! Find someone who swims like you!

✔ Assess: circulate — is the adverb AFTER the verb, and is it well not good? Recast, note names.

💪 Support: card shows the full question text. ⭐ Extension: add: Why? — Because it's easy!

Mime game / build

▶ Talk Partners mime game (act. 4). A player mimes a verb + a manner (swim slowly, play loudly); the team guesses: Swim slowly! — Yes! That's right! Point per correct guess; rotate the mimer — everyone mimes once.

Make true sentences (Showdown). Mini-whiteboards: you show a verb + a manner face (🚀 quickly / 🐌 slowly) → all write I cycle quickly. / I walk slowly. — Show me! Self-check. 4 rounds.

ICQ: Does the adverb come before or after the verb? (→ after!)
