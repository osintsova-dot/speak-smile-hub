🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Two reading moves that decide most marks: telling the whole-text question from a detail question, and choosing the word in a gap by the words around it, not by its meaning alone.
What is genuinely hard
Teenagers answer the general question first, from an impression, and then bend the details to fit. And in a gapped text they choose the word they know rather than the word the sentence needs — problem and trouble mean the same thing until you count them.
Typical error to expect
They pick the option that repeats a word from the text. The correct option is almost always paraphrased; the wrong ones copy the wording [exam report]
Students mark False where the text simply says nothing: they confuse «contradicts» and «not mentioned». In the exam that third option is Not stated [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
Each part is taught through one worked item rather than a list of rules, so the strategy arrives attached to a real question. Part 3 goes first because its single move — find the question that is not about a paragraph — is visible on the page and can be done before anything is answered; ex.1 in the book asks for exactly that, so the book is doing the teaching. Each part then gets a timed silent run, because a strategy that only works with help is not a strategy. Distractor analysis has a stage of its own at 58–70′ rather than being folded into feedback: looking at how the trap was built teaches far more than ticking answers, and it needs students to already own their choices. The OGE version comes last on purpose — by then the skill is warm, and the third option, «Not stated», is met while there is still somebody in the room to argue with about it.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Open page one hundred and twenty-three. Five questions, four paragraphs. One of those questions is not about a paragraph at all — find it first and the other four get easier. Then six gaps, and a warning: the word you know is not always the word that fits.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, the test handed back with one shared error repaired, and the bridge into the Prepare for the exam section

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last lesson rewind. Hand the test papers back and give exactly 2 minutes of silent reading. Look at your five mistakes. Do not compare with your neighbour yet.
  3. One error the whole block shares goes on the board and the class repairs it chorally: While I cleaned the kitchen, I saw the window. → While I was cleaning the kitchen, I saw the window.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): You showed what you know. Now look at what it is FOR — open page one hundred and twenty-three.
📺 On screen: the sentence to be corrected goes on the board — the board is the medium here and the papers are on paper, so nothing has to be displayed and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: look through their own papers and repair the shared sentence. Expect a calm reaction if the test was honestly «from the classroom». Typical problem: they start comparing marks at once — hold the rule «your own mistakes first»

Why: Frames the whole lesson: the skill is one, only the wrapping differs. That single idea stops the exam feeling like a separate subject.

WHAT WE DO: set the frame of the day: the skill is one, but it can be asked for in different ways

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Draw two columns on the board, without any drama. On the left, in the coursebook: one long text, five questions, three options each. On the right, in the OGE: one long text, seven statements, and THREE answers — true, false, not stated.
  2. The key line of the lesson, and it gets repeated: Different papers, same skill: find the line, then read what it actually says.
  3. Say the exam bit calmly and briefly: Some of you will take an English exam in Year 9, some of you will not. Today is not about the exam — it is about reading properly.
  4. Set the bar: By the end you can say WHY a wrong answer is wrong. That is the whole skill.
📺 On screen: a slide with the two columns «in the coursebook / in the OGE» (Backup: the same two columns in chalk on the board — they stay up all lesson and are needed again at 70–82′)
👦 Students do: compare the two columns. Expect the third option on the right to be spotted immediately — that is the way into the 70–82′ stage. Typical problem: they start worrying about the exam; take it off the table in one sentence

WHAT WE DO: the format of Part 3 and the one move that decides it (SB p.123 ex.1)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Warm the topic first, TB p.253: TB p.253, before anybody reads: is there anything good about living in a very small home, and anything bad about living in a very large one? Then, books open, look at the photo: what rooms and furniture are in that house?
  2. Read the Exam information: One long article and five questions with three options each. One of the five asks about the text AS A WHOLE; the other four ask about details, and they come in the same order as the information in the text.
  3. Put the four Exam tips on the board: 1) one or two questions test your understanding of the whole text; 2) the other questions test details, opinions or feelings; 3) the detail questions follow the order of the text — question 2 is answered before question 3 is; 4) don't just match a word in the option with a word in the text. Think about the meaning.
  4. Give them three minutes to read the article about Luke, then check comprehension before any question is answered: TB p.253, general comprehension before the questions: «Is this the first small house?» (no) · «How much did it cost to build?» ($1,500) · «How many rooms has it got?» (two — a kitchen and sitting area downstairs, a bedroom upstairs).
  5. Now ex.1, and it is the whole stage: The first move of the lesson, and it is ex.1 on the page: five questions, four paragraphs — find the question that is not about a paragraph. Key: question 1. How do you know? It asks about the purpose of the text, not about a detail.
  6. Kagan Think-Pair-Share: Five questions, four paragraphs. Which question is different, and how do you know?
📺 On screen: the article and the five questions on the screen, with the general question circled live once it has been found (Backup: everybody works from the book on p.123 and you write the four Exam tips on the board before the lesson)
👦 Students do: read, then find the whole-text question. Expect two or three of them to point at question 5. Typical error: they decide question 1 is «easy» and answer it immediately — the pencil cross rule stops it

19–33′
Reading Part 3 · exam conditions
solo → pairs
Why: Timed, silent, no help: the strategy only counts if it survives real conditions.

WHAT WE DO: questions 2–5 in exam conditions, then question 1, then a paired check where the reason is the answer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silent mode: Eight minutes. Alone. Questions two to five first. Question one LAST. Write the finish time on the board.
  2. After 8 minutes — Kagan RallyCoach: partners take turns explaining their choice. Not the letter — the line. 4 minutes.
  3. Collect answers with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Question four — number ones, boards up!
  4. Key (TB p.253): 1 B · 2 A · 3 C · 4 B · 5 C (TB p.253)
  5. Do not explain it yourself — ask the students who got it right to read out the line.
📺 On screen: the finish time written on the board — the board is the medium here, no backup needed; while they work nothing is displayed at all and the task is open in the book on p.123
👦 Students do: work in silence, then explain their choices in pairs. Expect 3–4 of 5 from most of them. Typical error: question 3 — they take A because Luke's dad is mentioned as helping, and miss the word coach

Reading Part 4 · format
class → pairs
Why: One worked gap shows that half of these items are decided by the word next door, not by the meaning of the word itself.

WHAT WE DO: the format of Part 4 and the first gap worked through together (SB p.124)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed, the lead-in from TB p.254: who did you write your last email to, and why? Then the title on the board: «The city where people can send emails to trees» (SB p.124): Melbourne gave each of its 70,000 trees an ID number and an email address so that people could report a problem — and got love letters to the trees instead. The workers answered, in the trees' name.
  2. Read the Exam information: A short factual article with six gaps. For every gap there are three words to choose from. Half the items are about which word simply goes with the others (collocation), half are about grammar.
  3. Put the three Exam tips on the board: 1) read the whole sentence before you choose — the gap is decided by the words around it; 2) look carefully at the words on both sides of the gap; 3) with some questions you have to think about grammar, not only meaning.
  4. Work gap 1 through together: Question 1 is done together, as the page asks. The answer is B letting: the expression is let someone know. Make someone aware and get to know exist too, but neither of them fits here (TB p.254).
  5. Pre-teach one word before the timed run: TB p.254: pre-teach join in — to become involved in an activity with other people.
  6. Name the physical rule of the stage: finger on the word before the gap, finger on the word after it, read the sentence to the end.
📺 On screen: the article with the six gaps on the screen, underlined live around gap 1 (Backup: copy the first sentence and its three options onto the board before the lesson — the students have exactly the same text open in the book on p.124)
👦 Students do: work gap 1 through in pairs and name the collocation. Expect making to be chosen by several of them first. Typical error: they translate the three options and choose the closest — the expression decides, not the meaning

Reading Part 4 · exam conditions
solo → pairs
Why: Second timed run, with one added rule: read to the end of the sentence before you choose.

WHAT WE DO: gaps 2–6 in exam conditions, then a paired check where the rule is named

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silent mode: Six minutes. Alone. Read the whole sentence before you choose. Write the finish time on the board.
  2. After 6 minutes — check in pairs, 4 minutes: Say the rule, not the letter. Why does that word fit and the other two not?
  3. Kagan Showdown for the two hardest, 2 and 5: the team writes the letter and reveals on command.
  4. Key (TB p.254): 1 B · 2 C · 3 A · 4 B · 5 B · 6 C (TB p.254)
  5. Say nothing about the errors yet — the analysis stage is next.
📺 On screen: the finish time on the board — the board is the medium here and no backup is needed, since nothing is displayed while they work and the text is open in the book on p.124
👦 Students do: work in silence, then justify in pairs. Expect 4–5 of 6. Typical error: gap 5 — explained looks right until somebody says the whole phrase out loud

Why the wrong answer is wrong
class
Why: Analysing distractors teaches more than checking answers: they see the trap being built, so they recognise it next time.

WHAT WE DO: analysis item by item: why this option is right and why the others are not

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go through Part 3 on the formula «line → why»: 1 B — the text describes his personal project. A is wrong: it is not advice for teenage builders. C is wrong: the text never says what other people think of small houses. · 2 A — «he was bored and looking for a fun activity» during the school holidays. B is wrong: there is nothing about his neighbours' house. C is wrong: the text does not say he met anybody. · 3 C — Luke's dad was his coach. A is wrong: the text says Luke did most of the work. B is wrong: Luke paid for the house himself. · 4 B — «there was a lot he didn't know». A is wrong: he «had no idea what to do» at the start, so it was not easy. C is wrong: nothing in the text says he broke anything. · 5 C — «he'd like to live in it full time». A is wrong: the next house will be bigger, not the same. B is wrong: there is no information about building for friends.
  2. Then Part 4, and here the formula changes to «phrase → why»: 2 C — problem means something wrong. Trouble is uncountable or plural, so «a trouble» cannot stand there. · 3 A — the expression is make something safe. · 4 B — the emails were unexpected, so it is a surprise. · 5 B — you tell somebody something, but you explain something to somebody and speak to somebody about something. · 6 C — wonderful means very nice. Glad means pleased and needs an infinitive after it: I'm glad to meet you.
  3. Name the conclusion for Part 3: Where marks go: they answer the general question first, from an impression, instead of last, from the whole text; and they take the option that repeats a word they can see.
  4. And for Part 4: Where marks go: they choose by the meaning of the single word and never read to the end of the sentence, so the grammar items (2, 5) go first.
  5. One question that turns the analysis into speaking, TB p.254: Are trees important in our town? Would people here write to them?
📺 On screen: highlight the evidence lines straight in the text on the screen (Backup: read every line out loud while students underline in their own books, and keep a «question → whose line» table on the board)
👦 Students do: find the lines and explain them. Expect the «no information about this» distractors to be the discovery of the lesson. Typical error: they keep hunting for the exact word — send them back to the line

🎯 OGE version · the third option
teams
Why: The same skill in the Russian exam wrapper, done live in the trainer so the transfer is experienced, not promised — and «Not stated» is met while there is somebody to argue with.

WHAT WE DO: the same skill in the Russian wrapper: True / False / Not stated in the school trainer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go back to the two columns on the board and open the right-hand one: One long text and seven statements. For each statement students choose one of THREE options: 1 True — the text says it · 2 False — the text says the opposite · 3 Not stated — the text simply does not say. Today's text: «The 1900 House» — a British reality programme in which the Bowler family lived for three months as people lived in 1900. The block topic exactly: a home and the past.
  2. What is the same: Same skill as Reading Part 3 in the coursebook: the answer is in the text, but said in other words — you have to find the line and read what it actually says. What is different: One difference, and it is the whole difficulty: the coursebook gives right/wrong, the Russian exam gives a THIRD option. «Not stated» does not mean «I didn't find it» — it means the text does not talk about this at all. Statements 2 and 7 are exactly that.
  3. Write the rule on the board before they start, it is the whole stage: The rule that settles every argument: False needs a line that says the OPPOSITE. If there is no such line, the answer is Not stated.
  4. Open the trainer link (the same one goes home as optional homework): https://speakandsmile.online/oge.html#/hw?reading:tf_E03AA3 — it opens straight away, no password.
  5. Teams of 3–4, one phone or laptop per team, 7 minutes. Seven statements. For every FALSE you must read out the line that says the opposite. If you can't find one, it's Not stated.
  6. Kagan Showdown: on your command every team reveals its answer to statements 2 and 7 at once. Check against: 1 False · 2 Not stated · 3 False · 4 False · 5 True · 6 True · 7 Not stated. The app shows the key itself the moment students submit.
  7. Trap workshop (3 minutes): each team writes ONE more statement about the same text that is Not stated but looks true. The winning trap is the one that fools the most people.
📺 On screen: the trainer task open on the screen or on the teams' phones (Backup with no internet: print the same task in advance through «Print worksheet» in the teacher mode of the app, one sheet per team — then nothing has to be displayed at all)
👦 Students do: do the OGE version and invent a Not stated statement. Expect statements 2 and 7 to be marked False by most teams first time — that is the point of the stage. Typical error: they treat «I didn't find it» as False; the line rule is the cure

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
Five questions, four paragraphs — is one paragraph used twice? → no: one question is about the whole text. Find it before you start.
An option contains a word from the text — good news or suspicious? → suspicious: the correct option is usually paraphrased.
«There is no information about this in the text» — does that make the option right or wrong? → wrong. In a multiple-choice question an option with no evidence is simply not the answer.
In the OGE, the text says nothing at all about it — is that False? → no, that is Not stated. False needs a line that says the OPPOSITE.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They answer question 1 first and then bend the details to fit itMake the order compulsory: question 1 is answered LAST. Mark it with a pencil cross before the timer starts, and check the crosses when you collect.
In the gapped text they choose by the meaning of the single wordOne physical rule: put a finger on the word before the gap and the word after it, and read the whole sentence out under your breath before you choose.
«Why are we doing two exams at once?» — at the OGE stageAnswer honestly and briefly: there is one exam, it is two years away, and not everybody here will sit it. Today is not about the exam but about the fact that one and the same skill can be asked in different ways.
They mark False where the text simply says nothingThe rule that settles it, and it is worth writing on the board: False needs a line that says the opposite. No such line — Not stated.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
