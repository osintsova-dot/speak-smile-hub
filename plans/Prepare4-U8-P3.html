🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the technology phrasal verbs (look up, plug in, shut down, switch on/off, turn up/down, take out) with the pronoun rule — turn IT off — and write a real informal email (~100 words) that answers every question, recommends and gives opinions.
What is genuinely hard
The particles look interchangeable (turn up? switch up?) and the pronoun position is invisible to Russian — turn off it feels fine. In the email, the killer is not grammar but DISCIPLINE: answering every question the friend asked.
Typical error to expect
«turn off it» — the pronoun must split the phrasal verb: turn it off, look it up. And «I recommend you to try» — the box phrase is I'd recommend it / You should try it.
Why the lesson is built this way
A two-part lesson with one spine: the phrasal verbs are learned in the first third and then WORK inside the email (Have you heard…? You should plug it in and turn it up). The verbs arrive against the book's picture panel with the particle logic made visible, go physical in the mime relay, and settle in the pronoun drill. The writing half is strict model-first CELTA: mine Paul's reply for craft (answers, recommendations, opinions, informal frames), plan against Abbie's questions, write ~100 words in class, peer-check against the checklist. This is B1 Writing Part 1 without saying so twice.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Your friend writes: long car trip, need games and podcasts — help! You have 100 words to save their holiday. First, the six little verbs every English speaker uses about technology — then the email that answers everything.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, passive clinic, and the bridge into phrasal verbs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — delayed feedback. Three passive slips from the fact-factory on the board, anonymous. Kagan RallyRobin: pairs fix them, fastest pair performs.

  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the page: Books open, page eighty-eight, bottom half. Eight pictures, eight buttons.

Why: The book's own pairwork run as a relay: actions are mimed, phrasal verbs are shouted, the particle earns the point. Movement in the middle of a writing lesson, and it still teaches.

WHAT WE DO: the book's pairwork as a relay: actions mimed, verbs called, particles win points

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two lines facing the board. You (or a strong student, TB's suggestion) mime an action from p.124: plugging in a kettle, turning down a radio, looking up a word.
  2. First line to call the FULL phrasal verb — with the pronoun inside — takes the point: Plug it in!
  3. Last round reversed: you say the verb, both lines mime; slowest mime sits. Sit everyone down on a clap — writing half begins.
📺 On screen: actions and voice — the body is the medium, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: watch, call, mime, compete. The particles are now attached to muscles; the room has spent its energy exactly before the quiet half

Peer review + polish
pairs
Why: A checklist swap turns every student into an editor: they catch each other's unanswered questions and missing opinions faster than they catch their own — both sides learn.

WHAT WE DO: checklist swap: every student becomes an editor

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Checklist on the board: 1 all three questions answered? 2 a recommendation phrase? 3 an opinion phrase? 4 informal opener AND closer? 5 one phrasal verb? (bonus)
  2. Kagan Pairs Compare: swap notebooks, tick the checklist in pencil, write ONE compliment and ONE fix at the bottom.
  3. Authors take their emails back, apply the fix, and read the compliment. Two minutes of silent polishing.
  4. You collect the three emails you underlined most — tomorrow's delayed feedback.
📺 On screen: the checklist in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: edit a partner honestly, take an edit gracefully, polish. Both sides learn; the checklist keeps the feedback kind and concrete

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«turn off it» — where does it live? → inside: turn it off. Nouns can go either side, pronouns can't.
«shut down» vs «switch off» — same? → shut down = the computer's long goodbye; switch off = the button. Both die, differently.
An email answers two of Abbie's three questions — full marks? → never. Every question gets an answer, that IS the mark.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Particles swap randomly (turn in, switch up)The picture logic on the wall: UP = more, DOWN = less/dead, IN = connected, OUT = removed, OFF = dead. Wrong particle → point at the wall map, student self-corrects.
The pronoun jumps outside: «look up it»The fist-beat drill: turn — it — UP, three beats, pronoun on the middle beat. Run it choral twice; then just beat the rhythm silently when the error returns.
Emails answer some questions and wander offThe plan grid is compulsory: three questions = three rows, one line each BEFORE writing. The peer checklist's first line is «All questions answered?» — a missing answer costs the point there, not in your red pen.
Fast finishers in the writing stageTB's cooler, quietly: list items (football results, radio, music, speakers) and match phrasal verbs to them; or upgrade one sentence of the email with a second opinion phrase.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
