🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use eight verbs of hopes and dreams (achieve, admire, aim, choose, dream, encourage, imagine, try your best) with their fixed patterns, and read a real article for the arguments behind five suggestions.
What is genuinely hard
These eight verbs are known passively and used wrongly, because each one carries a pattern that Russian does not signal: dream OF doing, encourage somebody TO do, aim TO do, keep ON doing. A candidate who knows the word and not the pattern writes «I dream to be a doctor» and loses a lexical mark. The second difficulty is the topic itself: teenagers asked about their future in April of an exam year produce silence or a joke, and the lesson has to give them something concrete to talk about instead.
Typical error to expect
«I dream about be a doctor», «he encouraged me study» — the patterns are fixed: dream OF + -ing, encourage somebody TO do, aim TO do, keep ON + -ing. A lexical-choice item is often decided by nothing but this.
«I achieved to pass», «he admired to her» — achieve takes a THING (achieve success, achieve your aim), not an action, and admire takes a person with no preposition at all.
Why the lesson is built this way
The verbs arrive through three teenagers who have actually done something — a pianist, a footballer and a girl going to study in the United States — so the field is concrete before it is personal. The gap-fill is run with the Teacher's Book clue method rather than as a memory test: look at the word AFTER the gap, because the preposition gives the verb away, which is simultaneously the exam skill. The reading is the article that saves the discussion — five practical summer ideas, one of which every student in the room could actually do — and the productive stage turns it into a plan with a date on it. This is the last new-vocabulary lesson of the course and it is worth ending it on something they can use in June.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Four weeks after this course finishes, you have about ten free weeks. The article on page one hundred and thirteen is five things you could actually do with them, and one of them earns money. Today: the eight verbs English uses for wanting something — and what each of them needs after it.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, marked emails returned with a class-wide headline, and three hopes on a timeline

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what is one thing you are going to do this summer? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — the emails come back, marked on the four notes first. By name, with one sentence each. Say the class-wide headline out loud: how many people answered all four notes. That number is the lesson.
  3. 1 minute — Kagan Think-Pair-Share: read your partner the note you answered least well and say in one sentence what you would add. Thirty seconds each way.
  4. Then the unit warmer, which takes ninety seconds and opens the topic sideways rather than head-on: three time phrases on the board — next week · next month · next year — and one thing you hope to have done by each. Model one of your own first.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the unit: Page one hundred and twelve. Unit twenty — the last unit of this book, and it is about what happens next.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4–5: the clue method, then the eight verbs used about themselves

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that drills the eight patterns

WHAT WE DO: the article turned into a plan with a date on it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say the frame out loud and write the number on the board: after the final test there are about ten free weeks. That is the planning horizon, and it is close enough to be real.
  2. Six minutes, silent, individually: write FIVE sentences about your own summer, each one using a different verb from the pattern wall, and at least three of them concrete enough to have a date or a place in them. Model two of your own on the board first, one ambitious and one small.
  3. Rule: the word after each verb is underlined. That is the only thing being corrected today.
  4. 🎯 Kagan Timed Pair Share, two minutes each way: read your five. The listener asks one question that makes ONE of them more concrete — When? With whom? What is the first step?
  5. Then Kagan Mix-Pair-Share, six minutes standing: three new partners, one sentence each time, and each new partner has to say whether they would join in. It is the last mingle of the course and it should be noisy.
  6. Four minutes, floor: three plans read out that somebody else offered to join. Write them on the board and leave them there for the next lesson.
  7. Delayed correction, three minutes: five patterns you heard wrong, on the board, repaired by the class by pointing at the wall.

Structure
When he was younger, my dad dreamed of being an astronaut. · Mum encouraged me to take up dance classes. · I aim to be fit by the end of the year. · You tried your best and no one can ask more.
Form
dream OF + -ing · encourage somebody TO + infinitive · aim TO + infinitive · keep ON + -ing · imagine + -ing or a clause · admire + a person, no preposition · achieve + a thing, not an action · choose TO + infinitive.
Pron. watch
achieve /əˈtʃiːv/ — two syllables, stress on the second, and the class will say a three-syllable version · imagine /ɪˈmædʒɪn/ keeps its stress in the middle · in a phrasal verb the PREPOSITION carries the stress and that is how a listener hears it: I missed OUT, we worked it OUT · and in verbs with two objects the sentence stress lands on the last word, which is why «she gave me a BOOK» and «she gave the book to ME» mean slightly different things
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I dream to be a pilot» — right? → no. Dream OF BEING. The preposition is part of the word.
«She encouraged me study harder» — what is missing? → to. Encourage somebody TO do.
Can you achieve an action? → no. You achieve a THING — success, a goal, a place at university.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«What do you want to do with your life?» produces silenceDo not ask it. Ask what they are doing in the ten weeks after this course finishes. Concrete, close, answerable — and the eight verbs fit it exactly.
The eight verbs get used without their patternsThe wall is a PATTERN wall from the first minute: nothing goes on it without the word that follows it. Every correction is a point at the wall.
The gap-fill turns into guessingTeach the clue method out loud on item 1 the way the Teacher's Book does: the word after the gap is OF, so the verb has to be dream. Then run items 2 to 8 the same way. It is also, exactly, how a lexical-choice exam item is solved.
Fast finishers in the readingThe Teacher's Book task: list any words or phrases they want to check in a dictionary, guess the meaning from context first, and report both after the class check.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
