🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Reactivate and interleave the language of four units — feelings and -ed/-ing (U13), TV/film and relative clauses (U14), computers and the passive (U15), experiments and conditionals (U16) — through the book's own Review 4 pages, run as team formats.
What is genuinely hard
Interleaving IS the difficulty: four grammars side by side mean the first job of every item is naming WHICH rule it tests. That skill — diagnose, then apply — is what separates review from repetition.
Typical error to expect
Cross-unit contamination: passives sprouting in perfect-tense items, will inside if-clauses, who/which swapped. The auction format makes teams SAY the rule before betting — the diagnosis becomes the game.
Why the lesson is built this way
The book's Review 4 spread (p.96–97) carried by team formats so nobody does a worksheet alone: the TB passive-columns warmer, the word-square race with category naming, a grammar auction on ex.1–2 where the rule must be named to collect, transformations with TB's structure hints on your desk, and the exam cloze (B1 Reading Part 6) run with the read-whole-text-first method. The finale is the FULL 21-card List it! deck — all four units in one deck for the first time — and the TB reflection cooler closes the block properly. Block 7 covered U13–14; nothing on the spread is off-limits.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Four units. Sixteen word categories. Four grammars. One lesson. Today nothing is new — and that's the point: everything on these two pages you already own. Let's prove it, loudly, in teams.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: hand-back, class-level feedback, and the Review 4 frame

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — test hand-back, engineered calm. Papers returned face down; scores are private. One class-level sentence: As a class we owned the vocabulary and leaked marks on the if-side — today repairs exactly that.
  3. Remind the standing homework: five wrong answers rewritten right — your entry ticket was this; I'll collect a glance later.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): frame the day: Pages ninety-six and ninety-seven. Four units, nothing new, everything yours. Teams in three minutes.
📺 On screen: papers handed back by hand — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: receive results without theatre, hear the class pattern, keep the individual story private

Review · grammar auction
teams of 3–4
Why: The Review sentences run as an auction: teams bet on their answers, and the rule must be NAMED to collect — because naming the rule (perfect? passive? conditional? relative?) is the transferable skill.

WHAT WE DO: Grammar ex.1–2 as an auction: name the rule, bet, collect

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules of the auction: each team starts with 100 points; for every item they write an answer, NAME the rule (perfect / passive / conditional / relative), and bet 5–20. Right answer + right rule = bet doubled; right answer + wrong rule = half; wrong = lost.
  2. Run ex.1 items 1–10. Key: 1 Have, read · 2 is · 3 isn't made · 4 does · 5 has, arrived · 6 is served · 7 forgets · 8 've, had · 9 downloads · 10 'll have to.
  3. Then ex.2 prompts (make sentences) for the brave — double stakes. Key: 1 Have you uploaded your film yet? · 2 This film isn't/hasn't been translated into Spanish · 3 Jon has just shut down his computer · 4 If I fill the kettle, will you make the coffee? · 5 The app opens when you enter your password · 6 The British actors aren't mentioned in the reviews · 7 The rubber ring won't float unless you fill it with air · 8 Sonia has already wrapped your present.
  4. Kagan Numbered Heads Together inside teams: you call the number, that member answers for the bet.
  5. Running scoreboard on the board; the rule-name column matters more than the points — say so.
📺 On screen: items read from the book, scoreboard in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: diagnose, bet, defend. Expect passive-vs-perfect confusion on items 3/6 vs 1/5 — exactly the interleaving the stage exists for

Review · exam cloze
solo → pairs
Why: B1 Reading Part 6 under classroom conditions with the TB method: read the whole text first, name the part of speech for every gap, accept both answers where both are right.

WHAT WE DO: Grammar ex.6 = B1 Reading Part 6, run with the ritual

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The ritual announced: Whole text first, no pencil. What is it about? A prize for young engineers. NOW the gaps.
  2. Solo, 5 minutes; for each gap name the part of speech before the word. Pairs compare. Key: B1 Reading Part 6 «Prizes for young engineers»: 1 are (auxiliary) · 2 a (article) · 3 few (quantifier) · 4 made/produced (verb — both correct) · 5 has (auxiliary) · 6 that/which (relative pronoun — both correct).
  3. Feedback names parts of speech aloud; gaps 4 and 6 announced as double-answer gaps — two right words exist, both score.
  4. One exam sentence, said once: This exact format is in the B1 exam — and you just did it cold.
📺 On screen: SB p.97 ex.6 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read whole, name the part of speech, fill, compare. Expect few (gap 3) to be the hardest — a quantifier nobody expects; the part-of-speech naming catches it

Why: The full 21-card List it! deck — all four units in one deck for the first time. The block goes out on a competition, not on a worksheet.

WHAT WE DO: List it! with the FULL 21-card deck — all four units, first time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The full deck for the first time: all 21 cards — the eight U13–14 cards block 7 played, plus the thirteen U15–16 cards from yesterday. Teams of one to three; a pile of «List 5…» cards face down. A player draws a card and reads it out. ALL teams race to write five valid items; the first to five shouts «Listed!» and everyone stops. The fast team reads its list; other teams tick off items they also have. Scoring: 1 point for finishing first + 1 per item nobody else has + 1 if the whole list is spelled correctly — so spelling wins games.
  2. Six rounds by the clock; spelling point strictly enforced — it is the whole point.
  3. File: course folder → материалы → Review Games Worksheets → PREPARE_4_Review_game_Units_13-16.pdf.

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«This film ___ (translate) into Spanish» — which machine? → passive: the film doesn't translate itself.
«I'll remind her if she ___ (forget)» — will in the if-side? → never: forgets.
«The actor ___ plays Juliet» — who or which? → who — she's a person; that also legal.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Hand-back emotions eat the lessonFive timed minutes, scores face down, one sentence of class-level feedback (as a class, the if-side cost us most — watch it today), then forward. Individual conversations wait for the break.
The auction rewards gamblers, not grammariansBetting is only valid AFTER the team names the rule; a right answer with a wrong rule-name pays half. The scoring enforces the pedagogy.
The cloze is rushed by winners' adrenalineThe method is a ritual: whole text read aloud once, THEN gaps, part of speech named for each. Both double-answer gaps (4, 6) are announced — two right answers exist.
Reflection becomes a formalityTB's cooler questions demand THREE concrete self-help moves written down — cut-up sentences, online practice, record yourself. Collect a glance at each list at the door.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
