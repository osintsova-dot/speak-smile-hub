Week 03.09–06.09 · Lesson 1✨ FIRST LESSON of the year~10 pupils · pairs + 2 teamsPupil's Book pp.4, 7 · AB pp.2, 5
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to give personal information — “I'm (Lily). I'm (nine). I live with my (grandparents).” — and ask the four questions: “What's your name? How old are you? Where do you live? Who do you live with?”
  • Subsidiary. Meet the course characters (Lily, Josh, Ravi & big brother Luke) and the lighthouse; re-establish routines & signals after the summer; sing «Welcome to the lighthouse!» (CD1 02); retrieve Level 2 vocabulary (activities · animals · transport · places · numbers) and count 1–100.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; re-train the signals patiently — this lesson sets the tone for the whole year. Give extra wait-time — words are rusty after summer.
By the end, pupils can…
  • ask & answer the four personal-information questions;
  • say 4 true facts about themselves (name · age · where · who with);
  • count and read numbers 1–100 (teen vs -ty).

Structure
What's your name? — I'm (Lily).  ·  How old are you? — I'm (nine).  ·  Where do you live? — I live in a (flat).  ·  Who do you live with? — I live with my (grandparents).
Form
Wh-word + are/do + you…? Answer with I'm (= I am) / I live with my + family. Numbers: teen (thir-teen) vs -ty (thir-ty) — stress moves.
Pron. watch
thirteen /θɜːˈtiːn/ vs thirty /ˈθɜːti/ (13–30, 14–40, 15–50); grandparents /ˈɡrænpeərənts/; contraction I'm /aɪm/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“How old are you? — do I answer with my name or my age?” → my age.
“Who do you live with? — the people at home, yes?” → yes, my family.

Assumptions: Pupils finished GMF2 in May: greetings, numbers 1–20, free-time activities, animals, transport and places are passive after the summer — plan extra recall time. Numbers 20–100 are shaky. They read & write short sentences (readers). Carpet & team routines need re-training after the break.

30–38′
Numbers 1–100 sprint
carpet → desks, 2 teams

Count & chant. Count in tens 10–100 with claps; then the teen/-ty trap on the board (13–30, 14–40, 15–50): thir-TEEN… THIR-ty! — you point, class calls, every child answers every time.

▶ Number Showdown (readers). Mini-whiteboards: you say a number — Write sixty-seven! — all write, Show me! Reveal together, team point per correct board. 8 rounds (mix teens & tens).

💪 Support: number line 1–100 on the desk to copy from. ⭐ Extension: write the number in words: sixty-seven.

✔ Assess: stand in the flow — can they choose are/do correctly? Note names for tomorrow's recycling.
