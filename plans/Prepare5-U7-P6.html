🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Recognise and use transitive and intransitive uses of the cooking verbs (boil the carrots / the soup boiled), and make suggestions, weigh options and reach an agreement with a partner — Cambridge Speaking Part 3 — while training Listening Part 1 on seven picture questions.
What is genuinely hard
English marks the difference with word order alone; Russian marks it with a reflexive ending (варится, жарится), so students look for a word that is not there and end up producing «the water is boiling the soup». The speaking is hard for a social reason rather than a linguistic one: at B1 a pair will agree in ten seconds to avoid the discomfort of disagreeing, and the exam gives marks for exactly the discussion they are skipping.
Typical error to expect
«I boiled» with no object, «the water boils the soup» — the same English verb can take an object or not, and the meaning flips. Russian marks the difference with a reflexive ending (варится), English marks it with word order alone.
In the speaking, the predictable loss is the instant yes: Sushi? — OK. Two turns, no marks. The Prepare to speak box exists to make the middle of the conversation happen.
Why the lesson is built this way
The grammar half is short and precise because the concept is small: underline the object, notice that two sentences do not have one, name it. Then the one-minute column game from p.136 turns it into production with a score attached — two points for the same verb used both ways, which is the only thing worth practising here. The listening is prepared the way the exam wants it prepared: the pictures are described BEFORE the recording, so the words are in the room when they are said. The speaking is built backwards — first the class decides what matters when choosing food, then they hear two people doing it, then they reconstruct the phrases from memory, and only then do they do the exam task. Doing the task twice with different partners is not padding; the second run is where the phrases actually get used.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two friends have one day and one cooking course. Pasta, sushi, bread, chocolate. They have to agree — and agreeing immediately is the one thing that loses marks. Today: how to disagree politely, in English.”

WHAT WE DO: the p.136 extra activity with its scoring, which is the whole point

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Send them to p.136 and read the rules out loud: SB p.136, Unit 14 Vocabulary: each student takes one column of three verbs (1 barbecue, roast, bite · 2 fry, burn, stir · 3 boil, taste, freeze · 4 steam, grill, bake) and has ONE minute. One point for a correct sentence, TWO points for a pair of sentences using the same verb transitively and intransitively
  2. Each student chooses a column of three verbs. ONE minute, silent, writing.
  3. Scoring is public and is done by the class: a sentence is read aloud, the class decides whether it is correct BEFORE the point is given. One point for a correct sentence, two for a pair using the same verb transitively and intransitively.
  4. Take four students' pairs to the board — I baked a cake. / The cake is baking. — because seeing the pair side by side is what fixes the concept.
  5. Second minute for anyone who wants to improve their score with one more pair. Then stop it: the game is a tool, not the lesson.
📺 On screen: the winning pairs written on the board side by side — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone writes against the same clock, and the class judges each sentence before any point is awarded

WHAT WE DO: ex.2, track 54: the exam task under exam conditions, then checked with evidence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Track 54, first hearing, straight through, no pausing. Pens down for nothing — they answer as they listen.
  2. Pairs compare answers, 90 seconds, and mark the ones they disagree on. Those are the ones to listen for.
  3. Second hearing, whole. Say what it is for: You are not deciding again. You are checking the two you rejected.
  4. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Listening Part 1, track 54, the seven pictures: 1 B · 2 A · 3 C · 4 A · 5 B · 6 B · 7 A. For any disputed item, replay those fifteen seconds and ask what was actually said about the other two pictures.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 (SB p.137): the exam task, then repeated with a new partner

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Send them to p.137 and read the task aloud: SB p.137: «Two friends are planning to attend a one-day cooking course. Here are some different things they could learn to cook. Talk together about the different things they could cook and say which would be most useful to learn.» The pictures are fresh pasta, chocolates, a loaf of bread, sushi, soup and a chocolate pudding
  2. Same-ability pairs, as the Teacher's Book asks. Two minutes to look at the pictures and note useful words; collect a few on the board.
  3. The rule, said once and enforced by walking past: No decision until you have considered at least three of the options aloud, with a reason each. Agreeing immediately is the only way to fail this.
  4. First run: three minutes. Monitor with a sheet and write down six good phrases WITH names and six errors WITHOUT them. Interrupt nobody.
  5. Change partners. Second run, three minutes, same task. It is faster, better and fuller — that is the point of doing it twice.
  6. Two volunteer pairs repeat their conversation for the class, three minutes: the class listens with the three columns visible and puts a mark beside every phrase they hear used.
  7. Deferred correction, three minutes: the six good phrases on the board first, with names, then the six errors, unattributed, repaired by the class.
  8. Mixed ability: anyone who needs more practice repeats with a third partner; stronger pairs repeat the task about a different dish they would like to learn.
📺 On screen: the three-column phrase board stays up during the task, and the good phrases with names go up at the end — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: every student speaks for six minutes in two different pairs, then audits two classmates against the phrase columns

Workbook p.59 + WB track 07
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can use the same cooking verb transitively and intransitively and can discuss options with a partner and reach a decision, using at least four phrases from the Prepare to speak box.
  • Subsidiary. Listening for specific information against pictures (tracks 54 and 55); predicting vocabulary from images before a recording; the polite disagreement pattern, which is intonation as much as words.
  • Personal. Deferred correction is the discipline of this lesson. Do not interrupt a single conversation. Collect six errors and six good phrases on your sheet, and put both up at the end — the good ones first, with names.
By the end, students can…
  • say boil the carrots and the soup boiled, and know why they differ
  • make a suggestion with How about…? and What if we…?
  • disagree with a reason instead of agreeing at once
  • close a discussion with a decision both people accept

Structure
Boil the carrots for five minutes. / The soup boiled for an hour. · How about sushi? · If we chose pasta, it would be cheaper. · Sushi might be a better choice. · Are you OK with that?
Form
transitive = the verb takes a direct object right after it (fry an egg) · intransitive = no object, and the thing that would have been the object becomes the subject (the egg is frying) · many cooking verbs do both: boil, taste, roast, fry, bake.
Pron. watch
the cooking verbs are short and the class will swallow the endings: roast /rəʊst/ against roasted /ˈrəʊstɪd/ — the -ed is a whole syllable after t and d, and only there · barbecue /ˈbɑːbɪkjuː/ has three syllables and the stress on the first · whose and who's sound identical, which is why the spelling has to be seen on the board · and the non-defining clause is heard before it is punctuated: read it on a LOWER pitch and the commas explain themselves
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«This ice cream tastes great.» What is the object of tastes? → there isn't one. «Can I taste your food?» → your food. Same verb, two jobs.
«The vegetables roasted for an hour» — who roasted them? → we don't say. That is what intransitive does.
Your partner says «Let's do sushi» and you agree straight away. How many marks is that worth? → almost none. What has to happen first? → another option, with a reason.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The transitive point is met with blank facesDo the Teacher's Book warmer on the board first — I made a pie last night, labelled subject, verb, direct object — and only then open the book. The label is what makes it visible.
The pairs agree in ten seconds and stopThe rule is announced before the task and enforced by you walking past: no decision before both options have been considered aloud with a reason. If they finish in thirty seconds, they get a second question, not praise.
The same three students dominate the class feedbackAll the speaking here is in pairs and everybody is talking at once. Public repetition is by invitation and rotates: the two volunteers who repeat their conversation for the class are chosen by you, and never the same two twice.
Listening Part 1 answered from a mentioned wordQuestion 1 is worked through as a class with the key words underlined first. Say the rule once: the answer is the picture that answers the QUESTION, not the picture that gets talked about.
Weak pairs run out of language in the exam taskThe three-column board (suggestions · options · decisions) stays up during the task. It is not cheating — in the exam these phrases are in their head, and this is how they get there.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Task 2 (the true / false / not stated dialogue) and the oral task 4, where two options have to be compared and one chosen.

Listening Part 1 trains the discipline the Russian task 2 punishes hardest: the answer is the option that answers the QUESTION, not the option that gets mentioned. And the speaking half is the closest thing in the course to the Russian oral comparison task — suggest, consider, disagree with a reason, decide. The phrases from the Prepare to speak box are directly transferable into it.
