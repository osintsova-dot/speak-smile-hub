🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Demonstrate the language of the first term on an official Cambridge-format paper from the course pack: Reading and Use of English Parts 1–3 (24 items) and Listening Part 1 (8 items) — 32 marks, 55 minutes — and then go through it, in this room, before the holidays start.
What is genuinely hard
Three of the four parts are productive or near-productive: an open cloze gives no options at all, and word formation demands a spelling. The listening is eight unrelated extracts, which means eight cold starts with no context carried over — the most tiring listening format there is, and the one that most rewards reading the question first.
Typical error to expect
Leaving an open cloze gap empty. There is no penalty for a wrong guess and a real cost to a blank — a sensible guess from the grammar (a preposition? a relative? an article?) scores about one in three.
Writing the unchanged base word in word formation — if the gap needs a noun and you wrote the verb, it is zero however well you understood the text.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson does three things in order and nothing else: twenty minutes of light, confidence-building warm-up on exactly the four formats (no new content, nothing frightening); the paper itself with the scale already on the board; and then — deliberately, in this room, not in January — the answers, the reasons and one thing each student is proud of. A paper handed back three weeks later teaches nothing.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
Fifty-five minutes of paper, and then we go through every answer together, today, before you leave for the holidays. The scale is on the board before you start so there are no surprises. This is a real Cambridge-format paper from your own course pack — and I have taken out the one part that tests grammar we meet in January, because you are not tested on what you have not been taught.

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting at the door with the marking scale already on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what was the last thing you bought? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — the scale goes up first, before anything else. Write it on the board while they come in and leave it there all lesson: 29–32 excellent · 24–28 good · 19–23 satisfactory · below 19 — we work on it together in January.
  3. 1 minute — pages down. Revision pages on the desk, closed. You've done the work. Today you show it.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Twenty minutes of warm-up on exactly these four tasks, then the paper. Nothing new, nothing hidden.
📺 On screen: the marking scale written on the board before the first student walks in. Backup: write it on a large sheet the evening before and tape it up — it must be visible from every desk.
👦 Students do: come in, see the scale, put the revision pages away.

Warm-up 1 · multiple-choice cloze and open cloze, at speed

Why: A warm-up before a paper should rehearse procedure, not knowledge. Four items in eight minutes settles the room and removes the «how does this task work?» question.

WHAT WE DO: the two reading formats rehearsed on four easy items, purely to establish the procedure

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four items on the board, two of each type, all from the term's vocabulary: (1) The award is ___ to all students. A vacant B clear C open D public. (2) It is easy to imagine that ___ much can survive there. (one word) (3) She ___ to complete several rounds. A had B must C should D ought. (4) There are several mammals ___ as sheep and rabbits. (one word)
  2. Pairs, ninety seconds, then check. The point is not the answers, it is the two procedures.
  3. State them, in one line each, and leave them on the board: Multiple choice: read the whole sentence, then test each option in the gap. Open cloze: decide what KIND of word is missing before you think of the word.
  4. One rule, said once and firmly: never leave an open cloze gap empty. A guess costs nothing.
📺 On screen: the four items and the two procedures on the board. Backup: dictate the four items; ninety seconds.
👦 Students do: answer four items in pairs, then copy two procedure lines.
board
13–20′
Warm-up 2 · word formation and one listening extract
pairs → desks
Why: Both remaining formats get a procedure line, so that the paper asks nothing procedurally new. That is the whole purpose of the first twenty minutes.

WHAT WE DO: the other two formats, also rehearsed on easy items

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six base words on the board, ninety seconds in pairs: ASSIST → ? · CELEBRATE → ? · SENSE → ? · PLEASE → ? · FORTUNATE → ? · COMPLICATE → the opposite? (assistant · celebration · sensible · pleased · fortunately · uncomplicated).
  2. The procedure line, on the board: What KIND of word does the gap need? Then change the word. Then check singular or plural, positive or negative.
  3. One listening extract as a rehearsal: read them a short situation-question with three options (any one from a previous unit's material), play or read it once, and take the answer. The point is the instruction: read the three options BEFORE it starts. On the second hearing, check the two you rejected — don't start again.
  4. Then stop. That is the warm-up. Desks apart.
📺 On screen: the six base words and the two procedure lines on the board. Backup: if the audio for the rehearsal extract does not work, read a short situation aloud yourself and offer three options — the instruction is the point, not the recording.
👦 Students do: form six words in pairs, then hear one extract and practise the two-hearing strategy.

20–25′
Room change + briefing
desks apart
Why: Naming what has been removed and why is what makes a test feel fair, and a paper that feels fair is a paper students work on rather than resent.

WHAT WE DO: the switch into exam conditions, with the paper named honestly

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Desks apart, bags on the floor, phones in the box, pens and pencils out, water bottles allowed.
  2. The briefing, once: Four parts. Parts 1 to 3 are reading and use of English, twenty-four items, forty minutes. Part 4 is listening, eight items, heard twice, about fifteen minutes. Fifty-five minutes in total. Write in pencil. Spelling counts in parts 2 and 3.
  3. Point at the scale, which has been on the board since they walked in: this is the scale. There are no surprises today.
  4. Say the honest sentence about what is NOT on the paper: this paper has a fourth reading part with transformations. I have taken it out — two of its six items need «I wish» and reported speech, which we meet in January. You are not tested on what you have not been taught.
  5. Two minutes of silent reading of parts 1 to 3 before any pen moves.
📺 On screen: the marking scale (already up) and the finish time written next to it; the audio cued and the volume tested before the lesson. Backup: if the audio fails, the full audioscript is in the course folder (B2_First_for Schools_1_audioscript.pdf) — read Part 1 aloud twice yourself, at a steady pace, with the same pauses.
👦 Students do: move the desks, put phones away, read the first three parts in silence before starting.

25–65′
Parts 1–3 · reading and use of English, in silence
desks apart
Why: Forty minutes is the correct length for twenty-four items of this type, and the enforced thirty-second break before the listening prevents the classic collapse in part 4.

WHAT WE DO: 24 items in 40 minutes: multiple-choice cloze, open cloze, word formation

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start the timer and write the finish time on the board.
  2. Sit down. Do not walk the aisles.
  3. Announce time twice: at twenty minutes remaining and at five.
  4. The only permitted question is about an instruction, answered in four words for the whole room.
  5. At five minutes remaining, one instruction aloud: No empty gaps in part 2. Check every word in part 3 for singular, plural and negative prefixes.
  6. At forty minutes, pens down for thirty seconds. Everyone stands, shakes out their hands, sits down again — then the listening. That thirty seconds is worth two marks in part 4.
📺 On screen: the finish time on the board beside the scale. Backup: nothing else is displayed — this part of the lesson needs no technology.
👦 Students do: work alone in silence for forty minutes, then stand and reset for thirty seconds.

65–80′
Part 4 · listening, eight extracts, heard twice
desks apart
Why: Eight cold starts is the most tiring listening format there is, which is why it goes last, after a physical reset, with ninety seconds of reading time protected.

WHAT WE DO: 8 items in about 15 minutes, with the standard exam procedure

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out or turn to the listening page. Ninety seconds to read all eight questions and their options, in silence.
  2. Say the procedure once: you hear each extract twice, one after the other. First hearing: choose. Second hearing: check the two you rejected.
  3. Play the whole of Part 1 of the recording without stopping, exactly as the exam does.
  4. One minute at the end to transfer and check.
  5. Collect all papers face down, at the front, in one movement.
📺 On screen: recording — B2_First_for_Schools_1_audio_Part_1.mp3 in the board app, volume tested before the lesson and cued to zero. Backup: the full audioscript is in the course folder — read the eight extracts aloud twice at a steady pace; the format survives it perfectly.
👦 Students do: read all eight questions first, then listen twice, choosing on the first hearing and checking on the second.

🎯 The review · in this room, today
whole class
Why: Review in the same room, on the same day, is the difference between a paper that teaches and a paper that only measures. And the last thing said before the holidays should be about progress.

WHAT WE DO: the answers, the reasons, and one thing each student did well — before anybody goes on holiday

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Papers stay at the front. Read the answers to Part 2 (open cloze) and Part 3 (word formation) — the two parts where the reasoning is worth hearing: 9 not/nothing · 10 of · 11 If/When · 12 them/these · 13 such · 14 that/which · 15 by · 16 the; 17 assistant · 18 celebration · 19 sensible · 20 uncomplicated · 21 pleased · 22 handful · 23 burning · 24 Fortunately.
  2. For four of them, ask why? and take the reason from the class — «such» because it's followed by «as» · «Fortunately» because the gap starts a sentence and modifies the whole thing.
  3. Then the sentence that closes the term: Turn to your neighbour and tell them one thing you found easier today than you would have in September. Take three aloud.
  4. Marks come in the first lesson of January, with two comments each. Say that clearly — nobody should spend the holidays wondering.
  5. Do not read the answers to Part 1 or the listening. Those are for January, with the paper in their hands.
📺 On screen: the two answer strings read aloud and written up as they are checked. Backup: the official answer key (B2_First_for Schools_1_answer_key.pdf) is in the course folder — have it printed, not on a screen.
👦 Students do: listen to the answers for two parts, give the reason for four of them, then name one improvement since September.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end of the lesson students will have demonstrated their command of the grammar, vocabulary and listening skills of Units 1–5 on an official Cambridge-format paper, and will have reviewed two of its four parts with reasons.
  • Subsidiary. Exam procedure for four task types; managing a fifty-five-minute paper; reviewing one's own work immediately rather than three weeks later.
  • Personal. Put the scale up before the first student arrives, not after. Everything else in this lesson is easier if that one thing is already on the board.
By the end, students can…
  • work through a multiple-choice cloze by testing each option in the gap
  • guess sensibly in an open cloze rather than leaving a gap empty
  • form the right word class in a word-formation task and check number and prefix
  • handle eight unrelated listening extracts with two hearings and a strategy
  • name one thing they can do now that they could not do in September

Structure
no new structure is presented; the paper samples the term's systems — tenses, future forms, comparison, articles, ability and modals — inside connected texts.
Form
part 1: collocation and vocabulary choice · part 2: one word per gap, mostly grammatical (articles, prepositions, relatives, quantifiers) · part 3: word class change with spelling, number and prefix · part 4: short extracts with three options, heard twice.
Pron. watch
not a focus in a paper, but say the eight listening questions aloud during the reading time yourself if the group is weak — hearing the question in a human voice before the recording measurably improves the first hearing.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
Open cloze: How many words in each gap? — One. What do you do if you don't know it? — Guess, from the grammar.
Word formation: Do you copy the word in capitals? — No, you change it. What do you check afterwards? — Singular or plural, and negative prefixes.
Listening: Do you read the options first or listen first? — Read first. What is the second hearing for? — Checking, not starting again.
Before the paper: How many parts? How many minutes? What is on the board if you want to know your grade?

Assumptions: Students have completed Units 1–5, both revision lessons and their own revision pages. The paper is printed, the audio is cued and the answer key is printed — none of that can be improvised on the day.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
A student panics in the open cloze, where nothing is given.The warm-up rehearses exactly this and the rule is stated twice: never leave a gap empty, decide the KIND of word first. If it happens anyway, walk past once and tap the next item — no words.
The audio fails.The audioscript is printed and in your hand before the lesson starts. Read the eight extracts aloud twice at a steady pace — the format survives it completely, and the class will not be harmed.
Some students finish parts 1–3 in twenty-five minutes.The checking instruction is prepared: no empty gaps, check number and prefixes in part 3, then reread part 1 with the options covered. No packing up, no phones.
The review at the end is squeezed by the paper overrunning.The timings are tight by design: the paper must start at 25 minutes. If it slips, cut the second warm-up, never the review — the review is the pedagogical half of this lesson.
Somebody wants their mark today.One answer, said in advance: marks in the first lesson of January, with two comments each. Nobody spends the holidays waiting for a number they can do nothing about.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 1–9, 10–18, 19–25 and 26–31 — the whole objective half of the written paper.

The mid-year paper is an official Cambridge-format paper from the course pack, and the four parts sampled map cleanly onto the Russian sections: multiple-choice cloze and open cloze are the grammar-and-vocabulary block, word formation is tasks 26–31, and the eight short listening extracts are the first Russian listening task with three options instead of matching.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
