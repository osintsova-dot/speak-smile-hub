🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Launch the unit rituals calmly: check-in + rewind opening, teams of 3, points board, timer on screen, English-first. Keep instructions ≤ 2 sentences + ICQ. No baby-tone: they are ‘crews’, not ‘kids’.

“How are countries different? Food? Money? Sport? School? ONE sticky note — stick it on the biggest difference. Go!” “Food wins! Interesting…” “This unit is OUR WORLD. By the end you'll say where anything on this planet comes from.”

0–6′
Into the lesson: greeting + rewind
Opening · check-in + rewind
desks → standing

WHAT WE DO: greeting at the door, a lightning Starter rewind (letters · numbers · internationalisms) for points, a bridge into the topic

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I stand at the door, to everyone coming in: Hi! What's new? One-word answers only! — I accept any word, smile, don't dig deeper.
  2. Once everyone's seated: Mood check: show 1 to 5 on your fingers. I scan the room, comment in one word.
  3. I announce the rewind: Starter rewind! Spell VERA — in the air! — we draw the letters in the air together, 2 names.
  4. I show on the slide / write on the board 4 numbers (17, 40, 65, 100): Shout them in English!
  5. I say 3 internationalisms (pizza · taxi · football): Thumbs up if English uses this word too! +1 to future teams for a clean round.
  6. TB warmer (p.18) with support: I write 5 countries on the board (the UK · China · the USA · Spain · Australia), I say two clue words each — the class POINTS and names the country: Football, London — ? Pandas, Beijing — ? Sydney, kangaroos — ?
  7. Bridge: Those words travel the world… Today we find out where EVERYTHING comes from.
📺 On screen: mood slide (Backup: mood smileys 1–5 on the board) · 4 numbers big on the board · the words pizza / taxi / football · 5 countries in a column (TB warmer)
👦 Students do: respond with one word at the door; show mood with fingers; write the letters in the air together and shout the numbers. Expected: “seventeen! one hundred!”. Typical error: “hundred” without “one” — I hold up finger “1”, the class adds it themselves.

6–10′
Sticker vote: how are countries different?
Hook · WDYT wall
board · standing

WHAT WE DO: everyone sticks a note on “the biggest difference between countries”, I split into teams, open the unit points board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand everyone a sticky note, open the slide with the unit question.
  2. I say: How are countries different? Food? Money? Sport? School? ONE sticky note — stick it on the biggest difference. Go!
  3. I read out the winning column: Food wins! Interesting…
  4. I announce: This unit is OUR WORLD. By the end you'll say where anything on this planet comes from.
  5. I split into teams of 3 (line-up planned in advance), hang up the empty unit points board, carry over the entry points.
📺 On screen: the “How are countries different?” slide + 4 icons (food · money · sport · school) (Backup: I write the 4 words in columns on the board) · an empty points board next to it
👦 Students do: come up and stick their note in the chosen column — everyone moves at once; sit down by team. Expected: a lively argument about “what matters more” — I cut it off with the signal “Eyes up in 3-2-1”.
sticky notes · WDYT slide · points board
10–20′
Quiz «Thank you… for…»
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: pairs guess where 6 world-famous things are from (SB p.12 ex.1), we check with the audio, points for correct answers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I say: Books open, page 12. Six world-famous things. Guess the country: a, b or c. Pairs. Three minutes.
  2. ICQ: Do you write sentences? — No, just circle!
  3. I start a 3-minute timer on screen, walk between the pairs, don't hint.
  4. I play audio 8 to check (or reveal the answers one by one on the slide): after each item Hands up if you got it! — +1 point for correct.
  5. I sum up: So — Hollywood is American, tapas are Spanish… Thank you, world!
📺 On screen: the SB p.12 ex.1 for each pair · a timer on screen · audio 8 (Backup: a slide with the answers — USA · China · Spain · Australia · the UK · Mexico)
👦 Students do: in pairs they circle an option for each of the 6 items; put their hands up at the check. Expected: 3–5 out of 6. Typical error: arguing in the pair in Russian — I accept the idea, ask them to repeat the choice in English: “Letter b!”

20–31′
Input: 13 countries + a suffix table
Discover · 13 pairs + suffix table
board → desks

WHAT WE DO: I build a -an/-ian/-ese/-ish table on the board with stress claps; teams race to fill in their own

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I draw 5 columns on the board: -an · -ian · -ese · -ish · ⚠ irregular.
  2. I say a pair, the class repeats ×2, I ask: China — Chinese. Which column? — I write it in from the class's answer.
  3. On long words I clap the stress: Ja-pa-NESE! — the class claps with me.
  4. I write the in yellow before UK / USA / Netherlands, a chant: the-UK, the-USA, the-Netherlands!
  5. Task for teams (ex.5–6): Copy the table. Add these seven: Brazil, India, Japan, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Russia, Turkey. First correct — plus three! 4-minute timer.
  6. I check the tables, announce the winner.
📺 On screen: board: a 5-column suffix table, built live and STAYS until the end of the lesson; yellow the; the Student's Book p.13 ex.5–6
👦 Students do: repeat the pairs in chorus and vote for a column; in teams transfer the table and sort the 7 countries from the box. Expected: Brazilian → -ian, Japanese → -ese, Dutch → irregular. Typical error: “Japanish” — I silently point at the -ese column, the team fixes it themselves.
💪 Support: for a weaker team — word cards, to sort physically by column. ⭐ Fast finishers (TB p.19): add 2 of YOUR OWN countries + nationalities — do the suffixes still work?		board table · SB p.13 · word cards
31–40′
The “Brand Detectives” game
Play · Brand Detectives
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: a projector quiz “Sony — where from?” (ex.7): a full sentence in 10 seconds + your own question for your neighbours

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I open the slide with the first item: Sony is a brand from …? Full sentence for the point! Team one — ten seconds!
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everyone writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they all lift them together — I see every answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. I go through 6 items rotating teams. My key: Japan · Dutch (Ajax) · Mexico (Cancún) · American (baseball) · Russia (Moscow) · Spanish (Iberia).
  4. I add a twist (ex.8): Write ONE your own: ‘___ is a brand from…’. Test the next team! 2 min.
  5. I run the exchange: a team asks → the neighbouring team answers in 10 sec; +2 if the neighbours can't but the authors know the answer.
📺 On screen: slides for each item of ex.7 (Backup: I write the item on the board); an answer frame on the board: “___ is a brand from ___ / It's a ___ brand”
👦 Students do: answer with a full sentence on the count of 10; make up their own question (gaming/football allowed) and quiz their neighbours. Expected: “Baseball is an American sport”. Typical error: “from American” — I tap the suffix table: country or nationality?
projector slides · mini-boards

50–61′
Speaking circle with a globe (the first I'm from…)
Try it · be in frames (no theory)
standing circle

WHAT WE DO: we throw a ball: catch a country card — say it by the frame “I'm from Japan! I'm Japanese!”, the class echoes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I put everyone in a circle, write the frames on the board: I'm from ___ . / I'm ___ . / It's ___ .
  2. I model on myself, slowly, twice: I'm from Russia. I'm Russian.
  3. I explain: Catch the globe → take a card → say the frame. Class — echo!
  4. I throw the ball, give a country card; after the phrase I conduct the class's echo.
  5. I keep the pace up: everyone catches 2–3 times; I correct ONLY the nationality word.
📺 On screen: board: 3 frames, large · 13 country cards in my hand · a soft ball/globe
👦 Students do: catch the ball, draw a card, say 2 phrases by the frame, the class repeats in an echo. Expected: “I'm from Japan! I'm Japanese!”. Typical error: “I'm Japan” — I point at frame No.2: country or person?
soft ball · country cards
61–72′
Snap card game: country–nationality
Play · Nationality Snap
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: pairs slap the matching pair of cards (Spain — Spanish!) and say it aloud; 2 rounds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand pairs decks of 26 cards (13 countries + 13 nationalities), demonstrate with a strong student: flip in turn, a match → slap + say the pair aloud.
  2. ICQ: Do you slap silently? — No! Slap AND say: Spain — Spanish!
  3. I start round 1 (3–4 min), walk round, listen for who mixes up -ese/-ish, note down 3 names.
  4. I have them swap decks with a neighbouring pair, round 2.
  5. Final: Count your cards! Winners — hands up!
📺 On screen: 5 Snap decks of 26 cards (prepared before the lesson); the suffix table on the board — peeking is allowed
👦 Students do: flip cards in turn; on a match, the first to slap it says the pair aloud; the winner has the most cards. Expected: excitement + loud “Turkey — Turkish!”. Typical error: they slap silently — the card doesn't count, the rule enforces itself.
✔ Assess: 3 names mixing up -ese/-ish — an exit check for them on the way out.		Snap packs ×5 · timer

Writing 4 sentences about myself
Build · first writing portion
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: write 4 sentences about themselves by the frames, then the first peer check on 2 boxes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I write a model on the board: I'm Oxana. I'm from Norilsk. I'm Russian. My favourite country is Japan.
  2. I erase everything but the frames: I'm ___ . I'm from ___ . I'm ___ . My favourite country is ___ .
  3. I say: Four sentences about YOU. Five minutes. Timer.
  4. I explain the first peer review: Swap notebooks. Check TWO boxes only: capital letters in countries? nationality from the table? Tick or fix — be kind, be exact.
  5. I walk round, +1 point for a clean check.
📺 On screen: board: the model → the frames; a slide with 2 boxes: “☐ Capital letters? ☐ Nationality from the table?” (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: write 4 sentences by the frames (a portion, not an essay), swap notebooks and check on 2 points. Expected: they find lower-case letters on countries. Typical error of the checker: fixes everything themselves — I remind them: underline it, the author fixes it THEMSELVES.

Wrap-up + reflection
Share & Reflect
circle

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: everyone reads one sentence, points, a finger vote on “how many nationalities I know”, homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I gather the circle: Lightning round: read ONE of your sentences. — round the circle, fast.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. I total the points on the board, applause for the team of the day.
  4. Reflection: How many nationalities can you say now? Show on fingers — five to thirteen!
  5. I show the homework slide, allow a photo.
  6. Teaser: Next lesson we fly to the Netherlands — the BEST country to be a teenager. True or not? We'll check.
📺 On screen: the points board · the homework slide (Backup: homework on the board — 13 pairs + 4 sentences + a Research bonus)
👦 Students do: read one sentence each without discussion; show a finger self-rating; photograph the homework. Expected: most show 8–13.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 13 countries and their nationality adjectives and say where things/people are from: “Where's paper from? — It's from China. / It's Chinese.”
  • Subsidiary. Meet the unit question WDYT? How are countries different?; sort nationalities by suffix (-an/-ian/-ese/-ish); watch the Video skills vlog with an active task; first contextual contact with be affirmative. Word stress noticing (SB p.116).
  • Personal. Launch the unit rituals calmly: check-in + rewind opening, teams of 3, points board, timer on screen, English-first. Keep instructions ≤ 2 sentences + ICQ. No baby-tone: they are ‘crews’, not ‘kids’.

Target language
Lexis

Structure
Where's (paper) from? — It's from (China). / It's (Chinese). · I'm from (Russia). · Language note (SB p.12): It's from Spain. = It's Spanish food.
Form
nationality = adjective, no article, capital letter; suffix groups -an / -ian / -ese / -ish + irregular (Dutch, New Zealand).
Pron. watch
word stress: Ja-PAN → Japa-NESE (stress moves!) · BRA-zil → Bra-ZIL-ian · CHI-na → Chi-NESE; Dutch /dʌtʃ/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Chinese — a country or a person/thing from the country?” → nationality word.
“Do we write ‘russian’ with a small letter?” → No — capital letter, always.
“He's Dutch. Which country?” → the Netherlands (irregular!).

Assumptions: After Starter block: alphabet, numbers, classroom English, international words are in place. Countries in L1 are known from geography; the English names + nationality system are new. be is NOT presented today — only heard in frames.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Nationality spelling feels randomAnchor the 4-suffix table on the board all lesson; every new word goes INTO a column, never floats.
“the” dropped (UK/USA/Netherlands)Board trick: write the in yellow before the 3 names; quick chant: the-UK, the-USA, the-Netherlands.
Video too fast for beginnersTask is visual (tick places you SEE), not verbal; play twice; subtitles off — success = noticing, not decoding.
Teams argue about pointsRule slide №1: “Judge's decision is final. Protest = −1.” Say it once, smile, apply it.
Someone answers in RussianAccept idea, recast in English, team repeats the English version for the point.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
