Get Involved! A1+ · Unit 1 “Our world” · Pair 1 — Vocabulary: Countries & Nationalities
A1+ teens (~11–13, near-beginners) · 90 min · Lead-in → Presentation → Controlled → Video skills → Production → Feedback · pairs & teams
Week 14.09–20.09 · Year-pair 5✨ NEW unit🐣 FOUNDATION (zero) line — slow full input, extra support~10–12 pupils · teams of 3–4📖 Student's Book pp. 12–13🔤 Pron: word stress (p116)
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name 13 countries and form their nationality adjectives (by suffix group) and say where things are from in context — “It's from Spain. It's Spanish.”
  • Subsidiary. Meet be (am/is/are) in context (not yet systematised — that is Pair 3); listening/viewing subskill — watch a travel vlog step by step; word-stress awareness on nationality adjectives; open the unit's WDYT? question with a low-stakes opinion vote.
  • Personal. Foundation pace: keep every productive task on a sentence frame; rehearse in pairs before anyone performs; low teacher-talk, one clear ICQ per task; celebrate attempts, not perfection.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name 13 countries and match their nationality adjectives;
  • sort nationality adjectives into suffix groups (-an · -ian · -ese · -ish · irregular);
  • ask & answer “Where's … from? — It's from … / It's …-ish.” with a frame;
  • catch the gist of a short travel vlog and give one opinion about it.

Structure
Where's (paper) from? — It's from (China). / It's (Chinese).  ·  Language note: It's from Spain. → It's Spanish food.
Form (be, in context)
It +'s (is) from + country. / It +'s + nationality. / I'm from … — am/is/are met as chunks only; full table comes in Pair 3.
Pron. watch
Word stress: JapanESE, PortuGUESE; AusTRAlian, RUSsian; /ˈtɜːkɪʃ/ Turkish. Clap the stressed syllable.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Spain — country or nationality?” → country. “Spanish?” → nationality.
“It's Spanish food. — do we use a country or a nationality before food?” → nationality.
“Where's it from? — am I telling the place or asking the place?” → asking.

Assumptions: 🐣 zero group — near-beginners just off Starter (hello, colours, numbers, alphabet, subject pronouns, a/an). They read single words slowly → pair every word with a flag/picture; they can NOT yet produce be freely → keep it as chunks on frames. This is their first real unit — protect confidence. Teams: 3–4; roles rotate (reader · asker · checker · scorer).

0–8′
Lead-in · WDYT?
board, seats

Open the unit. Reveal the unit title Our world and the question WDYT? — How are countries different? Show 4–5 photos (food, flags, landmarks, Hollywood sign).

▶ Opinion vote (sticky notes). Each pupil gets 2 sticky notes: Write ONE thing that makes a country different — food? language? weather? Stick on the board; cluster them together. Read 3–4 aloud, thumbs-up the ones you agree with. Keep it light — no “right answer”.

Set the goal. Today: 13 countries, how to say where things are from, and a travel video. Let's go!

 unit photos (board) · 2 sticky notes per pupil · Student's Book p.12
8–22′
Presentation · Countries
board → teams

Present with flags (choral). Show each flag + word: This is China. China. Class repeats ×2. Do all 13; then random flags — What country? Watch the “tricky two”: the Netherlands · New Zealand.

▶ SB p.12–13 ex 1 “Thank you … for …” quiz (teams of 3–4). Where do these things come from? Choose a, b or c with your team. (Hollywood · paper · tapas · Wi-Fi · Sherlock Holmes · chocolate.) ICQ: Do you write one answer per team or per person? (→ per team.) Play track 8 to check; teams score their own.

Continents (ex 4–5). Quick map task: Which continent is each country in? Match the box countries (Brazil, India, Japan, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Russia, Turkey) to continents — point on a wall map. Which continent is missing?

22–36′
Presentation · Nationalities
seats → notebook

Discover the suffixes (ex 2 & 6). Model the pair on the board: The USA → American. Then sort by colour: -an · -ian · -ese · -ish · irregular. Listen — which group? Play track 9; class repeats each and claps the stress: JapanESE, AusTRAlian.

📓 Interactive notebook — “Input: 13 countries + a suffix table”. Glue in the Vocabulary 1 “Our world” Visual Takeaway (A5): 13 countries with flags + the five suffix columns. Learners write the nationality adjective by hand in each colour column (this is their reference card for the whole unit). Circle the “tricky two” irregulars in red.

Checking for understanding. Mini-whiteboards / fingers: Spain → ? · Japan → ? · China → ? everyone shows at once. Re-drill any group that wobbles.

36–46′
Controlled practice
standing, mingle

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (Kagan). Each pupil holds ONE country↔nationality card (flag + country on front, nationality on back). Stand up, hand up, pair up! Partner A shows the flag: What's the nationality? Partner B answers — It's [Chinese]. — praise, swap cards, find a new partner. Everyone speaks every round; you monitor the suffix errors.

Support (Foundation). Keep the 📓 suffix card open on the desk as a lifeline; weaker pupils may peek. Give a 2-minute warning, then Freeze — back to your seats.

49–52′
Freer · Where's it from?
pairs, seats

Frame on the board (tiered). Heavy → light:
① Where's [paper] from? — It's from [China]. It's [Chinese].
② Where's ___ from? — It's from ___. It's ___.
③ your own thing + country.

▶ SB ex 3 + ex 7–8 pair drill. A asks about a thing, B answers with country + nationality (Sony · Ajax · Cancún · baseball · Moscow · Iberia). Then ex 8 — Change the underlined words — make your own sentence and test another pair. Rehearse twice before you call 2–3 pairs to perform.

52–68′
Video skills
screen, pairs

Before you watch (predict). Freeze the thumbnail: This is Isabel. It's a travel video. Guess — which 3 places? Pairs write 3 guesses (in L1 is fine at this stage).

Watch 1 — gist (ex 9). Watch. What are Isabel's top three places to visit? Play once; pairs check their guesses. Play again if needed — Foundation gets the second play as standard.

Watch 2 — respond (ex 10). Pairs discuss on frames: 1 Why are travel videos interesting? 2 What's your favourite place in the video? Why? 3 What type of video are the lions in? Why? Frame: I like … because … / My favourite is … It's … Take 2–3 answers to the class.

 Video skills p13 (Isabel · “top three places”) · screen · answer frames
68–80′
Production · mini-speaking
teams → class

Personalise with be in context. Give each team a mini-set of “famous things” cards (a dish, a brand, a landmark). Frame: This is [sushi]. It's from [Japan]. It's [Japanese]. Each pupil says at least one — teammates help, checker ticks the suffix card.

▶ Team challenge (light, no big prize). 60-second round: how many correct “It's from … It's …-ish” sentences can your team make from the picture pile? Timer on screen; count aloud together. ICQ: Country OR nationality after “It's … food”? (→ nationality.)

Close the WDYT? loop. Back to the opening question: So — how ARE countries different? Tell me one way in English or with a word we learnt.

 “famous things” picture cards (per team) · timer · 📓 card
80–86′
Feedback
board

Delayed error correction. Put 4–5 sentences you overheard on the board — 2 correct, 2–3 with a suffix/“from” slip: Find the good one. Fix the others with your partner. Elicit fixes; re-drill the stubborn suffix group once more.

Praise the risk-takers by name — You tried a new word — that's exactly the job today.

 board · overheard sentences
