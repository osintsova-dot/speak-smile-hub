WHAT WE DO: teams build a storyboard: 6 frame-scenes, keywords + support phrases; choosing locations and splitting phrases between EVERYONE

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out storyboard templates: Six boxes: sketch + keywords + WHO speaks. Everybody speaks in at least ONE scene!
  2. Locations: classroom/corridor/gym/library — from the agreed list; the town — only as an optional homework extra.
  3. A language checklist in the corner of the template: there is/can see/present continuous + 1 past scene — the director ticks it off.
  4. Gate: the storyboard is approved by me BEFORE anyone goes out to shoot (a plus-stamp).
📺 On screen: storyboard templates (asset) · location list · language checklist
👦 Students do: Students draw and label the scenes, splitting phrases between everyone. Expected: 10–12 min for every team to get approved. Typical error: one giant run-on scene — I cut it down to 2–4 sentences.

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Numbered Heads Together (guide-language gate) → rehearsing the scenes WITHOUT a camera: blocking, phrases at camera pace, action/cut commands

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Numbered Heads Together (3 min, gate): I number the teams 1–4. Question: how does a guide WELCOME viewers? Heads together — everyone must know! … Number THREES, stand and answer! 3 rounds (the welcome phrase · the stop phrase · the outro) — the named number answers, but everyone must know.
  2. Run-through in corners of the classroom: each team «plays out» the intro and 1 stop, the director commands Action! … Cut!
  3. Pace drill: on-camera speech = a third slower; in chorus we drill Wel-come to our school!
  4. Prompter rule: the director holds the keywords BEHIND an imaginary camera — the speaker looks INTO the camera.
  5. Check-in for each team: they show me the intro — I give the go-ahead to shoot.
📺 On screen: storyboard · corners of the classroom
👦 Students do: Students run through the scenes on their feet, drilling the shooting commands. Expected: after 2 run-throughs the phrases stick. Typical error: gabbling — a metronome hand gesture.

WHAT WE DO: shooting the classroom scenes: the intro + scenes where the location = the classroom; a 4-minute timebox per scene, maximum 3 takes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. Teams watch their takes: the editor logs the best TAKE on the storyboard; language is checked against the checklist.
  3. Decision: which scenes to reshoot/refilm (maximum 1 — the rest is a mini-group homework).

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, teams have a 6-scene video-guide script and most scenes shot on a phone (intro → 3 stops → outro), clean on-camera language against the checklist.
  • Subsidiary. The script follows a storyboard template; the guide's language = there is/are · can · present continuous on camera + a 🚀 bar: 1 scene with past («here we learned…»); roles work: the director runs the shoot, the operator films, the speaker is on camera, the editor logs the takes.
  • Personal. The shoot is organisationally the riskiest lesson of the season: strict rules for moving round the school, timeboxes per scene, I clamp down on «multi-taking»: maximum 3 takes per scene — otherwise we won't finish in time.
By the end, students can…
  • write a 6-scene storyboard in keywords
  • speak to camera with guide phrases
  • run a shoot in roles (director/operator/speaker/editor)
  • log takes and pick the best one

Structure
Welcome to our school — follow me! · This is our classroom. Here you can see… · We're standing in the gym. · ⭐ This is where we learned present simple! · That's the end of our tour — thanks for watching!
Form
a scene = 2–4 sentences; every stop: the place's name + there is/can see + 1 detail; intro/outro from the phrase bank; ⭐ 1 scene with past simple.
Pron. watch
speech INTO THE CAMERA a third slower than usual; the final line — with a smile (it's audible in the sound!); Follow me! — energetically.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Do we shoot everything as it comes?» → No: storyboard first. No scene in the script — no shoot.
«How many takes are allowed?» → Maximum 3. Take 4 = we move on, the editor will pick the best.
«Can you speak off a sheet?» → Not on camera: the director holds the keywords BEHIND the camera (a live prompter).

Assumptions: Roles and teams have been running since P1; phones have been brought in (spare: 1 phone per team is enough). Routes round the school have been agreed with the admin in advance; off-limits areas are named before the shoot.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Shooting = chaos and running aroundTimeboxes: 6 scenes × 4 min against the director's checklist; every team's route is approved before leaving the classroom; check-in with me after every 2 scenes.
Giggling on camera ruins the takesThe 3-take rule is strict; the director has the right to call «substitution» — another team member says the scene (everyone has roles, after all).
Empty/echoey sound in the corridorRule: the speaker stands 1 metre from the phone, the operator closer, we shoot in a quiet minute; a 5-second test take with a listen-back.
On-camera language has errorsA language checklist on the storyboard: the director checks the phrase BEFORE «action!»; an error in a take isn't a tragedy, but the best take should be clean.
A team without a phoneMy spare phone/school tablet; the same shoot, hand the file over to me right after the lesson.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
