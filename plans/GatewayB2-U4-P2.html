🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Describe a person's character with fifteen B2 adjectives (broad-minded, down-to-earth, resourceful, impetuous, laid-back, reserved, tactful, considerate, self-confident, untrustworthy, resilient …) and eight idioms (a live wire, a wallflower, a big mouth, a party animal), and read a 1,000-word blog for detail: four multiple-choice questions answered from the text, not from opinion.
What is genuinely hard
Not the meaning — the precision. A2 English has kind, nice, funny; B2 English has considerate, tactful, down-to-earth, and the three are not the same person. The second difficulty is grammatical: half these adjectives are compounds with a fixed hyphen and a fixed stress (broad-MINDed), and students who learn them as two words never say them fluently.
Typical error to expect
«creativeness», «lazyness», «inventer» — the stem changes when the suffix goes on (creativity, laziness, inventor). This is a spelling mark in tasks 26–31 and it is learned by writing, not by recognising.
«He is very sociable person» — a singular countable noun needs an article, and Russian does not have one to remind them. It comes back as a grammar point in Lesson 3; today just correct it in the air, in one word: a sociable person.
Why the lesson is built this way
The vocabulary is not presented as a list — it arrives inside the Big Five personality quiz on page 44, so every adjective is read as a description of a real person before it is ever written down. The reading then does the opposite job: Susan Cain's blog argues that the quiet half of the class has been undervalued, which makes the introverts in the room the experts for once. Vocabulary → text → opinion, with a standing stirrer in the middle so nobody sits for more than twenty-five minutes.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
Today you learn to describe people the way adults do. Not «he is nice» — «he is down-to-earth, but he can be tactless». Fifteen adjectives, eight idioms, and then a text by a woman who wrote a whole book to say one thing: being quiet is not a problem to be fixed. By the end of the lesson each of you will describe one person in this room without naming them, and we will guess who it is.

door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting at the door, then a one-sentence future-form round on last unit's language

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what is the best thing that happened to you this week? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — rewind, Unit 3. On the board: Say one thing about your plans for next summer — use a future form. Round the circle, one sentence each, no repeats. Correct only the future form; everything else waits.
  3. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Last unit you talked about what people DO. This unit is about what people ARE. Books at page 44.
📺 On screen: the rewind prompt written on the board. Backup: say it aloud twice — this stage needs no equipment at all.
👦 Students do: stand in a loose circle, one sentence each, about eight seconds per student.

5–14′
Lead-in · the Big Five, without the words yet
desks
Why: A personality quiz is the one context where a teenager wants the vocabulary before it is given — they have just described themselves and are short of words. That gap is the lesson.

WHAT WE DO: students take the personality quiz on SB p.149 before they meet a single new adjective

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books at page 44. Point at the five letters O-C-E-A-N down the side of the quiz: Psychologists say every personality fits five scales. Not five types — five scales. You are somewhere on each one.
  2. Send them to the quiz on p.149: read, tick, no dictionaries, three minutes, silence.
  3. RallyRobin in pairs: Which scale surprised you? One sentence each, take turns. Thirty seconds each way.
  4. Do NOT teach any adjective yet. If somebody asks what impetuous means, say hold it — page 44 in two minutes.
📺 On screen: the O-C-E-A-N letters copied onto the board while they read. Backup: the whole stage is book-only, so nothing breaks if the projector is off.
👦 Students do: read and tick the quiz individually, then one exchange in pairs.

14–23′
Before the text · what it is going to argue
desks
Why: A text that has been predicted is read actively. Three minutes of prediction saves ten minutes of translation.

WHAT WE DO: prediction and the words of the title, before a single line is read

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Title and pictures only. Think-Pair-Share: What is this text going to say about Unit 4 «Our strengths»? Two predictions each.
  2. Board the predictions in the students' own words. They get checked at the end of the reading, and being wrong is not a problem.
  3. Pre-teach only what blocks comprehension — three items, no more. Everything else is guessed from context later, which is the skill of the lesson.
  4. Set the first task before they read: One question for the whole text. Four minutes. Do not stop at unknown words.
📺 On screen: the title and the image on the board app, text covered. Backup: read the title aloud and describe the picture — the prediction works without a screen.
👦 Students do: predict what the text will say, then read once for the big question

23–32′
Reading 1 · gist in four minutes
desks
Why: Gist reading has to be timed or it becomes translation. One question, four minutes, pens down — the discipline that later makes the exam reading survivable.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.45 exercises 1–2 — what the blog is FOR, before any detail

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the frame first: Susan Cain wrote a book called Quiet Power. This blogger read it. Read fast — you have four minutes and one question.
  2. Exercise 2: Why did the blogger write this? Tell them where to look before they start — the answer to a why-question is almost always in the first and the last paragraph.
  3. Four minutes, books flat, no dictionaries, no pens. Then thirty seconds in pairs, then whole class. Expected answer: gist: the blog is about Susan Cain's book «Quiet Power» and about it being fine to be an introvert — quiet people add value to a team. The answer is in the first and the last paragraph.
  4. If a pair finishes early: find one sentence you disagree with.
📺 On screen: a visible countdown timer — four minutes, running. Backup: your phone timer held up, or the second hand on the wall clock, announced aloud at the halfway point.
👦 Students do: read silently against the clock, then compare in pairs before the open-class check.

32–47′
Reading 2 · detail, with the evidence named
desks → pairs
Why: Multiple choice is not a reading skill, it is an evidence skill. Rally Coach makes the evidence audible: a student who cannot find the line does not get the mark from the partner either.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.45 exercise 3 — four multiple-choice questions, each answer justified by a line in the text

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Teach the procedure out loud, because it is the whole skill: Read question 1 ONLY. Then read until you find the part of the text about question 1. Answer. Then question 2. Never read the whole text four times.
  2. Warn them off the classic trap: do not match words. The right answer says the same THING in different words.
  3. Individually, seven minutes. Then Rally Coach: partner A explains the answer to question 1 and reads the line that proves it; partner B checks and coaches; swap for question 2, and so on.
  4. Open-class check with the proof lines: 1 c (… I thought I'd share some of the ideas I learned — paragraph 1) · 2 c (… we should all be trying to be the life and soul of the party — paragraph 3) · 3 b (… the talents of the quieter members are taken less seriously — paragraph 4) · 4 a (… that doesn't mean you can't stand up and give a presentation — paragraph 5).
  5. Fast finishers: back to the quiz on p.149 — write three more quiz sentences that would show whether someone is an introvert or an extrovert.
📺 On screen: the three-step procedure written on the board as a numbered list — it stays up for the rest of the unit. Backup: dictate it and have them copy it into the back of the notebook.
👦 Students do: work alone, then coach each other with the text open — every answer has to come with a quoted line.

47–57′
Words from context + Critical thinkers
pairs → whole class
Why: Meaning from context is the only vocabulary skill that transfers to an unseen exam text. Numbered Heads stops the strongest student in each four from doing all the talking.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.45 exercises 4–5 — eight highlighted expressions worked out from context, then an argument about them

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Exercise 4 in pairs — the rule is: guess from the sentence around it, not from the dictionary. Five minutes. Answers: relate to = understand how somebody feels · countless = very many · the life and soul of = very lively at a social event · bring to the table = offer for discussion · see the bigger picture = see the whole situation · rise to the occasion = deal with something difficult successfully · take centre stage = attract all the attention · in the spotlight = getting a lot of public attention.
  2. Push the two that matter most for speaking: see the bigger picture and rise to the occasion — make each pair build one true sentence about a real person with each.
  3. Exercise 5 · Critical thinkers. The question is whether the blog is fair. Before they speak, seed it: think of three rich or powerful people. Introverts or extroverts?
  4. Numbered Heads Together in fours: agree one position with one example; you call a number and that student speaks for the group.
  5. Model the answer you want them to beat: Some of it is fair — society does pay more attention to extroverts. But it generalises: most people are a mix, and it depends on the situation.
📺 On screen: the four highlighted expressions you want recycled, written large on the board. Backup: no display dependency here.
👦 Students do: negotiate meanings in pairs, then argue a group position — one speaker per group, chosen by number, so all four prepare.

🎯 The workbook text · the exam format, in class
desks
Why: Two texts in one lesson is what the split makes possible: the first is taught, the second is measured. The workbook text used to go home unchecked; here it becomes the exam practice.

WHAT WE DO: the second text of the unit, done here rather than at home — with the clock running

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the workbook reading of the unit. Say the format and the time: Same skills, exam conditions. Twelve minutes, alone, no dictionary.
  2. They work silently. You do not answer content questions during it — note them and answer afterwards.
  3. Check in pairs first, then as a class, and every answer is justified with the line that proves it. An answer without a line does not count, even when it is right.
  4. The two questions the room got wrong get re-read aloud together: what exactly in the text made the wrong option attractive?
📺 On screen: the timer, and afterwards the answer key line by line. Backup: if the workbooks are not to hand, use the second text from the unit pages in the Student's Book on the same conditions.
👦 Students do: work the second text alone against the clock, then justify every answer with a line

Chunks worth stealing
desks → pairs
Why: Reading pays for itself only when something leaves the page. Chunks, not single words — that is what the writing and the speaking of this unit will need.

WHAT WE DO: the phrases from both texts, harvested for their own speaking and writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to both texts: Find five phrases you could use yourself — not words, phrases. Underline them in the book.
  2. Board the best eight as the class calls them out. Insist on the whole chunk, with its preposition.
  3. RallyRobin: partners take turns making a sentence of their own with a chunk from the board, alternating, until the board is used up.
  4. These eight go on the unit word wall and into the interactive notebook sheet — they are the ones that come back in the test.
📺 On screen: the eight chunks, written up as they are found. Backup: none.
👦 Students do: harvest five phrases each, then use the boarded chunks in their own sentences

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end of the lesson students will be better able to describe people's character using B2 adjectives and idioms, in the context of personality types and introversion.
  • Subsidiary. Reading for gist against the clock and for detail with evidence (four multiple-choice items, SB p.45); guessing meaning from context; word stress in compound adjectives.
  • Personal. Talk less. This lesson has four pair stages — stand at the back during two of them and write down three errors instead of correcting them aloud.
By the end, students can…
  • describe a person with three precise adjectives instead of «nice»
  • use eight personality idioms and explain the picture behind them
  • find the answer to a multiple-choice question and quote the line that proves it
  • work out the meaning of an unknown expression from the sentence around it

Structure
He's very down-to-earth — he says what he thinks and he doesn't think he's important. · She can be a bit tactless, but she's completely loyal. · He's a real live wire.
Form
adjective + noun (a resourceful person) · be + adjective (she is reserved) · be + a/an + idiom (he's a wallflower). Compound adjectives keep the hyphen: broad-minded, down-to-earth, laid-back, self-confident.
Pron. watch
word stress is what makes these adjectives sound English and it is worth four minutes: broad-MINDed · down-to-EARTH · reSOURCEful · imPETuous · conSIDerate · unTRUSTworthy · self-CONFident. Compound adjectives keep a clear beat on BOTH parts, which is why laid-back and easy-going are so easy to flatten into mush
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
tactful: Do I say what I think? — Yes. Do I hurt people? — No.
reserved: Do I dislike people? — No. Do I show my feelings? — Not much.
impetuous: Do I think first? — No. Do I act fast? — Yes.
ICQ before the gist reading: How many minutes? How many questions? Pens up or down?

Assumptions: Students met basic character adjectives (kind, funny, shy, hard-working) at B1 and can read a 1,000-word text with a clear aim. They have not met compound adjectives as a class of word, and idioms of character are new.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Fifteen adjectives arrive at once and half the class stops recording them.Do not let them copy the list. The record they keep is the exercise itself plus the three sentences they write in the production stage — words tied to a person are remembered, lists are not.
«He is very sociable person» — the missing article.Correct it in the air with one word, no explanation: A sociable person. Articles are a full stage in Lesson 3; today it is a reflex, not a lecture.
The reading is long and weaker students translate it.The timer is the answer, plus the three-step procedure on the board: question first, then hunt. Say aloud that nobody is expected to understand every word.
The «describe a classmate» stage turns unkind.The rule is announced before pens move: nothing you would not say to their face. One reminder, then stop the activity for anyone who breaks it — that costs you thirty seconds and buys the rest of the year.
Introverted students go silent in the mingling stage.They have written their three words first, so they read rather than improvise — and today's text is literally an argument that their way of working is valuable. Name that out loud.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 10–18 (the reading section) and tasks 26–31 (word formation, which lives on adjectives of character).

Personality is one of the standing topics of the Russian paper: it comes back in the reading section, in the email of task 37 and in the oral. The words that arrive today — reserved, resourceful, considerate, self-confident, untrustworthy — are exactly the ones a candidate needs and almost never has, so the answer to «What is your best friend like?» collapses into good and kind. The Cambridge B2 First for Schools link is direct too: Reading and Use of English Part 1 tests precisely this kind of adjective and collocation.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
