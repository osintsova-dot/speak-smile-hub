🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A pair made of two broadcasts: a spoken one (giving directions — a skill that works both in life and in the exam) and a written one (a description of a place with adjectives). The “lost in town” dramaturgy: half the class stands on the map and leads, half is looking for the way — the Key phrases become a working tool, not a list. The adjectives are served as “the editorial standard”: a description with no adjectives goes back to its author.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“You're standing outside a school in a town you've never seen. Someone stops you: 'Excuse me, can you tell me how to get to the library?' You have the map. They don't. Half of you are locals today — you lead. Half are lost — you follow, and if you end up at the petrol station, that's on your guide. Round two: the town you actually love. You write it — and here's the editor's rule: a description with no adjectives is not a description. It's a list. Maps out.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: you are standing outside a school in a town you don't know, and someone asks you the way to the library. Half the class are locals and lead, half are lost and follow. The second broadcast: we write about a place we really love. The editorial rule — a description with no adjectives is not a description, it is a list.

WHAT WE DO: 60 seconds: guide your partner from the desk to the classroom door in English only, no gestures; the partner walks it literally

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Sixty seconds. Guide your partner from the desk to the door — words only. No hands. They walk exactly what you say. GO!
  2. Kagan Timed Pair Share: partner A guides for the first 60 seconds, on the signal they swap — B guides for their own 60 seconds. Everyone gets to be the guide.

🔥 Lost in town · the living map
stations

WHAT WE DO: half the class are locals with the map, half are lost without one; Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; we run PERFORM twice round the circle, then swap roles

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: You're outside the school. Choose ONE place you want to get to. (the TB tip: the weaker ones take nearer places, the stronger ones further ones).
  2. PREPARE 4 min: the pairs write a dialogue with the Key phrases. PRACTISE: they swap roles.
  3. PERFORM “blindfolded” through Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: the locals stand in the inner circle with the maps, the lost ones in the outer circle without. Dialogue → the outer circle moves on by one → a new guide, a new route. The lost one has NO map. Follow the words with your finger on a blank sheet. Where did you end up?
  4. Constraint: only phrases from the Key phrases; Russian costs the crew a point.
  5. The TB Challenge for the strong ones: Two destinations in one dialogue.
  6. Roles swap after 5 minutes.
📺 On screen: a map for the “locals” · a blank sheet for the “lost” · the Key phrases on screen (backup — the Key phrases in big letters on the board and on cards on the desks)
👦 Students do: they walk around and speak for real. Expect: the information gap holds the English up with no reminders; ending up in the wrong place produces laughter and a re-run

Product: the description of a place
PRODUCT · the place description
solo → pairs

WHAT WE DO: THINK (choose a place + notes on 6 questions) → PREPARE (4 paragraphs) → WRITE → CHECK → peer review → the wall

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: Your favourite place. Six questions on the screen — notes only, no sentences yet. (why you go there · big or small · which places · what you do · what people eat there · why you like it).
  2. PREPARE: four paragraphs following the book's plan — the place and why · a general description and favourite spots · food and drink · why you like it.
  3. WRITE: Start: 'My favourite place to visit is … . I usually go there because …' At least five adjectives and three 'there is/are'.
  4. CHECK (ex.7): vocabulary of places? there is/are correct? adjectives in the right place?
  5. Peer review (ex.8) following Kagan RallyCoach: the author reads their text out loud line by line, the partner-coach works down the editor's checklist — underlines wrong vocabulary and grammar, writes a comment underneath and explains it out loud. Then they swap roles.
  6. Publication: the descriptions go on the unit wall. The town guide is LIVE!
📺 On screen: the editor's checklist · the unit wall · timer
👦 Students do: they write in class off a fresh model. Expect: peer review catches 80% of the errors before publication

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask for and give directions with 6 Key phrases using a map, and write a 4-paragraph description of a place with there is/are and at least five adjectives in the right position.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 prepositions of place on the map; the Archie video (ex.2–4) and the route on the map; ex.5 complete the dialogue; Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; Jayden's text about Brighton (ex.1–3); the subskill adjectives (ex.4–6); peer review twice.
  • Personal. We write in class, not at home — I see the process. The editorial rule: a description with no adjectives and no there is/are is not published, and the author fixes it, not me.
By the end, students can…
  • ask the way with two Key phrases
  • give directions with go straight ahead / turn left / go past / it's on your right
  • follow a route on a map from spoken directions
  • write a description of a place in 4 paragraphs with adjectives

Target language
Lexis
Excuse me. Can you tell me how to get to the (library)?Is there a (library) near here?Go straight ahead.Turn left/right.Go past the (skate park).It's on your left/right.
Structure
Excuse me. Can you tell me how to get to the library? · Is there a library near here? · Go straight ahead. · Turn left into Well Street. · Go past the skate park. · It's on your right, opposite the police station.
Form
prepositions of place: opposite · next to · between … and … · on the corner of … and … · near · the adjective goes BEFORE the noun (an exciting place), but AFTER be/look/feel (The place is exciting).
Pron. watch
an even, slow pace in the directions (the TB observation): we stress the street names and LEFT / RIGHT · Excuse me sounds like one word /ɪkˈskjuːz mi/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Go past to the skate park” — what doesn't belong? → to: Go PAST the skate park.
“Is there a library near here?” — why Is and not Are? → one library (the TB tip).
“It's a shop beautiful” — correct it. → a beautiful shop: the adjective goes before the noun.

Assumptions: SB pp.67–69, KTP pair 43. The skeletons of the dialogue and the description come ready from the book — the mission only frames them and adds a real audience. All the adjectives in ex.4 are known passively.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They read the dialogue instead of playing itRole cards: the local has the map, the lost one doesn't. The information gap makes the dialogue real.
They copy the description off JaydenThe editorial rule: the model's skeleton — yes, the model's facts — no. Their own place gives them their own material.
They pile the adjectives up at the endThe editor checks 3 positions: where the adjective stands before the noun, where it stands after be. They swap texts — I don't correct.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
