WB Unit 6 (pp.44–51) · notebook · points to the leaderboard next pair
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask and answer present continuous Yes/No and Wh- questions accurately and get real information at an information desk using the unit Key phrases.
  • Subsidiary. Word order (be BEFORE the subject), short answers with no contraction in the +; Wh- questions; Grammar round-up (cumulative U1–6); Key phrases + Real-world grammar “We're staying near there”; intonation and stress in Wh- questions.
  • Personal. Their weak point isn't understanding but WORD ORDER in writing and “Yes, he's” instead of “Yes, he is”. Keep the competitive interrogation frame — they need the questions in order to win.
By the end, students can…
  • build Yes/No questions with be before the subject
  • give correct short answers (Yes, he is / No, they aren't)
  • ask Wh- questions with correct word order and stress
  • ask for information about a place: times, price, how to get there

Structure
Are you having a good time? — Yes, we are. · Is she wearing glasses? — No, she isn't. · What are you doing? · Where are you sitting? · I'd like some information about this exhibition.
Form
Wh- questions: question word + be + subject + verb-ing? → What is she doing? · Where are you going? · Short answers: Yes, he IS (no contraction!) / No, he ISN'T (with contraction).
Pron. watch
ABCD TB p.92: in Wh- questions the intonation FALLS, with stress on the information word: What TIME is it open? · How MUCH does it cost? · HOW can I get there?
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“You are wearing? — is that a question?” → No: ARE YOU wearing? (be before the subject).
“Yes, he's — is that OK?” → No! In a positive short answer the full form: Yes, he is.
“What she is doing?” → No — What IS SHE doing? (be straight after the question word).
“We're staying near there — do they live there?” → No, temporarily: they've come for a few days.

Assumptions: Questions with be are not news to them (U1), but present continuous questions in writing + short answers are a gap; Key phrases for an information desk are a new functional block for both lines. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (-ing spelling / missing ‘be’ / question word order), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
