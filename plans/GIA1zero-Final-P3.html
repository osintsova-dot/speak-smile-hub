🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The final of the year for a beginner group: a year ago these kids didn't speak English AT ALL. Today everyone holds a dialogue and gets a certificate. I say this journey out loud to each of them personally — in the nomination.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Your score + two things that GREW + one summer focus. Private!” “The certificate is for the YEAR.”

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Inside-Outside Circle: a carousel of familiar questions with hints; I run the speaking check one at a time in the corner

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Inside-Outside Circle: Two circles! Inside — question card (a familiar one, with a hint). Ask! Answer! Swap roles. Outside — rotate two!
  2. Cards — the fair deck: no surprises, success is programmed in.
  3. My check in the corner: one at a time, 1–2 min — 3 familiar questions + 1 free one; ticks on a list; I praise specifically.
  4. The carousel never stops: the circle never stops!
📺 On screen: the deck of familiar questions · my checklist · the corner
👦 Students do: they talk for all 18 minutes on familiar cards while I listen to each one personally — a check with no queue and no fear. Expected: I get through 8–10; the rest — during the I-can block. Typical error: yes/no with no detail — the card's hint pulls out a full answer.

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Timed Pair Share (a run-through) → circle: everyone gives 2–3 lines of “I can… — for example…”; I finish checking the rest

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Timed Pair Share (45 sec per voice): Two-three ‘I can’ lines with examples — try them on your partner.
  2. Circle: everyone says their own lines (I can count — one, two, three! · I can say hello — Welcome!).
  3. The class answers everyone: That's growth! + applause.
  4. In parallel I finish checking, in the corner, the ones I haven't heard yet.

WHAT WE DO: the party: 2 games from the year, chosen by vote (from my list); no score — pure joy

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is speaking an exam?” → It's a chat with me, on questions you already know from the fair. With hints. Proven: you'll manage.
“Is the certificate for the score?” → It's for the YEAR. For everyone. With a personal nomination — for your strong side.
“Will I forget it all over the summer?” → The summer kit will help you remember — and we'll write ourselves a reminder letter.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Upset over the scoreThe score stays private, ALWAYS with 2 pluses alongside it; publicly — only the nominations; I go over to anyone upset during the carousel with a personal “look what grew”.
A freeze at the speaking checkFamiliar cards + hints; a freeze → I pick their most confident question (I remember it from the fair); the dialogue happens.
The party tips into chaos2 games from my list, chosen by vote, timing 8 min each, the one-clap signal lives on at the party too.
Tears of farewellA ready line: “September is close, the group continues” + the summer checklist as a thread; the finale is a group photo, not goodbye forever.
Parents wait for the resultsMini-summaries for parents (score + growth + a summer plan) — as a slip/message after the lesson.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
