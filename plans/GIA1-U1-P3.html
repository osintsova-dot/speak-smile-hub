📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can deliver a 60-second “live from New Zealand” broadcast with zero be-errors and 3+ precise place adjectives — grammar hour (exactly 45′) + scout hour (45′) inside ONE mission.
  • Subsidiary. Hour 1: gate exposes the written ceiling; tables ex.1/5 in a base sprint; «Where am I?» + contraction taboo; skeptic duel; Brain teaser; gate rematch closes the hour. Hour 2: opposites gate; 16 adjectives + taboo on nice/good/bad; listening subskill (visual clues), 3 passes, scout report; broadcast product feeds on BOTH hours.
  • Personal. Grammar is NEVER the announced topic — the show is. Phrase everything as broadcast standards («air quality»). At minute 45 don't «start a new lesson» — the studio just gets its second contract: the picture language.
By the end, students can…
  • write 8–10 contracted be-sentences fast and clean
  • flip aff↔neg in under-5-sec duels
  • produce 8 opposite pairs at duel speed
  • file a scout report from natural-speed audio and take it on air

Target language
Lexis
'm / 're / 's'm not / isn't / aren'tbeautiful / uglyclean / dirtyempty / crowdedhot / coldinteresting / boringmodern / oldquiet / noisysafe / dangerous
Structure
We're live from… · It's hot and it isn't crowded. · The beaches aren't crowded. · 🚀 bonus: This city has never been quiet. You'll love it.
Form
apostrophe = the lost letter's grave; I'm not (never «amn't»); adj + noun / be + adj — adjectives never plural; broadcast register: contractions ONLY (TB ABCD: contracted forms = speech + informal writing).
Pron. watch
weak forms /wɪə/ /ðeə/; isn't — one beat; crowded — 2 syllables; quiet vs quite; broadcast intonation — energy up at sentence starts.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“You've used be for four years. Can you write they're–their–there right at speed?” → gate 1 answers.
“Is ‘it's a nice place’ broadcast language?” → No — taboo; broadcasters say modern, safe, empty.
“Do scouts look at photos before the call?” → Yes — visual clues ARE the subskill.

Assumptions: be aff/neg 100% familiar orally (GMF4); real ceilings — apostrophes at speed + self-monitoring in speech. The 16 adjectives as a SET are partly new; NZ facts = genuine info-gap. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.
