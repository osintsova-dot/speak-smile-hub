Get Involved! A1+ · Unit 1 “Our world” · P3 — Grammar (be) + Vocabulary & Listening
A1+ teens (~11–13 y.o., readers & writers) · 90 min · double lesson (Grammar sprint + Vocab & Listening) · pairs / teams · SB pp. 16–17
Week 21.09–27.09 · Pair 7 · Lessons 13–14🚀 ACCELERATED (after GMF4)~12–14 pupils · teams of 3–4WDYT? How are countries different?
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be more accurate with be affirmative & negative (contracted forms in writing) and better able to describe a place with 8 opposite adjective pairs, both in speaking and writing.
  • Subsidiary. Retrieve countries / nationality adjectives from P1 & GMF4; develop listening using visual clues (New Zealand); build a short spoken comparison “my country vs. New Zealand” with sentence frames; logic (Brain teaser).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; every drill inside a game with points/instant feedback; run one hinge check before moving on; brain-break at the 45′ hinge.
By the end, pupils can…
  • write be aff/neg correctly with contracted forms ('m / 're / 's · isn't / aren't);
  • use 8 opposite adjective pairs to describe places (adjective + noun / be + adjective, never plural);
  • pick out specific information from a listening using visual clues;
  • say 3–4 sentences comparing their country with New Zealand.
Target language
Grammar
be affirmative: I'm · you're · he's / she's / it's · we're · they're  ·  negative: I'm not · isn't · aren't
Lexis (set 2)
beautiful↔uglyclean↔dirtyempty↔crowdedquiet↔noisyinteresting↔boringhot↔coldmodern↔oldsafe↔dangerous
Structure
a modern city (adj + noun) · The city is modern (be + adj) · It's / They're + adj · New Zealand is different from my country because it's…
Form
Contracted be in writing; agreement isn't (he/she/it) vs aren't (you/we/they); adjectives are never plural (a modern city ✓ · a moderns city ✗).
Pron. watch
weak forms in contractions /ə/; isn't /ˈɪznt/, aren't /ɑːnt/; word stress link → SB p116 (Unit 1 Pronunciation).
Listening
Subskill: using visual clues — read titles, photos & maps before you listen (New Zealand, SB p17, track 12).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“The parks aren't beautiful.” → positive or negative? (negative) How do we know? (aren't)
“She aren't Spanish.” — correct? (no → isn't) Why? (she → isn't)
“a modern city” or “a city modern”? (adj before noun) · Can I say “crowdeds”? (no — never plural)

Assumptions: countries + nationality adjectives known from P1 + GMF4; be is largely revision for this 🚀 group → treat as a diagnostic/accuracy sprint, not first teaching. Pupils read & write. Teams: 3–4; rotate roles. 🐣-line note: if teaching a from-scratch group, expand each be stage with full input + more controlled drill (TB Reinforcement); the 🚀 timings below compress be to a sprint and spend the saved time on production & the listening battle (TB Challenge).

Classroom management — signals
Countdown — “Pens down in 5…4…3…2…1.” → stop & look up.
“Showdown!” — teams flip mini-whiteboards up together on your call → instant scan.
Points / leaderboard — team points on the board; accuracy scores more than speed.
Hand up + silent — you raise a hand, class raises hands & stops talking.
Volume dial — raise/lower arm = louder/quieter for pair work.
“Freeze & swap” — stop, change partner/role, go.

Lead-in · retrieval
whole class

Retrieval warm-up. Board: You know this already — let's prove it. 60 seconds! Flash 6 country cards from P1; teams write the nationality adjective + a full sentence. Kagan Showdown: Sony is a brand from Japan. It's Japanese. Fast scan, 1 point per correct sentence.

Tie to the unit question: Our question this unit — how are countries different? Today we get precise: the verb “be”, then the words to describe a place.

Diagnostic, not teaching. Project the be table (SB p16 ex1). ▶ Be Table Race: teams fill full + contracted forms against the clock (5 min). Contractions win points — the apostrophe must be there.

Quick reveal, self-check by team; award points. Model the connected pronunciation once: /wiːə/ from Turkey → we're from Turkey.

21–30′
Controlled practice · quiz + error hunt
teams

▶ “Where am I?” quiz (SB p16 ex4): read each clue, teams choose the correct be form on whiteboards, Showdown! Points; the mystery place is the payoff.

▶ Spot the Mistake (Common errors round): show 6 sentences — She aren't Italian. · Theyre from Rio. · We not are students. Teams write the fix. You're the editor — correct it and say why. Keeps the accuracy target front and centre.

Fast finishers: write two true + two false be sentences about famous places for the next team.

 SB p16 ex4 · error slides · whiteboards
30–37′
Hinge check + Brain teaser
whole class → teams

Hinge question (mini-whiteboards, <2 min). Anna is Italian. She's from Rome. Choose: “She ___ from Milan.” A) isn't B) aren't C) not is D) amn't → A. Decision: 80%+ A → move on; many B → re-drill isn't (he/she/it) vs aren't; spread across C/D → 2-min re-teach of the negative form with the 📓 card.

▶ Brain teaser (SB p16 ex9) — Kagan Numbered Heads: teams solve the country-spelling puzzle together; a random number reports. Logic win, not language — everyone can shine.

 hinge slide · SB p16 ex9 · whiteboards
37–45′
Freer production · be about places
teams

▶ Four true, four tricky. Each team writes 4 sentences about real countries / cities using be — 2 affirmative, 2 negative, one with a deliberate mistake hidden: Brazil is in South America. · London isn't a small city. · The Netherlands are… (spot it!) Swap boards with another team — find the hidden error, tick the correct ones. Accuracy points.

This closes the grammar half and warms up “places” language for the second half: Now let's get the exact words to describe those places.

45′ hinge
🔄 Brain-break · Corners
stand up, move

Stand up — we're changing gears. Four corners = beautiful · boring · crowded · quiet. Call a place — Norilsk in winter! Moscow metro! A shopping centre on Saturday! — pupils move to the corner that fits their opinion and tell a neighbour It's [crowded] because… Bridges straight into the place adjectives.

47–55′
Vocab sprint · 16 adjectives + rule
desks → pairs

📓 Interactive notebook (~5 min). Glue in “DESCRIBE A PLACE” A5 note opposite the be card. Say the 8 pairs as a chain — beautiful — ugly, clean — dirty, empty — crowded… Language Lens box: a modern city (adj + noun) / The city is modern (be + adj) — adjectives are never plural.

▶ Personalise: circle the four adjectives that describe Norilsk and cross out one that never does. Compare in pairs: Norilsk is cold and modern. It isn't hot. (be + adjective — reuse this morning's grammar!)

Listening battle · New Zealand
teams

▶ Listen & choose (SB p17 ex5, track 12): one thing to catch — is or isn't? Teams answer on whiteboards, Showdown! (1 NZ is/isn't small · 2 Auckland is/isn't the capital · 3 Maori is/isn't the only language).

76–85′
Production · compare places
pairs

Sentence frames on the board (tiered). Support: New Zealand is ___. My country is ___. Core: New Zealand is [safe], but my country is ___. For example, ___. Stretch: Although New Zealand is ___, my country ___, so ___.

▶ Timed Pair Share (SB p17 ex8): 40 sec each — Is New Zealand similar to or different from your country? Why? Push past the frame: Good — now add “because”. Two pairs report one sentence to the class.

Fast finishers: write your two best sentences in your notebook under the adjective note.
