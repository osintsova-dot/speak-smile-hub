🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A long text frightens new students, so the way in has four steps: words BEFORE the text → predicting from photos and fact files (you cannot get it wrong) → reading in chunks in crews (each crew takes one village) → tasks with the «finger on the line» support. I give the unit subskill straight and simply: the first time we read FAST and only for «what it is about», we come back for the details. The product is modest: 4 opinion sentences.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two photos. In the first one it rains every single day — the wettest place on earth. In the second one it's minus fifty and phones freeze in your hand. Real villages, real kids, real schools. Today: six new words, then we read the article — in parts, your crew takes one village — and at the end you write four sentences: which place would YOU survive in? Your opinion, your words.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Two villages: one where it rains every day, and one where it is -50 and phones freeze. We learn six words, read the article in chunks (one village per team) and write four sentences — which place would you survive in.

Reading in chunks
crews

WHAT WE DO: the first run FAST (subskill) → ex.3 what the text is about → track 25 and reading village by village through Jigsaw → putting it together on the board → checking the sticky notes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. RULE OF THE DAY (subskill): First time — read FAST. Don't stop at new words. Just find out what it's about. 90 seconds for the whole text.
  2. ex.3: Weather disasters? How the weather is changing? Or living in extreme weather? TB key: c.
  3. We listen to the text (track 25) and follow with a finger — all of it, no stops.
  4. Kagan Jigsaw: first I show on the board what one finished fact looks like («In Mawsynram it rains every day — 11,871 mm a year»), and only then — Your crew owns ONE village. Find three facts. You will teach the class. Everyone reads half the text but will know all of it: you tell them about your village, they tell you about theirs.
  5. Putting it together: the teams report in turn, I build two columns on the board, MAWSYNRAM / OYMYAKON.
  6. Checking the sticky notes: Which guesses came TRUE? — we circle the matches.
📺 On screen: SB pp.50–51 (the text in 2 chunks) · audio track 25 (backup — I read the article aloud myself) · the board in two columns
👦 Students do: they own their village and listen about the other one. Expected: putting it together gives the whole picture without the fear of the length

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can get the main idea of an article first and then find facts with support, match 6 new words to definitions, and write 4 opinion sentences by frame.
  • Subsidiary. Fact files and vocabulary before the text (ex.1–2); ex.3, the main idea; ex.4 (track 25), six questions in pairs; ex.5 M / O / Both with support; Word work ex.6–7; Critical thinking in a simplified form (one hardship per village).
  • Personal. I cut the text into 2 chunks — one village per team, then we put it together. Vocabulary BEFORE reading, not during. I say the answers one at a time, not as a list.
By the end, students can…
  • read a text quickly and say what it is about
  • find an answer and point at the proof line
  • match 6 new words to definitions
  • write 4 opinion sentences with a frame

average /ˈæv(ə)rɪdʒ/ · noise /nɔɪz/ · grass /ɡrɑːs/ · roof /ruːf/ · destroys /dɪˈstrɔɪz/ · last /lɑːst/ (matching vowels: noise = destroys, grass = last — TB tip)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
average — does that mean «an average student»? → no: the typical value, the mean of them all (average rainfall 11,871 mm).
last — does that mean «the last one»? → here it is a verb, «to go on for»: My lesson lasts 45 minutes.
destroys — does that mean «breaks it a little»? → no, destroys it completely.

Assumptions: SB pp.50–51, KTP pair 31. They read more slowly than the «ours» line — so the text comes in chunks, the tasks come with frames, the answers come one at a time.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They are frightened by the length of the textThe text is split into 2 chunks by team (Mawsynram / Oymyakon); each team owns its own and teaches it to the others — everyone reads half and ends up knowing all of it.
New words stall the readingWord work BEFORE the text, with pictures and gestures; a mini-glossary in the margin.
«I don't know what to think» in the productAn opinion frame: I think… because… + a bank of reasons on the slide.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
