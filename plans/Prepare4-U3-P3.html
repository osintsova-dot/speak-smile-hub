🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Describe city problems with the nine Unit 6 words and quantify them correctly: some/any, much/many, a lot of, a few/a little — matched to countable and uncountable nouns.
What is genuinely hard
Not the words — the countability underneath them: Russian happily counts advice, information and news, and «трафик» feels countable too. Every quantifier choice stands on a countable/uncountable decision the students don't know they are making.
Typical error to expect
much people · many money · a few informations — countability guessed from Russian, where информация and совет count fine [high priority, A2/B1]
I haven't got some money / Have you got some ideas? — some leaking into negatives and questions [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
Vocabulary comes first because the reading and every grammar example are made of it. The reading about two teenage inventors carries the unit's exam-style cloze — run as ordinary careful reading — and hands the grammar its examples (some people…, any petrol), so nothing is taught out of context. The countable/uncountable sort is done standing and physically, because it is the invisible decision under every error. The practice is a timed race because quantifier choices must become fast to survive speech. The productive stage — my perfect place to live — forces the frames and the quantifiers into personal sentences.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two teenagers saw a city problem and got rich fixing it. One built an electric bike because of Beijing smog; one built a torch that runs on the heat of your hand. Today: nine city problems by name, the grammar to complain about them properly — and your own perfect place to live.”

Grammar · quantifiers discovery
class → standing
Why: The rules are extracted from the classroom and the article, not announced. The countable/uncountable sort is done standing, because the distinction — not the words — is what Russian speakers guess wrong.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–4: rules from the room and the article + the standing count/uncount sort

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Warmer from the room, books closed: TB warmer: some/any/much/many/a lot of/a few/a little on the board + classroom things (desks, chairs, water, money). Is there any water? How many desks are there? — one example per quantifier, from the room.
  2. ex.1: the some/any rule from the article examples. Key: a some (positives) · b any (negatives and questions).
  3. ex.2 fast, solo, 2 minutes. Key: 1 any · 2 some · 3 some · 4 any · 5 any · 6 some.
  4. ex.3: the seven examples → four rules. Pairs extract, you box the big one: positives love A LOT OF. Key: a many, much and a lot of = large amounts · b much/many avoid positive sentences (use a lot of) · c a few and a little = small amounts · d a few/a little avoid negatives.
  5. ex.4 STANDING: I call a noun — countable walk left, uncountable right: cities! traffic! petrol! people! light! toys! …and the traps: information! advice! graffiti! Key: countable: cities, people, toys · uncountable: traffic, petrol, light (watch the irregular plurals: people, children, men, women). Write both lists into the wall zones as they settle.
  6. Concept checks from the plan header — all three, cold-called.
📺 On screen: the quantifier shelf fills in chalk; the two wall lists are written as the sort settles — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: extract rules, then vote with their feet. Expect information/advice to send half the class the wrong way — that walk IS the lesson. Typical error: people sorted as uncountable because it has no -s

WHAT WE DO: ex.6 — the Corpus race with the TB's ten-second countdown

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.6 exactly as the TB runs it: pairs, one item at a time, TEN seconds on the countdown, a point per correct choice.
  2. Key: 1 a few, much · 2 a lot of, a little, some · 3 a few, much, many · 4 any, a little.
  3. After the race, the two most-missed items get thirty calm seconds each: the pair that got it right explains WHY.

solo → pairs → class
Why: The productive outcome: describe the perfect place to live using the frames and the quantifiers for themselves. Logged errors open the next lesson.

WHAT WE DO: ex.7: my perfect place to live — quantified, personal, out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two minutes solo: five sentences about your perfect place to live, each with a quantifier — frames on the board: There's a lot of… · There are a few… · There isn't any… · There's only a little… · There isn't much…
  2. Kagan Timed Pair Share, 90 seconds each way — mixed-ability pairs (TB): the listener collects one DIFFERENCE between the two perfect places.
  3. Four or five students report the difference, not their own list: In her place there isn't any traffic; in mine there's a lot — I like noise.
  4. You log quantifier errors silently — they open lesson 4.
📺 On screen: the five frames in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: quantify their dream town, listen for contrast, report. Expect strong personalisation (no school! a lot of cats!) — allow it, the grammar rides on it. Typical error: there is many parks — logged, not corrected

WB Unit 6 (p.24–27)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can name nine city problems and choose the right quantifier (some/any, much/many, a lot of, a few/a little) by deciding countable or uncountable first.
  • Subsidiary. Reading the inventors article for gist and detail; a multiple-choice cloze done as careful reading (SB p.37 ex.2); listening for speakers' problems (tracks 26–27).
  • Personal. Never say «Reading Part 5» out loud — the cloze is just careful reading. In the race stage keep the pace brutal and the tone light: speed is the point, errors are data.
By the end, students can…
  • name nine city problems and say which their own town has
  • decide countable or uncountable — including the traps (advice, information, graffiti)
  • choose some/any, much/many, a lot of, a few/a little correctly at speed
  • describe their perfect place to live in 4–5 quantified sentences

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I haven't got some money» — fix it. → any: a negative.
«There is much traffic today» — natural? → no: much avoids positives — There's a lot of traffic.
«a few informations» — what's wrong? → information is uncountable: a little information (advice and news too — Russian counts them, English doesn't).

Assumptions: SB p.36–38 (top), syllabus lesson 17. Some/any existed in Prepare 3; much/many were met but never systematised with a lot of and a few/a little. Of the nine city words the class usually owns two or three (graffiti as a loan word, traffic jam half-known). Countability of information/advice/graffiti is effectively new.

Likely problemWhat you do

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
