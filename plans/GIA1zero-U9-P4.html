Opening · check-in + rewind

WHAT WE DO: opening through the Key phrase of the day + Kagan Mix-Pair-Share; rewind P3: the keys in chorus

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Opening: Hi! How was your weekend? — every student answers it coming in (without knowing it yet — the phrase of the day!).
  2. Mix-Pair-Share to build energy: MIX — walk! …PAIR — nearest person! SHARE: ‘How was your weekend?’ — ‘It was… I…’ Thirty seconds each, then swap.
  3. Rewind P3: Secret keys check! Listen…? — TWICE! Pictures…? — BEFORE! + 2 facts about the festivals by show of hands.
  4. Bridge: You just used TODAY'S star phrase — ‘How was your weekend? It was great!’ Today we collect ALL the year's talking phrases — and write your best email ever.
📺 On screen: board: “How was your weekend?”
👦 Students do: they answer the phrase of the day and recognise it in the lesson topic. Expected: the “I already know this” effect.

pairs

WHAT WE DO: Skills boost step by step with a phrase menu: choosing a situation → 4+ phrases from the menu → 6–8 lines with a skeleton frame → rehearsal with a prompter

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: the pair chooses A (a friend + a photo) or B (the tourist office). The phrase menu = the table from the board + skeleton frames I have: A: How was…? → It was… → Who's this? → It's… → It's a great photo! · B: How can I get to…? → Take… → How much…? → It's… → Buy…
  2. PREPARE, 6 min: they write 6–8 lines FOLLOWING THE SKELETON (the skeleton already gives you half of it!).
  3. PRACTISE, 5 min: read-through → a prompter partner (the sheet stays with the prompter, the speaker tries without) → swap.
  4. I go round and help the B pairs with prices/routes: The bus from our school to the centre — how much?
📺 On screen: SB p.113 Skills boost · skeleton frames A/B (printouts) · phrase table
👦 Students do: they build a dialogue on a ready-made skeleton and rehearse with support. Expected: 6–8 lines from everyone. Typical error: a dialogue turns into a monologue — the ping-pong skeleton carries it on its own.

WHAT WE DO: performances: in parallel in groups of six → 2 pairs for the class; peer review with 3 questions; lesson A checkpoint

desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: writing tools from text B: opening/closing · apostrophes · and/but · because; a pair hunt

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A hunt in text B (Louis's email) in pairs: find — the opening (Hi Jasper, How are you?) · the closing · an apostrophe · and/but · because.
  2. Checking: because = WHY (I'm busy because we've got exams) · and = plus · but = surprise turn.
  3. The email frame goes on the board (STAYS UP): Hi ___, / How are you? / I want to tell you about my weekend. / On Saturday I… / Then I… / It was…! / See you later, / ___
  4. Counting on the frame: The template gives you ~40 words free. You add your weekend — that's the hundred!
📺 On screen: SB p.114 text B · board: email frame
👦 Students do: they dig the tools out of the model and get the frame. Expected: “40 words free” takes the fear away. Typical error: because at the start of a broken-off phrase — point at the model.

desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: writing from the frame: a 3-line plan → an email about their own weekend, ~100 words; a past verb bank; quiet music

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK, 3 min: a 3-line skeleton plan (who to? what did you do on Saturday? what on Sunday?).
  2. Verb bank on the board: went · played · had · saw · watched · met · was/were.
  3. WRITE, 12 min, from the frame, quietly: Follow the template line by line. Your weekend, your words. About a hundred — not exactly!
  4. I go round with targeted help: past forms, apostrophes.
  5. CHECK, 2 min, from the book: grammar? punctuation? is and/but/because there?
📺 On screen: frame on the board · verb bank · notebooks · timer
👦 Students do: they write the longest letter of the year from the frame — achievable for everyone. Expected: 80–110 words from everyone. Typical error: “I go to park yesterday” — point at the verb bank: went!
💪 Support: a half-filled frame (only plug in the verbs and places). ⭐ For the fast finishers: + a P.S. with a question for a friend (Do you like…?).		notebooks · timer

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can act out a short dialogue (6–8 lines) for situation A or B using 4+ Key phrases with the phrase menu, and write an email about last weekend (~100 words, from a template) with opening/closing lines and because/and/but.
  • Subsidiary. A Key phrases table by 4 functions; the audio 62 model dialogues read by roles; Skills boost step by step with a prompter; the year's genres identified; writing ONE genre (email — the most template-friendly) instead of three; peer review with a checklist.
  • Personal. Per the line's task: writing from a template of one genre. We choose email (B) — it has the strongest support (Hi… / How are you? / I went… / See you!). We hit 100 words with the TEMPLATE, not a race. Dialogues — with a prompt sheet, from memory only for those who want to.
By the end, students can…
  • sort 8 Key phrases into 4 functions with the table
  • act a 6–8 line dialogue with the phrase menu
  • name the year's text genres (blog/email/story)
  • write a ~100-word email from the template

Target language
Lexis
How was your weekend?It was great!How can I get to…?How much does it cost?It's a great photo!Who's this?Buy your ticket on the bus.Take the bus from the station.
Structure
How was your weekend? — It was great. I went to… · How can I get to the festival? — Take the bus. · Hi Jasper, How are you? … See you later!
Form
the email template: Hi + name, → How are you? → I want to tell you about… → 3–4 past sentences (went · played · had · saw) → It was… → See you later! + name.
Pron. watch
How was = /haʊ wəz/ · the question rises ↗, the answer falls ↘ · It was GREAT — with feeling!
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“An email about the weekend — what tense?” → Past: went, played, had.
“Is the dialogue straight from memory?” → No: first with the sheet, a prompter helps; from memory — whoever's ready.
“How much is 100 words?” → About 3 small paragraphs. The template will get you there on its own.

Assumptions: Key phrases have come up unit by unit; past simple is fresh after P2–P3. A ~100-word letter is the longest of the year for the from-zero line: without a template it won't fly, with one — everyone manages. The bar is honest: “about 100”, not “exactly 100”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
