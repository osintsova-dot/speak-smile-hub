WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Mix-Pair-Share: a rewind of your own unit-topic in the mix; a bridge to the fair via a «boring/alive stand» demo

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: Your fair unit — name it!
  2. ▶ Kagan · Mix-Pair-Share (2 rounds of 30 sec): MIX! PAIR! SHARE one thing you remember from your fair unit — then LISTEN to your partner's. A new pair for the second round — everyone speaks at once.
  3. Bridge-demo: I show a «boring stand» (a sheet of text) vs a «lively» one (a sheet + «guess what's in the box»). Which stand do YOU stop at? THAT's today's job.
📺 On screen: two demo sheets
👦 Students do: Students recall the units and vote with their feet for the lively stand. Expected: unanimous — the principle lands without a lecture.

Sprint: stand content
Produce · stand content

WHAT WE DO: a content sprint: a title, 5 unit facts/words IN THE UNIT'S GRAMMAR, a visual; the writer leads, the axes are on the desk

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Launch: CONTENT sprint — twelve minutes. Title, five facts in your unit's grammar, one visual. Writer leads, everybody writes.
  2. I go round with the axes: Content — is it FROM your unit? Language — is the grammar right? A fact outside the unit — out it goes.
  3. A swap spelling check: teams exchange drafts for 60 sec — an error hunt (+1 for every one found).
  4. Fast finishers ⭐: a 6th trap-fact «true or false?» for the guests.
📺 On screen: stand templates (asset) · SB Quick review pages · markers
👦 Students do: Students assemble the stand's language core — the unit review turns into a product. Expected: 5 facts in 12 min for everyone. Typical error: unit facts in Russian — English first, that's the Language axis.

Building the stand's game
Produce · the interaction
desks + floor · teams

WHAT WE DO: building the stand's game: choosing a mechanic from the year's game menu + a rule in English + props

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Mechanics menu (every game of the year!): guess-the-word from a fragment · a 3-question mini-quiz · «what's in the box» · a spelling challenge · mime-and-guess · a true/false trap.
  2. Gate: In your game the guest READS, SPEAKS or LISTENS in English. No English — no game.
  3. The game's rule, in writing: The rule is… You need to… You win if… (this is also the stand's text).
  4. A quick prototype + a 2-min test on a neighbouring team.
📺 On screen: craft props · mechanics cards
👦 Students do: Students choose a mechanic, write the rule, test the prototype on their neighbours. Expected: every team picks its own mechanic — the fair comes out varied. Typical error: a game harder to explain than to play — we simplify it down to 1 rule.

38–44′
Stand pitches (standing)
Pitch · 20-sec stand presentation
standing

WHAT WE DO: hosting training: 3 stand phrases in chorus and team by team, 20-sec pitches standing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hosting phrases in chorus with a barker's energy: Welcome to our stand! · Try our quiz! · You win a point!
  2. Every team — a 20-sec stand pitch to the class (against the timer), at least two people speak.
  3. The class responds like guests: Sounds fun! / What do we win?
  4. A drill of praise for the guest: Well done! · So close! · Try again! — with gestures.
📺 On screen: timer
👦 Students do: Students drill the barker phrases and pitch standing — the fair's energy is caught. Expected: voices switch on. Typical error: mumbling under the breath — «a barker is heard across the whole fair!»

Dress rehearsal
Run-through · dress rehearsal with test-guests
stands

WHAT WE DO: the dress rehearsal: guests go round the stands, play and ask; hosts receive them; roles rotate halfway through

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Launch: Guests — visit, play, ask. Hosts — welcome, run the game, answer. Six minutes!
  2. Rotation: hosts ↔ guests. Everyone has been in both roles.
  3. I go round as an incognito guest: I play at 2–3 stands, ask a question — the hosts' reaction goes into my notes.
  4. I listen to the Q&A: a freeze → I point at the survival formula on the board.
📺 On screen: stands · question deck · timer
👦 Students do: Everyone is both host and guest — a double run of the fair, the units' language runs in both directions. Expected: games get «worked out» on the fly — that's the dress rehearsal's purpose. Typical error: hosts playing their own game — «the GUEST plays!»

Polishing the stands
Upgrade · Quiz-Quiz-Trade + polishing
mingle → desks

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Quiz-Quiz-Trade (the tricky-question deck — Q&A training for the whole class at once) → teams fix 2 weak spots from feedback

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Quiz-Quiz-Trade (4 min): everyone has a question card from the deck. Find a partner: QUIZ them — they answer with a full sentence (or ‘Good question! I think…’). They quiz you. TRADE cards — new partner! 3–4 swaps — the survival formula drilled into everyone at once.
  2. Feedback round: every team gets 2 specifics from «their» guests (The rule was not clear. · Add a picture of…).
  3. Fixing: 2 weak spots, no more; I check the Language axis personally at every stand.
  4. The director fills in the readiness checklist: poster ✔ game ✔ 3 phrases each ✔ prize stamp ✔.
  5. ⭐ Bonus: a teaser phrase for tomorrow's guests (Only here: find out if YOU can survive Unit 8!).
📺 On screen: readiness checklists · materials
👦 Students do: Students make precise fixes from other teams' feedback — peer review works for the product. Expected: the main fixes are the game rules. Typical error: decoration creeps back in — hand them the axes again.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, teams have a ready fair stand (a poster against the axes + interaction for guests) and have survived a dress rehearsal with test-guests and Q&A.
  • Subsidiary. The stand = a unit review woven into a product (the unit's topic+lexis+grammar on the poster and in the interaction); Q&A training: we answer guests' questions in full sentences; the fair's hosting phrases go active.
  • Personal. Stand day easily eats up time on cutting and colouring: a hard rule «language before beauty» — texts and the interaction first, decoration in whatever time is left.
By the end, students can…
  • build a stand poster with the four axes in mind
  • run a mini-game for guests in English
  • answer surprise questions about their unit
  • pitch their stand in 20 seconds

Target language
Lexis
Welcome to our stand!Try our quiz! · Have a go!Guess…!You win a point!Our stand is about… · Come and try…You need to… · The rule is…Well done! · So close! · Try again!
Structure
Welcome to our stand! Our topic is Unit 3 — Eat well. · Try our quiz! The rule is: guess the food in ten seconds. · You win a point! Well done!
Form
the stand's presentation = 3 parts: welcome + topic → the game's rule → praise for the guest; the game's rule = imperative + need to; the unit's facts — in the unit's grammar!
Pron. watch
Welcome — an open «e», stress on the 1st syllable · a fairground barker's energy: the voice a half-tone above normal.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Is a stand just a pretty poster?» → No: a poster + a GAME. The guest has to DO something at the stand.
«Can the unit facts be in Russian?» → The poster and the speech are in English; Russian — only if a parent guest is completely lost.
«Is Q&A an exam?» → No: guests ask out of curiosity; if you don't know — honestly: Good question! I think…

Assumptions: Unit topics were handed out in P5, materials have been brought in. Every team has its unit review in hand: the SB Quick review of their unit + the P1 phrase bank — the stand's content sources.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
40 minutes spent colouring the titleTimeboxes: content 12′ → interactive 12′ → beauty in whatever's left. I check the axes: Content before Visuals — like the judges.
An interaction with no language (just throwing a ball)The interaction gate: in the game the guest READS, SPEAKS or LISTENS to English. Throwing a ball + a unit question = ok.
Q&A freezes at unexpected questionsWe drill the survival formula: Good question! I think… / Let me check! — a freeze turns into a phrase.
Test-guests «sink» their friends with easy questionsI hand out tricky-question cards myself: the test-guest draws a question from the deck, they don't invent one.
Materials forgotten at homeA spare kit: A3 paper, markers, printouts of the unit Quick reviews — the stand will come together regardless.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
