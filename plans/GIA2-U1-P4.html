🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The subjects are not introduced as a list but as currency: the crews build 'the perfect Monday' and defend it in a vote. The listening with Ansa is built in as an intelligence report: her comparison of the Finnish and British school gives them arguments for the defence. Yes/No questions surface naturally — as the format for interrogating a timetable (a bridge to Pair 5).
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Operation Monday. Your crew designs ONE perfect Monday — four subjects, your choice. But here's the catch: the class VOTES, and only one Monday survives. Intelligence report: Ansa from Finland spent a month in a British school — her report has arguments you'll want. Listen carefully. Then build. Then defend.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We build the perfect Monday out of 4 subjects and defend it. The class votes and only one timetable survives. Ansa's report is where the arguments come from.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can name 17 school subjects, build a 4-subject timetable and defend it in a class vote, using arguments from a listening text.
  • Subsidiary. The listening subskill: identify the type of recording (people · place · register) BEFORE the content; True/False on the conversation with Ansa; Yes/No questions introduced in the 'interrogating a timetable' context.
  • Personal. Two listenings: the first for the type only (the subskill), the second for the facts. Don't mix the order up! The weaker ones get a note-taking grid to lean on for the second listening.
By the end, students can…
  • name and spell 17 school subjects
  • identify the type of a listening (people · place · register) before details
  • catch 4+ facts from a conversation
  • defend a timetable choice with a reason

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'The maths is difficult' — can we say that? → no article: Maths is difficult.
What is PE anyway? → physical education, the sports lesson.
Is the type of recording what it is about? → no: WHO is speaking, WHERE, and how formal it is. Content comes later.

Assumptions: SB p.17, KTP pair 8. Half the subjects are familiar; new ones: citizenship, design and technology, ICT, English literature (vs language).

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
