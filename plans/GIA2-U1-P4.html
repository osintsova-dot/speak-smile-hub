Get Involved! A2 · Unit 1 “School life” · Pair 4 — Vocabulary & Listening: School subjects
A2 teens (~12–14, readers) · 90 min · Vocabulary + Listening (sub-skill: identifying the type of listening) · desks + open floor · SB pp.16–17, Pronunciation p.116
Sep–Oct · Unit 1 · Pair 8 (course-wide) / P4 (unit)✨ NEW lexis~12–14 pupils · teams of 4Line GIA2 “наши” (continuing A2)
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name and group 17 school subjects and talk about their timetable and favourite subject using the frame “My favourite subject is (…) because (…)”, and to listen for the type of a text (who / where / how formal) and for detail (T/F).
  • Subsidiary. Recycle daily-routines lexis & present simple aff./neg. from P1–P3; pronunciation — silent letters in subject words (SB p.116); recognise present-simple Yes/No questions in speech (seeds P5); speaking fluency (opinion + reason).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk lean; ICQ before every task; gist-before-detail on both listenings; run a stir→settle rhythm with one mid-lesson brain-break.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name 17 school subjects & sort them into families (science / languages / creative…);
  • ask & answer about the timetable + favourite subject with a reason;
  • identify the type of a listening and answer gist + True/False questions.
Target language
Lexis
artbiologychemistrycitizenshipdesign and technologydramaEnglish languageEnglish literatureFrenchgeographyGermanhistoryICTmathsmusicPEphysics · timetable · lesson · break · double (music)
Structure
My favourite subject is (…) because (…).  ·  Which is your favourite day? — (Tuesday), because I like (…).  ·  In speech: Do you study (French)? — Yes, we do. / Does your school (finish later)? (seeds P5 grammar).
Form
subject = uncountable, no article: I like maths (not the maths). PE /ˌpiː ˈiː/, ICT /ˌaɪ siː ˈtiː/ said as letters. Reason clause with because + clause.
Pron. watch
Silent letters (SB p.116): history /ˈhɪst(ə)ri/, design /dɪˈzaɪn/, technology, literature, ch=/k/ in chemistry, g-silent in geography's second g. Stress: biOlogy, geOgraphy, CHEmistry.
Listening skill
Sub-skill — identifying the type of listening (SB p.17): before/while you listen, think people (how many?), place (where?), language (formal or informal?). Gist first, then detail.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is chemistry a science or a language?” → science → SCIENCE family (with biology, physics).
“Do we say the ‘h’ in history?” → yes; and the ‘t’ in literature? → we swallow it — silent-ish.
“To spot the type of a listening, do I need every word?” → no → I listen for people · place · formality.

Assumptions: daily-routines lexis & present simple (aff./neg., 3rd-person spelling) are fresh from P1–P3; learners read fluently; Starter classroom lexis known. Teams: 4 max; split 3–4 (12=3×4 · 14=3+3+4+4 · 13=3+3+3+4 · 10=3+3+4).

Classroom management — signals
“3–2–1, eyes up” — count down → pens down, look up.
Volume dial — raise/lower hand = louder/quieter for pair work.
“Pause” — freeze the task mid-way to re-model or add an ICQ.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1 — last sentence.”
Numbered heads — 1–4 in each team; you call a number to report.
Wait-time — ask → count 5 silently → nominate; no hands-up race.

Read the timetable — SB p.17 ex.1. Find in the timetable: two sciences, one creative subject, one foreign language. Pairs scan; check as class. ex.2: add art · drama · German · physics to those groups. Drill new/long items chorally, back-chaining design and technology, English literature.

Hand out Unit 1 Vocabulary 2 “School subjects” visual take-away. ① Glue the subject map in (subjects grouped by family: CREATE · LANGUAGES · NUMBERS & DIGITAL · SCIENCE · PEOPLE & PLACES · PE). ② Circle every subject you actually study at your school. ③ Complete the frame at the bottom: My favourite subject is […] because […].

▶ Subject market (team race). Each team of 4 gets the 17 subject cards + six family headers on the floor. On Go! sort every card under the right family; first team all-correct wins the round. ICQ: Do you just drop cards, or say the subject as you place it? (→ say it).

Listen — which letter is silent? Play; pupils come up and cross out the silent letter. Choral drill the correct sound, then Track 60 to check.

Freer speaking · my timetable
floor, mingle

Model (SB p.17 ex.4). Which is your favourite day? — Tuesday, because I like French and we’ve got double music. Elicit the pattern: day/subject + because + reason.

▶ Find someone who (Stand Up–Hand Up–Pair Up): mingle and find classmates who — love a science · don’t like maths · have art on Monday · study two languages. Ask full questions: “Do you like…? / What’s your favourite subject?” Note names.

▶ RallyRobin report. Back in pairs, share findings with a reason — refuse one-word answers: “Say it with ‘because’.” Nominate 2–3 to report to class.

48–54′
Pre-listening · sub-skill
desks

Set up the sub-skill (SB p.17). Sometimes you don’t need every word — first work out the TYPE of listening. Three questions on the board: people (how many?) · place (where?) · language (formal or informal?).

Predict. Show the photo + speech bubble of Ansa from Hamina, Finland. Who is she? Where is she now? Will this be formal or friendly? Pairs predict (30 sec). ICQ: Do we listen for every word or for people, place and formality?

54–62′
Listening for gist
desks

Gist — SB p.17 ex.5 (Track 10). Listen once. Choose the correct description: a) an interview with Ansa on local radio · b) Ansa talking to a teacher · c) a dialogue between Ansa and a classmate. Play once, no stopping.

Hands down — A or B or C? What told you? — people, place or formality? Feed back the clue (informal, one classmate) → answer c. Praise reasoning, not luck.

62–72′
Listening for detail
desks → teams

Detail — SB p.17 ex.6. Listen again. Which sentences are true? (1–6, e.g. “Ansa starts school at half past eight.”, “Finnish students study subjects in separate lessons.”) Individual first, then compare in teams.

▶ Numbered Heads Together (Kagan): teams agree on each answer & make sure everyone can explain it; you call a number → that pupil reports for the team. ex.7: Now correct the false sentences. Teams rewrite them true; nominate to read out.

 SB p.17 · Track 10 (replay) · notebooks
72–83′
Post-listening · opinion
pairs

Discussion — SB p.17 ex.8. Would you like to go to Ansa’s school? Why / why not? Give graded opinion frames on the board:

support ↓ I’d like to go to Ansa’s school because […]. · I wouldn’t, because I prefer to […].  →  freer: The best thing about her school is… but…

▶ Timed Pair Share (Kagan): 30 sec each, then swap — partner B must respond, not just wait: “Really? I think…”. Fun fact tie-in (SB): In Finland, students start school when they’re seven. Round up 2–3 opinions to the class.
