🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): For new students the mission is simpler and about themselves: build your own look in English and learn to describe a person top to bottom. The route head → upper body → legs → feet is the scaffold that stops them drowning in 21 words: the words arrive in groups, not as a list. Present continuous is in every model («I'm wearing…»), but we do not work on the grammar today — we simply use the ready-made frame.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at these photos. Nine people, nine looks — a hoodie, boots, sunglasses, a scarf. Today two things: we learn to name ALL of it in English, and each of you describes your own look — six lines, from head to feet. At the end you read them out; you can hold your notes, this is not a test. Let's start with MY look…” (and I point at myself straight away: hat, sweater, jeans, shoes)
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Today we learn to name clothes in English and describe a look top to bottom: head → upper body → legs → feet. At the end everyone reads out their six lines, notes in hand if they want.

Warm-up · clothes from a model
desks → standing

WHAT WE DO: four groups of words with gestures: head → upper body → legs → feet; recognition first, naming after

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Group by group (picture → chorus → gesture): head and neck — hat, hoodie, scarf, sunglasses; upper body — T-shirt, top, sweater, shirt, jacket, coat; legs — jeans, trousers, shorts, skirt, leggings, tights; feet — shoes, trainers, boots, sandals.
  2. Gestures through Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols: hat — hands on head! scarf — wrap! jeans — pat your legs! trainers — run on the spot! First I do the gesture and say the word myself and the class copies me — only from the second round does the gesture work instead of the word.
  3. Recognition: I show the gesture — you shout the word. Then the other way round. Everybody shows at the same time: nobody has to answer alone out loud, and nobody can hide either.
  4. ex.1 p.36 with the word bank: Which words can you SEE in the photos? Key: hat B, C, D, G, I · shoes E · jacket G, H · skirt E, G · jeans C, H · shirt C · trainers C, D, F, G · trousers A, F · coat and dress are not visible.
  5. The Plurals box chorally: I like THESE jeans. They're great! — the poster goes on the wall until the end of the unit.
📺 On screen: SB p.36 ex.1 · the word bank on screen · the group slides (backup — the words on the board in four columns)
👦 Students do: take the words in groups with gestures. Expect: movement pulls them in; hoodie and trainers stick first. Error: a jeans — point at the Plurals poster

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 p.36 (nine descriptions ↔ photos A–I) together, with support → ex.3 p.37 by body part

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2 through Kagan RoundRobin: I read the first description out loud myself as the model, then round the crew — one description per turn, clockwise; if you are stuck you say pass. Then we hunt for the photo. Strategy prompt: Find one word you know first — then look at the photo. Key: 1 C · 2 F · 3 D · 4 A · 5 B · 6 E · 7 I · 8 G · 9 H.

  4. Which word goes in TWO groups? — hoodie (head and upper body), and dress covers upper body and legs at once.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 21 clothes and accessories and describe a look in 6 lines following the head-to-feet route.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1–5 pp.36–37 with support (the word bank stays on screen all lesson); the Plurals box as a rule-poster; Video skills (“The art of fashion”) with the task set BEFORE watching.
  • Personal. Nobody is called on without a draft. Every speaking task comes AFTER it has been written down. I give the 21 words in four groups by body part, not as one list.
By the end, students can…
  • name and spell 21 items of clothing
  • describe a look from head to feet in 6 sentences
  • use plural-only words correctly (these jeans)
  • understand short descriptions of outfits by ear

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Can we say “a jeans”? → no: jeans is always plural, we say some jeans or just jeans.
Are top and T-shirt the same thing? → top is wider: any top-half item worn as the first layer.
“I'm wearing” — is that about now or in general? → about now, this very minute.

Assumptions: SB pp.36–37, KTP pair 21. Some words are familiar (jeans, T-shirt, shoes), new ones: hoodie, leggings, tights, sandals, scarf, sweater, top. The pace is slower than with “ours” — that is normal.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
21 words at once will not fitFour groups by body part (head/upper/legs/feet), 4–6 words in each; recognition first (“point at it”), naming after.
Fear of speaking in front of strangersThe rule of the pair: write first, then read it to your partner; you only come to the board with a finished sheet.
They ignore the word bank and go silentThe word bank stays on screen all lesson; a “look up” gesture instead of feeding them the word.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
