🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The fourth pair closes the Starter and launches the year at the same time. There is NO graded test here — Macmillan has no Starter test at all, and the Teacher's Book puts a Diagnostic test at the end of the introductory block. So the diagnostic is presented honestly: the result does not go into the register, it turns into a personal gap list «what to fix before Unit 1». For our students that matters: the moment something smells of a graded test, their defences go up; the moment it is «a measurement before the season», their competitive drive switches on. The second half of the pair is the accreditation: the agent presents the dossier built over three pairs, and the class settles fully into the format of the year.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Accreditation Day. Three files are on your desk: passport, personal file, licence. Today they get stamped — or they don't. First, four stations: everything from Season Zero, fast. Then the diagnostic. Hear this clearly: no marks, no grades, nothing goes in the register. It exists for ONE reason — so you know exactly what to fix before the real missions start. Then accreditation on stage. And then Unit One opens. Stations — go.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Accreditation day: four review stations, then the entry diagnostic — no marks, nothing in the register, it exists only so that everyone gets their own list of «what to fix». After that — defending the dossier and opening Unit 1.

WHAT WE DO: 4 stations of 3.5 minutes each: countries/nationalities · classroom+family+appearance · be+possessives+have got · can

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four stations, three and a half minutes each. The protocol sheet leaves the station filled in, or the station doesn't count.
  2. Station 1 · Passports: 14 country↔nationality pairs at speed + the stress (Jap·a·NESE, Ca·NAD·ian) — with our Starter cards.
  3. Station 2 · Faces & families: the family and appearance cards, Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: question → answer → swap.
  4. Station 3 · Forms: mini-boards, three flips in a row: aff → neg → question (be / have got), Kagan Showdown.
  5. Station 4 · Licence: can — the form + a qualifier + a short answer, 5 seconds per turn.
  6. The personal gap list starts right here: Write down what YOU missed. That's your list, not everyone's.
📺 On screen: the station cards and protocol sheets · the Starter cards (75 of them) · mini-boards · timer (backup: four stations = the four corners of the room, the tasks on four A4 sheets on the desks)
👦 Students do: they go round four stations and log their misses honestly. Expected: station 3 (the forms) is the one most people fail, station 1 is the fastest. Mistake: they copy their neighbour's misses into their gap list — I remind them that the list is personal and is handed in to nobody
📓 Interactive notebook — Get_Involved_A2_Starter_Translation_Match_It_2up_A4 + Get_Involved_A2_Starter_Translation_Translate_It_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to English A–L (countries and nationalities, be, this/these, have got, the possessive apostrophe, can), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 written out in English from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the unit's exit line.

WHAT WE DO: they check themselves against the key, circle every slip and write out THREE personal gaps

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Keys out. Mark your own paper — honestly. Circle every single slip, including spelling and apostrophes. The papers stay with them, I do not collect them.
  2. Now the only number that matters: your THREE biggest gaps. Write them at the top of the page.
  3. The overall picture with no names, 4 typical misses onto the board: short answers (Yes, I am / Yes, he has) · the plural apostrophe (parents’) · has got in the 3rd person · can without to.
  4. While they do this I walk quickly down the rows and write MYSELF a map of the class: who needs support in Unit 1.
📺 On screen: the keys on the slide or printed for every desk · the feedback slide (Backup: I read the keys out and write the tricky ones on the board)
👦 Students do: they mark themselves and see their errors before any mark exists. Expected: everyone's three «gaps» are different — that is the whole point. Mistake: «I nearly had it right» — the rule to circle every slip removes that

PRODUCT · accreditation

WHAT WE DO: the accreditation: 60 seconds to present the agent — all four Starter systems in one performance

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 6 minutes to put it together in crews: One minute per agent. You must use all four systems: be (where you're from) · a possessive · have got (what you look like) · can + a qualifier. Miss a system — miss a stamp.
  2. A run-through in the crew, the checker counts the systems on their fingers.
  3. On stage: Sixty seconds. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness — and four systems. Cards up!
  4. A challenge from the floor after each one: One question for the agent — Wh-, not Yes/No!
  5. The accreditation: ACCREDITED — a stamp/signature on the form, points to the crew. A gold run (9/9 + all four systems) = +5.
📺 On screen: the students' dossier forms · 60-sec timer · judges' cards (backup: the «stamp» is the teacher's signature in marker in the corner of the form)
👦 Students do: they perform with the dossier built over three pairs. Expected: the growth since P1 is visible — there it was a passport of five fields, here it is a connected performance. Mistake: they forget the qualifier with can — the checker should have caught that before the stage

Rules of the year v2 · launching Unit 1

WHAT WE DO: we close Season Zero and open the year: how a unit is built, what the unit leaderboard is, which structures will be used

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Season Zero is closed. Everyone is accredited. Here's how the year works.
  2. A unit = 8 pairs. I count them off on my fingers: vocabulary → reading → grammar → vocabulary 2 + listening → grammar 2 → speaking → project → test. Every unit ends with a project AND a test. Both count.
  3. The unit leaderboard. The poster is reset to zero for every unit: New unit, new crews, new board. Nobody carries a bad start.
  4. The structures. I name the ones we have already used, so that from now on one word is enough: Showdown · Quiz-Quiz-Trade · Numbered Heads · Find Someone Who · Timed RoundRobin · Stand Up–Hand Up–Pair Up · Inside-Outside Circle.
  5. The hook towards Unit 1: Unit One: School life. The question is 'What is an ideal school?' and at the end YOU design one — timetable, subjects, start time. Some of you will not like what the others vote for.
📺 On screen: the leaderboard poster · the slide with the structure of the year (backup — I draw the 8-cell unit diagram straight onto the board)
👦 Students do: they see the map of the whole year. Expected: resetting the leaderboard every unit takes the anxiety away from those who started weakly. Mistake: they think the project replaces the test — I say it straight: both.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, agents can use all four Starter systems together (be · possessives · have got · can) in a 60-second self-presentation, and name their own three gaps from the diagnostic.
  • Subsidiary. A carousel review of all five Starter vocabulary sets at stations; the entry diagnostic (60 points, no mark) with immediate self-checking against the key; the launch of the year's format: the Unit 1 missions, the leaderboard, the Kagan structures, the roles.
  • Personal. I never say the word «test» — only «diagnostic» and «measurement». I never say anyone's score out loud and I never collect the scores into a common list. My job from this pair is to walk away with a map of the class: who needs support in Unit 1 and who can be loaded up straight away.
By the end, students can…
  • revise all Starter language at station pace
  • complete a 60-point diagnostic and self-check it against a key
  • name three personal gaps to fix before Unit 1
  • present a full agent dossier in 60 seconds

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Is this a test? Will it be graded?» → no. The diagnostic does not go into the register: it is there for you, so that you know what to fix before Unit 1.
«So what if I copy off my neighbour?» → you get somebody else's gap list and spend your time fixing somebody else's holes. Zero point, plenty of harm.
«Are lots of mistakes a bad thing?» → the bad thing is not finding them now. A mistake found in the fourth pair of the year is cheap; in the eighteenth it is expensive.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
