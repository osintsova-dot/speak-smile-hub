🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the six science phrasal verbs (add up, blow up, carry out, cut up, take away, work out) — all separable — listen to a real interview the exam way, and describe a photo with speculation frames (It looks like… · It could be…).
What is genuinely hard
work out and carry out look like twins and mean nothing alike; and the photo task terrifies students who «don't know the word» — which is exactly what the speculation frames are for.
Typical error to expect
«add up them» — separable phrasal verbs put the pronoun INSIDE (add them up); the P3 fist-beat comes straight back. And silence at an unknown object in the photo — the frame I don't know what it's called IS the correct answer.
Why the lesson is built this way
Three chained skills with one hero: the phrasal verbs are learned with the TB connected-words method and act-out game, then the Taylor Wilson interview runs the exam listening discipline — choose, then listen again to prove the other two options WRONG. The speaking closes the block's productive arc: a recorded model SURVIVES not knowing words by speculating — the class harvests its frames and then runs B1 Speaking Part 2 in pairs on the second photo. Describing beats knowing; that is the lesson.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“At fourteen, Taylor Wilson built a nuclear fusion reactor in the garage. His neighbours moved out. Today: the verbs scientists actually use, the interview about him — and the examiner's favourite skill: talking beautifully about things you can't name.”

Vocabulary · six lab verbs
pairs → class
Why: The science phrasal verbs arrive through matched halves with a method (find the connected words), then the separability rule — add them up, never add up them — before the acting game fixes them.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 matched halves with the TB method + ex.2 + separability

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB method modelled on item 1: add is about numbers — hunt the ending with numbers in it. Pairs match all six. Key: ex.1: 1 f · 2 b · 3 e · 4 d · 5 a · 6 c · ex.2: 1 work out · 2 add up · 3 cut up · 4 carried out · 5 blow up · 6 take away — all six are separable: add them up, never add up them.
  2. Definitions read aloud, class calls the verb; gestures attached (work out = temple, carry out = pour, blow up = boom, cut up = scissors, take away = minus, add up = plus).
  3. Separability drilled with the P3 fist-beat: add — them — UP. work — it — OUT. Choral, twice.
  4. ex.2 solo then pairs, Kagan RallyCoach: six completions checked.
📺 On screen: SB p.92 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: match by connected words, gesture, drill the beat, complete. Expect blow up to trigger joy — channel it into the potassium sentence

WHAT WE DO: the book's own game: write a sentence, act it, class guesses

Why: Exam-shape listening run the honest way: choose from memory, then listen again to prove the other two options WRONG — the discipline that separates listening from guessing.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2 (track 78): predict, choose, then PROVE the wrong options wrong

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 hook: the two photos (yellow suit, plasma ball, radiation sign) — What did he invent? Commit to a guess. Play once. Answer: a nuclear fusion reactor.
  2. ex.2 read-first: six questions scanned, key words underlined. First listening — choose. Pairs compare.
  3. Second listening with the one job: prove the other two options WRONG. Feedback requires it: 1 is B — because A is wrong (SHE didn't make the show first) and C is wrong (they never met online). Key: ex.1: he invented a nuclear fusion reactor · ex.2: 1 B · 2 B · 3 C · 4 B · 5 A · 6 A.
  4. One breath of wonder allowed on «a star in a jar», then move.
📺 On screen: track 78 in the board app (Backup: the audioscript is on TB p.275–276 — read the interview in two voices; both passes work identically by ear)

Why: The productive outcome, B1 Speaking Part 2 shape: one minute of connected speech about a science photo, speculation phrases compulsory, partner tracking which frames were used.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 (exam icon): B1 Speaking Part 2 on the dry-ice photo — a minute each, frames tracked

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pairs, new partners (TB's instruction). Speaker A gets one minute with photo two (the science demo): describe non-stop, frames compulsory. Partner B tracks: one tick per frame heard.
  2. Swap roles; same photo is fine — the second speaker must NOT repeat the first's guesses (harder, better).
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share discipline: the visible timer runs for the speaker; one restart prompt allowed (And what's happening behind…?).
  4. Two volunteers perform 30 seconds each for the class; class counts frames aloud afterwards.
  5. You log speculation-free sentences silently — P7's briefing material.
📺 On screen: the visible timer and the frame columns on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: speak a full minute, track a partner honestly, perform. Expect the second speaker problem (nothing left to say) to produce the best speculation of the day — that is the design

Structure
Carry out the experiment, then add the numbers up. · I can't work out the answer. · It looks like a kind of science demonstration. · I don't know what it's called, but it could be dangerous.
Form
all six phrasal verbs separable — pronoun inside (add them up); speculation frames + seem + adjective; could/might for visual deduction.
Pron. watch
phrasal stress on the particle (work it OUT); looks like runs together /lʊks laɪk/; the speculating tone RISES — certainty falls, guesses float
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«work out» or «carry out» — which one is thinking? → work out = find the answer in your head; carry out = do the experiment with your hands.
«add up them» — legal? → no: add THEM up. All six verbs today take the pronoun inside.
I see a strange machine in the photo and don't know its name — what do I say? → It looks like some sort of… — and that scores, silence doesn't.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
work out / carry out swapThe head-vs-hands gesture pair: work out taps the temple, carry out mimes pouring. Attach at first presentation, recall by gesture every time.
Listening answers chosen by keyword echoThe second listen has ONE job, said aloud: prove the other two options wrong. An answer without a dead option beside it doesn't count at feedback.
Photo descriptions die at the first unknown objectThe frames are on the board and the rule is explicit: not knowing is an OPPORTUNITY to use the best frames. Track the frames publicly — partner ticks a card per frame heard.
One minute of speech collapses into ten secondsThe visible timer runs FOR the speaker; the partner's follow-up prompt (And what's happening behind…?) restarts a stalled speaker once per turn.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
