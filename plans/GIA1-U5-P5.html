WHAT WE DO: the Spelling subskill as a system: 3 traps + a race to the board “find the word in the unit” + ex.7 corrections

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WB Unit 5 (pp.36–43) · notebook · points to the leaderboard next pair
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write and pitch an advert (35+ words) for a second-hand object using can/can't, adjectives and 2+ comparatives — with zero spelling mistakes in unit vocabulary.
  • Subsidiary. The Spelling subskill is worked as a competition (silent letters · double letters · sound↔spelling /iː/); Quick review p.69 is closed with a team quiz; peer review — 2 checks; test readiness is diagnosed.
  • Personal. Their writing is slow — we write in portions, purposefully: each portion is used in the pitch straight away. I don't present spelling as tedious, but as “one mistake = no sale”.
By the end, students can…
  • spot and fix the three types of spelling trap
  • write an advert with a heading, benefits, comparatives and a price
  • peer-review a classmate's advert with two concrete checks
  • pass a Quick review sprint across the whole unit

Form
the structure of an advert: heading → who it's good for → what you can do with it → comparatives → colour/age/condition → price + hook. Spelling: silent letters: cam(e)ra · Wed(nes)day · Feb(ru)ary · double letters: thinner · bigger · address · sound ↔ spelling /iː/: cheap · screen · piece · people · police.
Pron. watch
/iː/ is spelt in different ways: cheap · screen · piece · people · police (SB p.117) · bargain /ˈbɑːɡɪn/ · advert /ˈædvɜːt/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“camera — how many syllables, and why?” → two: CAM-ra, the e isn't pronounced but is written.
“thiner or thinner?” → thinner: doubling the consonant — the same rule as with the comparative.
“An advert with no price — will it work?” → no: the buyer looks for the price and the condition.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: they can write, but carry over the Russian logic of “as I hear it”. The advert as a genre is familiar to them from marketplaces — we use that. All the unit's language has already been taught: today is application, not input.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
