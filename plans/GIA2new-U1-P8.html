Why this lesson looks like this — the new-students frame
For many of these students this is the first “real” English test of their life. On the 🌱 line the design job is not to make it easy — it is to make it fair and un-scary, so that nerves don't hide what they can actually do. Two evidence-based tools run through the whole lesson: assessment validity (we model every task type first with the teacher's own examples, so we test English — not puzzle-reading; objective parts are marked to the key, writing & speaking to the same band descriptors every time) and self-efficacy (a mastery start on easy Vocabulary, credible “look how far you've come since Pair 1”, and a private, no-comparison close). Audio plays twice. Marks are never read out. Personal results come 1:1.
Say to the class: Everything on this paper is language we've built together for six weeks — your day, your subjects, the Present simple. There are no trick questions. Today you just show what you can already do.
По-русски для учителя: тёплый заход снимает тревогу; сравниваем ученика только с ним самим на P1, а не друг с другом.

WHAT WE DO: walk through every section with the teacher's OWN examples so the format is clear before the start — this protects fairness (we test English, not puzzle-reading)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step (own examples only):
  1. Vocabulary. Word-in-the-box: I ______ my bag before school. → pack. · Choose the word: We do / go shopping at the weekend → go. · Text gap: I return books at the ______ → library.
  2. Grammar. Present simple: She ______ (get up) at seven → gets up. · Time prepositions: at night · in the morning · on Monday. · Correct the mistake / adverb place: He is always late → not “He always is late.” · Questions: ______ you ______ (like) music? → Do … like. · Wh-word: ______ do you go to school? — By bus. → How.
  3. Use of English / Reading: Read the whole text first, then choose. Underline key words. · Listening: You'll hear it twice — listen first, write on the second play. · Writing: greeting → who you are → school & routine → free time → sign-off; use contractions; count your words (30–45).
  4. The strategy (theirs to keep): Easy first · mark the hard one · come back.
  5. Quick ICQs: How many times will you hear the audio? (twice) · How many words in the email? (30–45) · Where do you write — box or lines? (lines) Then: Turn your paper over — you may begin. Take your time.
📺 On the board: the strategy slide (Backup: write the three strategy points on the board before the lesson) · answer key stays with the teacher, never shown

WHAT WE DO: independent silent work on ex. 1–9; Speaking assessed 1:1 in parallel

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the room: Twenty-eight minutes. Eyes on your own paper. If you have a question, raise a hand — I'll come to you. Learners complete Vocabulary ex. 1–3 (/20), Grammar ex. 4–8 (/25), Use of English ex. 9 (/8). Easy Vocabulary first = a confident, mastery start.
  2. Speaking assessed 1:1 in parallel. Call learners one pair at a time to the side table, in the order the register was called, for the publisher Speaking task (ex. 16) — the five questions (name/age/origin · a typical school day · subjects you like/dislike · free time · your family). S&S oral-pair format: the two partners ask & answer the questions to each other while you score with the Macmillan band descriptors (/10). Then a fast EN↔RU vocabulary check: say 3 Unit-1 words in Russian → pupil gives the English (and 1 the other way). ~2–3 min per pair; smile, no visible marking; keep the voice low.
  3. Monitor discreetly, timer visible. Time call at 40′: Four minutes for this part — then we listen together.
📺 On the board: visible timer · current section name (Backup: no screen needed — this is paper work)
👦 Students do: work at their own pace; each pair comes calmly to the side table when called
💪 support: if a student freezes, quietly point to the strategy — “easy first, mark the hard one” · ⭐ stretch: a strong pair may take the Higher paper if they asked in advance

WHAT WE DO: Listening /12 — read the questions, then the audio twice with a pause between

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set up: Ex. 10 and 11 — a radio show. First, read the questions. Then listen once with pens down. You'll hear it twice. Give ~40 s to read ex. 10 (match people 1–6 to a–f) and ex. 11 (six True/False).
  2. Play 1 — pens down, just listen. Pause ~30 s: Now answer what you can. Play 2 — learners complete and check.
  3. No discussion of answers: Pens up — that's the listening. You did the hard part — you stayed calm and listened.
🎮 Audio: A2_U1_Test (Standard) audio — the radio show, played twice (Backup: read the audioscript aloud twice at the same steady pace)
👦 Students do: everyone silent on play 1; write on play 2 — twice-through removes memory pressure, so it measures listening, not nerves

WHAT WE DO: collect calmly → private self-check of the objective parts vs the key → 3 typical slips anonymously → the unit celebration

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect. Writing pages to me — those I mark myself. Keep the objective pages for a quick self-check.
  2. Private self-check (no comparison). Show the key for the objective sections on screen: Keys up. Circle your own slips — they're private, just for you. Name the three typical ones so nobody feels singled out: The usual three: the -s on he/she, “doesn't” + the bare verb, and capital letters. Everyone's circles look alike — you're not alone.
  3. Unit ceremony (self-efficacy, evidence-tied). Pair 1: your first six lines of English. Today: a whole test in English. THAT is the distance you've come. A nomination for everyone — bravest speaker · cleanest capitals · best question · word master… Teaser: Unit 2 — “Me time”: hobbies, music, football… and new missions.
  4. Results 1:1. Your own marked paper and your personal “what I've got / what to review” note come to you one-to-one next time — never read out to the class.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. To fairly and calmly assess what each new-line learner can now do with all Unit 1 language — daily-routine & school-subject lexis, Present simple (aff/neg, Yes/No & Wh- questions), object pronouns, time prepositions — across Vocabulary, Grammar, Use of English, Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking (Standard paper, /100).
  • Subsidiary. Build test-literacy through a model-first walkthrough (every task type is clear before the start); protect construct validity (we measure English, not puzzle-solving); support wellbeing so nerves don't mask real ability; celebrate the first unit and the P1→P8 growth.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk minimal once the test starts; project a visible timer; give a clear finished-early protocol; assess Speaking 1:1 in parallel without disturbing the room; never read marks aloud.
By the end, students will have…
  • completed the Unit 1 Standard test — Vocab · Grammar · Use of English · Listening · Reading · Writing (/90 on paper);
  • done the Speaking task 1:1 with the teacher (S&S oral pairs + EN↔RU check) — /10;
  • self-checked the objective parts privately and named one strength + one thing to review — no comparison, results delivered 1:1.

Test-room management — signals
Visible timer — on the board; update the section name + minutes left. The timer is the boss, not you.
Silent hand-up — a question? raise a hand, teacher comes over. No calling out.
“Eyes on your own paper” — quiet reminder, no discussion of answers.
Time calls — “5 minutes left… 1 minute… pens down, please.”
Listening protocol — “Pens down, listen.” Play 1 = listen; play 2 = answer.
Finished early? — turn the paper over, re-read, check the word count, then sit quietly. No phones.
