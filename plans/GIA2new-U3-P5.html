🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Choosing between the two tenses is the hardest spot in the unit, so the pair is built as a staircase with one clear scaffold: TWO CLOCKS. The “usually” clock (present simple) and the “right now” clock (present continuous). The time expression is the arrow that shows which clock to switch on. I give the stative verbs as a separate short step at the end, so that they do not interfere with the main rule.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two clocks on the wall. The first one shows what you do EVERY DAY: I go to school, I wear a uniform. The second shows what is happening RIGHT NOW: I'm sitting here, I'm wearing jeans. Both are true — but they use different grammar. Today you learn to see which clock a sentence needs. And the words 'every day' or 'right now' will tell you every single time.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Two clocks: one shows what happens usually (present simple), the other shows what is happening right now (present continuous). The time expressions tell you which clock to switch on.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 the contrast table → we formulate the rules ourselves → ex.2 choosing the form with a self-check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: we read the table out loud in pairs of sentences. My friend goes to a different school. / People are arriving. Which one is a habit? Which one is happening NOW?
  2. They complete the rules themselves. Key: 1 present continuous (actions in progress now) · 2 present simple (routines and habits). We write it in the notebook in both languages.
  3. ex.2 in writing, then the key on the slide and a self-check: 1 'm wearing · 2 don't go · 3 isn't raining · 4 Do you wear.
  4. We go through EVERY answer out loud on the algorithm: Time word? 'At the moment'. Which clock? Right now. Which form? I'm wearing.
📺 On screen: SB p.42 ex.1–2 · the algorithm poster · the key on the slide (Backup: the same written on the board before the lesson)
👦 Students do: they derive the rule and apply it step by step straight away. Expect: the algorithm has to be said out loud 3–4 times, then they start doing it themselves

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: the time-expression cards sorted under the two clocks by hand → check → flips

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The cards: every day · every Monday · sometimes · usually · on Saturdays · at weekends / now · right now · at the moment · today · this week.
  2. Sort them under the two clocks. Two minutes.
  3. Key for ex.3: present simple — every day, every Monday, sometimes, usually · present continuous — now, right now, this week, today.
  4. Careful: 'today' can go under BOTH. Why? — I explain it simply: today as a whole day (the usual business) and today as a moment.
  5. Flips through Kagan Showdown: I do the first flip on the board myself, saying the algorithm out loud; then I give a sentence with one time expression — everyone flips it into the other clock on their own mini-whiteboard, and on the call Showdown! all the boards go up at the same time.

👦 Students do: they sort with their hands before writing. Expect: the sorting goes easily, the flips work from the second go

WHAT WE DO: the stative box: five verbs we never put into -ing + five trap sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The p.42 box chorally and onto a poster: be, have got, like, love, want — these verbs don't move.
  2. I explain it simply: These aren't actions. You can't SEE someone 'wanting'.
  3. Five traps with a self-check: I'm wanting new trainers → I want · She's having a bike → She's got · He is knowing → He knows · I'm liking this → I like · They are being tired → They are tired.
  4. The pairs check against the key and underline their own slip.
📺 On screen: the stative poster · the trap sheet · the key on the slide (Backup: the same written on the board before the lesson)
👦 Students do: they repair sentences with support. Expect: they take the rule easily as long as it is not mixed with the main one — which is why it is a separate step

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can choose between present simple and present continuous using the time expression as a clue, and write 6 sentences — three habits, three about right now — checked against a key.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the contrast table and the rules; ex.2 choosing the form; ex.3 sorting the time expressions; the stative verbs box as a separate step; ex.4 mini-dialogues with a self-check; the mini-test.
  • Personal. No “feel the language”: an algorithm only — find the time expression → choose the clock → put in the form. Every written run comes with a key.
By the end, students can…
  • find the time expression and choose the tense
  • write habits in present simple and now-actions in present continuous
  • name verbs that never take -ing (be, have got, like, love, want)
  • correct a wrong-tense sentence

Structure
We usually wear a school uniform. / We are wearing normal clothes today. · He doesn't play football on Wednesdays. / He isn't playing football today. · Do you have lunch at school? — Yes, I do. / Are you having lunch? — Yes, I am. · What does he always do? / What is he doing now?
Form
present continuous — an action in progress RIGHT NOW · present simple — routine and habit · never in the continuous: be, have got, like, love, want (and know, need)
Pron. watch
the fast “do you” /djʊ/ in present simple questions against the clear “are you” — an audible clue
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I'm wanting new trainers” — why not? → want does not go in the continuous: I want new trainers.
“What are you doing every Saturday?” — what is wrong? → every Saturday = routine, you need the present simple: What do you do every Saturday?
You hear at the moment — which tense do you take? → present continuous.
“She is having a bike.” → have GOT for possession never goes in the continuous: She's got a bike.

Assumptions: SB p.42, KTP pair 25. Present simple was in Unit 1, present continuous in P3 of this unit. What is new is the CHOICE between them; hence the scaffold of time expressions.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They put continuous everywhere (the unit is about it, after all)The algorithm on the poster: 1) find the time word 2) choose the clock 3) put in the form. No time word — ask “now or in general?”.
Stative verbs break the ruleA separate short step AFTER the main one, with its own poster “these five never move”.
They get tired of choosing the formRuns of 6–8 minutes, with hands-on sorting and movement in between.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
