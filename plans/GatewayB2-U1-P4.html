🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Express preferences precisely with prefer, would prefer and would rather (including I'd rather you didn't), always with a reason attached; and write an informal email of 120–150 words that answers every question asked and keeps an informal register from the first line to the last.
What is genuinely hard
Three different structures with almost the same meaning and completely different grammar: prefer + -ing or to, would prefer + to, would rather + bare infinitive, and then would rather + past simple when the subject changes. Russian expresses all of this with one word. The writing difficulty is different and more expensive: students answer the interesting question and quietly skip the other one.
Typical error to expect
«I'd rather to stay», «I prefer read» — would rather takes a bare infinitive (I'd rather stay), prefer takes -ing or to + infinitive (I prefer reading / I'd prefer to read). Both appear in the speaking bank and both are tested.
«Dear Sir or Madam» in a letter to a friend, «I am writing to inform you» — an informal task answered in a formal register loses the style mark even when every sentence is grammatically perfect.
Why the lesson is built this way
The speaking half is built on five real students answering the same kind of question, and the second listening asks only one thing: did they give a reason? That single question is the whole lesson — the difference between a two-mark answer and a four-mark one, in any exam and in any conversation. The writing half then reuses the same content: Freya cannot choose between history and biology, and the students have spent forty minutes saying what they would rather do and why. The model email is analysed for structure and for register, planned in three paragraphs and started in class, so the teacher sees the first two paragraphs before they go home.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two questions. Would you rather study at home or in a library? Would you rather have exams or continuous assessment? Whoever answers with just «library» loses. The answer is the easy part — today we practise the part that comes after it.”

WHAT WE DO: SB p.14 ex.2–3 (track 04): which question, and did they give a reason?

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. Before the second play, set the only question that matters: Does this person give a reason, a personal detail or an example? Yes or no. Nothing else.

  5. Land the point: The choice is worth one mark. The because is worth the rest.

26–33′
The three structures, on the board and in the mouth
desks → board
Why: The structures are boarded as a vertical minimal set so the difference is visible at a glance; the choral drill puts the contracted /d/ into the muscle.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.14 ex.5–6: the pattern behind each expression, then seven gapped sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.5 with the whole class for prefer, then in pairs for would prefer and would rather — write the STRUCTURE, not a translation: subject + prefer + -ing / to-infinitive, and so on.
  2. Board the three lines and drill them chorally: I prefer studying alone. I'd prefer to study alone. I'd rather study alone. Then the pronunciation pair: I rather / I'd rather, three times.
  3. Add the one that always surprises them: My parents would rather I didn't study abroad. — Past form, present meaning, and it appears when the second person is different.
  4. Ex.6 alone, four minutes, then partners check. Key: 1 'd prefer not to go · 2 'd rather not speak · 3 'd rather have · 4 'd prefer to learn … than to study · 5 'd prefer not to leave · 6 prefer doing … to doing · 7 'd rather go.
📺 On screen: the three model sentences on the board, one under the other. Backup: none needed.
👦 Students do: write three structures, repeat three model sentences chorally, then complete seven items alone

🎯 Practice makes perfect · the six exam questions
open floor → desks
Why: Two rounds with different partners is the cheapest way to double speaking time and to make the second attempt noticeably better than the first.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.14 ex.7a–7b: ask, answer, then change partners and do it better

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Student A has three questions, Student B has three. Ask, listen, and when your partner stops after the choice, say «Why?» — that is your job.
  2. Timed Pair Share: one minute each way. Then ex.7b — change partners, swap roles, do it again. The second telling is always fuller.
  3. Monitor for «I rather» and for one-part answers, and note two good sentences you hear, with names.
  4. Thirty seconds of public praise using the two named sentences — then the correction of «I rather», unnamed.
📺 On screen: the CHOICE + BECAUSE card still on the board. Backup: not applicable.
👦 Students do: ask and answer six questions with two different partners under a timer

46–56′
The model email · what does she actually need?
desks
Why: Counting the questions before writing is the single intervention that prevents the commonest and most expensive writing error.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.15 ex.1–2: Freya cannot choose between history and biology

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 in pairs, two minutes: two lists — good reasons for studying history, good reasons for biology. Both partners write both lists; they are needed again in twenty minutes.
  2. Ex.2: read Freya's email. What help does she need, and why? — she cannot decide between history and biology and has to choose next year.
  3. Now the compulsory step: Underline every question mark in her email. In pencil. How many? Count them aloud together.
  4. Ex.3 in mixed-ability pairs: what in the email is typical of informal writing? Board what they find — contractions, exclamation marks, direct questions, an emoji, «Anyway», «By the way».
📺 On screen: Freya's email projected if you have the board app, so you can underline the questions on the screen. Backup: the email is printed on p.15 — underline in the book and hold your copy up.
👦 Students do: build two lists, read the model, count the questions, list the informal features

Why: Functional phrases are only retrievable if they are practised by function, not read as a list.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.15 ex.4: five categories of useful expressions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4: give the five sections of the Writing bank their titles. Key: 1 Greetings · 2 Introduction · 3 Asking for news · 4 Changing topic · 5 Ending the email.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.15 ex.5a–5b: decide the advice, then map three paragraphs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.5a in pairs: what advice will you give Freya, and for what reasons? Use the two lists from ex.1. Two minutes, notes only.
  2. Ex.5b: write the paragraph plan. Paragraph 1 — thank her for the email and tell her what you have been doing. Paragraph 2 — your advice and the reasons. Paragraph 3 — wish her luck and ask her to write back.
  3. RallyCoach: swap plans. The partner checks ONE thing — is every question from Freya's email answered somewhere in the plan?
  4. Take two plans from the floor and improve them publicly, in thirty seconds each.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.15 ex.6a: the writing starts in class, where you can see it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Eight minutes of silent writing. Paragraphs 1 and 2 only. Silent mode: no questions during the timer. Write.
  2. Then three minutes of peer check against exactly three points from the SB p.141 checklist: is there an introduction? are all her questions answered? is every expression informal?
  3. Walk the room while they write and mark ONE thing on each paper with a pencil dot — the place where the register slips. No commentary.
  4. Announce the finish: Paragraph three and the final version are your homework — Workbook page eleven, exercise seven, 120 to 150 words.
📺 On screen: a visible timer for the eight minutes of silent writing. Backup: a phone stopwatch on the desk.
👦 Students do: write two paragraphs in silence, then check a partner's text against three checklist points

Structure

Form
prefer + -ing / + to + infinitive · prefer doing X to doing Y · would prefer + to + infinitive (+ than to) · would prefer + object + to + infinitive · would rather + bare infinitive (+ than + bare infinitive) · would rather + subject + past simple.
Pron. watch
the contraction is the whole difficulty: I'd rather /aɪd ˈrɑːðə/ — the /d/ must be audible or it becomes «I rather», which is the commonest error in this unit's workbook · not «I would rather» in speech
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I'd rather to stay» — correct? → no. Would rather takes the verb with no to: I'd rather stay.
«I'd rather you didn't do that.» Am I talking about the past? → no, about now. The past form is a grammar rule, not a time.
«I prefer reading to watching TV.» What comes after to here? → -ing, because this to is a preposition, not an infinitive.
ICQ before the writing: how many questions does Freya ask? → count them. How many must your email answer? → all of them.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Answers of one word in the speaking stageMake the rule visible and mechanical: every answer must have TWO parts — the choice and the because. Hold up two fingers when a student stops after part one; do not say anything.
«I'd rather» loses its /d/Drill it as a minimal pair, chorally: I rather / I'd rather, three times each. Then only accept the version with the /d/.
Half the email prompt is ignoredBefore writing, students underline every question in Freya's email — in pencil, in the book — and count them aloud. Then the plan must show where each one is answered.
A formal register creeps inBoard the two versions side by side: «I am writing to inform you» / «I'm writing to tell you about». Ask which one Freya would write. Then it is their own decision, not your correction.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The speaking half teaches the habit that separates a two-mark answer from a four-mark one: never stop after the answer, add the reason.
