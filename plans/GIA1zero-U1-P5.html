WHAT WE DO: “one true thing about you”; a calm Unit 1 rewind — be + countries/nationalities in chorus with pair help; bridge to keypals

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door, warm and slow: One true thing about you — three seconds each. No English pressure yet, a smile.
  2. Rewind Unit 1, all in chorus, FULL sentences (this is the language of today’s writing): I’m from Russia. I’m Russian. Where are you from? Are you from Spain? — No, I’m not.
  3. Quick pair help: Turn to your partner — country and nationality. Help each other, no rush. (Russia–Russian, Spain–Spanish, Japan–Japanese…)
  4. Bridge: Today you meet a girl from Mexico and a boy from Japan. Then YOU write a message and make a keypal — a friend abroad.
📺 On screen / board: a small world map · the 4 be forms as a reminder strip
👦 Students do: they say one true thing, warm up be + nationalities with a partner. Expected: relaxed, some in Russian first — recast into English, no marking. Typical wobble: “I from Russia” → gentle echo “I’m from Russia”.

Hook · Adriana & Kenji

WHAT WE DO: what a keypal is; SB p.20 ex.1 — read Adriana (Mexico) and Kenji (Japan), choose the one you prefer and say why

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it up: A keypal is an online penfriend in another country. You write about yourself — they write back. Real teenagers do this to practise English.
  2. Ex.1 (SB p.20): Read the two messages. Which one do you prefer? WHY? Read Adriana’s and Kenji’s aloud once at listening speed so weaker readers hear it.
  3. Pairs answer with a frame: I prefer …’s message because … (music · sport · funny · friendly). Take 3–4 out loud — accept all reasons warmly.
  4. Plant the goal: By the end, YOUR message will be this good — and someone will want to reply.
📺 On screen: SB p.20 — Addie7878 (Adriana, Mexico) & Ken_TYO (Kenji, Japan) (Backup: read both messages aloud; key facts on the board.)
👦 Students do: they read both messages, choose one, give a reason in a frame. Expected: “I prefer Adriana’s — she likes music.” Typical wobble: reason in Russian — recast into the English frame.

Model → the 6-move skeleton (profiles)
Model · genre = a keypal message
desks → board

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 copy & complete the two profiles → this becomes the 6-move skeleton on the board: how a keypal message is built

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.2 (SB p.20): Read again. Copy and complete profile A (Adriana) and profile B (Kenji): Name · Age · Country · Nationality · City · Languages · Hobbies. In pairs, calm, 4 min.
  2. Reveal the genre: a keypal message always does the same 6 moves. I build it on the board and it stays up all lesson:
    ① Hello → ② name + age + country/nationality → ③ your city + 2 adjectives → ④ languages & hobbies → ⑤ WHY you want a keypal → ⑥ invite: “Write to me!”
  3. Point to each move IN Adriana’s message: Here’s move one — “Hi!”. Here’s move five — “I want to meet people from other countries.” Here’s move six — “Send me a message!”
  4. Say WHY each move matters: Move five is the point of the whole message — it tells the reader you actually want a friend. Never skip it.
📺 On screen: SB p.20 profiles · board: the 6-move skeleton (stays up)
👦 Students do: they fill both profiles, then read the 6 moves back in chorus. Expected: profiles complete; they can point to move ⑤ in the model. Typical wobble: mixing moves ④/⑤ — the numbers on the board hold it.

WHAT WE DO: the 7 capital-letter rules taught slowly; ex.3 find examples in the messages; ex.4 rewrite 6 sentences with capitals — one at a time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the rule box (SB p.20) together, slowly. Capitals for: I · countries · nationalities · languages · cities · days of the week · the first word · names.
  2. Ex.3 (SB p.20): Find TWO examples for each rule in the two messages. Do it together on the board, ticking each rule (Mexico · Spanish · Rosalia · Monday · I · Japanese · Tokyo…).
  3. Ex.4 (SB p.21): rewrite with capitals, one sentence at a time on mini-boards — no rush: i’m from india · i speak portuguese and french · istanbul is a big, beautiful city in turkey · i’m half dutch and half german · my favourite day of the week is saturday · i live in moscow, the capital city of russia.
  4. After each: Boards up! — then chorus the fixes together: Capital I, capital India — country! Celebrate, don’t score.
📺 On screen: SB p.20 rule box · SB p.21 ex.4 · mini-boards (Backup: the 7 rules and the 6 sentences copied onto the board before the lesson.)
👦 Students do: everyone writes each fixed sentence at the same time, holds the board up together — nobody hides, nobody is behind. Expected: 5–6 of 6 clean by the end. Typical wobble: forgetting the capital I mid-sentence — freeze frame: “I is ALWAYS big.”

desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 find the 3 keypal expressions; then plan YOUR message in key words on the notebook template

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.5 (SB p.21): Find expressions in the messages to: start a message · say why you want a keypal · ask people to write to you. (e.g. Hi! · I want to meet people from other countries · Send me a message! / Write to me!) — these are your ready phrases for moves ①/⑤/⑥.
  2. THINK (ex.6): You can be yourself, or invent a new identity — your choice. Slow and safe.
  3. PREPARE — notes in WORDS, not sentences: name · age · country + nationality · city + 2 adjectives · languages · hobbies · one reason (why a keypal). Fill the notebook template.
  4. ICQ: Full sentences in the plan? — No! Key words only. No timer here — this is thinking time.
📺 On screen: board: the 3 ready phrases · the 6-move skeleton stays up · note template
👦 Students do: they gather their own facts in key words. Expected: “Norilsk / cold, friendly / Russian, English / football, drawing / meet people abroad.” Typical wobble: writing full sentences already — “Notes first, message after the break.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.6 write the keypal message in two calm portions; frames from the model on the board; capital-letter self-watch

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model → frame → own words. I write move ① on the board thinking aloud: “Hi! My name’s [ ]. I’m [ ] years old and I’m from [ ]. I’m [nationality].” Then: The frame gives the words — YOU put in your facts.
  2. WRITE portion 1 (moves ①–③, ~6 min, gentle): hello → name/age/country/nationality → city + 2 adjectives. Frames stay on the board.
  3. WRITE portion 2 (moves ④–⑥, ~7 min): languages & hobbies → WHY you want a keypal → invite (“Write to me!”). No timer race — “finish your thought, then stop.”
  4. Capital-letter self-watch: Before you finish a line — check: country, nationality, city, day, name, I — all BIG?
📺 On screen: board: frames per move (“My name’s… · I’m from… · My hobbies are… · I want to…”) · the 6-move skeleton stays up
👦 Students do: they write step by step in two passes, from the frame into their own facts. Expected: 5–6 sentences. Typical wobble: blank-page freeze — a finger on frame ① “Hi! My name’s…” — take it and go.
💪 Support: a printed skeleton with the first words of every move + a nationality/adjective word bank (fill the gaps only). ⭐ Stretch: cover the board frames and write from the plan alone; add one question back to the reader (“Where are you from?”).		notebooks · board frames · support skeletons · word bank

WHAT WE DO: ex.7 swap messages; three friendly checkboxes; the author fixes; choose a keypal and say why

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame it kindly first: Peer review is help, not marks. We find good things AND three checks.
  2. Ex.7 — three ticks only: ① Is “be” correct? ② Are the CAPITAL letters correct? ③ Did they say WHY they want a keypal? Reviewer underlines gently; the AUTHOR fixes it themselves.
  3. Then the nice bit: Choose one classmate to be your keypal. Say why. (“I choose Dima — we both like football.”)
  4. Sign off: Full writer’s cycle — plan, write, check. Well done.

WHAT WE DO: messages on the wall; everyone walks, reads, puts a ♥ on one they’d reply to; two authors read aloud — supportive, not a contest

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Post the messages on the wall/desks. Everyone gets one paper ♥: Walk, read three messages, put your heart on ONE you would reply to. Not your own!

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write a 5–6 sentence keypal message by steps — following the 6-move skeleton, using be + countries/nationalities, and applying capital letters correctly — then peer-check it by 3 boxes.
  • Subsidiary. Adriana/Kenji models → 6-move genre skeleton; subskill: capital letters (7 rules, taught slowly); writing cycle THINK→PREPARE→WRITE→CHECK in gentle portions; scaffolding faded model → frame → own words; Quick review = a calm map of the test.
  • Personal. FOUNDATION group — this is NOT a sprint. Protect thinking time (no timer on THINK/PREPARE), praise over scoring, mutual help everywhere, easy ✔ wins (🐣). Every child writes on a mini-board at the same time — nobody sits and watches.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone writes, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody is behind.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; “library whisper” while writing.
Thinking time = no timer — on THINK/PREPARE the clock is OFF; beginners need calm to plan.
Help, not marks — peer review finds good things + 3 checks; the author fixes their own work.
English-first, kindly — ideas in Russian welcome; recast into English, praise the attempt.
