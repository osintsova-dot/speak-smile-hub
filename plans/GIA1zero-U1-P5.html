WHAT WE DO: an emoji mood on mini-boards; a lightning rewind of P4: a short-answer quickfire + finishing off Key phrases

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Entry: Draw your mood as ONE emoji on your board — boards up!
  2. Rewind of P4 — quickfire: Are you 11? Is our town big? Are we in England? — in chorus with FULL short answers; the etiquette rule card is back on the board.
  3. Finish it off in chorus: Nice to meet — …? Where are you — …? How old — …? (you! from! are you!)
  4. Bridge: You can MEET a stranger. Now let's WRITE to one — a message that could find you a friend in Tokyo or Mexico.

19–28′
A hunt for capital letters (7 rules)
Discover · capital letters hunt
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: teams hunt in the messages for 2 examples of each of the 7 capitalisation rules; then “fix the crime” on mini-boards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I announce: English capitalises SEVEN kinds of words. Hunt in the messages: two examples per rule. Four minutes!
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everyone writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they all lift them together — I see every answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. We check, I build the 7-rule poster on the board (start · I · countries/nationalities · cities · languages · days · names).
  4. The Fix the crime game: I display in lower case — i'm half dutch and half german.
  5. I command: Rewrite with capitals — fifteen seconds — boards UP! 3 rounds, +1 for clean.
📺 On screen: the 7-rule poster (built live, stays) · slides of lines with no capitals (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: hunt for examples in the messages, then fix sentences on mini-boards and lift them. Expected: I'm half Dutch and half German. Typical error: fixing dutch, forgetting i'm — we count the “crimes” on our fingers: two crimes!

Accuracy sprint · ex.4–5

WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin: we fix the ex.4 sentences (TB hint: exactly 15 letters!) and sort the ex.5 message expressions into three moves

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4 (p.21): Rewrite with capitals. TB hint: exactly FIFTEEN letters to change — count as you go! (TB Reinforcement — the letter count gives support.) Pairs, 4 min, a check with the rule explained.
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we take turns with one answer each — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!
  3. Ex.5: message expressions → sorted into the moves S/W/A (start / why / ask) in 2 min, a choral check.
  4. TB Extra (p.28): 30 sec — write YOUR OWN sentence with no capitals, pass it to a neighbour to fix.
📺 On screen: the Student's Book p.21 ex.4–5 · the 7-rule poster · a timer
👦 Students do: fix the sentences counting to 15, sort the expressions into moves, swap traps. Expected: “we found all 15!” — the thrill of counting. Typical error: a missed i'm — the count doesn't add up, they go looking themselves.

WHAT WE DO: your own profile, a plan by the 3 moves, a bank of support phrases; the last minutes — the first 2 sentences of the message

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Workbook U1 · notebook
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write a 5–7 sentence keypal message by steps with correct capitals and be — and revise the whole unit in a supportive Quick-review game.
  • Subsidiary. The Adriana and Kenji model messages; the subskill capital letters (7 rules by hunting); the three moves of a message; writing in timed portions; peer review on 3 boxes; a vote for “which message gets a reply”; Quick review p.21 with peer help — test prep with no fear.
  • Personal. A “format” lesson: the full writing cycle, step by step, with a tracker. Never 15 minutes of silence — 5-minute portions. I present Quick review as “the map of the test”: it's all familiar already, no fear.
By the end, students can…
  • find the three message moves in a model
  • apply 7 capital-letter rules
  • write a keypal message by steps and peer-check it
  • clear the Quick-review rounds with team support

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“i'm from spain — how many ‘crimes’?” → two: I + Spain.
“Do we plan before we write?” → Yes: THINK → PREPARE first.
“In peer review do we say ‘it's bad’?” → No — check the boxes and help fix.

Assumptions: All the be forms + questions are active (P3–P4) — the pair converts grammar into a social skill. The keypal format motivates; writing in portions keeps up with this age's slow handwriting.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
