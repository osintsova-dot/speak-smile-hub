🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use nine verbs for making and repairing things (create, customise, decorate, design, fix, mend, rebuild, recycle, sew) and tell a past story with the past simple and past continuous working together: while they were studying, they found new manufacturers.
What is genuinely hard
The verbs are near-synonyms that Russian collapses: fix and mend, create and design, customise and decorate. The grammar is not new — it is the CHOICE that is hard, and it goes wrong in one predictable place: the long background action gets the past simple, and the whole picture inverts.
Typical error to expect
«When I walked home, I saw a fire» for the long action — English marks the background with the continuous: While I was walking home… Russian uses aspect, not tense, so the choice has to be made consciously.
«While I saw him, he was running» — while goes with the LONG action, when with the short one. Getting them the wrong way round inverts the whole picture.
Why the lesson is built this way
The words arrive through four before-and-after photographs of real objects — tyres that became plant pots, a pallet that became a go-kart — and three teenagers describing what they made. Meaning is built by contrast (what was it before? what is it now?), which is the fastest way to separate synonyms. The reading is the best story in the block: twins who built a clothing brand at fifteen, read as a Reading Part 4 gapped text where every answer is a pronoun or a linker. The grammar then comes out of that same story, and the productive stage is a two-minute personal anecdote that cannot be told without both tenses.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“At fifteen these twins sold their trainer collection to pay for their own brand. Their first shoe failed. Rihanna wore the second one. Today: the English verbs for making things — and the two past tenses that let you tell a story instead of listing events.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a 90-second repair of the last block's weakest point, and the bridge into Unit 5

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what's the best thing about today so far? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the block-2 rewind. On the board: Tell your partner two things you used to do when you were ten and don't do now.
  3. Kagan Think-Pair-Share, 90 seconds. Take three answers and check the form aloud — used to was the weakest section of the last test, and this is the cheapest possible repair.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the unit: Page thirty-two. Unit five — You made it! Four photographs, and every one of them used to be something else.

👦 Students do: speak in pairs, then three answers to the room with the form checked in public

30–34′
Move break · what was it before?
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that spends the nine verbs immediately

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six objects on the board or held up (a tyre, an old shirt, a wooden box, a broken phone, a plain T-shirt, a bicycle).
  2. Everyone stands. You point at one; the class has ten seconds to tell a neighbour what they would do with it, using a verb from the wall: I'd customise it. I'd recycle it.
  3. After each object, take one answer aloud and make it a full sentence.
  4. Freeze and sit: Page thirty-three. Two brothers who did exactly this — and made it their job.
📺 On screen: six object pictures or real objects, prepared in advance — nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone speaks six times in four minutes, to a neighbour rather than to the teacher

46–58′
Reading · the exam gapped text
pairs → class
Why: Reading · the exam gapped text

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 = B1 Reading Part 4: five sentences removed, eight offered, three extra

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the exam information aloud: Reading Part 4 — a text with five sentences removed. Eight sentences are offered, in the wrong order, and three of them are not needed.
  2. Do gap 1 together with the method on the board: 1 read the sentence before · 2 read the sentence after · 3 find the word that links across · 4 choose. In sentence C, «really good ones» points back to trainers, and «they built up a collection» follows naturally.
  3. Pairs do gaps 2–5. Monitor and ask the useful question rather than giving answers: Which word in your sentence points back?
  4. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together. For every answer the student names the linking word: F Meanwhile (Chet against Betts), A however and continued, H This wasn't easy, D Thanks to its publication.
  5. Then the question that teaches distractor logic: Why doesn't B fit anywhere?
  6. ex.3: the five highlighted words, two minutes, checked fast.
📺 On screen: the four-step method on the board, permanently. Backup: none needed — book and board only
👦 Students do: work in pairs, argue about links, then justify each answer by its linking word. Everyone answers at least once because the check is by number
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE task 11 (the gapped text). Five sentences removed, every one recoverable from a pointing word — the Russian task is the same machine with fewer options.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: after the book task, show one Russian-format item (a text with two gaps and four sentences) for five minutes in pairs. Take the time from the Talking points stage.

WHAT WE DO: the unit's own discussion, tied to the verbs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3: four rules from the story, then choice and production

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books at page 34. Warmer from the Teacher's Book, books closed, thirty seconds: put these words in order — fashion / while / found / the / manufacturers / studying / twins / were / new / they. The sentence comes out, the two tenses get named.
  2. ex.1: four examples from the article, four rules. Draw the picture on the board while you check — a long line for the continuous, a cross for the simple, the cross landing on the line for rule d.
  3. ex.2 individually, then pairs. Six items, each one a decision. Check by asking for the RULE, not the form.
  4. ex.3 in RallyCoach pairs: the beach text, ten verbs, one writes and one coaches. Set the gist question first so it is read: What did he do with the things he found?
  5. Fast finishers match every answer in ex.3 to a rule from ex.1. Grammar reference is SB p.151.

🎯 Speaking out · the thing I made
pairs → class
Why: 🎯 Speaking out · the thing I made

WHAT WE DO: a real two-minute anecdote that needs both tenses

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Each student thinks of one real thing they made, repaired, decorated or ruined. Two minutes of notes, with three compulsory elements on the board: 1 What were you doing before it started? 2 What happened? 3 How did it end?
  2. Element 1 is the trap and the point: it can only be told in the past continuous.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share, ninety seconds each way, then swap partners and do it again — the second telling is always better and always more accurate.
  4. Three anecdotes to the whole class. The class listens with a job: hold up a flat hand for a continuous, a fist for a simple.
  5. Log two or three tense-choice errors silently; they open lesson 2.
📺 On screen: the three compulsory elements on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: make notes, tell the story twice to two different partners, then listen actively with a signalling job

Structure
While the twins were studying fashion, they found new manufacturers. · Rihanna was wearing a Lucid FC jacket when someone took a photo of her. · Celebrities were soon wearing it too. · At fifteen they created a streetwear brand.
Form
past simple for completed actions and the events of a story · past continuous for actions in progress, for the background, and for the long action interrupted by a short one; while + long action, when + short action.
Pron. watch
sew /səʊ/ — it rhymes with go, not with new, and this is the single most mispronounced word in the unit · design /dɪˈzaɪn/ with a silent g · customise /ˈkʌstəmaɪz/ · and the past continuous is heard as a weak form in fast speech: /wəz ˈwɔːkɪŋ/, not /wɒz/
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I was walking home when I saw the fire.» Which action is longer? → walking. Which one interrupts? → seeing. That is why they wear different tenses.
«He fixed the bike» and «he mended his shirt» — could we swap them? → not comfortably. You fix machines and mend fabric.
«While» or «when» in front of the long action? → while. Say the pair aloud twice: while I WAS doing, when I DID.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The nine verbs collapse into make and repairThe wall carries them in pairs, and every wrong answer gets the same three-second treatment: point at the pair, say the difference in five words (machines / fabric · from nothing / on paper).
The long action gets the past simpleTeach it as a picture, not a rule: draw a long line and a cross on the board. The line is the continuous, the cross is the simple. Every disputed item gets drawn, not explained.
The gapped text is answered by topicForce the mechanism: for each answer, the student must point at the WORD that links across the gap — They, He, This, Meanwhile, Thanks to its publication. No linking word, no answer.
Fast finishers in the readingThe Teacher's Book task: write a note explaining each choice. Their notes become the whole-class justification in the check.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text — the past tenses are a named line of the codifier) and tasks 10–18.

Past simple against past continuous is one of the two most frequent grammar-cloze items, and the deciding signal is always the same: a long action interrupted by a short one, or a background described before the story starts. Today's text — two fifteen-year-olds who built a clothing brand — is also the exam's favourite kind of reading: a success story with a chronology.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
