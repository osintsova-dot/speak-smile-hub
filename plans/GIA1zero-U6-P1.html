12–22′
Input: rooms and furniture
Discover · 7 rooms and 9 objects
board · desks

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: introducing the lexis step by step: ex.2 photos A–E ↔ rooms + ex.3 “who does what where” + ex.5 furniture in the photos; choral drill

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I draw a house plan on the board and label the rooms one by one, a chorus after each: bedroom · bathroom · kitchen · living room · dining room · hall · garden.
  2. Pairs, 3 min: Photos A–E and seven words. TWO words are extra! Key: A bedroom · B living room · C kitchen · D bathroom · E hall (extra: dining room, garden). (Reinforcement TB p.83: first name the rooms WITHOUT the word list.)
  3. Ex.3 in chorus round the chain: 1 kitchen · 2 bedroom · 3 living room · 4 bathroom · 5 dining room · 6 garden.
  4. Ex.5: Nine new words — find them in the photos! (bath · bed · ceiling · chair · floor · shower · table · toilet · wall). Key: 1 floor, ceiling, wall (in all photos) · 2 bath, shower (in none) · 3 bed–A · chair–B · table–B and A · toilet–D.
  5. Pronunciation drill by syllables with claps: CEI-ling · FUR-ni-ture · BATH-room.
  6. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
📺 On screen: board: house plan with labels (stays up the whole lesson) · SB pp.72–73 · picture cards for the wall
👦 Students do: label the rooms, look for furniture in the photos, repeat in chorus with claps. Expected: the set settles in in 10 minutes. Typical error: dining room ↔ living room — the gesture “dine = eat” and a hand to the mouth.

Input: prepositions through movement
Discover 2 · prepositions through the body
standing → desks

WHAT WE DO: introducing 9 prepositions physically (a student + a chair), then ex.6 diagrams and ex.7 choice; trap errors TB p.84

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Live input: I set out a chair, call students up one by one — Stand BEHIND the chair. IN FRONT OF the chair. NEXT TO the chair. NEAR the chair — no, further! BETWEEN the chair and the desk. The class calls out the commands in chorus.
  2. I work through the trap pair live: two students NEXT TO (shoulder to shoulder) and NEAR (a metre apart).
  3. Anchor gestures on the board: IN (a fist into a palm) · ON (a flat palm on top) · UNDER · ABOVE.
  4. Ex.6 in pairs, 4 min: nine diagrams. Key: a between · b in · c above · d on · e near · f in front of · g under · h next to · i behind.
  5. Ex.7 in chorus round the chain: 1 above · 2 on · 3 between · 4 in front of · 5 next to. In yellow on the board: in front OF — I say “in front”, the class shouts “OF!”
📺 On screen: a chair + a desk for a live demo · board: 9 prepositions with gestures (stay up the whole lesson) · SB p.73
👦 Students do: follow the commands with their bodies, then sort the diagrams in pairs. Expected: physical input sticks better than any explanation. Typical error: “near to” — chorus: NO TO!

Play · Memory game «Where is it?»
teams

WHAT WE DO: a reinforcement game: ▶ Memory game (TB p.6) using a room photo — teams remember what was where

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Memory game: I show a room photo (SB p.73 or my own) for 30 seconds. I cover it.
  2. Teams write as many sentences as possible: There's a lamp on the table. The chair is next to the bed. 3 min.
  3. Check: I uncover the photo, the team reads out — a correct sentence +1, correct WITH a preposition +2.
  4. A second round with another photo and the rule “no repeating a preposition”.
  5. Fast finishers: write 2 FALSE sentences — the class catches them.
📺 On screen: 2 room photos on the projector · timer (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: watch, memorise and write as a team — everyone works, everyone writes in turn. Expected: 6–10 sentences per round. Typical error: “There's two chairs” — chorus: THERE ARE.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 16 rooms & furniture words and say where things are with 9 prepositions (There's a bed in the bedroom. The chair is between the table and the wall.).
  • Subsidiary. WDYT? “What is art?” opened by a vote; describing your own room/flat from a support; Video skills (the house in Cornwall) watched step by step; the first present continuous phrases FROM A MODEL (The woman is standing…) — no theory, the theory comes in Pair 3; pronunciation /e/ vs /ɜː/.
  • Personal. The topic is very visual — success is guaranteed if you keep the supports on the board till the end of the lesson. Don't rush the prepositions: set in/on and near/next to with GESTURES, not explanation.
By the end, students can…
  • name 16 rooms and furniture words
  • use 9 prepositions of place with a picture
  • describe their own room in 4–5 sentences from a frame
  • react to the video house in English

Target language
Lexis

Structure
There's a bed in the bedroom. · There's a clock on the table. · The chair is between the table and the wall. · This is my flat. It's got two bedrooms.
Form
There's + singular · There are + plural · on = surface · in = inside · near ≠ next to · in front OF · furniture — uncountable (some furniture).
Pron. watch
/e/ short: bed · next · test · friend · head. /ɜː/ long: FURniture · girl · word · work (SB p.117). Plus ceiling /ˈsiːlɪŋ/, floor /flɔː/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Picture IN the wall or ON the wall?” → ON (a surface).
“Near = next to?” → near = somewhere close by; next to = right beside, touching.
“In front the door — is that all?” → no, you need OF: in front OF the door.
“There is one bed. There's or There are?” → There's a bed.

Assumptions: Rooms are partly familiar from school (bedroom, kitchen), there is/are was done in U3 — we build on it. Prepositions of place as a system and the words ceiling/hall/shower are new. Present continuous hasn't been introduced yet.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Mix up in / onAnchor gesture: IN — a fist into a palm · ON — a flat palm on top. I hold the gesture every time instead of explaining.
near ↔ next toTwo students stand up: “near” — a metre apart, “next to” — shoulder to shoulder. A living picture.
Drop the of in in front ofI write in front OF in yellow and set up a choral reflex: I say “in front” — the class shouts “OF!”
Long words scare them (ceiling, furniture)A choral drill by syllables with claps + a picture card on the wall until the end of the unit.
The video is fast, they don't understand it allWe watch it TWICE, in chunks; the task is simple and visual: count the rooms, show fingers.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
