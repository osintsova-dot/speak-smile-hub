🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Complete a set of notes while listening — one to three words per gap, exactly as heard, correctly spelled — and use the past perfect simple and continuous to show which of two past actions happened first, and whether the focus is on completion or on duration.
What is genuinely hard
The listening task is not about understanding; it is about writing. Students who follow the recording happily still lose marks because they rewrite what they heard in their own words, or because they miss the part of speech the gap needs. The grammar is the classic Russian gap: Russian orders past events with words, English with a tense, so the past perfect has to be chosen consciously every single time.
Typical error to expect
«The room was cold because Aidan left the window open» — the earlier action takes the past perfect: had left. This is the sentence the Teacher's Book opens the grammar with, and it is the sentence the exam gaps.
«had went», «had drank», «had wrote» — the past perfect takes the THIRD form. The irregular list belongs on the desk before the exercise, not after it.
Why the lesson is built this way
The listening comes first because it supplies the grammar: the four sentences the students analyse in Grammar in context 2 are sentences from the podcast they have just heard about the human computers at NASA — the women, many of them Black, who did the calculations that put John Glenn in orbit. Predicting the gaps before listening is a separate stage with its own product (what PART OF SPEECH goes here?), because that is the technique the exam pays for. The grammar then moves from meaning (which happened first?) to form to a text about a girl who set up a juice company from rejected apples, and ends on the students' own past.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“In 1962, astronaut John Glenn refused to take off until one woman had checked the computer's calculations by hand. Her name was Katherine Johnson. Today: listening the way the exam asks for it — and the tense that says which of two past things happened first.”

Why: Prediction is the difference between a candidate who hears the answer and one who recognises it. It takes nine minutes and pays for the rest of the year.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.24 ex.2: what KIND of word is missing from each of the twelve gaps

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Do gap (a) together, exactly as the Teacher's Book models it: What comes immediately before the gap? «the». So what kind of word do we need? A noun. And the sentence says she is an expert in something — so what kind of noun?
  2. Pairs, five minutes: for each of the twelve gaps write NOUN, VERB, ADJECTIVE, NUMBER or NAME, and a guess if they have one.
  3. Read the Exam tip aloud and turn it into three rules on the board: 1 Read the gaps first. 2 Predict the part of speech. 3 Write what you hear — spelling counts.
  4. Do not confirm any prediction. That is the recording's job.
📺 On screen: the three rules on the board, left there for the listening. Backup: dictate them.
👦 Students do: label twelve gaps for part of speech and guess where they can

WHAT WE DO: SB p.24 ex.3 (track 08): the twelve gaps filled with the words actually said

Past perfect · which came first
desks
Why: Meaning before form, and the examples come from a text they have processed for content — so the grammar arrives already attached to something.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.24 ex.1a–1c: meaning, then choice, then form

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Warmer on the board: The room was cold because Aidan left the window open. Pairs: is there a mistake? → had left, because it happened first.
  2. Ex.1a: four sentences from the podcast, decide which action happened first. Key: the checking, the work, the human computers' work, the women's work.
  3. Ex.1b: two choices. Key: 1 before · 2 continuous (duration) · 3 simple (completion).
  4. Ex.1c, form, elicited onto the board with gaps: past perfect simple = had + ____ · past perfect continuous = had + ____ + ____-ing.
  5. Drill the contraction chorally five times: I'd been working /aɪd bɪn/. Not «I had been».
📺 On screen: the two form lines with gaps on the board. Backup: none needed — this stage is board and voice.
👦 Students do: decide the order in four sentences, choose two rules, then say both forms aloud

Structure
The astronaut only took off when Katherine Johnson had checked the calculations. · Digital computers began to do work that human computers had done for years. · A group of women had been working there for five years before the war started.
Form
past perfect simple = had + past participle, the action that happened first, focus on completion · past perfect continuous = had been + -ing, focus on duration or on an action that was still going on · contraction: I'd, she'd, they'd.
Pron. watch
the -er / -ee pair moves the stress and changes the person: employer vs employee, trainer vs trainee, interviewer vs interviewee · the past-perfect contraction is one syllable: I'd been /aɪd bɪn/ · linking in the negotiating phrases: «I agree_up to_a point», «What_about_you?»
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«When I arrived, the film had started.» Did I see the beginning? → no. That is the whole tense in one question.
«She had been waiting for two hours.» Is she still waiting at that moment? → probably yes, and the two hours are the point.
«had went» — possible? → no. Had takes the third form: had gone.
ICQ before the listening: how many words per gap? → one to three. Can you change them into your own words? → no, never.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Answers are written in the students' own wordsSay it before the first play and again before the second: Write the words you hear. Not your version of them. Then, in the check, refuse a correct paraphrase once — publicly and kindly — so the rule is felt.
The audio will not playRead the script yourself from the Teacher's Book p.124, twice, at natural speed and without helpful pauses. It is a podcast with two voices: change your position for the presenter and the guest.
«had went», «had drank»The irregular list goes on the desk BEFORE the exercise. Then every answer is said with all three forms: go, went, gone.
Fast finishers during the grammar practiceThe Teacher's Book questions on the Karina text: how long has she lived in Wales, why did she set up the company, why did shops reject the apples, what was the first product, how does the company protect the environment?
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 1–9 (listening) — and specifically the note-completion discipline: write the words you actually hear, spelled correctly.

The Cambridge task on page 24 asks for one to three words written into a gap. The Russian paper asks for a number or a letter, but the underlying habit is identical and it is the habit that decides the mark: read the gaps BEFORE the recording, predict the part of speech, and do not rewrite what you heard into your own words. The past perfect in the second half of the lesson is a named line of the grammar codifier.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
