A5 grammar card — PRESENT SIMPLE: ROUTINE ENGINE: affirmative mode (I/you/we/they + base verb · he/she/it + verb + s) with the third-person SPELLING PIT-STOP (work→works · go/do/watch/finish→goes/does/watches/finishes · study→studies · have→has), then negative mode (don't / doesn't + base verb) and the warning after DOESN'T — base verb, no -s

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 three spelling rules = three gestures; verbs into columns; a choral /ɪz/ drill

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Gesture 1 “the snake” (-es after sibilants + o/x): watch → watchES · go → goES · do → doES. Chorus + gesture.
  2. Gesture 2 “the scissors” (consonant+y → -ies): study → studIES. Counter-example: play → plays (vowel+y — just S).
  3. Gesture 3 “the crown” (irregular): have → HAS.
  4. Ex.2: teams sort 6 verbs into columns (do · finish · go · have · relax · study) — 3 min, checking in chorus.
  5. The /ɪz/ drill in chorus ×3: finishes · relaxes · watches (p.116).
📺 On screen: board: 3 columns with gestures · SB p.52 ex.2 · p.116
👦 Students do: they learn the rules with their body and sort the verbs. Expected: the gestures stick instantly. Typical error: plays → -ies — the “vowel+y” gesture cancels the scissors.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can make present simple affirmative and negative sentences with correct 3rd-person forms (He gets up · She doesn't relax) — the course's first big verb grammar, introduced through the full cycle.
  • Subsidiary. Spelling rules (-es/-ies/has) with gestures; Common errors as “mines”; written consolidation in portions; after 45′ — 16 free-time activities + listening about the app step by step (subskill: completing sentences); finale — a chain game with he/she.
  • Personal. ⚠️ Let the grammar BREATHE: up to now we've had be/have got/there is — this is the first “real” verb with the do-system. Don't rush: choral drills, a table in the notebook, a check after every step. Success today = clean sentences, not speed.
By the end, students can…
  • state the 3 rules (routines/facts · infinitive · +s)
  • spell 3rd-person forms (-es/-ies/has)
  • make negatives with don't/doesn't
  • name 12+ free-time activities and catch answers in the app listening

Structure
They get up early. · She makes music videos. · I don't write songs. · He doesn't relax after school.
Form
I/you/we/they = infinitive; he/she/it + -s; -es after -s/-o/-ch/-sh/-x; consonant+y → -ies; have → has; don't/doesn't + infinitive WITHOUT -s.
Pron. watch
-es = /ɪz/: finishes · relaxes · watches (SB p.116) · doesn't /ˈdʌznt/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Present simple — is it about right now or about every day?” → about routines and facts (every day).
“He watch or watches?” → watches (-ch → -es).
“She doesn't likes?” → No! doesn't has already taken the -s for itself.

Assumptions: PS phrases sounded from a model in P1–P2 (I get up · he getS) — today the system for the first time. Free-time words are partly familiar from life (gadgets), the 16-item set as a system is new. We don't assume any transfer from be: we explain do-negation from scratch.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
