🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): A lesson of two live shows: a spoken one (showing interest — a skill that decides things both in the exam and in life) and a written one (an email about a trip). The drama is 'the reaction test': the storyteller falls silent if nobody reacts for five seconds — the Key phrases stop being a list and become the oxygen of a conversation. The TB asks you directly to work on intonation: 'No way!' warm and impressed, 'Sounds boring!' disappointed; a phrase without intonation does not count. The sequencing words are presented as 'the editorial standard': an email with no order of events goes back to its author.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Reaction test, live. One of you tells a real story — where you went, what you saw. The other one has to keep it alive. Five seconds of silence and the storyteller STOPS. Dead air. Your tools: What was it like? No way! You're kidding! That's so funny! Sounds boring! And here's the catch — the words are nothing without the voice. Say 'No way' flat and the story dies anyway. Round two: the same trip becomes an email, and the editor rejects anything without First of all, Then, After that. Go live!”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the reaction test: the storyteller falls silent if there is no response for five seconds. The interest phrases are the oxygen of the conversation, but only with intonation. The second show is the same trip in writing, and the editor rejects any text without sequencing words.

WHAT WE DO: Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: the outside circle tells the story of a trip, the inside one keeps the show alive with reactions; the five-second rule; then they swap roles

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: Outside circle tells the story of a real trip. Inside circle keeps it alive. Ninety seconds, then rotate.
  2. The rule: Five seconds without a reaction — the storyteller stops and folds their arms. Everyone sees it.
  3. The constraint: the same reaction twice in a row does not count; Russian costs the crew a point.
  4. The TB technique between the circles: you say something amazing or something boring and point at a student — they react and pass the baton on.
📺 On screen: Key phrases cards on the screen and on the desks (Backup: the phrases on the board in two columns: Asking / Responding)
👦 Students do: walk around and talk for real. Expect: the five-second rule makes English compulsory with no reminders — staying silent does not pay

Order of events: the editorial standard
Sequencing · the editorial standard
pairs

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 (find five linkers in the email) → the TB rule → ex.5 the theme park plan turned into a text against the clock

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4: Find five sequencing words in Josef's email. (TB key: First of all, · Then · After that, · Next, · Finally,). The TB asks you to notice the comma after the linker.
  2. The rule (TB tip): after that, then, next — in any order; first of all and finally — only the beginning and the end.
  3. ex.5 through Kagan RallyCoach, 5 minutes: in a pair the first student turns a line of the plan into a sentence out loud and names the linker, the second is the coach: checks the past tense form, watches the comma after the linker and praises; on the next line they swap roles. The theme park plan → a text in past simple. The TB model on the board: 'Last week, we went on a trip to a theme park. It was brilliant! First of all, we visited the 'Lost Kingdom' and saw 'living' dinosaurs. Then we went on the two giant rollercoasters…'
  4. The upgrade for our students: Add ONE line with 'but' and ONE negative: We didn't go on the water slides because…
📺 On screen: SB p.81 ex.4–5 · timer
👦 Students do: draw the rule out of the model and rewrite the plan as a text. Error: finally in the middle — sort it out on the spot

PRODUCT · the email

WHAT WE DO: THINK (choose a trip) → PREPARE (notes by paragraph) → WRITE (6–8 lines) → peer review → the unit wall

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: A place you really visited. Zoo, farm, museum, another city. Notes only. (they can take the place from the P5 Research)
  2. PREPARE following the plan on SB p.81: paragraph 1 — where, when, what they liked most; paragraphs 2–3 — where they went and what they saw.
  3. WRITE: Six to eight lines. Five sequencing words. At least two irregular verbs — went, saw, had, met. The opening is given: Hi … / How are things? I/We went on a trip to …
  4. CHECK against the four questions on SB p.81 + peer review (ex.7): the partner says whether they would like to go there themselves and why.
  5. Publication: the emails go on the unit wall. Mail is LIVE!

WB Unit 6 · the emails on the unit wall
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can show interest with 8+ Key phrases and the right intonation in a real conversation, and write a 6–8-line email about a trip using past simple and five sequencing words.
  • Subsidiary. Megan and Lukas's video (ex.1–3); track 40 for intonation; Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; Josef's email as the model (ex.1–3); the subskill sequencing words (ex.4); ex.5 the theme park plan → a text; peer review ex.6 and ex.7.
  • Personal. They write in class, not at home — you see the process. The editorial rule: an email without five sequencing words and without paragraphs is not published, and the author fixes it, not you. Drill the intonation chorally before the live show, otherwise the phrases sound robotic.
By the end, students can…
  • ask What was it like? / Was it fun? at the right moment
  • respond with No way! / You're kidding! / Sounds boring! with real intonation
  • order events with First of all, Then, After that, Next, Finally
  • write an email about a trip in three paragraphs

Structure

Form
First of all · Then · After that · Next · Finally (Jozef's email uses exactly these five). TB adds: after that, then and next can go in any order; first of all and finally go only at the beginning and at the end. Put a comma after the linker.
Pron. watch
TB: compare HOW Lukas says «No way!» and «You're kidding!» (warm, impressed, excited) and HOW Megan says «Sounds boring!» (disappointed, sympathetic). The phrase does not work without the intonation. · after a sequencing word — a comma in writing and a slight pause in speech: First of all, … ↗ we visited …
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'That's so funny!' said in a flat voice — will it work? → no: the TB asks you directly to compare HOW No way! and Sounds boring! sound. Without intonation the phrase does not count.
Can finally go in the middle of a story? → no: first of all and finally are for the beginning and the end only (TB tip). Then, next, after that — anywhere.
The email is finished but it has one sequencing word — do we publish it? → no, the editor will send it back: you need at least five, like Josef.

Assumptions: SB pp.79–81, syllabus pair 51. Past simple was drilled in P3 and P5 — here it is a working tool, not a novelty. The dialogue and email skeletons come ready from the coursebook; the mission only frames them and adds a real audience.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They read the dialogue instead of playing itThe five-second silence rule: the storyteller physically stops. The listener understands instantly what the phrases are for.
The reactions sound roboticChoral drilling on track 40 + the TB technique: you tell them something amazing or something boring and point at a student — they must react appropriately and with intonation, then point at the next one.
They copy the email off the modelThe editorial rule: Josef's skeleton and linkers — yes, Josef's facts — no. Their own trip (they can take it from the P5 Research) gives them their own material.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
