🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Close the first term before it is measured: re-sits taken, missed lessons caught up, unfilmed speaking filmed, and four stations built out of the group's own error logs.
What is genuinely hard
Not the language — the logistics. Three different jobs run in one room for twenty minutes, and the lesson only works if every student knows which zone is theirs before they sit down. The second difficulty is honesty: the status board names who owes what, publicly, and that has to be done without a single word of blame.
Typical error to expect
The term's five recurring errors, which is what the stations are built around: present perfect vs past simple («I have seen him yesterday»), will for arrangements («I will meet my friend at six» for a fixed plan), double comparison («more happier»), missing articles («she is student»), and mustn't ≠ don't have to.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built for the two things that actually happen in a Norilsk winter — missed lessons and unfilmed speaking — and it refuses to waste the students who missed nothing. They get four stations made from the group's real errors, a production task that forces the range of the whole first term, and the full briefing for the paper. Everybody leaves with the same three revision points, and nobody leaves owing work without a date.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today everybody works, but not everybody works on the same thing. If you owe me a paper, you write it. If you owe me four minutes on camera, you speak. And if you owe me nothing — you get the four tables that were built out of the mistakes this group actually made.”

Opening · check-in + the honest status of the group
door → desks
Why: A reserve lesson dies when it looks like a free lesson. Two minutes of visible, personal administration turn it into the most focused lesson of the first term.

WHAT WE DO: one English sentence each, then the real state of play on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. One question each at the door, one full sentence back.
  2. 3 minutes — the status board. Write three columns while they settle: DONE · TO CATCH UP · TO RE-SIT, and put names under the last two from your own mark sheet. No comment, no tone — this is administration, not judgement.
  3. Say the shape in one line: Today is the buffer lesson. Everybody works, but not everybody works on the same thing.
📺 On screen: the three columns on the board. Backup: none — board and voice.
👦 Students do: read the board and find their own name before sitting down

Frame · three jobs in one room
board
Why: Three parallel jobs need three named places. Told once, with the zones on the board, the room re-organises itself in ninety seconds and stays organised.

WHAT WE DO: who does what for the next seventy-five minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the three jobs and where they happen: Re-sits at the back — the written sections you lost. Catch-up at the middle desks — the mid-year paper you missed. Everybody else on the stations.
  2. Say the speaking rule now: Anybody whose speaking was not filmed gets called to the corner today. Four minutes, three questions, filmed like everybody else's.
  3. Set the outcome so the lesson has an end, not just a middle: By the end, every name is out of the two right-hand columns — or has a date.
📺 On screen: the three zones drawn on the board plan. Backup: none needed.
👦 Students do: go to their zone with the right paper in hand
board, the re-sit papers, the catch-up worksheets
10–30′
Re-sits and catch-up · the parallel twenty minutes
three zones
Why: Everybody is doing their own real work at the same time. This is the only lesson in the first term where three different needs can be served without any of them being a compromise.

WHAT WE DO: the students who owe work do it now, under the same conditions as everyone else

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Re-sits (back desks). The same paper, the sections they lost, on the clock. Same silence rules as the test. Mark them in the room before the lesson ends — a re-sit that goes home to be marked never closes.
  2. Catch-up (middle). The mid-year paper of the lessons they missed: the workbook pages and the model text, with the key on the desk. Their job is not to hand it in — it is to be able to do the station work twenty minutes from now.
  3. Stations (front). The rest start the first two stations below, in pairs, while you set the other two zones going.
  4. You circulate on a fixed loop: re-sits, catch-up, stations, repeat. Ninety seconds each, no long conversations — anybody who needs more than that gets a note in your book and two minutes at the end.
📺 On screen: the timer for the re-sit group only. Backup: a phone stopwatch.
👦 Students do: work in their own zone, silently in the re-sit corner
re-sit papers, missed workbook pages, answer keys, station cards
30–34′
Move break · the retrieval line
open floor
Why: Retrieval on their feet between two long sitting stages, and it doubles as a live inventory of what the group actually kept.

WHAT WE DO: stand up, one item each, the whole period in ninety seconds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Line them up. Each student says one item from the first term — a word, a structure, a linker — that they now own. No repeats down the line.
  2. Anyone who repeats or freezes sits down; the line closes up and carries on. Two rounds, thirty seconds each.
  3. Finish standing: everybody says the one item they will use in the production stage later.
📺 On screen: nothing — this is floor and voice.
👦 Students do: stand in a line and produce one item each, twice
open floor
34–50′
Stations · built out of your own error logs
four tables
Why: The content comes from what they actually got wrong, so no minute is spent on what the group already owns. Kagan pairs mean every student produces at every table.

WHAT WE DO: four tables, four weaknesses, four minutes each

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The four stations are not decided by you tonight — they are decided by the error logs: take the four commonest entries across the group and make one table each. Typically: the tense that keeps slipping, the two words everybody confuses, the transformation format, and spelling.
  2. Four minutes a table, on a timer, in pairs. Each table has a card: eight items, the key face down, and one line at the bottom — write your own example.
  3. RallyCoach at every table: one writes, the partner coaches, then swap. Nobody works alone here.
  4. Students who came from the catch-up zone join the stations now — that was the point of the twenty minutes.
📺 On screen: the timer and the four table numbers. Backup: if there is no timer, run it by the clock on the wall and call the changes yourself.
👦 Students do: rotate four tables in pairs, coach each other, write one own example per table
four station cards + keys, timer
50–66′
The corner · the speaking that was missed
corner + tables
Why: Speaking is the skill that disappears when a lesson is missed, and the video is what makes progress visible later. The room carries on working, so nobody sits waiting for a turn.

WHAT WE DO: filmed speaking for everybody who owes it, while the room keeps working

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Call the pairs to the corner in the order on your mark sheet. Four minutes each pair: three questions each way, reasons and a personal detail expected.
  2. Film every answer, exactly as in the test: name, unit and date said into the camera first. Mark it as the pair leaves the corner, not from the video afterwards.
  3. The rest of the room continues the station rotation — they have cards enough for a second lap, and the second lap is where the improvement shows.
  4. If nobody owes speaking, use the corner anyway: take the three quietest students in the group and film four minutes each. In December those three videos will be the ones you want.
📺 On screen: the phone on its stand, facing away from the room. Backup: the school tablet; rename the files the same evening or they are lost.
👦 Students do: speak in pairs on camera for four minutes; the others carry on the stations
phone on a stand, mark sheet, station cards
66–80′
🎯 Production · the first term in one text
desks
Why: A reserve lesson must end in production, or it reads as filler. This is also the fairest diagnostic you will get before the paper.

WHAT WE DO: everything of the period, used once, by everybody, for themselves

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set one task that forces the range: Write eight to ten sentences about your own first term — what you did, what changed, what you would do differently. Use at least six structures from the board and eight words from the list.
  2. Board the six structures before they start, then leave them up. Underlining what they use is part of the task — it makes the range visible to them, not only to you.
  3. Timed Pair Share at the end: sixty seconds each, read it to a partner, the partner names the two best sentences.
  4. Collect them. These are the two minutes of the lesson where you find out what the first term actually built.
📺 On screen: the six structures on the board. Backup: dictate them.
👦 Students do: write eight to ten sentences using six structures, then read to a partner
notebooks, board
80–86′
The paper explained · format, version, camera
board
Why: Everything the group did today feeds one number: the score on the paper. Naming the format, the choice and the three revision points turns the reserve lesson into preparation, not a pause.

WHAT WE DO: exactly what happens next lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Board the shape of the mid-year paper and leave it up: seven sections, one hundred points, ninety minutes.
  2. Say the order and the reason: listening first, writing last, speaking taken in pairs in the corner while the room writes, filmed as always.
  3. The version is a choice. Standard or Higher — same sections, same hundred points, harder wording. Decide tonight; you may swap in the first five minutes.
  4. Name the three things to revise tonight, from what you saw today — not a generic list. Write those three on the board and let them copy it.
📺 On screen: the paper map on the board. Backup: dictate — it is seven lines.
👦 Students do: copy the seven sections and the three revision points, decide the version
board

Wrap-up + homework
desks → door
Why: Self-assessment against the aim they heard at the start, so the lesson closes the loop it opened. Homework comes from their own workbook, not from a photocopy.

WHAT WE DO: self-check against today's aim, then the homework said once and written on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Self-check (60 seconds): Look at your five pages. Which unit are you least sure of? Write the number on the exit slip — that is the station I run again next lesson.
  2. Homework: your own revision pages — add five sentences of your own to each. Plus any Unit 4 checkpoint exercises you left unfinished (Workbook p.38–39), checked against the book in a different colour.
  3. Bring the pages back next lesson. They are the only thing you need.
📺 On screen: the homework on the board in one line. Backup: same in the class chat.
👦 Students do: write a unit number on the slip and leave with three sheets in the bag.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, every student is either fully up to date for the mid-year paper or has a date, and each of them can name three things to revise before the paper.
  • Subsidiary. Re-sitting written sections under test conditions; speaking on camera for four minutes; coaching a partner through a known weakness; producing eight to ten sentences with a required range.

Structure
the term's structures, all retrieved: I've been studying since … · She had already left when I arrived. · I'm meeting him at six. · far more resourceful than … · I was able to … · You needn't have paid.
Form
no new form is presented today; the lesson samples the five systems of the term in production: present simple / continuous / perfect, narrative tenses with used to and would, five future forms, comparison and articles and ability, and the two modal systems.
Pron. watch
keep the weak forms honest in the spoken stations: /ˈhæftə/ · /ˈɔːtə/ · /wəz ˈeɪbl tə/ · /ˈmʌstəvbɪn/. A term of grammar said in strong forms sounds like a list; said in weak forms it sounds like English.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
Present perfect: «I have seen him yesterday» — is «yesterday» finished? — Yes. So which tense? — Past simple.
Future: It's in my diary and someone else knows — will or present continuous? — Present continuous.
Articles: «She is student» — one of many? — Yes. So? — a student.
ICQ for the stations: Books open or closed? When do you turn the key over? How many lines do you write on your page?

Assumptions: The buffer lesson of the first term, immediately before the mid-year paper. In this town the reserve is not a luxury: actirovannye days close the school without notice and the lesson has to absorb them. Before it, the teacher needs the mark sheet up to date, the re-sit papers printed, and the four commonest entries from the group's error logs — those are the four stations. If the group has missed nothing and owes nothing, the reserve runs as full revision of the first term: everybody on the stations, two laps, and the corner used for the three quietest students in the room.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The lesson turns into a free period for the students who owe nothingThey have the stations from minute ten, and the station cards carry enough for two laps. The production task at minute sixty-six is collected and read — say that at the start.
A re-sit student asks for «the same test I did before»It is the same paper, the sections they lost. Do not build a new one: the point is the language, not the novelty, and a second version costs an evening you do not have.
The corner runs late and the last pair is not filmedThe corner closes at minute sixty-six anyway. Write the names down and film them in the first ten minutes of the next lesson — before the papers go out, not after.
A student who missed four lessons cannot catch up in twenty minutesThey are not supposed to. Twenty minutes buys the model text and one workbook page — enough to work the stations. Everything else goes home with a date, in writing, and the parents are told the same evening.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25, 26–31 and 32–36 — the whole grammar-and-vocabulary block of the first term.

Half a year of language in one room. The revision is built around what the exam actually samples: tense choice in a narrative, future forms, comparison, articles, ability and the modal cluster. If a student can do those six things under time pressure, tasks 19–25 stop being frightening.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
