🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The taking-action collocations are not introduced as a table but as 'the language of a plan': if the verb is wrong, the line is deleted from the plan. The 'Save our wildlife!' leaflet openly suggests choosing two things to do this month — and that becomes the mission. The podcast about Mikaila Ulmer is built in as intelligence: a teenager who actually did it, and her moves are ready-made fuel for defending the plan. The subskill (true/false) is held by a rule: underline the key words in the options first, then listen.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Thirty days. That's your budget. Not money — time. The leaflet says: choose two things and do them in the next month. Your crew writes the plan our school could actually run: five actions, each one a verb plus a noun, each one with a reason. Wrong verb — the line gets deleted. We RAISE awareness, we DONATE money, we JOIN an organisation. And before you write: a thirteen-year-old girl who did it for real. Listen, then build.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: thirty days is the crew's budget. Build a plan of five actions (verb + noun), each with a reason. A wrong verb and the line is deleted. The intelligence is the podcast about Mikaila, who did it for real.

WB Unit 6 · points on the leaderboard
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can use 8 taking-action collocations correctly (donate · join · organise · protect · raise · solve · start · visit), catch true/false statements in a podcast, and pitch a five-action plan with reasons.
  • Subsidiary. The collocations are drawn out of the leaflet (ex.1) and consolidated by matching (ex.2); the subskill identifying true/false (ex.3–4); numbers by ear (ex.5); the discussion in ex.6 as the way into the product.
  • Personal. The listening runs strictly in two goes: no.1 — only the two true statements (ex.3), no.2 — the details (ex.4–5). After the first listening, ask not for the fact but for HOW they eliminated the false option (TB rule).
By the end, students can…
  • choose the right verb for 24 taking-action collocations
  • underline key words in true/false options before listening
  • explain why a statement is false
  • pitch a five-action plan with a reason for each action

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'raise a campaign' — can we say that? → no: raise awareness / raise money. A campaign you start or organise.
'join a museum' — and here? → visit a museum. Join is for a club, a group, an organisation.
You heard a key word from the option — does that make the option true? → no: the TB warns you about negatives and synonyms. Only what is true AS A WHOLE is true.

Assumptions: SB p.77, syllabus pair 49. The eight verbs are familiar one by one; the difficulty is the partnership and the speed. The TB asks you to pre-teach wildlife, entrepreneur and volunteer.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
