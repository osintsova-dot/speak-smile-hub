Discovery: what didn't does
Hook · finding didn't in the text
board

WHAT WE DO: discovery: we go back to yesterday's text about scooters and find the form there that we only heard yesterday

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I write three sentences from the text p.99 on the board: Scooters didn't become popular until later. · He didn't want to drive. · Razor didn't plan to start a sport.
  2. Yesterday you saw these in yellow. What do they all have? → didn't.
  3. And what comes after ‘didn't’? Look: become, want, plan. NOT ‘became’, NOT ‘wanted’.
  4. I draw the anchor: DIDN'T 🦖 eats the past — the dinosaur eats -ed and went; the plain form is left. The “ate it” gesture — we learn it right away, the whole class together.
📺 On screen: the board: 3 sentences + the anchor dinosaur (stays for the whole unit)
👦 Students do: they notice the pattern in the three sentences themselves. Expected: “after didn't, an ordinary verb!” — their own discovery, not handed to them. Typical error: they think didn't = don't — I compare a pair of sentences.

Discover · the negative in the past
standing → desks

WHAT WE DO: the full input step by step: ex.1 the rule → a choral transformation drill → ex.2 “the world of 1910” with support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 p.100 together: Which word makes the negative? — didn't. What form after it? — the infinitive.
  2. Everybody stand up! A choral transformation drill STANDING, with the “ate it” gesture on every didn't (my line → chorus): I went by bus → I didn't go by bus · She took a taxi → She didn't take a taxi · They travelled by plane → They didn't travel by plane. 8 pairs, I speed up; we sit down after the drill.
  3. Ex.2 p.100, the first two together, then in pairs: In 1910, children didn't use scooters as transport… Key: didn't travel · didn't go · didn't have · didn't see · didn't do.
  4. Language note on the board: yesterday · last weekend · last year · a month ago · a hundred years ago — these words tell you: past!
📺 On screen: SB p.100 · the board: the anchor + a list of past time expressions (stay up)
👦 Students do: they work out the rule, drill the transformations in chorus, rewrite 1910 in pairs. Expected: after 8 choral pairs the form sticks. Typical error: “didn't went” — the “ate it” gesture, chorus.

20–27′
Try it: the truth about myself + contractions
Try it · about myself + contractions
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.3 (the truth about myself: +/− with 5 verbs) → a micro-drill of contractions SB p.117

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.3 p.100 solo, 4 min, support on the board: I walked / I didn't walk to school this morning. Five items (walk · travel · play · go · get up).
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYBODY writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift them all together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. Checking in pairs: they read to each other, the partner listens for the form. I encourage the explanation (TB p.113): I didn't walk to school. I came by bus.
  4. Contractions (SB p.117): did not → didn't · was not → wasn't · does not → doesn't · have not → haven't. In chorus, we write them on mini-boards — the apostrophe where the letter O used to be.
  5. +1 to the team for clean apostrophes.
📺 On screen: SB p.100 ex.3 · SB p.117 · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they write 5 true sentences, read them to a partner, drill the contractions. Expected: 4–5 out of 5 correct forms. Typical error: “did'nt” — I show where the O sits in did not.

WHAT WE DO: Quiz-Quiz-Trade: a game drill: everybody has “yesterday evening” on a card; the detective asks questions from a support sheet, the suspect answers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.6 p.100, first in writing, 2 min: Answer the six questions so they're TRUE for you. — this is the statement.
  2. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the unit's printed cards (handout GIA1-QQT: the question on top, the answer on the back after folding; no printout — the children write their own cards in 2 min).
  3. Commands: Stand up, hand up, pair up! Quiz your partner — praise — TRADE cards — find a new partner! 3–4 swaps, EVERYBODY asks at the same time.
  4. ▶ Alibi in pairs: the detective has a sheet with ready-made starters — Did you …? · What did you …? · Where did you …? · When did you …? Asks 4 questions, the suspect answers with a short answer + one detail.
  5. They swap roles (90 sec), then swap partners — everybody talks at the same time.
  6. Final: 2 volunteers in front of the class, the class asks one question each (+1 for a correctly built question).
📺 On screen: “yesterday evening” cards · a detective's support sheet for everybody · timer
👦 Students do: they take turns questioning and answering from supports — everybody's engaged. Expected: laughter, questions get built faster and faster. Typical error: they forget did — the partner points to the strip-diagram.

WHAT WE DO: writing in a portion: 5 sentences about the way to school from a support with collocations + reading to a partner

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can make past simple negatives (didn't + infinitive) and ask and answer Did-questions with short answers (Did you watch TV yesterday? — Yes, I did.), and can use 8+ transport collocations to describe a journey.
  • Subsidiary. The negative is introduced from the text about scooters (already heard); choral drills and the “alibi” game from supports; Wh-questions step by step; contractions SB p.117; collocations — by icons and a journey chain; Listening step by step with the subskill “choosing the best summary” and a pause after each answer.
  • Personal. This is the FIRST big topic “past questions” — don't rush it. We set the anchor DIDN'T eats the past once and keep it for the whole unit. Everything through supports and chorus, in writing — in short portions.

Structure
Scooters didn't become popular until later. · In 1910, children didn't go to school by bus. · Did you watch TV yesterday? — Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. · How did you come to school today?
Form
didn't (= did not) + the INFINITIVE (not -ed, not went!) · Did + who + infinitive? · Wh- + did + who + infinitive? · short answer: Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.
Pron. watch
did you → /ˈdɪdʒə/ · contractions didn't · wasn't · haven't · doesn't (SB p.117, audio 75).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He didn't wanted — is that OK?” → No: DIDN'T ate the past, WANT is left.
“Did you go? — Yes, I went?” → No: Yes, I DID.
“I go on foot — how do you say it about yesterday, negative?” → I didn't go on foot.

Assumptions: Past simple affirmative (regular + irregular) was introduced in U7; past negatives and questions haven't come up yet — this is the pair's main new topic. Transport set 1 is active after P1.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“didn't went / didn't wanted”The anchor picture: DIDN'T 🦖 eats the past + the “ate it” gesture. Chorus after every fix, no shame.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
