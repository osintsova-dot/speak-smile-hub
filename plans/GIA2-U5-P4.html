🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The vocabulary of places in a town is introduced with a real stake: you have twelve minutes of line to someone in another city, and your questions decide whether you go there or not. The unit's subskill (predicting vocabulary) becomes the condition for winning: the crew that wrote out the expected words in advance hears the recording twice as well. Is there…? and How much/many come up in every one of Mia's lines, but they are NOT announced as the topic — that is P5's job.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Connection in three minutes. Mia is moving to Buenos Aires for a year. She has one call with her cousin Ethan and twelve minutes of line. Sports, food, transport, music — what do you ask FIRST? Here's the trick every scout knows: before the call, you write down the words you expect to hear. Do that, and you'll catch everything. Skip it, and the call is noise. Your crew writes the question list, then we listen to what Mia actually asked. Line's open.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Mia has twelve minutes of line to her cousin in Buenos Aires — she is moving there for a year. Sports, food, transport, music: what do you ask first? The scout's trick: before the call write down the words you expect to hear — then you hear everything.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: the two word builders (… station / … centre) + a duel “guess the place from the definition” (ex.4), 8 sec per turn

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: before listening we write out the words we expect for each of the four topics

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. The TB tip: What TYPE of information will you need? A number? A kind of transport? Underline it.

WB Unit 5 · points on the leaderboard
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can name 12 places in a town and build compound names (… station / … centre) and listen for specific information by predicting vocabulary first — order of topics + 8 note gaps + 6 detail questions.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the map of 12 places; ex.2 “where you go for what”; ex.3 the two word builders; ex.4 describing a place from a definition; the subskill predicting vocabulary (ex.5); track 33 in three passes (ex.6, 7, 8); ex.9 the pros and cons of their own town.
  • Personal. I do NOT announce the question forms as the topic — they are “the scout's tool”. I collect “Are there a library?” errors silently for P5.
By the end, students can…
  • name 12 places in a town
  • build compound names with station and centre
  • predict the vocabulary of a listening before hearing it
  • note down 8 details from an informal conversation

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
are bus stop and bus station the same thing? → no (TB note): a stop is the small one on a route, a station is the big one where journeys start and end.
Where do you go if your wallet is stolen? → police station. To fill up the car? → petrol station. If you're ill? → medical centre.
Why write the words out BEFORE listening? → so you recognise them in the stream; if you expect a number, you hear the number.

Assumptions: SB p.65, KTP pair 41. Some of the words are familiar (museum, library); the new ones are juice bar, music venue, cycle lane, bike station, department store, underground station.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The map of 12 pictures is “too easy”I set a 3-minute timer and the spelling rule: recognising is easy, writing is not.
The audio goes past them: “I didn't understand anything”ex.5 is a compulsory step BEFORE listening: we write out the expected words for each topic. Without the list I don't press play.
They shout answers out from their seatsAll answers go on mini-whiteboards, “Boards up!” all at once — nobody hides and nobody solos.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
