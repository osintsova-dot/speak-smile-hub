🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Two real-life things in one pair: the phrases you need to ask for and give directions, and their first description of a place. For newcomers the dialogue is built through shadowing the video (repeating after a voice takes the embarrassment away) and a walk over the map with a finger, and the writing goes up a strict ladder: four paragraphs → notes → the text on a model → a checklist. The adjectives are given with pictures before the writing.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today you learn the phrases that work in any city in the world: Excuse me, can you tell me how to get to the library? Go straight ahead. Turn left. Go past the skate park. It's on your right. First we watch Archie do it — twice — then we walk the map ourselves. And then your own favourite place, on paper: four paragraphs, with a model in front of you. Step by step.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: the phrases you need to ask for and give directions in any city in the world: we watch Archie do it, then we walk the map ourselves. After that — a description of your own favourite place: four paragraphs, with the model in front of you.

Mini-broadcast · pairs with the map
pairs → stage

WHAT WE DO: Skills boost: we write our own dialogue on the model → rehearse it → volunteers to the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: You're outside the school. Choose ONE place — a close one. (TB tip: give the weaker ones the nearer places).
  2. PREPARE 5 min: the pairs write a dialogue on the ex.5 model, with the phrase cards on the desks.
  3. PRACTISE as Kagan RallyCoach: one reads the directions, the other is the coach: they trace the map with a finger and say where they ended up. Wrong place — together they find which phrase misled them and repair that line. Then they swap roles and run it again.
  4. PERFORM: volunteers to the board. The audience ticks the Key phrases they hear (ex.7 peer review): Follow the route on the map. Are the directions clear?
  5. The constraint is a soft one: if you forget a phrase, take the card — that is fine.
📺 On screen: Key phrases cards · the map · the phrase checklist
👦 Students do: they write first and speak after — the fear is gone. Expect: with a card in hand everyone speaks

WHAT WE DO: the Jayden text (ex.2–3) with support + the adjectives with pictures (ex.4–6)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB warmer: Look at the photos on page 68. What can you see? Use There is/are.
  2. ex.2–3 paragraph by paragraph. TB key ex.3: 1 his cousins live there · 2 the old part is full of interesting shops and cafés · 3 the shop has 20+ flavours of ice cream · 4 to the beach · 5 play volleyball and swim · 6 a beautiful and exciting place.
  3. ex.4 we find the ten adjectives (TB key: favourite, small, modern, busy, old, interesting, quiet, crowded, beautiful, exciting) — and copy them onto cards with pictures.
  4. ex.5 the definitions (TB key: 1 modern · 2 busy · 3 crowded · 4 quiet · 5 exciting).
  5. A run through the cards as Kagan Flashcard Star: I demonstrate the round myself on three cards, then in pairs — the coach shows the picture, the partner names the adjective. Right — the card leaves the pile; wrong — the coach prompts and the card goes back to the bottom. The pile melts before their eyes, and that is the main motivator.
  6. The TB rule goes on the poster: Adjective BEFORE the noun (an exciting place), but AFTER 'be' (The place is exciting).
  7. ex.6 in pairs (TB key: 1 busy · 2 beautiful · 3 modern · 4 small · 5 quiet · 6 exciting).

PRODUCT · my favourite place
solo

WHAT WE DO: the ladder: choose a place → notes on the 6 questions → 4 paragraphs on the model → checklist → swap

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1 (THINK): Your favourite place. Six questions on the screen — NOTES only. (why you go there · big or small · what places there are · what you do · what people eat there · why you like it).
  2. Step 2 (PREPARE): we lay the notes out across the four paragraphs — the plan is on the slide, the Jayden model is next to it.
  3. Step 3 (WRITE): Start: 'My favourite place to visit is … . I usually go there because …' At least three adjectives and two 'there is/are'. The word bank is on the screen.
  4. Step 4 (CHECK, ex.7): place vocabulary? there is/are correct? adjectives in the right place?
  5. Step 5 (ex.8 peer review): swap with a partner — underline anything unclear, write one comment at the bottom, then explain it out loud.
  6. The descriptions go on the unit wall!
📺 On screen: the 6 questions · the paragraph plan · the model · the checklist · word bank
👦 Students do: they write up the ladder with supports. Expect: everyone has four paragraphs; peer review catches the adjective order

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask for and give directions with 6 Key phrases on a map and write a description of a place in 4 paragraphs with there is/are and at least three adjectives.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 prepositions of place on the map; the Archie video (ex.2–4) with a finger-walk; ex.5 complete the dialogue; Skills boost; the Jayden text (ex.2–3); the adjectives subskill (ex.4–6); peer review against the checklist.
  • Personal. We build the dialogue in writing BEFORE playing it. Only volunteers come to the board, and only with a finished dialogue. The writing happens in class with my support; at home they write it out neatly.
By the end, students can…
  • ask the way with two Key phrases
  • give directions with four Key phrases
  • follow a route on a map
  • write a description of a place in 4 paragraphs

Structure
Excuse me. Can you tell me how to get to the library? · Is there a library near here? · Go straight ahead. · Turn left. · Go past the skate park. · It's on your right.
Form
prepositions of place: opposite · next to · between … and … · on the corner of … and … · near · the adjective goes BEFORE the noun (a beautiful place) but AFTER be (The place is beautiful).
Pron. watch
Excuse me sounds like one word /ɪkˈskjuːz mi/ · say it slowly and clearly, stressing LEFT / RIGHT (TB observation)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Go past to the skate park” — what is the extra word? → to.
“Is there a library near here?” — why Is? → a library is one thing (TB hint).
“a shop beautiful” — correct it. → a beautiful shop.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
