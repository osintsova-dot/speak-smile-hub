WHAT WE DO: everyone assembles a portfolio page: 2–3 best pieces of work with fact-captions + the year's I-can checklist

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Task: Pick 2–3 works. Caption each: This is my… I'm proud of it because…
  2. The year's I-can checklist (asset): tick honestly; tick means you CAN, not you HEARD about it.
  3. I go round with «archival» help: for anyone without materials — classroom notebooks/project photos.
  4. 💪 Support: caption frames on a card. ⭐ Fast: a 4th exhibit «work that was hard — and worked out».
📺 On screen: work brought in · portfolio templates · I-can checklists
👦 Students do: Students curate their own year — a review through selection, everyone in their own material. Expected: they get absorbed in old notebooks with «argh, is that how I used to write?!» — that IS visible growth. Typical error: a caption with no because — we add it.

26–36′
«Before/after» in a carousel of circles
Before/after · Inside-Outside Circle
two circles

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Inside-Outside Circle: «before/after» sentences (couldn't → now I can) in a carousel — everyone tells their growth to 3 new listeners

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame: In September I couldn't ___ . Now I can ___ . Everyone writes 3 pairs of sentences (from the I-can sheet), a neighbour checks the forms (couldn't/can + infinitive).
  2. ▶ Kagan · Inside-Outside Circle: Two circles, face a partner. Say your before/after lines. Partner answers: ‘That's growth!’ Outside — rotate! 3 rotations, then SWITCH the circles' roles.
  3. I listen to the carousel, collect the strongest lines on the board — the class's wall of growth.
📺 On screen: frame on the board · I-can sheets · two circles
👦 Students do: Students formulate their growth grammatically precisely and repeat it three times to new people — the personal «I've grown» is fixed by speaking it, everyone talking at once. Expected: the carousel is warm. Typical error: couldn't + -ing — the frame fixes it.

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Timed Pair Share: talk rehearsals — the speaker talks for 90 sec against the timer, the coach listens and gives 2 checkmarks; 2 runs × 2 roles

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Timed Pair Share: Speaker — your full 90 seconds, coach LISTENS silently with the timer and two ticks (eye contact · shows the work). Time up → 30-sec feedback → SWAP roles.
  2. Run 2 with an upgrade: Fix ONE thing your coach named.
  3. I listen to the quiet ones first: their talk needs to happen with me present.
  4. 2–3 volunteers — for the class (applause, no judging: no points today).
📺 On screen: timers · checkbox cards
👦 Students do: Students run twice with precise feedback; the quiet ones get a teacher's shoulder. Expected: almost everyone holds 90 sec. Typical error: eyes on the card — the eye-contact checkbox does its job.

WHAT WE DO: a writing product: an invitation to parents for Showcase Day using the frame + a neighbour's check + my final check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame: Dear Mum and Dad, please come to our SHOWCASE DAY on [date] at [time]. You will see my English year: my talk, our video and our fair. I want you to be proud. Love, …
  2. Written on nice blanks; at the bottom — 1 line in Russian (date-time for parents).
  3. A neighbour's checklist: Dear? date+time? please come? a signature? → corrections → my final sign-off.
  4. Packaging: fold it, address it to the parents' name.

👦 Students do: Students write a real text for a real addressee — the year's writing subskill at live work. Expected: they try harder than ever: the addressee is Mum. Typical error: forgetting the time — the checklist catches it.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners have a portfolio page and an 8–10 sentence «My year in English» talk (In September I… → Now I can… → Next year I want to…), rehearsed against the timer.
  • Subsidiary. Selecting the best work = a meaningful review of the year; «before/after» sentences drill past vs present against the students' own lives; invitations to parents are written and signed; the Showcase Day plan is laid out, MCs are chosen.
  • Personal. The season's most personal lesson: this isn't a competition, it's pride. The leaderboard is paused; we compare everyone WITH their September self. Special attention to the quiet ones: their growth is often the biggest.
By the end, students can…
  • choose and caption their best work
  • say before/after sentences about their skills
  • deliver a 60–90 sec growth talk
  • write a short parent invitation in English

Target language
Lexis
In September I…Now I can…My favourite unit was… because…Next year I want to…I'm proud of… · My best work is…I couldn't… — now I can…Dear Mum and Dad… · Please come to…
Structure
In September I couldn't say a full sentence. Now I can order food, talk about my week and make a video in English. My best work is my Unit 7 comic. Next year I want to speak faster.
Form
growth = couldn't → can (+ past simple for September, present for now); the talk is 8–10 sentences: start → 3 «now I can» → best work + why → next year; the invitation = Dear… + date/time + Please come + a signature.
Pron. watch
couldn't /ˈkʊdnt/ (the l is silent!) · proud /praʊd/ · pride intonation — falling at the end of statements, not a question tag.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Is My year a report on grades?» → No: a story of GROWTH. What I couldn't — what I can — what I'm proud of.
«Is Best work the prettiest page?» → The one where growth or effort shows; a blot with progress beats a clean copy without it.
«Should the invitation be duplicated in Russian?» → Yes: 1 line in Russian at the bottom for parents — but the English part is the main one.

Assumptions: The best work has been brought in (homework from P7); for anyone who forgot — classroom notebooks to choose from. Showcase Day dates are agreed with the timetable in advance (pair 69).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«I don't have anything good from this year»I sit down next to them for 2 min, we flick through the notebook together — I find the growth as a fact: «the September page vs the April page. See it?»
Strong students' boasting hurts the weaker onesThe rule «compare with YOURSELF only»; every frame phrase is first-person about your own growth.
The talk balloons into an essayA limit of 8–10 sentences and a 90-sec timer; a keywords card, not a full text.
An invitation with errors goes homeA double check: a neighbour against the checklist (Dear? date? please come? a signature?) → my final look before it's packaged.
Parents won't be able to comeA second option in the invitation: «or watch the video version» — on Showcase Day we'll film everything on a phone; no child is left without an audience.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
