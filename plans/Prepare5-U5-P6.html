🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Pull back and use everything the first half-year owns — the vocabulary of Units 1–10 and the grammar from determiners and the past tenses to modals, the perfect, comparatives, futures and conditionals — and then produce it once, alone, on paper, to a scale that is known in advance.
What is genuinely hard
Not the individual items. What is hard about a half-year paper is the DISTANCE: Unit 1 was in September and has not been touched since October. The second hard thing is the arithmetic — a teenager who does not know what the paper is worth spends the first ten minutes managing fear instead of answering questions.
Typical error to expect
The four that come back on every paper of this half-year: the dated present perfect (yesterday I have bought), mustn't used for don't have to, will after if, and the double comparative (more taller). All four are on or around this paper, and all four are the clinic at the end of the lesson.
Why the lesson is built this way
Three decisions, and all three are arguable, so here is the reasoning. First: the scale goes on the board before the papers are handed out, and the bottom of it is a retake rather than a two. When the rules are known in advance a paper stops being a threat and becomes a task. Second: the paper is the official Cambridge Term 1 Standard, whole — eighty self-checkable marks plus the email. It covers Units 1–7 and stops there; the publisher's Term 2 paper was rejected because it opens on Units 8–14 and is full of language nobody has seen. The gap is covered honestly elsewhere and said aloud: Unit 8 sat in the block 4 test, Units 9–10 sat in the block 5 test LAST LESSON, and station 3 today re-airs them anyway. Nothing home-made is stapled onto a Cambridge paper. Third: they mark it themselves, here, and leave with three lines instead of a number. A mark handed back in January teaches nothing; an error log written eight minutes after the paper teaches for a term.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Half a year. Ten units. Today we close it. Twenty-six minutes: everything comes back. Then one official Cambridge paper — eighty marks plus one email, forty-five minutes. The scale is on the board now, before you start, and it does not move: seventy-two is a five, forty-four is a three, and under that is not a two — it is a retake in January on your own sections. Then we mark it here, together, and you leave with three lines instead of a number.”

door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, three tenses from every student in ninety seconds, and the bridge into the half-year

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what are you doing this evening? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — ten units in one round. On the board, three prompts: One thing you have never done. One thing you did last weekend. One thing you'll be doing on the first of January.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share, ninety seconds each way. Every student produces three different tenses before the lesson has properly started.
  4. Take three answers to the room, one per prompt, and check the form of each in public. Any dated perfect goes straight onto the briefing board — it will be in the clinic at the end.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the day: Half a year. Ten units. Today we close it — and the first thing you get is the scale.
📺 On screen: the three prompts on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: produce three tenses each in pairs, then hear three checked in public

Lesson frame + the scale
board
Why: The one stage that decides how the other eighty-two minutes feel. Every student spends the revision knowing exactly what the paper is worth and what happens if it goes badly.

WHAT WE DO: the scale goes up before anything else happens

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Put the scale on the board without a word, before anybody asks. It is printed in full in the specification table below this procedure — copy it exactly.
  2. The scale goes on the board BEFORE the papers are handed out, and it does not move. Say it in these words: Eighty marks. Seventy-two and up is a five. Sixty is a four. Forty-four is a three. Under forty-four is not a two in the register — it is a retake in January, on your own sections only. You know all of that before you start, which means nothing today is a surprise. Three things take the fear out of a paper and all three happen before it begins: the scale is visible, the format is familiar, and the bottom of the scale is a second chance rather than a verdict.
  3. Name the email separately, once: Section eleven is an email. It has no key, so I mark it — you get it back in January with comments. It is not in your eighty.
  4. Then the shape of the lesson in twenty seconds: Three short revision stations. A briefing. A break. Forty-five minutes of paper. Then we mark it here, in this room, together.
  5. Set the bar for the revision half: Every answer comes with the reason. «Past simple» is half an answer. «Past simple because of yesterday» is an answer.
📺 On screen: the scale written in marker on the board — deliberately in chalk and marker rather than in anything that can be switched off, because it has to stay visible for the next eighty-two minutes
👦 Students do: read the scale before any explanation. Expect the first question to be about the bottom line, and that is good — the answer removes half the anxiety in the room

teams
Why: Station 1 · the words of the half-year

WHAT WE DO: Review 1 and Review 2 vocabulary in one run: Units 1–8, spoken then written

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four categories on the board, one per team: SHOPPING AND PEOPLE (Units 1–2) · WEATHER AND MAKING THINGS (Units 4–5) · HEALTH (Unit 6) · MUSIC AND BUILDINGS (Units 7–8).
  2. Ninety seconds per team, spoken: as many words as you can, one person writing. Then rotate the categories and repeat — two rounds, four minutes total.
  3. Then the written check, ninety seconds: everybody writes the five words their team said that they could not spell. Not the easy ones — the ones they avoided.
  4. Take the five hardest from the room and write them correctly on the board: they stay there through the paper, because the paper spells four of them.
  5. Both review spreads are open on the desk (SB p.30 and p.52) as reference, not as exercises.
📺 On screen: the four categories and then the five hardest spellings on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: produce forty words as a team in four minutes, then confront the five they cannot spell

15–22′
Station 2 · the grammar of the half-year
individual → pairs
Why: Station 2 · the grammar of the half-year

WHAT WE DO: every tense since September, and the reason for each one

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Eight sentences on the board, one per structure, each with a gap: determiners · past simple · past continuous · present perfect · modal of obligation · comparative · future form · conditional.
  2. Four minutes, silent, individually: complete all eight AND write the reason in four words next to each.
  3. Check with Kagan Showdown — everybody reveals at once, and the REASON is read out before the form.
  4. Three minutes of repair on the two that went wrong most. Draw them rather than explain them: the line and the cross for the two past tenses, the two boxes for mustn't against don't have to.
  5. Say the honest sentence about section 7 of the paper: One section mixes past simple, past continuous and present perfect in eight items. It is the hardest thing on the paper and you have just done it.
📺 On screen: the eight gapped sentences on the board, filled and reasoned by the class — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: produce eight forms with eight reasons, reveal simultaneously, then watch two repairs drawn rather than explained

22–26′
Station 3 · Units 8, 9 and 10 — the units this paper does not test
pairs, standing
Why: The three units the paper does not reach get four honest minutes, so nobody leaves for the holidays believing that the last two units did not count.

WHAT WE DO: four minutes on the three units that are covered by the block tests instead

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say why this station exists, in one sentence: This paper stops at Unit seven. Units eight, nine and ten were your last two tests. We air them anyway, because they are still yours.
  2. Everybody stands, in pairs, four rounds of sixty seconds. Round 1: compare two buildings you know (Unit 8). Round 2: what will you be doing at this time tomorrow (Unit 9)? Round 3: finish If people don't change, … (Unit 10). Round 4: one strong adjective about this term, with the right adverb.
  3. New partner every round. No feedback between rounds — this is circulation, not correction.
  4. Sit down on the fourth answer. The transition is the briefing.
📺 On screen: the four round-prompts written on the board before they stand — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everybody speaks four times to four different partners in four minutes

Why: Test briefing

WHAT WE DO: the sections named, the listening moment named, and the four errors named

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the eleven sections in twenty seconds, using the specification table in this plan. Do not describe them — name them, so the shape is known.
  2. Name the listening moment explicitly: Twenty minutes in, everybody stops and we do the listening together, twice. Don't start it on your own — you can't, it's my recording.

⚡ The half-year paper
exam rows, silent
Why: ⚡ The half-year paper

WHAT WE DO: the official Cambridge Term 1 Standard: 80 keyed marks plus the email, 45 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silence, and you sit down. Do not walk the rows for the first ten minutes.
  2. At minute 20 of the paper (that is minute 51 of the lesson): the listening. Stop everybody, read the task-9 rubric aloud, play the recording, pause thirty seconds, play it a second time. Then let them go back to whatever they were doing.
  3. Answer only procedural questions. Never a linguistic one, even the easy ones — especially the easy ones.
  4. Write the remaining time on the board at 20 minutes, at 10 and at 5. Say nothing else.
  5. At minute 35 of the paper, one sentence: If you haven't started section eleven, start it now. The email is a hundred words, and it is the only part I mark.
  6. At the end: pens down, papers stay on the desk face up. They are NOT collected — the next stage is self-marking.
📺 On screen: the finish time and three time-checks on the board; the mp3 cued and tested before the lesson. Backup: the full audioscript is printed in the answer key PDF and you read it twice
👦 Students do: work silently for 45 minutes, stop together for the listening, and keep their own paper at the end

Self-check + the three-line error log
individual
Why: Self-check + the three-line error log

WHAT WE DO: they mark their own eighty, convert on the scale, and write three lines

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out one copy of the answer key per student, face down. Then the instruction, before anybody turns it over: You mark your own. A cross is information, not a failure. Nobody says a number out loud, including me.
  2. Four minutes: mark sections 1–10 and 12A. Total out of eighty at the top of the page. Section eleven, the email, is left alone — that one is mine.
  3. Ninety seconds: convert on the scale on the board and write the grade next to the total. This is the moment the scale pays for itself.
  4. Then the log, and it is the point of the whole lesson: Three lines. Not five, not twenty. The instruction is exactly this: Look at your crosses. Find the three mistakes that came back more than once. Write three lines — what I get wrong, and next to it the correct version in my own words. The log is the thing that goes in the notebook and comes back out in January; the number does not.
  5. Collect the papers AND the emails at the end of the stage. The error logs stay in their notebooks — they are the holiday homework.
📺 On screen: the scale still on the board from minute five — chalk and marker, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: mark their own eighty marks against a published key, convert on a known scale, and write three personal lines

84–88′
Error clinic · four errors, four minutes
board, whole class
Why: Error clinic · four errors, four minutes

WHAT WE DO: the four sentences from the briefing, corrected in public and anonymously

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The four wrong sentences are still on the board from the briefing. One minute each, and nobody in this room is named.
  2. For each one: the class says what is wrong, one student writes the correct version, and you say the reason in one line. Yesterday is finished time. Mustn't is a prohibition, don't have to is a free choice. No will after if. One comparison per adjective.
  3. Ask for a show of hands on each: Who had this one on their paper? Hands go up, and that is the whole point — it is a room of people with the same four errors, not a room of individuals with private failures.
  4. Do not add a fifth. Four is what a person remembers.
📺 On screen: the four errors and their corrections side by side on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: correct four errors in public with their hands up, having just found the same errors on their own papers

WHAT WE DO: the half-year closes on what they can do, and two lines of holiday work

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Close on the list, not on the marks, and say it as a list because teenagers underestimate a half-year: Since September: shopping and friendship, the weather, making and mending, health, music, architecture, technology and endangered animals. Ten units. Two past tenses, the perfect, modals, comparatives, four futures and three conditionals. That is a lot of English.

  4. Then the last sentence of the term, and it is worth saying properly: The emails come back in January with comments. Have a good break.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students have completed the official half-year paper under exam conditions, marked their own eighty keyed marks against the key, and written a three-line personal error log.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The paper is treated as a verdict rather than a taskThe scale goes up first, before anything else happens, and the bottom line is said out loud in these words: under forty-four is a retake in January, not a two. Half the anxiety in the room disappears in one sentence.
«Why isn't Unit ten on it?»Answer it in the briefing, not when it is asked mid-paper: this is the publisher's Term 1 paper, it covers Units 1–7, and Units 8, 9 and 10 were tested in the two block tests. Nothing is missing from the year.
The listening is played at the wrong momentDecide the moment in advance and announce it: the recording is played twenty minutes into the paper, twice, and everybody stops writing for it. Write PLAY AT :20 on your own copy of the plan.
Self-marking turns into self-punishmentThe instruction is a job, not a judgement: count the crosses, find the three that repeat, write three lines. Nobody reads anybody else's paper and nobody says a number out loud.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: The whole written paper. A half-year paper is the first time this group meets an exam that does not warn them which section is coming.

The transferable thing today is not a language point, it is the mechanics: a scale known in advance, a fixed time, a self-check against a key, and three lines of personal error log. Russian candidates lose marks in June for exactly the habits this lesson builds — starting without reading the rubric, not checking, and not knowing what the paper is worth.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
