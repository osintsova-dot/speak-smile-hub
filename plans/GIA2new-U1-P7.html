🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): This pair does double duty — the Unit-1 project and the Unit-1 review — so the review is not a dry test but the fuel for the project. First a mixed retrieval quiz (all four Unit-1 strands at once, not one at a time — interleaving), then the project uses exactly that language: subjects, present simple, times. New students get a model timetable first, a grid to fill, a speaking frame, and a 3-line rubric they see before they start, so “present a project” never means “stand up with nothing”. The Showdown has self-check, not a public leaderboard.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“You are the head teacher for one day. You design the school: which subjects, how many times a week, what time it starts, how long the breaks are — and you say WHY. First a quick warm-up to wake up everything we learned this unit, then you build your timetable in threes, and at the end you give a two-minute tour of your school. Your school, your rules, your reasons.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: this lesson wraps up Unit 1 — we revise the words and grammar through a quiz, then use them to design and present our own ideal school timetable, and each of us fills the Match It / Translate It page to see our score out of 12.

WHAT WE DO: a Showdown quiz that mixes ALL Unit-1 strands together — routines, subjects, present simple aff/neg, Yes/No questions, object pronouns — with self-check, no public leaderboard

STEP 1 THINK (SB p.22)
Choose subjects · give reasons
solo → crews

WHAT WE DO: which subjects matter most and WHY — critical thinking on a sentence frame

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Solo first (2 min): from the subject bank, tick your top six subjects and cross out two you would drop.
  2. Speak the reason on the frame (write it on the board): I think ___ is important because ___ . We don't need ___ every day because ___ .
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share (40 sec each): defend two choices to your partner. Partner reacts with a Key phrase — I think that's a good thing. / Really? I'd prefer… / Me too.
  4. Crews of three agree on one shared shortlist — that becomes the school's subjects.
📺 On screen: the subject bank + the “because” frame + reaction phrases (Backup: bank and frame on the board)
👦 Students do: turn opinions into full reasoned sentences. Expect: the “because” half is where the real English happens — insist on it

STEP 2 PLAN — the timetable grid
Build the week
crews of 3

WHAT WE DO: fill the week grid (Project planner p.118) with times, subjects and breaks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the blank grid (days across the top, times down the side). Model one row aloud: Monday: school starts at nine. First, maths. Then a break at eleven.
  2. Give three decisions each crew must make and be able to justify: What time does school start and finish? How long is the break? Which subject is every day — and which is only once?
  3. Roles inside the crew (rotate so nobody hides): Timekeeper (fills times), Subject chief (fills subjects), Reasons scribe (notes one “because” per choice).
  4. Monitor: check present simple 3rd-person and times as you pass. 💪 Support: pre-filled Monday for crews who need a start.
📺 On screen: the grid template + a filled sample row (Backup: draw the empty grid on the board once, they copy)
👦 Students do: every member owns a column of the grid — nobody watches. Expect: arguing over “PE every day” — that IS the critical thinking

WHAT WE DO: turn the grid into a poster and rehearse the two-minute tour from notes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Crews copy the grid onto A3 as a clean timetable poster, add the school's name and one “because” line under it.
  2. Give the presentation frame on the board — three sentences each speaker must include: Welcome to our school. School starts at ___ and finishes at ___ . We have ___ because ___ . We don't have ___ .
  3. Split the tour: each of the three speaks one part (start/finish · subjects · reasons). Rehearse once quietly — Kagan RallyRobin in the crew, notes allowed, no reading word-for-word.
  4. ⭐ Stretch: add a Yes/No question to the audience — Do you like our timetable?
📺 On screen: the presentation frame + a 10-minute timer (Backup: frame on the board)
👦 Students do: build the product and split the talking three ways. Expect: the frame means even a shy new student has an exact line to say

STEP 4 PRESENT + peer review
Gallery · rubric scoring
stage / gallery

WHAT WE DO: each crew gives its two-minute timetable tour; the audience scores by the 3-line rubric and asks one question

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Crews present in turn (poster up, speak from notes). Keep it to two minutes with the visible timer.
  2. Audience task (everyone active): each listener fills a tiny peer-review slip — the 3 rubric lines (grid · present simple · reason) with 🟢/🟡/🔴, plus writes one question using Do/Does. After each tour, take two questions from the floor: Does your school have homework? Do students wear a uniform?
  3. Model warm feedback once: One star — a clear reason. One wish — say the finish time. New-students line: praise before wish, always.
  4. Collect a class “best idea” for each rubric line — not a winner, a highlight per criterion.
📺 On screen: the rubric + a 2-minute timer per crew (Backup: rubric on the board, slips on paper)
👦 Students do: present from notes and score peers by clear criteria. Expect: the peer slip keeps the audience busy — no “I'm bored while others talk”

📓 Interactive notebook — Unit 1 · Translation: Match It! + Translate It! (2-up A4, one A4 = 2 pupils) — the two sheets go in as one review block.
12 sentences pulling the whole unit together: daily routine · present simple in all three forms · school subjects · object pronouns · the checking phrases.
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the 12 Russian sentences to English A–L in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page; write the English from memory, without looking back (start now, finish at home).
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.

In class: Match It fully + begin Translate It. At home: finish Translate It and self-check.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can design a week timetable and give a short spoken tour of it using present simple + reasons with “because”, and consolidate all of Unit 1 (routines, subjects, present simple aff/neg, Yes/No questions, object pronouns) through mixed retrieval.
  • Subsidiary. Interleaved review quiz (Quick review p.21) across all four strands at once; STEP 1 THINK — critical thinking / logical decisions with reasons; STEP 2 PLAN — the grid; STEP 3 CREATE + rehearse; STEP 4 PRESENT with peer review by rubric; Match It / Translate It self-scored.
  • Personal. Model the finished timetable and show the rubric BEFORE they start. Keep the quiz self-checked — no public leaderboard for the new line. Insist on the “because” half of every sentence. Praise before wish in all peer feedback.
By the end, students can…
  • build a week timetable grid with times, subjects and breaks
  • give a 2-minute spoken tour using present simple + “because”
  • ask & answer Do/Does questions about a timetable
  • self-score the Unit-1 translation out of 12

Structure
School starts at 9 and finishes at 3. · We have art three times a week. · We don't have homework. · Do you study Latin? — No, we don't. · I think maths is important because … · I meet my friends and play with them.
Form
Present simple: he/she/it + verb-s (spelling +s/+es/-ies · have→has); neg don't/doesn't + base verb. Yes/No: Do/Does + subject + base verb → Yes, we do. / No, it doesn't. Object pronouns: me · you · him · her · it · us · them (after a verb / preposition).
Pron. watch
3rd-person -s: /s/ starts · /z/ goes · /ɪz/ finishes; connected Do you… /dʒə/; silent letters in some subjects (from SB p.116) — e.g. the gn cluster in design.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“She go or she goes?” → goes — she/he/it adds -s.
“After DOES, do we add -s to the verb?” → no: does already carries it → base verb.
“I play football with my friends → with THEY or THEM?” → them (object pronoun).
“Is a good project just fun subjects?” → no: every choice needs a reason with because.

Assumptions: SB pp.22–23 project + Quick review p.21; Unit-1 pair 7 (syllabus pair 11). Starter language (be, have got) is known and gets woven into the school theme. Project planner is on SB p.118. This is the review + project pair; the test is P8.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for crew rehearsals.
«Showdown!» — mini-boards written face down, all flip together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-project.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
