🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): With new students you CAN announce grammar as the topic — they need clarity, not camouflage. But it is delivered through discovery: they build the line and the position rule out of sentences they already know from the P2 text. After that — many short written runs with a self-check against a key: confidence grows out of “I got it right”, not out of an explanation.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Six little words. Always, usually, often, sometimes, hardly ever, never. They tell people HOW OFTEN — and English puts them in a strange place: after 'is', but before all other verbs. Today you get the full map. And the second half: love, like, don't mind, don't like, hate — and the -ing that always follows them. By the end you write three sentences about yourself, and they'll all be correct.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: six frequency words + the attitude line love → hate and the -ing that follows them. We find the rule ourselves in the sentences about Brighton, and then we write three correct sentences about ourselves.

WHAT WE DO: four sentences from the P2 text → the “often → rarely” line (ex.1) → percentages to hold on to

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Sentence cards from the blog: she usually does homework · she always practises · she is sometimes nervous · she hardly ever has free time.
  2. Put them on a line: most often on the left, least often on the right. TB key ex.1: 1 always · 2 usually · 3 sometimes · 4 hardly ever.
  3. Percentages onto the poster (the TB suggests it): always 100% · usually 80% · often 70% · sometimes 50% · hardly ever · never 0%.
  4. Chorus with a gesture (hand up — often, hand down — rarely). First I show the gesture for each word myself, then Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols: I say the word — the class silently shows the height of the hand, and only then we say it in chorus. always — hand above the head, never — hand to the floor.

PRODUCT · three sentences about myself

WHAT WE DO: 3 sentences about yourself (frequency + attitude) → self-check against the checklist → read it to a partner → volunteers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame: I usually … at the weekend. I hardly ever … . I love … -ing, but I don't like … -ing.
  2. Self-check against a 3-point checklist: is the adverb in the right place? -ing after like/love/hate? third person -s if it is about a friend?
  3. We read to a partner through Kagan Timed Pair Share: first I read MY three sentences as the model, then A speaks for 60 seconds (B only listens and at the end says “I liked that you…”), bell — B speaks for 60 seconds. The sheet in your hand is allowed.
  4. Then 3–4 volunteers read to the class — also with the sheet in hand.
📺 On screen: frame · checklist
👦 Students do: they go through the full cycle: wrote it → checked it → read it. Expect: 3 correct sentences from everybody

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can place adverbs of frequency correctly (after be, before other verbs) and use love/like/don't mind/don't like/hate + -ing or noun, checked by themselves against keys.
  • Subsidiary. Guided discovery from the P2 text (ex.1–2); ex.3 rewriting with a prompt sheet; ex.4–5 the attitude line; ex.6 the sentence builder; ex.7 the text about Carissa Yip; a mini-test with self-check.
  • Personal. The pace is half that of the “ours” line — and that is normal. Every stage ends with a self-check against the key: the error is found privately, before anyone sees it.
By the end, students can…
  • put the six adverbs in the right order (always → never)
  • place the adverb correctly with be and other verbs
  • use like/love/hate + -ing and + noun
  • write three correct sentences about myself

Structure
We're never late for class. · I'm sometimes nervous before exams. · We usually watch TV in the evenings. · My brother hardly ever does sports. · I love skateboarding. / I love sports. · He likes practising the piano. / He likes music. · I don't mind drawing. / I don't mind art. · We don't like playing chess. · They hate watching TV.
Form
with the verb be the adverb goes AFTER the verb: She is sometimes nervous. · with all other verbs — BEFORE the verb: She always practises in the skate park. · after love/like/don't mind/don't like/hate comes either a noun or a verb with -ing; don't mind = «it's OK with me», love/hate = strong feelings
Pron. watch
often — both /ˈɒfən/ and /ˈɒftən/ are fine · hardly ever runs together, no pause
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«She plays often chess» — what's wrong? → often goes BEFORE the main verb: She often plays chess.
«He always is late» — and here? → with be it's the other way round, AFTER: He is always late.
«I like play football» — correct it. → I like playing football: after like comes -ing.

Assumptions: SB p.28, KTP pair 15. For some of them the position of the adverb and the -ing after like really is new; I build it on sentences from the text they have read.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They have heard the rule but do not apply itI do not explain first — they build the line out of examples and formulate the rule themselves; I confirm it and put the poster up.
Fear of making mistakes in writingEvery written run has a self-check against the key, before anyone else sees it.
They mix up like + -ing and like + nounI give it as “two legal routes” on the poster: I love sports / I love skateboarding. The only illegal one is I love skateboard.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
