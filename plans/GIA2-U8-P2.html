🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The blog about the VR holiday is read not “because it was set” but as a commercial file: the agency from Pair 1 decides whether to take VR tours into its catalogue. The unit subskill (identifying facts and opinions) becomes a working tool — you don't sign a contract on an opinion. The main trap of the lesson comes straight from the TB: sentence 7 looks like a fact (“VR glasses will get cheaper”), but it is a prediction wrapped in “I'm sure that…” — so it is an opinion. The Critical thinking section of the book turns into the “risks” part of the final verdict.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Your travel agency got an offer. A company wants to sell Virtual Reality holidays through you. Climb Everest before lunch. Swim with dolphins while the person next to you visits a waterfall. No passport, no sun cream — four hundred dollars for the glasses. The blog says it's amazing. But you don't sign contracts on the word ‘amazing’. Read it. Sort every line: FACT or OPINION. Then give me your verdict in sixty seconds — do we sell VR holidays or not? Two facts minimum, or no deal.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the agency has an offer on the table: selling VR holidays. The blog praises them, but nobody signs a contract on the word “amazing”. The crew reads the blog, sorts the claims into facts and opinions and gives a 60-second verdict: do we take it or not? Two facts minimum, or no deal.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 predicting from the photos → ex.3 checking against the introduction → ex.4 the best summary (track 49) → ex.5 five true/false claims with a line of evidence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  6. Run ex.5 in pairs as Kagan RallyCoach: the first says True/False and reads the evidence line out loud, the second works as the coach — finger on the text, accepting an answer only with its line — then they swap. No line, no point — coach, do your job.

🔥 Challenge · the opinion trap
crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.6: seven statements — fact or opinion, 8 seconds per turn, the signal word must be named

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Seven statements. Fact box or opinion box — and NAME the word that gave it away. Eight seconds.
  2. Key, TB ex.6: Fact — 2, 3, 5, 6 · Opinion — 1, 4, 7.
  3. Take 7 apart separately (TB note): 'VR glasses will get cheaper.' It looks like a fact. But can you CHECK the future? No. And the writer says 'I'm sure' — that's his opinion, and he gives no evidence.
  4. The constraint: No signal word named — the point burns.
  5. Turns are handed in through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: in eight seconds the crew agrees on the answer AND on the signal word, and you call a number to answer. Number fours — which box, and which word gave it away? Counting on “let the strong one say it” does not work: you call the number.
  6. The TB Extra activity as a finisher: Read the comments again. Lucien writes about food, Ryan asks about money. Write ONE more comment for this blog.
📺 On screen: SB p.98 ex.6 · 8-second timer · the two boxes FACT/OPINION
👦 Students do: they sort the statements with signals. Expect: number 7 splits the class — the best argument of the pair

WHAT WE DO: a level for our students: the TB Culture note as expert data + a list of risks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Give each crew a card with the Culture note (TB p. 112): VR is a three-dimensional computer environment; you need a headset or gloves; it is used not only in games but also to train fighter pilots and surgeons. PRODUCT · the VR verdict
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second verdict: do we take VR tours or not + two facts from the text + one risk + the decision

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 6 minutes to prepare: Do we sell VR holidays? Two FACTS from the text — with the line. One risk. One decision sentence.
  2. The rule: If your evidence is an opinion, the contract is void.
  3. Sixty seconds per crew. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness.
  4. Cross-examination: You said it's cheap. Fact or opinion? Show me the line.
  5. The finish — Critical thinking 4: Hands up: who would actually go on a Virtual Reality holiday? (a class vote, as the TB asks for).

👦 Students do: they perform and defend under cross-examination. Expect: the hottest part is when a crew is caught using an opinion instead of a fact

WB Unit 8 · the Word work cards stay in the classroom
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can tell facts from opinions in a travel text (and name the signal word), use 6 new words in context, and deliver a 60-second verdict with two facts and one risk.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the three Holiday facts as a warm-up; ex.2–3 predicting from the photos; ex.4 (track 49) choosing the summary; ex.5 true/false with evidence from the text; the subskill facts vs opinions (ex.6); Word work parachute · waterfall · nightmare · passport · book · climb (ex.7–8); Critical thinking 1–5.
  • Personal. The reading speed of the strong ones drowns out the weaker ones — hold the rule “an answer only counts with a line of evidence”. Log will errors silently: tomorrow that is the topic of Pair 3.
By the end, students can…
  • say whether a sentence is a fact or an opinion and name the signal word
  • prove a true/false answer with a line from the text
  • use six new words (parachute, waterfall, nightmare, passport, book, climb)
  • give a 60-second verdict with two facts and one risk

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“VR glasses will get cheaper” — is that a fact? → no: it is a prediction, and the author says “I'm sure” himself (TB note).
Is “book” a book here? → no, it is a verb: to reserve. Take a look before you BOOK.
“climb” — how does it sound? → /klaɪm/, the b is not pronounced.

Assumptions: SB pp.98–99, syllabus pair 63. They read confidently; the new discipline is telling the checkable from the personal. All 6 Word work items are in the official Wordlist.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They skim and then argue “I'm right”The evidence-line rule: an argument is settled only by a line from the text, with a finger on that line.
Anything that sounds confident is taken for a factWork through trap 7 out loud with the TB note: the future cannot be checked, so it is an opinion, however confident it sounds.
The verdict turns into “cool / not cool”The verdict rubric: 2 facts from the text + 1 risk from Critical thinking 3 + the decision in one sentence.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
