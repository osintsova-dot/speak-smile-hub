🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The eight action verbs are not served as a list but through the “Save our wildlife!” leaflet, where every verb already sits in a real sentence. The podcast about Mikaila Ulmer gives a human story — new students need to see that a teenager really did this. The listening is broken into steps: first underline the key words, then listen, then check. The product is modest and doable: three actions for the month, on a frame.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Eight verbs. That's all you need to talk about helping. Donate. Join. Organise. Protect. Raise. Solve. Start. Visit. Look at this leaflet — every verb is already in a real sentence. Then we listen to a girl of thirteen who sells lemonade all over America and gives twenty per cent of the money to bees. And at the end, each of you writes three things YOU could do this month. Three lines, with the word bank on the screen.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: eight action verbs are all you need to talk about helping. We work through the leaflet, listen to Mikaila's story and write three things each of us could actually do this month.

WB Unit 6 · the word bank stays on the wall
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use 8 taking-action verbs with the right nouns and write and say three actions they could do this month.
  • Subsidiary. The ex.1 leaflet as context; ex.2 matching with a self-check; the identifying true/false subskill step by step (ex.3–4); numbers by ear ex.5; ex.6 discussion with a frame.
  • Personal. Three listenings, not two (that is what new students need): #1 the general picture, #2 the true statements, #3 the details. Before each one, underline the key words. Keep the script to hand as the last resort.
By the end, students can…
  • match 8 verbs to the right nouns
  • underline key words before listening
  • say why a statement is false
  • write three actions with verb + noun

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“raise a campaign” — is that possible? → no: raise awareness / raise money.
“join a museum” — and this one? → visit a museum. Join is for a club or an organisation.
You heard a familiar word — does that make the statement true? → no, check the whole sentence (the TB rule).

Assumptions: SB p.77, syllabus pair 49. The eight verbs are partly familiar; the difficulty is the collocation. The TB asks you to pre-teach wildlife, entrepreneur and volunteer.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
24 collocations at once will not fitGive the eight baskets one at a time, with a gesture for every verb; recognition first, naming second. Only the collocations from Quick review go into the test.
Listening frightens themThree listenings instead of two + underline the key words before each one + the script as the last resort. No “from memory” questions.
The product turns into fantasyThe rule: three things you could really do this month. Check with the question: Can you do it before May?
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
