Week 05.04–11.04 · Lesson 2✨ Focus · +clap · dance · listen · sing · What are you doing?~8 pupils · circle + tablesPH 28 (/juː/ ue ew u-e · p.77–78) · Genki: What Are You Doing?(58)
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, children will be better able to name four MORE music activities — clap · dance · listen · sing — and ask & answer: “What are you doing? — I'm playing the piano! / I'm singing!”
  • Subsidiary. Mime the four new activities; sing the language song «Listen, clap, dance!» (CD4 T5) with actions; play Guess what I'm doing! & Genki Karuta (31); reprise Genki «What Are You Doing?» (58); Phonics Hero — the /juː/ spellings ue ew u-e, blend & speed-read p.77–78; listen & find the new words (PB p.79–80).
  • Personal. Model What are you doing? many times with the puppet before children say it. Keep speaking short & choral first, then paired — no long waiting. Praise every mime named.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name four new activities (clap · dance · listen · sing);
  • ask & answer “What are you doing? — I'm playing the piano!”;
  • say “I'm singing!” with the action.

Structure

Form
Question chunk What are you doing? → answer I'm playing the + instrument / I'm + action-ing. Present continuous as a fixed chunk.
Pron. watch
rising What are you doing? ↗; the -ing ending sung, not dropped; I'm playing linked.
Phonics
Phonics Hero 2.0 — the long /juː/ sound written ue · ew · u-e. Jolly action → blend (b-l-ue → blue, n-ew → new, c-u-be → cube) → speed-read p.77–78.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I'm singing — do I use my voice or my hands?” → my voice!
“I ask What are you doing? — do you tell me your name or your action?” → my action!

Assumptions: The six instruments from L1 are fresh (checked in the warm review) and re-used. The four activities (clap · dance · listen · sing) are new; the question What are you doing? is understood receptively — producing it is today's stretch. Reading continues on /juː/.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Confuses clap / dance / listen / singOne big clear action each: hands together (clap), spin (dance), hand to ear (listen), open mouth (sing) — action first, word second.
Says only the action, not the sentencePuppet models the frame I'm… SINGING!; child echoes with the action.
Question too long to sayChunk it: What are you (shrug) + doing? — build in two claps.
Same partner every timeMusic on = walk, music off = new partner (Mix-Pair).
Waiting = wrigglingEveryone mimes & answers chorally, then pairs — nobody just watches.
Classroom management — signals
“Class? — Yes!” — you: “Class!” → pupils stop & echo “Yes!”
“WiFi” 🖐 — say “WiFi” + raise a spread hand → pupils silently mirror & tune in.
Volume control — raise/lower your arm = louder/quieter → settler.
“Give me five” — eyes · ears · mouth · hands · feet.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1… pencils down.”
“Freeze!” — statue, mid-movement reset.

9–17′
Phonics Hero 🔤 ⚡
carpet, moving

① Recognise / review. Phonics Hero lesson 28 · long /juː/ — sounds ue · ew · u-e  (all say /juː/). 🦝 Speaky shows each with its Jolly action (gesture technique); children do the action and NAME the sound — they already know all 26. We don't drill bare sounds: teacher voices, children gesture.

② Blend / build. Build the word: b-l-ue → blue, n-ew → new, c-u-be → cube — sweep the sounds together + a picture. ▶ Sound hop — children hop to the /juː/ spelling Speaky calls (ue / ew / u-e), then blend a word with it.

③ Speed reading. open Phonics Hero 2.0 (our workbook) p.77–78 — finger under each word, read the row FAST as a group (whole words, playful, celebrate speed — real reading from zero, GMF1-style).

Tricky words: read could · would together as whole words (we don't sound tricky words out).

Curiosity input · four activities + What are you doing?

Now you ask. A confident child mimes; the class asks What are you doing? — the child answers.
