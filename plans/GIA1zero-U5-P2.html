WHAT WE DO: Subskill step by step: we write the words we expect → fast reading ex.4 → we check our guesses

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Subskill box, out loud and simply (TB p.72): Before you read again, write words you EXPECT to see: car, kitchen, piano, parts. Then look for them — you'll read much faster.
  2. Everyone writes 4 expected words (90 sec), we compare 3–4 lists out loud.
  3. Ex.4: Read quickly — two minutes — and check your guesses from exercise 3. Key: Photo A: walk, run, climb · Photo B: cook, clean · Photo C: walk, dance, climb, play the piano · Photo D: walk, carry.
  4. Whoever guessed the most gets a point for the team. I point out: Predicting made you faster. That's the skill, not luck.
📺 On screen: SB pp.62–63 · board: 4 expected words (examples)
👦 Students do: write a prediction, then read fast and check. Expected: 2–3 hits each — the subskill becomes tangible. Typical error: they write generic words (robot, good) — I ask for something specific.

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: ex.6 in a game wrapper: 6 statements, the false ones must be CORRECTED and the line pointed to

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rule: True — say true. False — correct it AND point at the line. Only corrected sentences get a point.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. Teams, 4 minutes. Key: 1 True (para 1) · 2 False — it has only got arms (para 2) · 3 False — it cooks AND cleans the kitchen (para 2) · 4 False — Lindsey works in a museum (para 3) · 5 False — most robots cost thousands (para 4) · 6 True (para 4).
  4. Check round the chain of teams; disputed ones — we read the line out loud together.
  5. Fast finishers (TB p.72): invent two more types of robot and what they do.
📺 On screen: SB p.62 ex.6 · team scoreboard
👦 Students do: look for the lines, correct out loud. Expected: 4–6 out of 6; «Moley has a body» gets a happy catch. Typical error: «False» with no correction — I don't give a point, they go and find the line.

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.7–8 step by step: meaning from context → part of speech → transfer into new sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  5. Ex.8 on mini-boards, by volley: I show the gap — everyone holds up the word. Key: 1 pet · 2 stairs · 3 hungry · 4 costs · 5 customers · 6 guide.
  6. Pronunciation: guide — /ɡaɪd/. Chorus ×2.

WHAT WE DO: Quiz-Quiz-Trade: two short consolidation games: ▶ a Memory game on abilities + a can sentence chain

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can find specific information in a web page and say what each of 5 robots can and can't do in full sentences from a frame.
  • Subsidiary. The subskill “reading for specific information” is given step by step (first we predict words, then we search); the 6 Word work words are introduced through context; Critical thinking works through sorting cards and voting, not a long discussion.
  • Personal. The text is long — we don't read it whole-class from the front. Each team is responsible for its OWN robot and reports to the rest: everyone reads a little, everyone ends up knowing everything.
By the end, students can…
  • predict words before reading and find them in the text
  • complete a table with each robot's abilities
  • correct false sentences using the text
  • use six new words in new sentences

Pron. watch
guide /ɡaɪd/ · robot — stress on the FIRST syllable /ˈrəʊbɒt/ · stairs /steəz/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Nybble costs $200 — is cost a thing or an action?” → an action, a verb.
“A guide — a machine or a person's job?” → a job: helps visitors.
“Moley — a robot with legs?” → no, only arms.

Assumptions: Texts of this length are new for them: they need chunking and supports. can/can't is familiar after Pair 1 (from a model). The topic of robots motivates by itself.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Scared by the length of the textI show it physically: «your chunk is this paragraph, 5 lines». Not «read the page».
They're tempted to translate every wordRule: we look for the WORD from our own list, the rest doesn't need to be understood (Subskill p.62).
They guess True/FalseA point only for correcting the false ones + pointing at the line.
Word work: they reach for a translatorTB p.72: first we read the sentence AROUND the word, guess, then check.
Discussion drifts into RussianFormat — cards in columns + one English phrase from a support, not free discussion.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
