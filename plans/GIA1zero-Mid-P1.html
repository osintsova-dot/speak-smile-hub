🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): For a zero-level group, the mid-year test is the first big one. The pair's main goal is to lift the fear: we show the whole format in advance, the mock is in pairs and unmarked, supports are legal. “You've already done all of this in the units” is the day's refrain.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“September-you knew ZERO. December-you? Look at this map!”

🎯 Switchboard · which helper?
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: a switchboard by Kagan Numbered Heads Together: 8 questions across 4 helper columns; anchor gestures; a table on the board for good

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 4 columns with gestures: is/are (palms as scales) · have got (hugging a backpack) · do/does (running on the spot — routine!) · there is (pointing at the desk).
  2. By Numbered Heads Together: numbers 1–3 in the team; Heads together! — they sort 8 cards into the columns with an example as support; at check time I call a number — THAT student explains one card for the team. 4 min.
  3. A check in chorus WITH GESTURES: we read the question — we show the column's gesture.
  4. The anchor stays on the board for the whole lesson: “Lost — look at the columns”.
📺 On screen: question cards · a 4-column anchor table with examples
👦 Students do: they sort by helper verb with support and gestures — anti-mush #1 for a zero-level group. Expected: does causes the most argument — that's normal. Typical error: Do she like — the column gesture + the -s tail on does.

WHAT WE DO: 5 stations of 6 min with rotation and GENEROUS support: Starter games · the U1 map · the U2 family monster · the U3 café · the U4 my day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. S-Starter: name spelling + a number-grab (cards) + 2 commands to your trio. S-U1: the map: Where is he from? — He's from Spain. He's Spanish (support: a table of countries). S-U2: the monster card: describe it from the frame It's got… eyes. Its hair is… S-U3: the café: sort the menu into C/UC + an ordering dialogue from the frame Can I have some…? Is there any…? 47–60′
Grammar in games: three shells
⚡ Grammar games · three shells
teams

WHAT WE DO: a grammar review in games: “stand-up-sit-down be” → Family truth by Kagan Timed RoundRobin → “spy the present simple mistake”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Stand up if it's TRUE (be): I'm eleven! Our classroom is pink! — true = stand up; along the way I fix the forms in chorus.
  2. ▶ Family truth by Kagan Timed RoundRobin: round the circle in the team, 20 sec each, nobody skipped — 1 truth about the family from the frame: Clockwise, twenty seconds each — I've got a brother. He's got brown hair. The team responds: Same! / Different!
  3. ▶ Spy mistake (present simple): I read out sentences — where's the mistake, clap: He get up at seven (CLAP!) — he getS up! 6 rounds, traps for -s and don't/doesn't.
  4. We fix every caught mistake IN CHORUS — the course's ritual.
📺 On screen: frames on the board · my list of spy sentences
👦 Students do: they play three grammar games with their whole body and in chorus — review with no desks. Expected: the spy claps are loved; the class catches -s. Typical error: hasn't got/doesn't have get mixed up — I point at the anchor columns.

Mock in pairs · the format with no fear
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: getting to know the test format: I show ALL 5 parts on screen → a mini-mock IN PAIRS with support, a self-check against the key

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Showing the whole format: 5 parts on slides — Part 1 words… Part 2 grammar… Part 5 — a message about YOU. Look: you did ALL of this in units! For every part — “where we did this” (the Quick review pages).
  2. A mini-mock IN PAIRS (1–2 items from every part): consulting each other is ALLOWED, the anchor supports on the board are in play.
  3. A self-check against the key slide; I do NOT collect the score: This number is YOURS only.
  4. Every pair writes their “revise by Tuesday” list: 2 items between them.
📺 On screen: format slides · mini-mocks · a key slide (Backup: the format talked through and written on the board, the key read out)
👦 Students do: they see the whole format in advance, try it in a pair with nothing at stake, find their own 2 items. Expected: “oh, it's just like in the unit!” — the fear drops before your eyes. Typical error: panic over the Writing part — I show the support frame that WILL be in the test (the standard variant).

🏁 Build & Share · the “deluxe survival sheet”
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: a legal product: teams make the half-year's “deluxe survival sheet” (1 page: anchors, tricky words, frames) — and present it in 30 sec

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Task: One page: your team's SURVIVAL SHEET for the test. What goes on it: the 4 helper columns · tricky words (Wednesday! forty!) · the message frame (Hi… I've got… I like… Bye!) · the past/to time anchor.
  2. The teams gather it from THEIR OWN notebooks and the board anchors (copying it out = revising — that's the whole point!).
  3. A 30-sec presentation: Our top tip for the test is…
  4. Finale: “You can't bring the survival sheet into the test. But it's already in your head — you wrote it YOURSELF.”
📺 On screen: A4 sheets · markers · their notebooks
👦 Students do: they gather the half-year's personal anchors onto one page — a structured retrieval; they present a tip. Expected: they take the sheets home to prepare — perfect. Typical error: copying from a neighbour — “from YOUR OWN notebook: your hand = your memory”.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners have recycled Starter–U4 language through supported stations and grammar games, tried a no-marks mini-mock in pairs and know exactly what the Mid-year test looks like.
  • Subsidiary. 5 vocabulary stations with support; a grammar review of be/have got/some-any/there is/present simple in game shells; a “helper switchboard” with an anchor; a mini-mock IN PAIRS with no marks = getting to know the format; confidence before the test.
  • Personal. For a zero-level group, the mid-year test is the first big one. The pair's main goal is to lift the fear: we show the whole format in advance, the mock is in pairs and unmarked, supports are legal. “You've already done all of this in the units” is the day's refrain.
By the end, students can…
  • recall the five blocks' vocabulary with supports
  • sort questions by helper verb (is/got/do/does)
  • work through a mock in pairs calmly
  • name their personal “revise by Tuesday” list

Form
be (all forms); have got (all forms); possessive 's; countable/uncountable + some/any/a lot of; there is/there are; Present simple (aff/neg/questions + 3rd person -s) — with the helper-switchboard anchor on the board for the whole lesson.
Pron. watch
the half-year's targets on review: nationalities · half /hɑːf/ · Wednesday · -es /ɪz/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Have you got…? — do we need do?” → No: a got question manages on its own; do/does are for routine verbs.
“Is today's mock for marks?” → No! In pairs, with support — we're just getting to know the format.
“Forgot a word at a station — what do you do?” → Flip the support over: peeking and solving is better than getting stuck.

Assumptions: All the Starter–U4 language has been introduced and drilled unit by unit; the risks: a mush of helper verbs (is/got/does) and panic over the “big test”. The station format is loved and familiar. Course rule #1 (said out loud EVERY Starter pair): mistakes are how we learn — here nobody laughs at mistakes, here we grow from them. The teacher makes mistakes on purpose and fixes them out loud.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
