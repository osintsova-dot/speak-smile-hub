WHAT WE DO: the predicting subskill as bets: 6 loud claims — written T/F BEFORE reading, slips face down

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I put up 6 claims: 26 people live in one house · they've got three cars · each child has got a bedroom · they eat at ONE big table · the house is quiet · the grandparents' kids' names all start with «B».
  2. I say: Commit: T or F, in writing, NOW. Pro readers PREDICT before they read — that's the subskill. A bet without commitment is worthless. 2 min.
  3. Slips face down. (Key for myself: T/F/F/F/F/T.)
📺 On screen: the 6-claims slide · betting slips (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they commit their guesses in writing. Expected: 3–4 get caught out on “three cars” and “one big table”. Typical error: peeking — “Closed books! Betting is the point!”

🔥 Challenge · timed fact raid
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: a race through ex.3 (7 facts in 4 min) → ex.4 T/F with a two-move correction → we open the bets with a source

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Raid ex.3: Seven facts, four minutes — RAID! Short answers: Montana · house · grandparents · four, 16 · small · bus · six. +1 per fact.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everyone writes AT THE SAME TIME, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.
  3. Ex.4 on mini-boards: T/F in 10 sec → we fix the false ones with two moves: They haven't got a car — they've got a BUS! (TB key: F·T·F·T·F·F).
  4. We open the betting slips: Verify WITH the source: False! Paragraph 4 says… +1 for a correction with a source.
  5. I quietly collect have got slips for the P3 clinic.
📺 On screen: the SB p.27 · mini-boards · timer · betting slips
👦 Students do: they raid the text at speed, fix the false claims with have got negatives, check their bets against the source paragraph. Expected: “My bet was wrong — no car, a BUS!”. Typical error: a fact with no source — no point given.

A duel on 6 words from the text
Word work duel
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: Numbered Heads Together: partners split the 6 definitions of ex.5 in half and drill each other on the 5-second rule; a sentence about their own family

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Roles: Partner A owns definitions 1–3, B owns 4–6. Find your bold words — two minutes.
  2. I run the check as Kagan Numbered Heads Together: numbers 1–4 in the crew, Heads together — make sure EVERYONE in your crew can answer! → I call a number — that exact person answers, no prompting from the floor.
  3. Duel: Five-second rule! Sad because you aren't with people? — Lonely! Use or have with another person? — Share!
  4. Final: each pair — a sentence about THEIR OWN family/class with any word; the crew's best one out loud (+1 richness).
  5. Key ex.5: lonely · together · unusual · share · reality show · fun.
📺 On screen: the SB p.26 ex.5 · timer
👦 Students do: they own their trio of words, drill their partner, claim the words for themselves. Expected: “I share a room with my brother”. Typical error: lonely = alone — CCQ: “Alone = by yourself. Lonely = sad about being alone.”

🚀 Bonus level · interview a Putman
pairs

WHAT WE DO: role-play: one is a Putman child, the other a journalist with 3 “Have you ever…?” questions (GMF4 structures)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I raise the status: Beginners ask: ‘Have you got a bedroom?’ You can dig into his whole LIFE — not in this book.
  2. Frames: Have you ever (been alone in the house / driven the school bus / eaten dinner alone)? — Yes, I have. / No, never!
  3. Role-play: A = a Putman child (text facts + imagination), B = a journalist, 3 questions. Swap.
  4. The class's best question → +2 to the leaderboard.
📺 On screen: board: the Have you ever…? frames · the text p.27 alongside
👦 Students do: they interrogate “the Putman” with the perfect, in character. Expected: “Have you ever been alone? — NEVER. Twenty-six people!” — humour from the facts. Typical error: “Did you ever” — I accept it, praise both versions.

🏁 PRODUCT · buy or pass debates
trio circles

WHAT WE DO: trios: the “buyer” against the “skeptic” (90 sec each), the judge waits for 3 sourced facts; we rotate roles 3 times; a final board meeting

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Roles and cards: Buyer (Buy it! 26 people — drama every day, text says…) · Skeptic (Pass! It's just dinner at six…) · Judge (card: ☐ 3 facts? ☐ answered the opponent? verdict).
  2. Rules: 90 sec per side + 30 sec verdict; facts ONLY from the text/bank; an invented fact = a point to the opponent.
  3. I rotate the roles ×3.
  4. Final — the board: Final board vote: BUY or PASS? Thumbs! And the Evaluate question: the ideal number of people in a family — shout your number + one reason.
📺 On screen: role cards · judging cards · the argument bank
👦 Students do: they debate by the rules, judge by the card, vote at the end. Expected: quotes like “paragraph 5 says they eat together!”. Typical error: emotion instead of facts — the judge doesn't tick the box.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses ≤3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — the form is clean (1–3) · Richness — adjectives, details, bonus structures (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		role cards · judge cards

WB Unit 2 (pp. 12–19) · points to the leaderboard next pair
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can verify bold claims against a TV-guide article and argue a «buy or pass» verdict supported by 3 sourced facts.
  • Subsidiary. Reading subskill (predicting) turned into betting; fact raid at speed; Word work as a duel; Critical thinking Understand→Apply→Evaluate = argument bank + board debate; GMF4 bonus: interview a Putman kid with Have you ever…?
  • Personal. Reading is where continuers actually stretch — the text is new even if have got isn't. Keep hunts timed and competitive; verdicts get the full judging bar. Never say ‘we're reading a text’ — say ‘we're fact-checking a show’.
By the end, students can…
  • commit predictions and verify them with evidence
  • extract 7 facts against the clock
  • define/use 6 Word-work items
  • argue buy/pass with facts and win a board vote

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Analysts read every word?” → No — they scan for THE fact.
“A verdict without a source — does it survive the board?” → Never.
“Have you ever…? — which tense, grads?” → Present perfect. Not in this book. In you — yes.

Assumptions: have got is fluent orally; the Putman content and Word work are genuinely new. Debate needs frames + judge cards. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
