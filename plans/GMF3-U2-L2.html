Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to talk about how often people do chores: “My (dad) always (cooks dinner). / My (sister) never (washes up).” and ask & answer “Does your (mum) (wash up)? — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.”
  • Subsidiary. Understand Ravi & Josh's dialogue about who does the chores (CD1 32); discover the rule — with he/she/it the verb changes (cooks, washes) & we use does / doesn't in the question & short answer (Grammar clue); recycle the 12 chores from L1; write true sentences about the family (AB follow-up).
  • Personal. Drill the frequency ladder with height gestures (always = highest, never = lowest); insist on the -s for he/she; keep the survey strictly timed; model the full short answer, recast bare “yes/no”.
By the end, pupils can…
  • place always/often/sometimes/never before the verb correctly;
  • add -s for he/she/it: “My dad cooks dinner.”;
  • ask & answer “Does your …? — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.”

Target language
Lexis

Structure
My (dad) always (cooks dinner). / My (sister) never (washes up).  ·  Does your (mum) (wash up)? — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
Form
Adverb goes before the verb: My dad always cooks. He/she/it → verb + -s (cooks, washes, tidies). Question/short answer use does / doesn't (not the -s again): Does she cook? — Yes, she does.
Pron. watch
third-person endings: cooks /s/, washes /ɪz/, tidies /z/; weak does she /dəʃi/; frequency stress — AL-ways, NE-ver.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“My sister ALWAYS washes up — every time or never?” → every time!
“Does she cook? — do I say Yes, she cooks or Yes, she does?” → Yes, she does.

Assumptions: 12 chores are fresh from L1 (checked in warm-up). Always/never met before as words; the new focus is word order (adverb before verb) + the he/she -s and does-support. Pupils can read the dialogue speech bubbles & the grammar table.

Anticipated problems & solutions

-s dropped (“My dad cook dinner”)Magnet -s on the board that “jumps” onto the verb for he/she; beat drill: dad — cook — S.
Adverb after the verb (“cooks always”)Sentence-strip build: [My dad][always][cooks][dinner] — pupils physically order the strips; adverb-strip is a bright colour.
Double marking (“Does she cooks?”)Rule card: after does, the verb loses its -s; cross it out with a gesture.

19–27′
Tic-Tac-Toe · Does he/she…?
desks, 2 teams

▶ Noughts & Crosses — chores. 3×3 grid of chore pictures. To claim a cell, the team ASKS the other team — Does your mum cook dinner? — and the other team answers in chorus with a frequency word: Yes, she does. She always cooks dinner! Both sides speak every turn. Rotate the asker.

🎮 Projector/board option: draw the 3×3 grid on the whiteboard; a rotating child taps the winning cell as the WHOLE team says the question aloud.

ICQ: Do you say “cooks” or “cook” after “does”? (→ cook!)

📓 Interactive notebook — GMF3_Unit2_Lesson2_How_Often_A5
A5 glue-in grammar map — FREQUENCY POWER bar (always · often · sometimes · never) + a “THE -S MOVES!” panel (She washes up → Does she wash up? → She doesn't wash up) + spelling rules cook→cooks, wash→washes, tidy→tidies
  • Glue the map facing the L1 chore trail — chores on the left, grammar on the right.
  • Class reads the FREQUENCY POWER bar top-to-bottom with a hand falling: always — often — sometimes — never.
  • Everyone traces the -s in washes and then draws an arrow to the -s in Does: DOES carries the -s. The verb goes back to base!
  • The map stays open during Tic-Tac-Toe; a square is only won if the -s is in exactly one place.
When: ~4 min at the start of the 19–27′ “Tic-Tac-Toe · Does he/she…?” stage (desks, 2 teams)Prep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Glue sticks + one coloured pencil each (for the -s arrow).

27–37′
Family frequency survey
mingle → desks

Model first. Show YOUR family: My dad often cooks dinner. My sister never makes her bed. Height-gesture each adverb.

▶ Talk Partners info-gap (act. 4-style). Each child has a family grid (mum · dad · brother · sister) + chore cards. Music → mingle; off → pair: A Does your dad wash the car? B Yes, he does. He sometimes washes the car. Tick the grid, TRADE, new partner. 5–6 exchanges.

✔ Assess: circulate with the class list — who keeps the -s AND uses does in the question? Recast, note names.

💪 Support: question stems + adverb ladder printed on the sheet. ⭐ Extension: add: How often? — Every day!
