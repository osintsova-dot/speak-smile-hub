🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): For new students the mission is simple and about themselves: learn to name jobs in English and talk about a dream job — who that person is, what they do, where they work, why me. That gives them a safe product (I talk about myself, on a frame, with notes in my hand) and at the same time brings in the 25 words of the set. The superheroes in the book are not the task, they are the hook: everybody has a “day job”, and it is easy to guess.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at this page. Bruce Wayne — a businessman. Clark Kent — a journalist. Peter Parker — a photographer. Every hero has a normal job. Today two things: we learn to name jobs in English — and each of you tells us about YOUR dream job, six lines: what it is, what the person does, where they work, why you. At the end you speak with your notes in your hand. This is not a test. Let's start with MY dream job…” (and I show the model straight away)
The gist, if you need to restate it: every superhero has an ordinary job — that is where we start. We learn to name jobs in English and build a story about a dream job: six lines — what it is, what the person does, where they work, why me. At the end we speak with our notes in our hands.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 25 jobs, say what each person does (A nurse looks after sick people) and describe a dream job in 6 lines.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 “Who am I?” with support; ex.3 (track 44) the superheroes' day jobs; ex.4 who does what; ex.5 categories; Job suffixes and the article a/an; Video skills (Susie Wolff) with a task set before watching.
  • Personal. Nobody is asked to speak without a draft. Every speaking task comes AFTER they have written it down. The word bank stays on screen all lesson.
By the end, students can…
  • say and write 25 job words
  • say what a person in each job does
  • use a/an with a job (She's a nurse. He's an engineer.)
  • describe my dream job in 6 sentences

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“She's lawyer” — what is missing? → the article: She's A lawyer.
“He's a engineer” — why not? → before a vowel sound, AN engineer.
“teacher” — where is the stress? → on the first syllable, the ending is quiet: TEA-chə.

Assumptions: SB pp.84–85, syllabus pair 54. Some of the words are familiar (teacher, doctor, singer); new ones are accountant, receptionist, sales assistant, web designer, mechanic, engineer, journalist, businessman/businesswoman.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Afraid to speak in front of people they don't knowThe rule of these lessons: first we write, then we read it to a partner, and we come to the board with the notes in our hands.
25 words at once do not fitGive them in portions by the categories of ex.5 (uniform · office · health · restaurant · cars), with a gesture for each; recognition first, naming second.
They ignore the word bank and go silentThe word bank stays on screen all lesson; use the “look up” gesture instead of feeding them the word.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
