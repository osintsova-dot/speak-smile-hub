🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Match people to websites by MEANING, name twelve internet nouns and verbs, and write a short email that answers all three questions and starts every sentence differently.
What is genuinely hard
Two things. In the reading, the site descriptions never repeat the readers' wording, so word-matching fails. In the writing, the third question quietly goes unanswered — and an unanswered question is a lost mark whatever else is on the page.
Typical error to expect
An email that answers two of the three questions. In Writing Part 6 and in OGE task 35 alike, an unanswered point is a lost mark whatever else is on the page [exam report]
Swim in the sea is lovely instead of Swimming in the sea is lovely — the ‑ing form when the verb is the subject of the sentence [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson starts from the sites they actually use, so the six coursebook websites arrive as a comparison rather than as six texts to be understood. The vocabulary stage comes between the two readings on purpose: the twelve words are IN the six descriptions, so they are met in context first and defined second. The gapped Catherine Cook text is the block's second run at the strategy from block 4 — decide the word class first — and a strategy only becomes a habit on the second run. The writing is built from a counting task, not from a model to copy: they count how the descriptions start their sentences, and that is what stops five sentences all beginning «I like».
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Three questions in the email, three answers in your reply. Not two. That is the whole test today, and it is also how every email you will ever write in an exam is marked. Before that: six websites, and one of them is exactly what you have been looking for.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, five verb-pattern gaps named by box, and the bridge into the websites

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last lesson rewind. Five gapped sentences on the board, and the class calls the form: I'm worried about ___ (lose) my phone · I decided ___ (join) the club · ___ (study) alone isn't easy · I can't stop ___ (watch) videos · Thank you for ___ (help) me.

  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the second spread: Books open, page sixty. Six websites. One of them is exactly what you have been looking for and you don't know it yet.

WHAT WE DO: name the language aim of the lesson and the standard an answer has to reach

WHAT WE DO: Vocabulary ex.1 p.61 (track 80): six nouns and six verbs, found in the texts they have just read

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Why: A table with three columns and four rows — the densest listening of the block, and the one that most rewards knowing what kind of answer each row needs.

WHAT WE DO: Listening ex.1–2 p.61 (track 81): three young developers, then a four-row table

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: play track 81 once and match each speaker to what their app helps you do. Key: Speaker 1 — c (play games) · Speaker 2 — a (use your time well) · Speaker 3 — b (learn things).

  5. Where marks go: Where marks go: the prices. 99p and £1.50 are said fast, and «free» is not a number at all — students leave that cell empty waiting for a figure.

🎯 Writing out · your email
solo → pairs
Why: Writing built on a model and a three-question checklist, started in class so nobody takes an empty page home; the peer IMPROVE stage is where the missing third answer gets found.

WHAT WE DO: WRITE and IMPROVE: 25 words or more, three answers, three different openings

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The three rules, written on the board before they start: 1 Answer all three questions. 2 Twenty-five words or more. 3 Three different sentence beginnings.
  2. Begin Dear Sal, and end with your own name — say it, because the book says it and it is a habit that starts carrying marks in two years.
  3. WRITE: 7 minutes on the timer, silent mode.
  4. IMPROVE in pairs, 4 minutes. Kagan RallyRobin: the partner puts a tick in the margin next to 1, 2 and 3 for each question ANSWERED, and circles any two sentences that begin the same way.
  5. Put the two repair sentences on the board while they check: IMPROVE, the two sentences to repair on the board (TB p.118): There is many photos of animals. (There are) · To search for information is easy. (Searching for information is easy) — the second one is this unit's grammar, which is why it is here.
  6. Show the model answer only AFTER the peer check (TB p.118): Model answer, TB p.118: «Dear Sal, My favourite website is DC Comics. It's about Superman and other super heroes. You can get information about the characters and there are games too. I like it because I love Superman! Dan»
  7. Log errors silently; the feedback slot is the start of the next lesson.
📺 On screen: the three rules and the sentence-opening menu on the screen, with the model answer revealed at the end (Backup: all of it in chalk on the board — the three rules in one column, the openings in another, and the model answer written up while the pairs are checking)
👦 Students do: write 25+ words, then check a partner's for three ticks. Expect one email in three to be missing an answer, and the ticking to find it. Typical error: To search for information is easy — last lesson's grammar, which is exactly why that repair sentence is on the board

Structure
There are facts about different animals. · You can upload your own videos onto the site. · This site is all about looking after the Earth. · On this site, you can watch a daily news programme.
Form
the twelve words split into six nouns (the web, message board, link, site, blog, menu) and six verbs (record, search, post, upload, download, save). Sentence openings: There is / There are … · You can … · This … · On this site … · If …
Pron. watch
ˈupload and ˈdownload are stressed on the first syllable as verbs here; menu is /ˈmenjuː/, not /ˈmenu/. And search rhymes with church.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I'm interested in nature and want to learn about ways to help the planet.» Which site? → A. Does the word «nature» appear in the description of A? → no: it says «looking after the Earth». Meaning, not the word.
«You can ___ your music and upload it.» Which verb? → record. Upload comes after, so the gap is what you do first.
«Write 25 words or more.» Do I get a better score for 60 words? → no (TB p.255). But you do lose a mark for an unanswered question.
«To search for information is easy» — what is better? → Searching for information is easy. The verb is the subject, so it takes ‑ing. That is last lesson's grammar.

Assumptions: SB p.60–61, syllabus lesson 31. Verb patterns are one lesson old and come back in the IMPROVE stage. The gapped-text strategy was taught in block 4 on SB p.48, so this is a return rather than an introduction. Most of the twelve internet words exist in Russian as loanwords, which helps meaning and does nothing for form.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
