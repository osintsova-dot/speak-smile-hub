🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The book's WDYT project is turned into a real task: in a month a teenager moves into town who knows nothing here, and the crew makes the map that saves them. The unit's Super skill (considering the needs of other people) works as the condition for handing it in — a map made “only for us” is not accepted. The Quick review is stitched in as stations in the middle, as “checking the map before it goes to print”: revision for the test without the word “test”. The WDYT question opened in P1 is closed here.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Next month someone your age moves here. They don't know where to eat, where to skate, where to go when it's minus thirty and everything is shut. Your crew makes the map that saves them. One rule from the boss: a map made only for YOU gets rejected. Sporty people, broke people, people who hate crowds, people who can't walk far — they all land here too. Then we open the unit question again: what actually makes a town a good place to live in? You've had five lessons to find out. Draw.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: in a month a teenager your age moves here and knows nothing. The crew makes the map that saves them. A map made “only for yourself” is not accepted: sporty people, people with no money, people who hate crowds — they all arrive too. And at the end we come back to the question of the unit: what makes a town a good place to live in?

WHAT WE DO: the project video (Sophia and Liam about Poole) + the TB Model project notes as a layout checklist

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: Would you like to live there? Why? We watch with a task: Write every place they name.
  2. The key from the video: Poole Park with a juice bar and a lake · Baiter Park with a skate park by the sea · The Lighthouse (a cinema and arts centre) for a rainy day · Tower Park — cheap fast food.
  3. The TB follow-up questions through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Where is Poole? Where can you go if it rains? Where's a good place to eat if you don't want to spend much? — the crew agrees one answer, and the number I call gives it. The point: the answer has to be with everyone, not with the fastest one.
  4. The Model project notes go on the board (TB p.81): Title. Main places labelled. Only the main roads and the railway — keep it simple. Icons. Arrows when there's no space. Information boxes with EXTRA useful facts. Short, informal language: 'There's…', '+', 'you'.
  5. What will YOU do differently from Sophia and Liam?
📺 On screen: the Project video on SB p.70 (backup — I read the video script from TB p.142 while the class draws the places on a sketch) · Model project SB p.71
👦 Students do: they take the model apart as a layout, not as “a nice picture”. Expect: they notice the information boxes and take the trick for themselves

18–30′
Design · five categories and different people
crews

WHAT WE DO: STEP 1–2: the diagram in ex.2–3 → choosing the scope (the whole town or one district) → Key phrases → their own diagram in ex.6

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2 off the model map: Add the places to the diagram — five categories. (TB key: places to eat — fast-food restaurants, juice bar · places to do exercise — country park, skate park, sports centre, park · entertainment — cinema, arts centre, museum · shopping — supermarket, department store · other — station).
  2. ex.3 we extend it (sample TB additions): market, food stalls, music venue, tennis courts, hospital, shopping centre, football stadium.
  3. STEP 2 ex.4: The whole town, or one district? Decide in thirty seconds. (the TB tip: in a big town I hand different districts to different crews so they don't duplicate).
  4. The Super skill rule: Name four kinds of people who land here — sporty, broke, creative, someone who can't walk far — and ONE place for each.
  5. Drilling the project Key phrases: Don't forget … · What about somewhere for people who … ? · I think we need something for someone who … · There aren't any places for … · Let's add something about transport.
  6. The planning runs as Kagan Talking Chips: four Key phrase cards each. You speak — the card goes to the middle of the table. Until all four are down, we don't start the second round; whoever has run out of chips listens. That way the quiet ones get into the project and the leaders don't take the map over.
📺 On screen: SB pp.70–71 · Key phrase cards · Project planner / Graphic organiser p.120
👦 Students do: they design the map and share out the roles. Expect: the fourth kind of people is the hardest — and that is exactly where the best ideas come from

The check before print · Quick review as stations
stations

WHAT WE DO: 4 stations of 4 min each: food and the groups · places and the compounds · countable/uncountable + there is/are · Is/Are there + How much/many (SB p.69)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before you print, the map goes through checks. Four stations, four minutes each.
  2. The stations: a blitz over the 24 food words and the seven groups · places + the station/centre word builders · a/an/some/any and there is/isn't/are/aren't · questions and How much/many.
  3. The first station runs as Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: everyone has a “word — group” card, you ask, you answer, you swap cards, you move on to the next person. In 4 minutes each student gets through 8–10 words, not two.
  4. A personal gap list: Write down what YOU missed — that's your test prep list, and it's short.
📺 On screen: SB p.69 Quick review · the station cards · timer
👦 Students do: they go through the stations and log their holes honestly. Expect: the gap lists are all different — that is the point
📓 Interactive notebook — Get_Involved_A2_Unit5_Translation_Match_It_2up_A4 + Get_Involved_A2_Unit5_Translation_Translate_It_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to English A–L (food, countable and uncountable, there is/are, places in a town, How much / How many, directions), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 from memory

  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.

  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the unit's exit line.

Create · the map and the information boxes
crews

WHAT WE DO: STEP 3: we draw the map off the layout checklist + write the boxes (there is/are, How many, adjectives)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Places and labels FIRST. Details later. Twelve minutes — the clock is the boss.
  2. The TB tip: Don't draw every street. Main roads only. Use icons. Use arrows if a place is off the map.
  3. The boxes are compulsory: Each box: what's there, how many, and one adjective. 'There are three cafés here. The best one is cheap and never crowded.'
  4. Ideas for the boxes (the TB questions): Are there places worth naming outside the map? What exactly can you do at each place? Which places are good in bad weather?
📺 On screen: poster paper/a tablet · markers · the layout checklist
👦 Students do: they draw and write at the same time, in their roles. Expect: without a hard timer the drawing eats everything — the timer decides it

PRODUCT · the survival map
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: STEP 4: presenting to another group + peer review + the answer to the unit's WDYT question

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Preparing to go on (the TB tip): Where do you stand? Who holds the map? Who points? Everyone speaks once.
  2. Ninety seconds per crew. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness.
  3. Peer review (ex.9): Before each presentation, write down ONE thing that is useful, interesting, unique. And prepare ONE question.
  4. WDYT: we come back to the unit question from P1 — So: what makes a town a good place to live in? Answer with your map, not with an opinion.
  5. Final reflection (SB p.71): is the map clear? did you take different people into account — give an example? which language from the unit did you use — give an example?
📺 On screen: the maps · timer · the rubric · SB p.71 Final reflection
👦 Students do: they present and defend. Expect: the question from the other group is the most valuable moment of the pair

WB Unit 5 · the gap list · the maps stay in the classroom · the test is next pair
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can design and present a map of useful places for a newcomer, arguing for the needs of different people, using there is/are, How much/many and adjectives; the whole unit is revised for the P8 test.
  • Subsidiary. the Poole video as the model; STEP 1 the diagram of five categories; the Super skills Key phrases; Model project notes (layout, content, language); Quick review p.69 as stations; Final reflection; the WDYT question gets closed.
  • Personal. The project is the weaker students' moment of glory: roles to suit their strengths (the map · the box texts · the presentation · questions from the floor). I never call the Quick review preparation for the test — it is “the check before it goes to print”.
By the end, students can…
  • sort places into five categories for a town map
  • argue for the needs of different people with project Key phrases
  • present a map to another group and answer a question about it
  • pass all Quick-review stations of Unit 5

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
A map with only the places you go to yourself — is it accepted? → no: the Super skill rule is ideas for different people.
40 streets and 3 places on the map — is that good? → no (TB Model project notes): only the main roads, otherwise the map can't be read.
Is the Quick review a test? → no, it is the check on the map before it goes to print: find your own holes today.

Assumptions: SB pp.69–71, KTP pair 44. All the unit material has been covered; what is new is taking other people's needs into account and defending the map in front of another group.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The project eats the revisionA hard frame: the revision stations take 16 minutes IN THE MIDDLE of the pair, before the map gets drawn — they are “the check before print”.
A map “for ourselves”I check the Super skill rule BEFORE the drawing: name four different kinds of people and one place for each. If they can't, the map doesn't go into production.
The drawing eats all the timeThe TB tip: the places and the labels first, the detail afterwards. The drawing timer is strict, the information boxes matter more than the beauty.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
