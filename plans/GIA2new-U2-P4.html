🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The 19 collocations come in three baskets with gestures — the whole list at once would sink them. We work the rule out from the sports centre advert, not out of thin air. For new students the listening has three steps: first the safe question “what is this about at all” (you can hardly get it wrong), then the ideas, then the details — and three plays if that is what it takes.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Three little verbs. We DO karate, we GO swimming, we PLAY hockey. Why? There is a rule and you will find it yourselves — it's hiding in this sports centre advert. Then we listen to Ben, a real hockey player, on the radio: why does he love a team sport? And at the end, your own Saturday at the sports centre — four activities, with the right verb.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: three verbs do/go/play: the rule is hidden in the sports centre advert — we find it ourselves. Then we listen to Ben, a real hockey player, and build our own Saturday at the sports centre out of four activities.

Sports words in groups
desks → standing

WHAT WE DO: three baskets with gestures: do · go · play; recognition → naming → ex.4 sorting round the circle

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Basket by basket: picture → chorus → gesture: do karate — chop! · go swimming — swim! · play hockey — stick!
  2. Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols: the gesture belongs to the verb — do — a chopping movement · go — fingers “walking” · play — throwing a ball. First I show it and say it, then I show it silently — the class finishes it.
  3. Recognition: I show the gesture — you shout the whole phrase, with the verb.
  4. ex.4 with their hands through Kagan RoundRobin: first I sort two cards out loud as the model (“swimming — ends in -ing, so GO”), then the pile goes round the circle — everybody takes a card, says the whole phrase with the verb and puts it in a basket, the next person checks. TB key: Do — aerobics, hip-hop, athletics, gymnastics, yoga · Go — swimming, roller-skating, skateboarding, cycling, horse-riding, surfing, skiing · Play — hockey, basketball, volleyball, badminton, table tennis.
  5. Spelling station, the 6 frequent ones: swimming · cycling · karate · yoga · hockey · basketball.

👦 Students do: they take it in baskets, with gestures. Expect: gymnastics and athletics are the newest

WHAT WE DO: track 15 in three steps: what it is about at all → which ideas → details (with the note-taking table)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1 (safe): Don't catch facts. Just: who is talking? Is it about rules, keeping fit, or team sports? TB key ex.6: c — advantages of team sports.
  2. Step 2: ex.7 — which ideas are mentioned (TB key: 1, 3, 4, 6). We listen again.
  3. Step 3: ex.8 with the note-taking table (age | players | when | other sport). TB key: 1 13–15 · 2 16 in total, 11 play · 3 Monday and Wednesday after school · 4 goes cycling.
  4. Between the plays — Kagan RallyCoach: first I say one answer out loud with the evidence (“I hear sixteen players — I write it here”), then in the pair one reads their answer and says where they heard it, the other is the coach: checks it against the table and suggests what to listen for next time; on the next item they swap.
  5. If they are sinking — a third play, with pauses.

WB Unit 2 · the do/go/play rule poster stays up in the classroom
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can choose do, go or play correctly for 19 activities, catch the general idea and 3–4 details of a radio interview, and present their own Saturday with a frame.
  • Subsidiary. The collocation rule from the advert (ex.1–3); ex.4 sorting with their hands; the subskill listening for the general idea; a table to take notes in; ex.8 details.
  • Personal. Audio frightens them more than a text — I play it three times if I have to; after the first play only the safe question “what is this about at all”. I never ask for a fact after the first play (the TB's rule).
By the end, students can…
  • choose do, go or play for 19 activities
  • explain the rule in my own words
  • say what a recording is about before catching details
  • present my Saturday programme with a frame

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I do swimming” — is that allowed? → no: swimming ends in -ing → go swimming.
“I play athletics” — how about that? → no: it is not a ball game → do athletics.
The first question for any recording? → what it is about at all, not the details.

Assumptions: SB p.29, KTP pair 16. The names of the sports are mostly recognisable; the difficulty is choosing the verb.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
19 collocations at once will not fitThree physical baskets on the desks, 5–7 cards each; recognition first (put it in the basket), naming second.
Panic during listeningA note-taking table with the columns ready; three plays allowed; the first question is always a safe one.
Spelling gymnastics/athleticsThe spelling station covers only the 6 frequent ones; the rest are recognition only.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
