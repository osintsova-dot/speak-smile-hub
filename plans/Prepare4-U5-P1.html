🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the nine money-and-shopping words (save up, receipt, special offer, checkout…) and the five money-trouble verbs (afford, pay back, regret, run out of, suffer from) to talk about real buying habits — theirs.
What is genuinely hard
Not the concepts — the collocational glue: take something BACK, give something AWAY, save UP, run out OF. Russian has single verbs where English has verb + particle, so the particles fall off in speech. Plus the silent p of receipt.
Typical error to expect
receipt (silent p!) · delicious · disgusting · juicy — the block's misspelling traps; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
A one-word reply («Sweet.», «Yes.») does not count as an answer at all: an answer needs a verb in it [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The quiz photos open the lesson because they put the words into everyone's mouth before any teaching happens; the book's own Wizard-or-Waster quiz then makes every word personal. The reading about Alison the shopaholic does double duty: careful reading for detail AND the quarry for the five verbs — and it quietly plants «I've bought hundreds of things» ground that lesson 2's present perfect grows from. The productive stage is a money interview at 60′, after the words have been met three ways, and the advice round recycles should/shouldn't from Unit 7 onto today's vocabulary. Errors are logged silently and open lesson 2.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“A girl in England bought so many things she needed help to stop. Today you read her true story — and by the end you can interview anyone about money like a bank manager: Do you save up? Do you keep receipts? What do you regret buying? And you'll find out who in this room is a Money Wizard and who is a Money Waster.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a £100 airport question with two partners, and the bridge into the unit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — hot round: the block-4 goodbye. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share, one question, two partners: You're at the airport with £100. What do you buy? Thirty seconds each way, swap once.
  3. Collect three answers and price them on the board: £40? £95? — Interesting. Some of you save… some of you spend.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the unit: Books open, page fifty-four. Unit nine — Shop till you drop. By the end of today I'll know who here is a Money Wizard… and who is a Money Waster.
📺 On screen: three prices in chalk — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: mingle, answer, listen to the prices land. Expect airport words from block 4 to resurface by themselves — that is the rewind working

WHAT WE DO: About you + ex.1: match the money words to photos A–H

Why: The words arrive from two real speakers on the recording, and the completion task recycles them immediately — by the reading they are working words, not a list.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 (track 41): Gemma and Leo do the quiz; ex.3: complete the sentences; drill the sound traps

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Play track 41 — ex.2: Gemma and Leo answer the quiz. Write G next to Gemma's answers. Who is the Wizard? Key: track 41 — Gemma's answers: 1a 2b 3a 4a 5c 6b 7b 8c → Gemma is a MONEY WIZARD (Leo is the waster).
  2. ex.3: complete the five sentences with quiz words — pairs, 4 minutes, Kagan RallyCoach (one pen, partner asks for the reason). Key: 1 checkout, change · 2 bank account, save up · 3 special offer, price · 4 take, back, receipt · 5 give, away.
  3. Drill the sound traps by ear, three choral repetitions: receipt /rɪˈsiːt/ — the p is SILENT · change /tʃeɪndʒ/ · special offer /ˈspeʃəl ˈɒfə/ · bought /bɔːt/ — no w sound.
  4. Particle chant, thirty seconds: take it BACK · give it AWAY · save UP · pay it BACK — chorally, with a clap on the particle.
📺 On screen: track 41 in the whiteboard app (Backup: read Gemma and Leo from the audioscript, TB p.267 — two voices, and the task works identically by ear)
👦 Students do: listen, complete, drill, clap the particles. Expect ex.3 item 4 (take, back, receipt) to need the wall. Typical error: receipt with the p sounded
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit9_Vocabulary1_Smart_Shopping_A5
A5 glue-in journey — SMART SHOPPING in four steps drawn as a real purchase: SAVE (save up = save money for something — «I'm saving up for a new phone.» · bank account) → SHOP (price — «The price is £25.» · special offer · checkout = the place where you pay) → CHECK (receipt = proof of payment — «Keep the receipt.» · change = money returned after you pay, shown on a real receipt with total, paid and change) → RETURN / GIVE (take something back — «I took the jacket back to the shop.» · give something away — «I gave my old books away.»), under the line SAVE UP → CHECK THE PRICE → PAY → KEEP THE RECEIPT
  • Glue the journey in — the words are learnt in the order you use them.
  • Read the receipt on the sheet aloud: price, total, paid, change.
  • Cover the labels and retell the four steps from the pictures.
  • Say two sentences about a real purchase with save up and receipt.

Why: Gist first, then exam-shaped choices done as ordinary careful reading, then the verb harvest — the CELTA order. Alison's story also plants Have you ever…? ground for the next lesson's grammar.

WHAT WE DO: Alison's true story: gist → five careful choices → line-pointing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hook, books closed: A girl of twenty-three has a room FULL of things she has never used. Trainers with the price tag still on. Why does she keep buying? Take three guesses.
  2. Gist race, 3 minutes, books open — ex.1: Three statements, one is NOT true. Find it. Key: statement 2 is NOT true — the problem is not modern: psychologists described it in 1915 — the 1915 line surprises them; let it.
  3. ex.2 — five choices A–D, run as ordinary careful reading. Solo first (4 minutes), then Kagan RallyCoach: for items 2 and 4 the partner must HEAR the line of the text that decides. Key: 1 C (the writer informs about a problem) · 2 D (she bought hundreds of things online) · 3 B (soon after buying, shopaholics feel miserable) · 4 D (she is in control now) · 5 A (ask someone close to you for help).
  4. Close with the exit questions Alison uses now, chorally: Do I need it? Can I afford it?
📺 On screen: track 42 is the recording of the article — optional (Backup: skip the audio entirely; the stage is built on silent reading)
👦 Students do: read for the false statement, then argue the choices from the text. Expect item 3 (experts) to split the class — make them point at the line. Typical problem: choosing by feel — the words around the answer vote, count the votes

🎯 Speaking out · the money interview
pairs, then swap
Why: The productive outcome: the money words and verbs in real questions about real habits, under time pressure. Errors are logged silently — correcting now would kill fluency.

WHAT WE DO: a real interview about money habits — words and verbs compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frames on the board: Do you save up or spend straight away? · Have you got a bank account? · Do you keep receipts? · What do you regret buying? · Do you ever run out of money before the weekend?
  2. One minute solo: everyone notes two honest answers with a detail each.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share, 2 minutes each way: A interviews B with the frames plus ONE follow-up per answer; swap.
  4. Swap partners once (interviewers move one seat right) and repeat faster.
  5. You walk with a paper: log particle-dropping and receipt slips SILENTLY — they open lesson 2.
  6. Close the loop: two interviewers report the most surprising answer — She has never run out of money. Never!
📺 On screen: the five interview frames in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: interview, answer with details, follow up, swap. Expect fluent questions by round 2. Typical error: save money up for… word-order wobble — logged, not corrected

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class

  • name the nine shopping words — with their particles (take back, give away)
  • use afford, pay back, regret, run out of, suffer from about money
  • ask and answer money-habit questions in full sentences
  • give a verdict with a reason (You're a wizard, because…)

Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Keep the receipt» — is a receipt money? → no: the paper that PROVES you paid. And the p? → silent: /rɪˈsiːt/.
«I can't afford it» — do I want it? → maybe yes; can I pay for it? → no. Afford is about money, not desire.
«I regret buying it» — did I buy it? → yes, and now I feel sorry. Which form follows regret? → -ing.

Assumptions: SB p.54–55, syllabus lesson 26. After the block 4 test and the Review 2 lesson the class is warm on Units 5–8. Of the money words the class usually owns two or three (price, change as a loan from maths, checkout from computer games). The five reading verbs are effectively new.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Particles fall off (take back → take, give away → give)Anchor the rule as rhythm: say the word WITH its particle every time, chorally — take it BACK, give it AWAY. The wall chart never shows a bare verb.
receipt read as /rɪˈsept/ or spelled recieptDrill by ear before the word is seen; then a ten-second whiteboard spelling round at the wrap-up. The p is silent, the ei is the trap.
The About-you survey stalls («I don't get pocket money»)Widen the frame out loud: birthday money, transport money, canteen money all count — everyone handles SOME money.
One-word answers in the interviewThe rule of the lesson: one word is not an answer — an answer needs a verb and a detail. The interviewer asks a follow-up every time.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
