Mission brief + game setup
board → groups

WHAT WE DO: the Snake Challenge rules per the TB: groups of 3–5, dice/coins, markers, Key phrases; 🚀 twists announced

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules per TB p.129: Groups of three to five. Your marker on START. Roll — move — DO the square. First to FINISH wins. Coin = heads 2 moves, tails 1.
  2. Special squares in chorus: Miss a turn! Go back three! Go back to START — the cruel one.
  3. 🚀 twists: One — answer PLUS one example. Two — start speaking within FIVE seconds. Three — one-minute squares strictly timed.
  4. Game Key phrases at every table: Whose turn is it? It's my turn! — the whole game is IN ENGLISH.
  5. Reinforcement TB: 2 min looking silently at the squares and planning answers.
📺 On screen: SB p.115 · dice/coins · markers · Key phrases cards · timers/hourglasses
👦 Students do: sit in groups, learn the special squares, plan answers silently. Expected: tactical reading of the squares. Typical error: not understanding a square — What's the question? — a legal phrase!

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. Lap 2 = RallyRobin in pairs: Ping-pong with your partner: rooms and things in a house — one each, back and forth, six minimum, no repeats! (square 2).
  3. Lap 3 pairs: Compare two people in our class — politely! (square 8; taller/younger, nothing hurtful).
  4. 2 minutes to spare → ▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade flash round with the GIA1-QQT-U9.pdf cards (the course's grammar layer — a direct warm-up for the squares).
  5. Warmed up? Markers on START. The snake is waiting.

🐍 SNAKE CHALLENGE · main run
groups of 3–5

WHAT WE DO: the main run: the full game with twists; the teacher = a travelling judge; points for detailed answers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start: Roll! And remember — answer PLUS example, five-second rule!
  2. I walk between the groups: listening, throwing +1 to the leaderboard for gorgeous answers (clean past simple on square 11, a plan on 15 with U4 Key phrases).
  3. One-minute squares (10 · 20) — the group's timer; disputes — my one-phrase arbitration.
  4. Those who finish early: a second lap with challenge squares — they draw questions from the «10th squares» (P2 homework) for any square.
  5. 3 min to go: Last rolls! The group winners = Snake Champions.
📺 On screen: SB p.115 · dice · markers · timers · the «10th squares» bank from the P2 homework · leaderboard
👦 Students do: play the full game in English with twists — everyone speaks every 2–3 minutes. Expected: square 16 (back to START) delivers drama. Typical error: a one-word answer — the group chants: Example!

WHAT WE DO: an express mock: Reading Part 1 (4 texts, 6 min) → a listening tactic by ear (the teacher reads a mini-dialogue) → Speaking Part 1 (questions in pairs); quiet, by the timer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Part 1 (6 min): the Reading Part 1 format — 4 short texts with A/B/C options (I use the structure of WB p.84 ex.4, my own equivalent texts on the slide). Hack: text TYPE first!
  2. Part 2 (6 min): I read a mini-dialogue aloud twice, in the style of Listening Part 1 (following the model of the TB scripts) — 3 a/b/c questions on the slide. Mark options BEFORE I read. Question mark and move on!
  3. Part 3 (6 min) = Kagan Timed Pair Share: Speaking Part 1 per WB p.93 — Partner A: examiner, ask the four questions. Partner B: twenty-plus seconds per answer, A times it silently. Then the long one: ‘Tell me about your typical day.’ Swap roles!
  4. Self-check against the keys on the slide; the score isn't handed in — only for themselves.
📺 On screen: mock slides (prepared before the lesson from the WB pp.84/90/93 formats) · timer (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: go through 3 exam formats in a row with tactics — calmly, no marks. Expected: they apply tactics aloud («text type first!»). Typical error: panic on the listening part — a reminder: twice!

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can perform any of the year's speaking tasks on demand (Snake Challenge squares: count backwards · describe · compare · past simple recall · plans · can/can't) with 🚀 twists: answer + example, a 5-sec start.
  • Subsidiary. Lesson B: an express mock through A2 Key parts (Reading Part 1 + listening tactics + Speaking Part 1 questions) with a readiness checklist; a season reflection; closing the leaderboard and the titles.
  • Personal. The finale should be a CELEBRATION of mastery, not a check-up: fair play, varied titles (not just «the fastest»), reflection «look what you know» — literally the unit's title.
By the end, students can…
  • do the 21 Snake squares in English with answer+example
  • use the game Key phrases to run the game themselves
  • pass a mini-mock of A2 Key parts with tactics
  • self-assess readiness 🟢/🟡/🔴 honestly

Target language
Lexis

Structure
the squares call on ALL the year's grammar: comparatives (square 8) · past simple (11) · present simple + time (12) · would love to (14) · making plans (15) · can/can't (17) · present continuous/clothes (19).
Form
🚀 twists: an answer + ONE extra example/detail beyond the task · start answering within 5 sec · one-minute squares strictly timed · a challenge square from the opponents (a question from the «10th squares» of the P2 homework).
Pron. watch
dice /daɪs/ · Whose turn? /huːz/ · fluency: no «umm» pauses longer than 3 sec on the one-minute squares.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Landed on square 11 — do I talk about today?» → No: lunch YESTERDAY — past!
«The 5-second rule — what is it?» → Start speaking within 5 seconds, or the turn burns.
«Is the mock a grade in the register?» → No: it's a readiness map. Red = a plan, not a verdict.

Assumptions: The whole year is active after 4 review pairs; the game runs in English if the game's Key phrases are handed out and required. The mock is short — one sample per format. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (mixed-year items), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our revision zone before the exam formats”.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do

They argue about rules/squaresA blitz rules review BEFORE the game (TB): Miss a turn · Go back three · Go back to START; the group's timer-judge settles disputes.
The strong one finishes for the weakHelp = a leading question in English, not an answer; the group's judge watches (the role rotates).
The mock scares them after the celebrationThe order matters: first the game-celebration, then a short mock «for the readiness map» — no marks, only 🟢/🟡/🔴.
They finish the circuit too fastA second lap with a new twist, OR challenge squares: questions from the «10th squares» from P2.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
