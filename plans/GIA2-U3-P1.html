🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The 21 clothes words arrive not as a list but as the agency's working material: every crew has its own brief (a beach · a skate park · a school stage · a winter city), and they need the words to dress the client from head to feet. The present continuous lives inside every description (“she's wearing a long scarf”), but it is NOT announced as the topic — otherwise the “we know this already” starts. The Plurals box (jeans, trousers, shorts, tights, leggings) works as an agency rule, not as a grammar point.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Agents, the Style Agency is open. Four briefs on my desk: a beach day, a skate park, a school stage, a winter city. Each crew takes ONE brief and dresses the client from head to feet — every item in English, no Russian. At the end you SELL your look to the class in sixty seconds: what the client is wearing and why it works. Judges are watching. Crews — take your briefs!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We are a style agency. Each crew gets one brief (beach / skate park / stage / winter city). We dress the client from head to feet in English, and at the end we sell the look to the class in 60 seconds. The judges give points.

🔥 Challenge · catwalk duel
pairs → crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 p.37 (8 gaps) against the clock → track 19 (ex.4) → a RallyRobin duel “describe a look in 8 seconds, your opponent guesses the photo”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5 as a race, 3 minutes. Key: 1 sandals · 2 boots · 3 trainers · 4 coat · 5 T-shirt · 6 shorts · 7 leggings · 8 hoodie.
  2. Track 19 (ex.4): Four people, four photos. Which photo is each person talking about? Key: 1 B (Collection 1) · 2 F (Mickey) · 3 D (Jenna) · 4 I (Collection 2).
  3. A duel in pairs through Kagan RallyRobin: Eight seconds: describe a photo, your partner names the letter. Then swap. Turns run strictly in order: the first one describes — the second names the letter and immediately describes their own frame; you may not skip a turn.
  4. Constraints: repeat an item your opponent has already named and your turn burns; a plural mistake (a jeans / this shorts) — minus one point from the judge next to you.
📺 On screen: SB p.37 ex.4–5 · audio track 19 (backup — I read the four lines myself, the script is in the plan) · 8-second timer
👦 Students do: they duel with descriptions in turn. Expected: they hold the tempo, by the 3rd turn the descriptions get longer. Error: “He wear a hoodie” — the judge catches it, I say nothing

PRODUCT · sell the look
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second pitch: what the client is wearing and why it fits the brief; roles are shared out, judges have the scale

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes of preparation through Kagan Jigsaw: Keywords only, no scripts. Everyone owns one zone: head and neck · upper body · legs · feet + the closer says WHY it fits the brief. Everyone prepares THEIR OWN zone separately and only then brings it into the pitch — without somebody's piece the client comes out undressed, and everybody sees it.
  2. Sixty seconds per crew. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness — cards up!
  3. After each pitch — a challenge from the floor: But it's a winter city — where's the coat?
  4. I count plural errors with a separate flag: three flags = minus one accuracy point.
📺 On screen: 60-second timer · judges' cards · the rubric on the screen (backup — the rubric on the board)
👦 Students do: they perform in their roles, judged against the rubric. Expected: the bar holds the format, all four zones are heard. Error: reading off the page — the judges take fluency points off

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can describe a full outfit from head to feet using 21 clothes and accessories words, and pitch a look in 60 seconds, saying what the client is wearing and why it fits the brief.
  • Subsidiary. The gate exposes the real vocabulary ceiling; ex.1–5 pp.36–37 are covered as a race; the Plurals box is practised as an agency rule; Video skills (Central Saint Martins) is watched “as clients”; the opinion phrases come from track 19.
  • Personal. I do NOT name the present continuous as the topic — only “we describe what a person is wearing right now”. Form errors (he wearing) I log silently — for the clinic in P3.
By the end, students can…
  • name 15+ items of clothing in 60 seconds
  • describe a whole outfit head-to-feet in English
  • use plural-only words correctly (these jeans, not this jeans)
  • pitch a look and justify it against a brief

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I like this jeans” — what is wrong? → jeans is always plural: these jeans, They're great.
Are top and T-shirt the same thing? → top is wider: any first-layer item on the upper body (not a sweater and not a hoodie).
“You have been saying I wear jeans for four years. Can you dress a client from head to feet in 60 seconds?” → gate.

Assumptions: KTP material: SB pp.36–37 + Video skills. Half the words are familiar from A1+ — what is new is the volume (21 words), the plural-only rule and the speed of describing a real person.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
