🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The second portion of the first big grammar — questions through do/does are counter-intuitive for beginners (with be it was just a swap!). Lots of choral drills and “living sentences”; don't rush: short answers first in chorus, then in pairs.

WHAT WE DO: step-by-step input: Do/Does + subject + infinitive; comparison with be; short answers in chorus; the table of ex.1

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I write 2 sentences: You play video games. → a question? I show the “machine”: DO jumps forward: Do you play video games?
  2. 3rd person: She plays… → DOES she play…? — scissors: does has taken the -s!
  3. Anchor comparison: Are you happy? — be. Do you play? — ALL other verbs. Two colours on the board.
  4. Short answers in a tennis rhythm, in chorus: Do you do sport? — Yes, I do! / No, I don't! · Does it help? — Yes, it does! ×4.
  5. Ex.1: we fill in the tables together (use · Does · do · Yes, · doesn't).
📺 On screen: board: the “question machine” + the be/do anchor in 2 colours (stay up) · SB p.54 ex.1
👦 Students do: they look at the “machine”, drill the tennis pairs in chorus. Expected: the rhythm of the short answers sinks in through the chorus. Typical error: Does she plays — the scissors before I even say it.

Drill-play · phrase tennis

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: a phrase drill in a tennis rhythm: invitation → response; substitutions of days/times/activities round the circle

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Tennis in chorus: me — Are you free on Saturday? class — Sorry, I'm busy!; me — What about Sunday? class — Sunday's good! ×3, speeding up.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. Substitutions: I change the day/time/activity by gesture — pairs run mini-exchanges: Why don't we play basketball? — Yes, great!
  4. Politeness rule: No = Sorry + a reason! A bank of reasons on the board (TB): singing lesson · homework · birthday.
  5. 4 mini-exchanges per pair, swap roles.
📺 On screen: Key phrases + a bank of reasons on the board
👦 Students do: they drill the phrases with rhythm and substitutions — everybody speaks at once. Expected: the phrases go on autopilot. Typical error: a refusal with no Sorry — a “politeness!” gesture.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask and answer Yes/No and Wh- questions in the present simple (Do you…? Does she…? What time do you…?) and make weekend plans with Key phrases from a scaffold dialogue.
  • Subsidiary. Input step by step: Yes/No → short answers → Wh-; love/like/hate + noun (hearts); object pronouns in a game; a Grammar round-up with scaffolding (cumulative — with unit hints); video model Amy/Owen; PERFORM in small groups (safely).
  • Personal. The second portion of the first big grammar — questions through do/does are counter-intuitive for beginners (with be it was just a swap!). Lots of choral drills and “living sentences”; don't rush: short answers first in chorus, then in pairs.
By the end, students can…
  • make Yes/No questions with Do/Does
  • give short answers (Yes, I do / No, she doesn't)
  • make Wh- questions from a table
  • make plans with Key phrases from a scaffold

Target language
Lexis
Why don't we (meet)?Are you free on (Saturday)?What about (Sunday / 11:30)?Is (Sunday / 10:00) OK?(Sunday)'s good. / OK, fine. / Yes, great.Sorry, I'm busy. (+ a reason!)
Structure
Do you do sport? — Yes, I do. · Does your mother speak English? — No, she doesn't. · What time do you get up? · I love them! · Are you free on Saturday? — Sorry, I'm busy.
Form
Do/Does + subject + infinitive (does takes the -s!); Wh-word + the same order; me·you·him·her·it·us·them after the verb; on + a day, at + a time.
Pron. watch
do you = /dʒʊ/ fast · busy /ˈbɪzɪ/ · question intonation ↑.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Does she plays tennis?” → No — does has already taken the -s: Does she PLAY tennis?
“Do in a question and do ‘to do’ — the same word?” → No: Do you DO sport? — the first do is an auxiliary.
“I love Taylor Swift → I love she?” → HER (after the verb — an object pronoun).

Assumptions: Aff/neg are working after P3 (with the cheat sheet); they know be-questions (Are you…?) — today we explain that with “ordinary” verbs the question is built THROUGH do/does, not by a swap. Key phrases were partly heard in U1–3 dialogues.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
