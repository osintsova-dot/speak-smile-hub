🔥 Challenge · schedule raid (subskill)

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: subskill reading for detail put to real use: ex.4 the trainee's schedule for speed (audio 30 or silent reading)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Subskill 20 sec: Look at the GAP first: is it a time or an activity? Then hunt. That's reading for detail.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. Ex.4: Complete the academy schedule. Six gaps. Crews — five minutes, evidence ready! (audio 30 in the background, or silence).
  4. Check with the evidence rule: answer + where it says so (key: get up · 8:00 am · 5:00 pm · have lessons · 10:00 pm · go to bed).
  5. Ex.5 the finishing move: 6 true/false WITH REASONS round the chain of crews (+1 for the answer, +1 for the reason).
📺 On screen: SB pp.50–51 · audio 30 · key slide ex.4 (Backup: no track — I read the script aloud at listening speed; everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: hunt the gaps by word type, answer with the line as evidence. Expected: 5–6 out of 6. Typical error: an answer with no evidence — I don't give the point, the crew dives back in.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can read for detail (subskill) and deliver a 60-sec verdict «Academy life: worth it or not?» backed by 3+ facts with times from the text.
  • Subsidiary. Reading for detail applied at race speed (schedule quest ex.4); Word work (fan/star/lucky/breaks/concerts/become) as a duel; Critical thinking Apply+Evaluate = ranked debate; GMF4 bonus: interview with Have you ever…? / would like to become.
  • Personal. Reading is the place where the continuers really grow: the text is new even when the grammar isn't. Everything against the timer and with “the text says…” evidence.
By the end, students can…
  • commit predictions and verify them in the text
  • complete a schedule reading for detail under time
  • define/use the 6 Word-work items
  • argue a verdict with facts + times, win a trio debate

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Reading for detail = read every word?” → No — the gap first, then hunt for the fact.
“A verdict without a fact — does it count?” → Never. Text line or it didn't happen.
“Have you ever…? — which tense, grads?” → Present perfect. Not in this book. In you — yes.

Assumptions: Present simple is fluent orally; the academy content and Word work are genuinely new. We keep the debate on frames + judging cards. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (time-in-words / -s spelling / question word order), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
