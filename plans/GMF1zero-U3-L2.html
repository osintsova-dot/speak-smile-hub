Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to describe a circus clown / themselves with have got + body part (affirmative): “I've got a (red nose). I've got (big ears). I'm a clown!” (Pupil's Book p. 29 dialogue), and act out the clown dialogue.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle real prior learning — L1 body parts + Genki colours & numbers, Unit 2 big/small — woven into the have-got frame (“I've got two BIG feet!”, “I've got a red nose”); phonics — Phonics Hero ур.21 (a) первое знакомство: Magic E i-e /aɪ/ (kit→kite, bike, five, time, ride, smile: meet → blend → trace, тетрадь стр.58–59); listen for detail (PB p. 29 ex. 2). Light preview of L4 He's / She's got….
  • Personal. Insist on the ’ve (“I've got”, not “I got”); model plural parts with no “a” (big ears, not “a big ears”); every task keeps ALL children responding (own cards / touch own body / all pairs at once) — never “2 play, 10 watch”.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say “I've got a (red nose). I've got (big ears).”;
  • build a clown face & describe it: “I'm a clown! I've got a big mouth!”;
  • ask a friend “What have you got?” and answer;
  • blend & read the first i-e words (kite, bike, five, time, ride, smile) and trace the pattern.
Target language
Lexis

Structure
I've got a (red nose).  ·  I've got (big ears).  ·  I'm a clown!  ·  What have you got? — I've got… (preview: He's / She's got purple hair.)
Form
I've got = I have got (’ve = have); I've got + (adjective) + body part. Affirmative only today. Plural parts take no “a”: big ears, small eyes (not “a big ears”).
Pron. watch
keep the ’ve: /aɪv gɒt/ — not “I got”; link “I've got a” /aɪvgɒtə/; ears / eyes end in /z/; contrast big ↔ small with a big / tiny voice.
Phonics
Phonics Hero ур.21 (a) первое знакомство: Magic E i-e /aɪ/ — the i says its name (kit → kite) — kite, bike, five, time, ride, smile (тетрадь стр.58–59) · sight words: always, upon.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Point at Speaky's red nose: “Do I say I've got a nose or I got nose?” → I've got a nose! (keep the ’ve)
Two big ears: “I've got a big ear… or big ears?” → big ears (two = no “a”, add s).
Phonics: “kite — the magic e makes i say…?” → its NAME /aɪ/! — bike, five!

Assumptions: the 10 body parts & “What's this? — It's my…” were met in L1; numbers·colours·big/small from the Genki block & Unit 2. Phonics Hero ур.20 (a-e) consolidated last lesson; today Part 3 continues with i-e. Pupils are non-/emergent readers → picture grids & audio, not printed text. Carpet routines & pair-work signals known. Группы: команда — максимум 4; при другой численности делим на команды по 3–4 (12=3×4 · 10=3+3+4 · 9=3×3 · 8=2×4 · 7=3+4).

Likely problemWhat you do
Drop the ’ve: “I got a nose”Hand-on-chest gesture for I've; back-chain …a nose → got a nose → I've got a nose.
“a big ears” (article + plural)Two fingers = two = no “a”; drill the pair a big nose / big ears side by side.
Magic E read as two sounds: kite = /ки-те/Cross out the ghost e with a finger; “the e is silent but makes i say /aɪ/” — kite!

Volume control — raise/lower your arm = louder/quieter → settler.

Procedure — staging
highlighted = exact words to say (teacher / class) 🦝 = Speaky lines ▶ = game […] = fill-in slot 🎮 = on-screen (projector) interactive
⏱ Pacing — honest 60 min. The core = clown dialogue model (24–33′) + the clown-face build + the board game. Only I've got + body part is truly new — parts, colours & big/small are all recycled, so the frame carries the challenge; scaffold it (chest gesture, back-chain) and keep everything else light. Buffers to drop first if short: the 2nd board-game round, the 3rd Mix-Pair-Share swap. Better one full I've got a big red nose! said well by every child than three stages half-done. Offline reserve: every 🎮 has a paper twin (flashcards / craft parts / dice).

24–33′
Presentation · Dialogue
carpet → pairs

Model with Speaky (PB p. 29 ex. 1, CD1 50). 🦝 Speaky the clown: Look at me! I've got a red nose! I've got big ears! I've got a big mouth! I'm a clown! Books open / picture on screen; play the dialogue, children repeat line by line. Drill the chunk: chest gesture on I've, big/small voice on the adjective. Back-chain: …a nose → got a nose → I've got a nose.

🎮 Интерактив (проектор): Build-a-Clown spinner ✅ ГОТОВ (reskin of the “What's this? spinner” engine — body deck) · 🎮 whats-this-spinner-u3
Screen: a clown outline + a wheel of body parts & colours. Mechanic: tap to spin → lands on e.g. “red nose” → the part appears on the clown. Pupils: the WHOLE class says the frame each spin — I've got a red nose! — pointing to their own part. Teacher: hold the reveal until you hear the FULL frame, then tap. 🧠 Predict: What's next — big ears or a big mouth? Guess! Offline: a felt-board clown + paper parts, or a paper spinner.

Act out (ALL pairs at once). Pairs on the carpet; each takes turns being the clown: A puts a hand on the chest — I've got a big nose! I've got two big ears! I'm a clown! B mirrors the gestures, then swaps. ICQ: Do you say “I got” or “I've got”? (→ I've got!)

◆ Support: weaker child copies each line after the partner, with body cards fanned in front to read the pictures. ▲ Extension: add a colour + size — I've got a big red nose!

To your tables — make your OWN clown! Craft (everyone builds their own). Each child gets a clown face outline + a small envelope of parts (nose, ears, mouth, hair in colours). Teacher calls one part at a time; children stick & SAY it: I've got a red nose! I've got purple hair! I've got a big mouth! — every child talks while sticking.

Listen & check — PB p. 29 ex. 2 (CD1 51, all react). Play the mini-descriptions — children point to the matching clown / thumbs up if it matches theirs. Check chorally: He's got a big red nose — yes!

◆ Support: pre-cut parts + a model clown on the board to copy. ▲ Extension: add two facts joined with and — I've got a big mouth AND green hair!
