🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The free-time vocabulary comes in not as a list but as an audit tool: the crew has 48 hours of weekend and has to break them down into line items — like a report. The US statistics on p.24 give a benchmark to compare against, Ryan's vlog is a live case. always/usually/hardly ever appear as «the auditor's language of precision», not as the topic «adverbs of frequency» — that is P3's job, where they become a tool.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Agents, open the file. Forty-eight hours. That is your weekend — every weekend. American teenagers spend 4.3 hours a day on media and 3.8 on hobbies. Where do YOUR hours go? Each crew runs an audit: activities, hours, and one honest verdict — where the time leaks. Sixty seconds at the end, judges are watching, and 'I don't know' is not a verdict. Start the clock!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: You have 48 weekend hours. Each crew audits them: what you do, how many hours, and where the time leaks. At the end — a 60-second verdict.

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second report: the crew's time profile (top 3 activities + hours + comparison with the US + the verdict «where it leaks»)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can name 18 free-time activities and audit their own weekend (activity + hours + comparison with US teenagers) and deliver a 60-second verdict.
  • Subsidiary. The gate exposes the real vocabulary ceiling; the table p.24 ex.2 and the box p.25 ex.3 get closed by a race; Video skills (Ryan's vlog) is watched like analysts; the first use of usually/hardly ever is in context, with no topic announced.
  • Personal. I do NOT announce adverbs of frequency as the topic: they appear as «precision in the report». Position errors (she plays often) I log silently — that is the material for the clinic in P3.
By the end, students can…
  • write 12+ free-time activities in 90 seconds
  • sort activities into media / sport / art and music / other
  • say how many hours a week an activity takes
  • compare own weekend with US teenagers and give a verdict

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«We know this already» from the doorwayI don't argue — I produce the gate: not recognition, but WRITING for 90 seconds, spelling counts. The result is private, the rematch is at the end of the pair.
They invent the hours by eye and the audit turns into fictionThe audit rule: the crew's total must come to 48 hours — otherwise the report is not accepted. They count honestly.
The strong ones take over the air time in the verdictRoles in the report: the figures · the comparison with the US · the verdict · the most honest line. The judges take a point off for a «one-man show».
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
