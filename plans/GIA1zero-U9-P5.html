WHAT WE DO: the Snake Challenge rules, simple and visual; the game's Key phrases in chorus; support cards on the desks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules with a board diagram: Groups of three-four. Marker on START. Roll the dice — move — DO the square. First on FINISH — Snake Champion! A coin: heads = 2, tails = 1.
  2. Special squares with humour: Miss a turn — rest! Go back three — oops! Go back to START — the CRUEL square, if you land there you're the hero of the day!
  3. Key phrases in chorus from the cards: You start! Whose turn is it? It's my turn! What's the question?
  4. Base 🐣: team help is legal (the first word, not the answer) · support cards on the desk: went-verbs · a comparison frame · a time frame · numbers 90–100.
  5. TB Reinforcement: 2 min looking at the squares silently and thinking through answers.
📺 On screen: SB p.115 · dice/coins · counters · Key phrase cards · support cards
👦 Students do: they learn the rules and phrases, lay out the supports, plan their answers. Expected: the supports on the desk are reassuring. Typical error: not understanding a square — we rehearse What's the question? in chorus.

WHAT WE DO: a warm-up on 3 squares with the whole class (squares 2 and 17 — Kagan RallyRobin) — take the fear out of the first move

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. In chorus: Count 100 to 90 backwards — all together! (square 1 — the scariest one, defused in chorus).
  2. RallyRobin in pairs: Ping-pong: rooms in a house — one each, back and forth, no repeats! (square 2).
  3. RallyRobin round two: Now ping-pong ‘I can… / I can't…’ — two each! (square 17).
  4. Ready! Markers on START!
📺 On screen: SB p.115
👦 Students do: they warm up the hard mechanics together. Expected: 100→90 in chorus is funny and no longer scary.

🐍 SNAKE CHALLENGE · base run
groups of 3–4

WHAT WE DO: the main run: a full round in the base version; team help; the teacher — a roaming helper; points for boldness

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start: Roll! Team, help with FIRST WORDS only!
  2. I go round and help on the hard squares: 11 (lunch yesterday — the went-card) · 12 (my day — the time frame) · 19 (clothes — is wearing…).
  3. +1 on the leaderboard for bold, full answers (I hear a clean past — I comment out loud: ‘I had soup and rice’ — beautiful past simple!).
  4. The one-minute squares (10, 20): base = 4–5 sentences; the team can ask helper questions.
  5. Groups that finish: a second lap, or the role of “judge of the best answers” for a neighbouring group.
  6. At the 3-min mark: Last rolls! Group champions stand up — applause.
📺 On screen: SB p.115 · dice · counters · support cards · leaderboard
👦 Students do: they play a full round in English with support — everyone talks every 2–3 min. Expected: laughter on square 16; the supports make the hard squares manageable. Typical error: one-word answers — the team stretches it out with leading questions.

WHAT WE DO: calm consolidation per the syllabus: 3 mini-games on the “yellow zones” of the P1–P4 checklists (time · past · spelling); rotation

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Game 1 “Clock snap” (time, from the P1 station): clock cards — pairs race to say It's half past… (a calm pace, saying it out loud matters more than speed).
  2. Game 2 “Yesterday chain” = Kagan Timed RoundRobin (past): round the team, 20 sec per player — Yesterday I… (went/played/had/saw), repeating the previous one: Maxim went home, and I played football. Everyone speaks in turn, no skipping; support: the verb bank.
  3. Game 3 “Spelling tiles” (spelling): letter tiles — build the category word from the picture; pairs check each other.
  4. A swap game (if a zone isn't “yellow”): ▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the GIA1-QQT-U9.pdf cards — walk and swap, checking the answer by flipping the card.
  5. Rotate every 5 min; we play GENTLY: no elimination, only bonus points.
📺 On screen: clock cards · verb bank · letter tiles · handout GIA1-QQT-U9.pdf · timer signal
👦 Students do: they polish their personal yellow zones in three games without stress. Expected: the past chain flows noticeably easier than in U8 — growth is audible. Typical error: “I goed” in the chain — a neighbour points at the bank: went!

🌱 Build · the grow-up wall
desks → wall

WHAT WE DO: reflection-product “how I've grown”: a “September me / Now me” sheet with frames → we hang it on the growth wall

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The sheet folded in half: SEPTEMBER ME: I could say… (hello, my name is…) / NOW ME: I can… — THREE true sentences!
  2. Frames on the board: I can talk about… · I can write… · I can understand… · I can play a whole game in English!
  3. They write for 7 min; I go round reminding them of the evidence: You wrote a 100-word email! You understood a podcast!
  4. We hang the sheets on the “growth wall” — everyone reads ONE of their own sentences aloud at the wall.
  5. Applause after each one — protocol, for everyone.
📺 On screen: A5 “September/Now” sheets · frames · a wall/board for the sheets
👦 Students do: they fix their growth in writing and voice it at the wall — the year's climax. Expected: a touching moment; “I can understand a podcast” sounds proud. Typical error: an empty “I can English” — the frames lead to specifics.
💪 Support: choose 3 ready frames and complete them. ⭐ For the fast finishers: + the sentence “Next year I want to…”.		A5 sheets · wall

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can play the full Snake Challenge board game in English (base version) — doing the squares with team support and running the game with its Key phrases (Whose turn is it? It's my turn!).
  • Subsidiary. Lesson B: a calm consolidation of the year's hard spots through games (per the personal checklists from P1–P4) + reflection “how I've grown this year”; closing the unit leaderboard with titles for everyone.
  • Personal. Per the syllabus: “answers with support, team help” and “calm consolidation… reflection ‘how I've grown’”. NO mock exams or tests for the from-zero line at the finale — a celebration of mastery and confidence. Everyone leaves with a personal “I can…”.
By the end, students can…
  • play a board game fully in English with support
  • do 15+ of the 21 squares (with team help)
  • use the game Key phrases naturally
  • say 3 true «I can…» sentences about their year

Structure
the squares in the base version with supports: counting backwards (1) · rooms (2) · spell a country (5) · compare (8) · lunch yesterday (11) · my day + time (12) · plans (15) · can/can't (17) · your neighbour's clothes (19).
Form
the base version: no stopwatch pressure on the one-minute squares (half a minute is fine) · team help is legal (a hint, not the answer) · support cards lie on the desk (numbers · rooms · went-verbs · comparison frames).
Pron. watch
dice /daɪs/ · Whose /huːz/ · the victory phrase in chorus: We did it!
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Don't know the square — do I lose my turn?” → No: say What's the question? and the team will prompt the first word.
“Is square 11 about today?” → About YESTERDAY: I had… (past!).
“Is losing bad?” → No: today everyone who played in English wins.

Assumptions: After 4 review double lessons the whole year is refreshed; the game runs in English on two conditions: Key phrases on the desk + support cards under the hard squares. The finale is emotional: reflecting on growth matters more than any points.

The game slides into RussianKey phrases on the desk; a soft rule: a game phrase in Russian — repeat it in English from the card and keep playing.
The hard squares (8, 11, 12) stall the gameSupport cards on the desk: went-verbs · a comparison frame (taller than) · a time frame (at half past…); half a minute to think — that's fine.
The weaker one is shy to talk for a full minute (square 10)Base: 4–5 sentences instead of a minute; the team can ask leading questions (that's English too!).
Excitement → resentment over “go back to START”Talk it through in advance with a laugh: “square 16 is the meanest one, whoever lands on it is the hero of the day”; turn the drama into humour.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
