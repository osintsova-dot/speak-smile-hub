🎤 SPEAKING CHECKPOINT + carousel
teacher desk + 3 stations

WHAT WE DO: I check one at a time (2–3 min: About me → interview questions → follow-ups → a mini-opinion); the class — a carousel of 3 stations

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The 1-on-1 check (2–3 min each): 1) Your About me — go! 2) 3–4 questions from the bank (I vary them); 3) follow-ups: Why? How many? And at the weekend? 4) an opinion: Holidays or school — what do you think? I fill in the grid: fluency / accuracy / vocabulary / interaction 1–4.
  2. Station A “Interview gym” via Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade (GIA1-QQT-Mid cards): everyone has a question card — Ask! — Answer! — TRADE cards — new partner!; the goal — 2 follow-ups for every answer.
  3. Station B “Board game review”: a Starter–U4 revision board game (a board with task squares, a die; from our own unit materials).
  4. Station C “Letter to May-me”: a quiet letter to yourself in May, using the frame (see the Self-assessment block — they start it here, finish it later).
  5. Station rotation ~ every 11–12 min; checks run continuously.
📺 On screen: speaking grid (one sheet for the class) · question bank at station A · game board B · letter frames C
👦 Students do: everyone goes through their personal check and all 3 stations; no idle waiting. Expected: the interview station genuinely prepares them for the check — the queue works for quality. Typical error: one-word answers at the check — a follow-up pulls out more, honestly logged in the grid.

47–65′
Rematch quiz: test mistakes → points
🔥 Test rematch quiz
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: a rematch quiz via Kagan Showdown: the test's top-8 mistakes (anonymised) — EVERYONE fixes them on mini-boards, boards up on command; a personal error log in the notebook

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 8 rounds = 8 typical test mistakes on slides (anonymised): crews fix them on mini-boards + name the rule-word (helper! / -s! / any! / apostrophe!).
  2. On the command SHOWDOWN! — boards up, all at the same time; +2 for a clean fix, +1 for the rule.
  3. After every round, 20 sec: Open YOUR test — did this one bite you? Tick it.
  4. Finale: everyone writes an error log: my top-2 + fix in their notebook; I say it out loud: January-you will thank December-you for this list.
📺 On screen: 8-mistake slides · mini-boards · their tests (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they fix as a team, check against their own work, keep a personal error log. Expected: the rematch mechanic turns the review into a game; the logs are accurate. Typical error: a tick with no log entry — I walk around and check the sheets.

WHAT WE DO: the half-year summary: an I-can list for Starter–U4 with checkmarks + finishing the “May-me” letter; personal goals for the 2nd half-year (2 of them)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. An I-can list (handout): 12 lines of half-year skills (I can introduce myself · say the time in words · describe my family · order food · talk about my day…) — honest checkmarks: ✔ easy / ~ ok / ✗ not yet.
  2. A letter to yourself in May (started at station C): the frame Dear May-me! Now I can… My favourite moment was… In May I want to… — an envelope, a signature, I KEEP it until May.
  3. 2 goals for the half-year from the error log + the I-can list: specific ones (ask questions with does · spell days).
  4. Pairs: exchange ONE goal out loud.
📺 On screen: I-can lists · envelopes · the letter frame
👦 Students do: they assess themselves honestly, write the letter to May, set 2 goals. Expected: a touching moment — they put effort into the letters. Typical error: a vague-cloud goal (“learn better”) — I ask for a verb+topic.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, every learner has passed the individual speaking checkpoint (interview + follow-ups + mini-opinions), fought the test-rematch quiz over the corrected Mid-year test, and closed the half-year with a written self-assessment.
  • Subsidiary. Partner interviews with follow-up questions as a format; logging fluency on a speaking grid (for comparison in May); going over the test as a rematch quiz (mistakes → points); the “half-year summary”: an I-can list + a letter to yourself; a festive finale.
  • Personal. The year's last meeting: three tasks — an honest record of speaking progress, going over mistakes with no gloom (a rematch!), and a warm note before the holidays. Everyone should leave saying “I really have grown” with a concrete growth sheet.
By the end, students can…
  • hold a 2–3 min interview with follow-up questions
  • give mini-opinions (I think… because…)
  • turn their test mistakes into rematch points
  • write an honest half-year self-assessment

Lexis

Structure
What's your name? How do you spell it? · Where are you from? What nationality are you? · Have you got brothers or sisters? What have they got? · What's your favourite food? Is there…? · What time do you get up / go to bed? · I think… because… · Me too! / Really?
Form
interview etiquette: question → full answer → follow-up → returning the question (What about you?); an opinion = a statement + because + a reason.
Pron. watch
intelligibility is assessed, not accent; a rising follow-up intonation; numbers/time in words, cleanly.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is the speaking checkpoint a scary-faced exam?” → No: a 2–3 min chat with me over familiar questions; the grid is for comparison with May.
“Answered in one word — is that fine?” → A full sentence + a detail; a follow-up will pull out more.
“Is the rematch quiz another test?” → No: your mistake from yesterday, fixed today = a point for the team.

Assumptions: The test has been checked (by me, before the lesson), typical mistakes are collected into the rematch quiz. A 60-sec “About me” is prepared at home. The atmosphere is pre-New-Year — use it, don't fight it.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
A queue for the individual checkThe class works a station carousel WHILE the checks are running: interview pairs, a board-game revision game, a letter to yourself; I call them one by one.
Shyness 1-on-1 with the teacherI start with their strength (About me is prepared), a smile, nods; I fill in the grid unobtrusively, not “scoring in their face”.
Going over the test = gloomOnly the rematch format: the mistake is anonymised, fixed at the board as a team — a point. Personal results — whispered into everyone's hands.
Pre-holiday restlessnessI hold the festive part AT THE END as a magnet: “checks and rematch are done — now we open the finale”.
Someone missed the testDuring the carousel they write a mini-version of the test in the corner; the speaking check — in the general queue.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
