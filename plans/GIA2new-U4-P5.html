🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Modals are the most useful structure there is for a teenager: rules surround them all day. So the pair is built as a staircase: three colours (must · may · must not) → the tables of forms out of the guide's familiar phrases → rewriting the park rules → their own rules. I separate the colours physically so that the choice is seen with the eyes rather than recalled. Weak and strong can (SB p.116) comes at the end as «how to sound real».
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Four little words run the whole world. MUST — you have no choice. CAN — you're allowed. CAN'T and MUSTN'T — no way. Brett said them all in Yosemite: 'You can take photos, but you mustn't take flowers.' Today we sort them by colour, build the tables, and at the end each of you writes six rules for a place you choose — your room, our classroom, a park. And they will all be correct.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Four words that run everything: must (you have to), can (you may), can't and mustn't (you may not). We sort them by colour, build the tables and write six rules for a place you choose yourself.

WHAT WE DO: Brett's phrases on coloured cards → we lay them out in three columns (ex.1) → what the columns mean

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Cards in three colours from the P4 briefing: You can take photos (green) · All food must go in these special boxes (blue) · You mustn't take any plants (red) · You can't leave any food in your backpack (red).

👦 Students do: they work out the three columns by colour. Expected: the colour holds the choice more reliably than the rule does

Pronunciation · can
desks

WHAT WE DO: SB p.116: weak /kən/ in questions and statements, strong /kæn/ in short answers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Listen: 'Can I go?' — that's /kən/, quick and quiet. 'Yes, you CAN.' — that's /kæn/, loud.
  2. We go through the dialogue on p.116 ex.2 together. TB key: 1 /kən/ · 2 /kæn/ · 3 /kən/ · 4 /kən/ · 5 /kæn/ · 6 /kən/.
  3. I say one line together with a student at the board as a model, then Kagan RallyCoach: one reads the line, the other is the coach with one job — thumbs up on a weak /kən/ and a fist on a strong /kæn/; on the next line they swap. I conduct with my hand: weak — quiet, strong — loud.
  4. Plus a TB fact: in mustn't the first t is not pronounced — /ˈmʌs(ə)nt/.

👦 Students do: they pick up the «grown-up» sound. Expected: the weak can lands as a trick — they ask to do it again

WHAT WE DO: 6 rules for a place they choose (my room · our classroom · the park) → self-check → read to a partner → volunteers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. The frame: You must … . You mustn't … . You can … . You can't … . Can you … ? — Yes, you can. / No, you can't.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use must / mustn't / can / can't correctly in affirmative, negative and question forms, checked by themselves against keys.
  • Subsidiary. Guided discovery from the P4 briefing phrases (ex.1); ex.2, two tables of forms; ex.3, rewriting by the symbols ?, −, +; ex.4, the park rules; ex.5, school rules; the pronunciation of can /kən/ and /kæn/ (SB p.116); a mini-test with self-check.
  • Personal. The three colours stay up all lesson (red — not allowed, green — allowed, blue — obliged). I watch two things: no to after must/can and no -s in the 3rd person. The Grammar round-up (ex.6) I give as a bonus for the fast ones.
By the end, students can…
  • put a rule in the right colour (obligation / permission / prohibition)
  • build a question with Can and a short answer
  • rewrite a rule as +, − or ?
  • write six correct rules for a place I choose

Structure
Obligation: All food must go in these special boxes. · Permission: You can take photos. / Can we take food into the park? — Yes, you can. · Prohibition: You can't leave any food in your backpack. / You mustn't take any plants or flowers with you.
Form
subject + can / can't / must / mustn't + bare verb (no to and no -s: She can swim, NOT 'She cans swim') · question: Can + subject + verb? · short answer: Yes/No, + subject + can/can't
Pron. watch
SB p.116: in questions and statements can is unstressed — /kən/; in short answers it is stressed — /kæn/. can't is /kɑːnt/ in British, /kænt/ in American English. In mustn't the first t is not pronounced: /ˈmʌs(ə)nt/ (TB tip)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'You must to wear boots' — what is extra? → to: You must wear boots.
'She cans swim' — correct it. → She can swim: modals take no -s in the 3rd person.
'Can we to take photos?' — and here? → Can we take photos? — the verb is bare.

Assumptions: SB p.54 + Pronunciation p.116, KTP pair 34 (the KTP says p.55 — a shift of +1, the real page is 54). can is familiar in the sense «I am able to»; can for permission and must/mustn't are new.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
must to / can toA «MODAL + bare verb» poster on the wall; when there is an error I point at the poster and make a «take the to away» gesture, I do not say it out loud.
They mix up can't and mustn'tFor this line I simplify (following the TB): both = not allowed; the subtlety I explain in one phrase — can't is «that is the rule», mustn't is «I forbid it»; I do not count it as an error.
They build the question like a statementColoured strip cards: CAN — subject — verb; we physically move the strips around until the order becomes automatic.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
