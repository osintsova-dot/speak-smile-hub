🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that the vocabulary and grammar of two units work without the book: 31 words, the present continuous for the future, will / may / might, and correct spelling.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the gap between oral and written. Out loud the class knows almost everything; on paper air conditioning loses a letter and might not becomes mightn't.
Typical error to expect
becouse · tomorow · alot · wich · beatiful · intresting — the most frequent misspellings; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam In our block that means bookcase written as two words, waterskiing with one i, first-aid kit with no hyphen.
Plus the modal errors: a to after will, and a third-person ‑s on may.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built as retrieval practice, not re-teaching: the two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it and what shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which forms still break. This is the first block whose book Review is genuinely available — Review 2 covers Units 5–8 and all four have now been taught, so the stations run ON the book page rather than on invented material. The game sits between the stations and the briefing because it is the settler that turns revision into a reason to speak, and a class that has just laughed walks into a test calmer. Nothing new is introduced anywhere today.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. Everything on this test you have already done in this room. First forty-five minutes — we play it back, straight off page fifty-two. Then forty-five minutes — you show it. Your score is not the point: the point is your own list of five mistakes to fix.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast round of the block's words, and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — block rewind. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade as a hot round: cards for all 31 words of the block, two trades each. Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest with them: Everything you just said is in today's test.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, read the words out from a list and students write the translation in pairs
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety dropping. Typical problem: they stumble on air conditioning and zip wiring

Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: put the test specification up in advance — transparency is what takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the nine parts of the test on the board, one line each: holiday activities · kit and get-phrases · home and appliances · words with two meanings · present continuous for the future · will, may, might · also, too, as well · pronunciation by ear · five sentences about your own weekend.
  2. Look at the board. Have we done all of this in class? Yes or no? The class answers chorally — the answer is always yes.
  3. Say the Review 2 boundary out loud: ⚠️ Review 2 covers Units 5–8. Everything in it has been taught — Units 5–6 in block 3, Units 7–8 in this block — so the page is used in full. The TEST, however, is only Units 7–8: do not carry school subjects or adjective order into the paper.
  4. The rule of the day: Your score goes in the register. Your five mistakes go in your notebook. The second one matters more.
📺 On screen: the nine specification lines in chalk — the board is the medium and they stay up for the whole first half of the lesson, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the specification and answer chorally. Expect visible relief. Typical problem — they don't believe «we have done all of it»: ask them to name the lesson where each line was done

WHAT WE DO: Review 2 Vocabulary 1–2 (SB p.52) plus a written spelling round

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, on the book page: Review 2 Vocabulary ex.1 — get / go / take, nine gaps, in pairs, 4 minutes. get / go / take, nine gaps. Key: 1 take · 2 went · 3 get · 4 are going · 5 got · 6 Take · 7 got · 8 take · 9 going
  2. Round 2, still on the page: Vocabulary ex.2 — sort eighteen words into Materials, Home and Adventure. This is our block's vocabulary sitting inside a four-unit task. sort into three columns. Key: Materials — cotton, gold, leather, metal, plastic, silver · Home — air conditioning, bookcase, fridge, heating, roof, stairs · Adventure — compass, first-aid kit, map, sleeping bag, tent, torch
  3. Round 3, books closed: Kagan Showdown — you say the word in Russian, the team writes it in English and raises the board on command. Put the traps in deliberately: air conditioning, bookcase, waterskiing, first-aid kit, waterproof, zip wiring.
  4. Every misspelling goes into a separate «watch out» column on the board — it stays there right up to the test.
📺 On screen: the Review 2 page on the screen so the class works on the same exercise together (Backup: it is printed on SB p.52 in the students' own book — run all three rounds from the page and keep the «watch out» column in chalk)
👦 Students do: do the two book exercises, then spell against the clock. Expect almost everything orally and a visible drop in the written round. Typical error: book case as two words — which is exactly why the column is needed

Review station 2 · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar — and it shows you, twenty minutes before the test, exactly which form still breaks.

WHAT WE DO: Review 2 Grammar 1–2 (SB p.52) — the tenses and the modals of the block

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Grammar ex.1: choose the right word in 1–4 and correct 5–8, in pairs, 5 minutes. Items 3 and 7 are ours — the present continuous for the future. choose and correct. Key: 1 good · 2 art · 3 are meeting · 4 your · 5 I went to the beach and swam with my parents, then we flew a kite. · 6 I want to be a friend of his / his friend because he's funny. · 7 I'm going to Mexico to visit Manuela, a friend. · 8 We are meeting at the park at two o'clock because before that I'm going to the dentist's.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the reason, not the answer — Say WHY, not just the answer.
  3. Grammar ex.2: word order with will, may and might, eight items, 5 minutes. Every one of these is ours. word order with modals. Key: 1 My brother will study · 2 It might not snow · 3 We may not get · 4 Will you visit · 5 will they get · 6 I may not take · 7 People might share · 8 will we learn
  4. Collect the answers with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Sentence six — number fours, boards up!
  5. Boil every explanation down to one line: fixed plan + time word → ‑ing; sure → will / won't; possible → may / might; and after all three modals the verb is bare.
📺 On screen: the Review 2 grammar exercises on the screen, one item at a time (Backup: they are printed on SB p.52 — uncover them on the page with a ruler and keep the one-line summary in chalk on the board)
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with an explanation, then report by number. Expect ex.2 to be cleaner than ex.1, because the modals are only two lessons old. Typical error: It might not to snow still slips out from 2–3 students — they get a line of their own in the error list

Review game
two teams
Why: A game that hides revision inside a reason to speak; nerves before a test drop when the last thing they did was laugh.

WHAT WE DO: the game «What's the word? (Review game Units 5–8, Cambridge, PHOTOCOPIABLE 2019)» — one team reads, the other finds the missing word

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Two teams, sheet A and sheet B, which must not be shown to each other. Team B reads out a sentence with a gap (they say «blank» at the gap and do NOT read the word in capitals at the end — that is the answer). Team A chooses the word from the box at the bottom of their own sheet. Right first time — 3 points; second try — 2; third — 1; after that nothing. Then the teams swap roles.
  2. ⚠️ Important: ⚠️ The sheet covers Units 5–8, so run only the lines from our block, and let the Units 5–6 lines stand as free revision if the group is fast. Ours on sheet A: STAIRS · MAP · WALKING BOOTS · TORCH · ROOF · BARBECUE · COMPASS · TRAINERS · FRIDGE · SNACKS. Ours on sheet B: AIR CONDITIONING · DRAWER · HORSE RIDING · CAMPING · HIKING · BACKPACK · WASHING MACHINE · SAILING · BIN · SLEEPING BAG. Words deliberately left unused: TENT on sheet A, GLASS on sheet B.
  3. Set the bar: Read the whole sentence. No pointing, no miming.
  4. Follow-up from the game book, if there is a minute left: teams swap sheets and try to remember the example sentence that was read out for each word.
📺 On screen: one printed A sheet and one B sheet per team, run off in advance and cut along the dotted line — paper is the medium here, so no backup is needed; with no printer, read the sentences out yourself and let the two teams compete to call the word
👦 Students do: read and guess against each other. Expect the liveliest stage of the review half. Typical error: the reading team says the capitalised answer out loud — remind them once, then it self-corrects

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions for the test

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go back to the «watch out» column on the board: the five or six words that were misspelt. Look at these for thirty seconds. Then I close them.
  2. Instructions: 38 minutes, nine parts, 77 marks; phones in the bag; finished early — part 9 (five sentences about your own weekend). Part 8 is done TOGETHER at the start: you read it out, so nobody can be late for it.
  3. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  4. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; everything else in the room is switched off so there is nothing to look at
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect silence to settle in 20 seconds if the flat hand goes up before you start talking

Block test
solo, silent mode
Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats. Everything in the paper has been done in class, so the test measures learning, not surprise.

WHAT WE DO: the block 4 test: nine parts, 77 marks, 38 minutes (the first 5 are the part 8 dictation)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. You have thirty-eight minutes. Start.
  2. Part 8 (pronunciation) comes first and runs to your voice — you read the six stress sentences twice and then the six will/won't items twice, 5 minutes, after which students work on their own.
  3. Walk round the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  4. Write the time left in chalk: 20 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  5. Anyone who finishes early writes part 9 (five sentences about their own weekend) and sits quietly.
  6. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; nothing at all is put up in the room while the test is running
👦 Students do: work in silence for 38 minutes. Expect parts 1–3 to be closed fast and part 7 (also / too / as well) to slow them down. Typical error: they skip part 9, taking it for an optional extra — which is why it is on the specification on the board

Test wrap-up
circle
Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip of paper, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the feedback lesson.
  3. Homework: rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly once the papers come back; for now — the Vocabulary or Grammar worksheet Unit 7–8 (Standard or Plus, teacher's call) if anything is still open.
  4. Teaser: Next lesson you write a story from three pictures — and then five people say five things, and you have to catch what they MEAN, not what they say.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is already full, simply read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to be about the linking words most often — that is the plan for the next feedback clinic

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
