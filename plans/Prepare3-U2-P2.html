🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Ask for information and understand the answer — eight holiday words, the phrases of a service conversation, and a matching read where the answer is said in different words.
What is genuinely hard
Two different things on one page. In the reading, the same word appears in all three texts and only one of them is the answer. In speaking, the difficulty is not the question but the moment after it: you have to react to what you actually heard, not to what you prepared.
Typical error to expect
They pick the option that repeats a word from the text. The correct option is almost always paraphrased; the wrong ones copy the wording [exam report]
A one-word reply («By bus.», «Moscow.») does not count as an answer at all: an answer needs a verb in it [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built backwards from the role-play at 62′: to run a conversation at an information desk you need the words, the model and one repair phrase, so those three arrive in exactly that order and every one of them is used the minute it is met. The reading goes first because the three Moscow texts hold both the topic and the words, and because a matching task is the calmest possible start to a lesson. Talking points comes straight after it, while the six photographs are still in front of them — an opinion is cheap to produce and expensive to produce later. The listening is placed before the speaking on purpose: hearing the receptionist do the job is easier than doing it, and it rehearses the same phrases one more time. Feedback in the productive stage is on two things only — did you get the information, and did you say more than one word.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today you are a tourist and nobody speaks your language. Eight words, one conversation, and one rule: if you did not understand, you say so — Can you repeat that, please? Silence is the only answer that fails.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a clinic on yesterday's three past simple errors and the bridge

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last lesson rewind. A clinic on yesterday's correction sheet: three sentences on the board with no names attached — I goed to my grandmother. · Did you went by train? · We was very tired. Thirty seconds — fix all three on your board.
  3. Check them together; the student whose hand went up first explains why it has to be that way. No lecture: three sentences, ninety seconds.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Yesterday you told me where you went. Today you have to GET something — information, from a person who won't repeat it twice.
📺 On screen: the three sentences written on the board before the lesson — the board is the medium, no backup needed
👦 Students do: correct them on mini-whiteboards. Expect the first and third to be fixed by everybody and the second by about two thirds. Typical error: they change went to go but leave Did out

WHAT WE DO: name the language aim: get the information, and prove your answer with a line

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two lines on the board — the whole lesson is built this way: 1 Read for meaning, not for the word. 2 Ask until you understand.
  2. Say the reading rule honestly: TB p.41, and it is the whole skill of the task: the same word can appear in two texts or in all three. The right answer is the one that says the same THING, often in different words — on foot for walking, for instance.
  3. Set the bar: Every answer needs a line. And every question needs an answer with a verb in it.
  4. Say the point of the day out loud: today it is not the teacher who checks you, it is whether you actually got the information you needed.
📺 On screen: a slide with the two lines (Backup: the same two lines in chalk on the board — they stay up all lesson and are needed again at 62–78′)
👦 Students do: listen; one student puts the standard into their own words. Expect a groan at «every answer needs a line». Typical problem — they treat it as a formality: make the first check at 8–22′ strictly by the line

Why: Three short texts, seven questions — the first time this year that the answer is hidden behind a paraphrase rather than a matching word. The rule «no line, no point» is the point of the stage.

WHAT WE DO: «Teen travel tips: Moscow» on p.22 — gist first, then seven matching questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed, the lead-in from TB p.41: TB p.41: books closed, write МОСКВА on the board and ask whether anyone can read it. Then: «Where were the 2014 Winter Olympics?» (Sochi) · «What three colours are in the Russian flag?» (red, blue, white).
  2. Books open, ex.1 against the clock: Two minutes. Three girls. Who visited which place? Key: Monique – Aquarium · Carla – Metro, Red Square, Aquarium · Olivia – Old Arbat, Metro
  3. Now the trap, before ex.2: Who talks about walking? (Monique and Olivia) — Does Monique LIKE walking? (no) — What phrase does Olivia use for walking? (on foot). That is the whole skill of this task in one minute (TB p.41).
  4. ex.2, seven questions, 6 minutes solo. Kagan RallyCoach to check in pairs: the partner reads out the LINE, not the letter.
  5. Key: 1 O · 2 C · 3 C · 4 M · 5 O · 6 M · 7 C
📺 On screen: the three texts on the screen with the evidence lines underlined in marker as you go (Backup: everybody works from the book and you keep the letters M / C / O in a column on the board as answers are given)
👦 Students do: read for gist, then match with a line. Expect questions 3 and 5 to split the class. Typical error: they choose Monique for question 5 because the aquarium is in her text too

Talking points
pairs
Why: A short opinion exchange so the lesson is not only mechanics: they use the unit's language to say something they actually think, about a city they can picture.

WHAT WE DO: the six photographs of Moscow: which of these places would you actually go to

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Look at the six photos on page twenty-two. Which two would you go to, and which one would you skip? Two minutes in pairs.
  2. One line of background, not more (TB p.41): Moscow was first written about in 1147 as a small town on the river Moskva; St Basil's on Red Square was built in the sixteenth century.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share: 40 seconds each, then swap.
  4. Collect three opinions from the class and stop — this stage is a change of gear, not a debate.
📺 On screen: the six photographs on the screen, large (Backup: the same six photographs are in the book on p.22 — students point at them in their own copy and you keep score of the votes on the board)
👦 Students do: give an opinion with a reason in pairs. Expect the cat theatre to win the vote. Typical error: an opinion with no because — ask for it every time

Vocabulary 2 · holiday words

WHAT WE DO: eight holiday words on the photograph of a hotel reception: ex.1 p.22 (track 22) and ex.2 p.23

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Concept-check before the book, in this order: TB p.41, ask them in this order: «Who gives you your room key?» (receptionist) · «And the person who takes the key?» (guest) · «This person does not stay in a place all the time» (visitor) · «This person travels for fun, not business» (tourist) · «If you are lost, you need a …» (map) · «You put your clothes in a …» (suitcase) · «A lot of suitcases together are …» (luggage) · «You use a … to learn about a new city» (guidebook).   4. ex.2 p.23 — eight gapped sentences, 4 minutes solo. Key: 1 map · 2 tourists · 3 guidebook · 4 suitcase · 5 guests · 6 luggage · 7 receptionist · 8 visitors

WHAT WE DO: John and the hotel receptionist (track 23): the situation first, then eight right/wrong statements

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 before anything is played: Possible answers (TB p.42): 1 the reception of a hotel · 2 a boy, probably a guest, and a receptionist · 3 the boy is checking in, his bag is on the floor and the receptionist is showing him a map.
  2. Pre-listening, TB p.42: TB p.42, before track 23: «What questions will John, the guest, ask the receptionist?» Collect ideas. Pre-teach explore — to go around a place you have never been in order to find out what is there. Then: «John has been to Moscow before — right or wrong?» (wrong).
  3. Play track 23 once for ex.2: Why is John talking to the receptionist? Key: Because he wants some information about sightseeing in Moscow.
  4. Play it a second time for ex.3 — eight statements, right or wrong. A wrong one is not ticked, it is REPAIRED: say what the recording actually said. Key: 1 ✗ (He has a sister.) · 2 ✓ · 3 ✗ (It's not very far.) · 4 ✗ (It's on the other side of the street map.) · 5 ✓ · 6 ✗ (No, it isn't his.) · 7 ✓ · 8 ✓

Speaking · the model dialogue
class → pairs
Why: The phrases are given before they are needed, and repeated after the recording, so the productive stage is not a guessing game. This is the scaffold, not the performance.

WHAT WE DO: track 24: what John and his family are going to do, and the phrases that got them there

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1, one listening: They want to go to the space museum.
  2. Play track 24 again, ex.2, pausing after every line — the class repeats it chorally (TB p.42 asks for exactly this).
  3. The model on the page: SB p.23 ex.2, the model to be repeated line by line: Can you give me some information about the space museum, please? — Yes, certainly. The museum's near the centre, and you can book online. You'll really enjoy it. — That's perfect. Thanks. — It's the best way to learn about the history of our country. — That's a really good idea. Oh, by the way, have you got any information about the Kremlin? — Yes, of course. It's all on our website. Have a good day.
  4. Pull four phrases out onto the board — they are the scaffold for the next stage: Can you give me some information about …? · Have you got any information about …? · Oh, by the way … · Can you repeat that, please?
  5. ex.4: build the five questions from the prompts, in pairs, 3 minutes. Key: 1 What's the address? — It's 24 Green Street. · 2 Is it open every day? — No, it isn't. It's closed on Sundays. · 3 What time does it close? — It closes at 5 pm. · 4 How much are the drinks? — All the drinks are 50p. · 5 What's the web address? — It's www.tourvisit.com
  6. The poster they read the answers off: TOURIST INFORMATION CENTRE · 24 Green Street · Monday–Saturday · Hours 9 am – 5 pm · Coffee, tea and juice: 50p · Come in for maps and a chat · Visit www.tourvisit.com
📺 On screen: track 24 in the whiteboard app with the dialogue on the screen (Backup: read both parts aloud yourself from the book, twice, and write the four phrases on the board)

62–78′
🎯 Speaking out · tourist information
pairs, rotating
Why: The productive outcome: a real exchange of information with roles, a swap and a new partner. Two rounds, because the second one is always the one that flows.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 p.23: a role-play at a tourist information centre about a city they both know

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it up in one minute: Choose a city you both know. Four places a tourist would want. One of you is the tourist, one is the clerk.
  2. Round 1, 5 minutes: everybody is the tourist. The clerk may invent everything, but must answer in a full sentence.
  3. Bell. Round 2, 5 minutes: swap roles. Same city, different four places.
  4. Round 3, new partner, 4 minutes — and one added rule: The tourist must ask ONE question that is not on the list.
  5. Two things only get watched: did the tourist get the information, and did anybody answer with a single word. Everything else goes on the correction sheet in silence.
📺 On screen: the four phrases and a timer on the screen (Backup: the phrases stay on the board from the previous stage and you time it on the wall clock, calling the swap yourself)
👦 Students do: run the conversation twice with a role swap, then once with a new partner. Expect round 2 to be twice as fluent as round 1. Typical error: the clerk answers Yes and stops — the rule «a full sentence» is on them, not on the tourist

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can ask for and understand information at a hotel or an information desk, using the unit's eight holiday words.
  • Subsidiary. Reading three short texts and matching seven questions to them by meaning (SB p.22 ex.2); listening for gist and for detail (track 23); recycling the past simple in a true story of their own.
  • Personal. Bring back yesterday's past simple errors from the correction sheet — three sentences, no names attached. And hold the «one word is not an answer» rule in the role-play, gently but every time.
By the end, students can…
  • use eight holiday words correctly, including the uncountable luggage
  • match a question to the right text and quote the line that proves it
  • ask for information politely and react to the answer
  • tell about a real trip in five past simple sentences

Form

Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«The word metro is in two of the three texts — so which one is the answer?» → the one that says the same THING as the question, not the one that repeats the word.
«How much luggage did you take?» — can I answer three luggages? → no, luggage is uncountable: a lot of luggage, two suitcases.
You did not catch what the receptionist said — what do you do? → Can you repeat that, please? Silence is not an answer.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They answer the matching questions from memory, without going back to the textThe rule is mechanical and it works: an answer counts only with a finger on the line, and in the pair check the partner reads out the line, not the letter.
Monique and Carla get mixed up — both of them talk about the aquariumSeparate them on the board in advance with one contrasting word each: Monique — presents and Russian; Carla — metro and street food; Olivia — on foot and Old Arbat.
In the role-play the stronger student takes all the air timeFix the roles hard and swap them on a bell: first everybody is the tourist, then everybody is the clerk. Nobody chooses their role.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
