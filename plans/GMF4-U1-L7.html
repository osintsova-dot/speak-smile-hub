Give Me Five! 4 · Unit 1 “Let's eat!” · Lesson 7 — Unit 1 Test
A1+ young learners (~10–11 y.o., confident readers) · 60 min · Summative test (Listening · Reading · Writing · Speaking) · model-first, low-stakes · desks + quiet side table
Week 05.10–11.10 · Lesson 10📝 TEST~12 pupils · individual workHigher-level test · /80 marks
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. To measure fairly what each pupil has learnt in Unit 1 — ingredients & cooking verbs, the quantifiers a lot of / some / a few / a little, and need to (do) — across listening, reading, writing and speaking.
  • Subsidiary. Give a calm, well-modelled test so the score reflects knowledge, not test-navigation or nerves; run a short 1:1 oral (translation pairing) in parallel; close on a self-efficacy note.
  • Personal. Keep instructions crystal-clear (ICQ each task before pupils start); protect the quiet; never announce marks aloud; normalise “this is just to see what you know”.
By the end, the teacher can…
  • report each pupil's Unit 1 grasp of ingredients, cooking verbs, quantifiers & need to;
  • hear each pupil read English↔Russian pairs aloud (oral check);
  • spot who needs a light re-teach before Unit 2 — with no child feeling “tested to failure”.

Structure
I need a lot of / some / a few (nuts). · He needs a lot of / some / a little (cream). · What do we / does he need to do? — need(s) to (chop / heat / mix…).
Form
a few + countable plural (nuts) · a little + uncountable (cream, sugar) · some / a lot of both. need to + base verb.
Pron. watch
(oral check) /ʃ/ in sugar; final cluster in biscuits; coconut stress; raisins /ˈreɪznz/.
Phonics
Not a separate test section this level — i_e–ie (L5) is not examined here. Spelling is checked inside Writing ex 5 (anagrams).
Concept checks (before the test) · Assumptions
“A few or a little for sugar?” → a little (you can't count it).
“Chop — do we do it with a knife or a spoon?” → knife (verb = check meaning, not translate).

Assumptions: pupils are confident readers & writers; all six lessons of Unit 1 are done and the L6 Review is complete; pupils know the coursebook test icons (circle / tick / match). Stakes are low — this informs teaching, it is not a public ranking. Groups: whole class writes silently; the teacher pulls pupils one at a time (or pairs) to the side table for the oral pairing.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Test anxiety — a pupil freezes at “test”Reframe: This just shows me what you already know. You've practised all of it. Slow, calm voice; no timer race.
Pupil doesn't understand the task, not the language (construct-irrelevant)Model each rubric first with a throw-away example (not a real answer); ICQ Do you circle one or two?
Missed the listening audioPlay each listening track twice; pause between items; nobody is penalised for one hearing.
Oral pairing feels like being “put on the spot”Do it privately at the side table, quiet voice; start with an easy pair; never announce the score.
Fast finishers disturb the quietSilent challenge card: Write one more sentence — what do YOU need to make a cake?
Classroom management — calm test signals
“Eyes on your own paper” — set once, warmly, at the start; not policing.
Silent hand-up — a question? raise a hand, teacher comes over; no calling out.
“Traffic light” — desk card green = fine · yellow = a question · nobody speaks.
Countdown (gentle) — “Two minutes left — finish your sentence.”
Volume floor — lower arm = quieter; the room stays at whisper for the oral corner.
“Pens down, thank you” — clean, unhurried collection of papers.

0–4′
Settle & reassure
desks

Calm entry. Pupils sit at spaced desks, papers face-down. Greet warmly, unhurried: Good morning. Today's a quiet lesson — a little test on Unit 1. This just shows me what you know, and you've done all of it already.

Reset the body (physiological state). Sit tall, shoulders down, one slow breath in… and out. Ready. Point to the desk “traffic-light” cards: Green means you're fine. Yellow means you have a question — hand up, I'll come to you.

 test papers (face-down) · traffic-light cards · spaced seating
4–10′
Model-first walkthrough
board

Show HOW, not the answers. On the board, one throw-away example per rubric so the mark measures knowledge, not test-reading:

① Listen and circle — You circle ONE letter, a, b or c. ICQ: How many do you circle? (→ one) · ② Read and tick — one box per item · ③ Read and write (recipe gap-fill) — one word on each line · ④ Order, write and match — un-jumble the letters, then match the picture (e.g. iol → oil → d) · ⑤ Write the questions, look and write the answers — model item 1 only: What does Ben need to do? — He needs to heat the water.

If you're not sure, do the ones you know first, then come back. Nobody talks — hand up if you need me.

 board · one example per task (not real answers)
10–22′
📝 Listening [ /20 ]
desks, whole class

Turn over your papers. Page 1 — Listening. Pencils ready.

Ex 1 — Listen and circle (Track 04) [ /10 ]. 6 questions (what to buy · what Richard's going to make · how much salt · what to do next · what they don't need · what to add). Play twice, a short pause between items. Circle one letter each time.

Ex 2 — Listen and match, 2 extra names (Track 05) [ /10 ]. Match 6 chefs in the picture to names (Fred · Peter · Mary · William · Helen · Robert · Emma · Sarah). Model given: Robert = 3. Play twice. Two names are extra — you won't use them.

Everyone listens together (audio is the one section that must be synchronised). Well done — page 1 finished. Turn to page 2.

22–45′
📝 Reading + Writing [ /40 ]
desks, silent & individual

Silent, at own pace. Pupils work through pages 2–3; the teacher circulates quietly and, in parallel, runs the oral corner (row below).

Reading p.2. Ex 3 — Read and tick [ /10 ]: 6 picture pairs, choose the one that matches (a little butter · some jam in the fridge · isn't any flour · a lot of milk · pass the salt · a few biscuits). Ex 4 — Read and write [ /10 ]: read the Banana Split recipe, fill 6 gaps (e.g. fruit given) with fruit / banana / mix / sweet / two / straight away-type words.

Writing p.3. Ex 5 — Order, write and match [ /10 ]: un-jumble & match to the fridge (iol→oil (d) · trubte→butter · usrag→sugar · remac→cream · nrsiias→raisins · tbcisisu→biscuits). Ex 6 — Write the questions & answers [ /10 ]: cooking-verb pictures — What does Ben need to do? — He needs to heat… (they/they/Sally/they/Jack → chop · mix · pour · add · stir).

Gentle checkpoint at 40′: Five minutes. Finish the question you're on. Fast finishers get the silent challenge card.

 Test p.2–3 · pencils · silent challenge card · red pen (teacher, later)
22–45′
(parallel)
📝 Speaking — oral pairing [ /20 ]
quiet side table · 1:1 / small groups

Runs during the silent writing. Call pupils one at a time (or in twos) to the side table, ~1.5–2 min each; the rest keep writing. Whisper voices only.

The task — match & read the pair aloud. Spread the Unit 1 word cards: English cards (flour · oil · nuts · biscuits · sugar · salt · raisins · butter · coconut · cream · stir · mix · chop · heat · pour · add) against Russian cards / picture cards. The pupil makes the pairs — English ↔ Russian (or picture ↔ word) — and reads each pair aloud: flour — мука · chop — резать… ~6–8 pairs each, mixing ingredients & verbs.

Model first, then let go. Demonstrate one pair yourself, then the pupil works. Prompt, don't correct harshly: Good — try this one. Do not say the score aloud. Quietly note ✓/half on your list. While waiting, the others simply carry on with the written test — nobody is idle.

 English word cards · Russian / picture cards · teacher tally list (silent)
45–52′
Finish & hand in
desks

Two minutes — check your name is on the paper, then read your answers through once. Encourage a quiet self-check (physiological calm, no rush).

Pens down, thank you. Collect papers unhurriedly, face-down, so no one sees anyone else's work. Confirm every pupil has done the oral corner; catch the last one or two now.

 completed papers · teacher folder
52–57′
Low-stakes debrief
desks → circle

No answer-race, no marks. Attribution-focused reflection (self-efficacy): Which task felt easiest today? Why? — hands up, a few share. Name the strategy, not the ability: You did well on the recipe because you read the whole text first — that's a reader's trick.

Quick “I can” pulse (private, on fingers under the desk): Show me — 1 to 5 — how sure you feel about ‘a few / a little'. This tells you who to re-touch in Unit 2, without any child feeling exposed.
