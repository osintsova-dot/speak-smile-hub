🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The adjectives arrive not as a list but as the currency of an argument: without smart / casual / uncomfortable / old-fashioned there is nothing to defend a position with on air. The listening is built in as intelligence from the field — three students have already said what our crews will need. The unit subskill (listening to the interviewer's QUESTIONS, not only the answers) is served as a radio presenter's craft: the question tells you whether a fact or an opinion is coming.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“School radio, live. One day a year the uniform disappears — No Uniform Day. Our station is running the debate, and the school votes at the end of the show. But a debate needs WORDS: smart, casual, baggy, old-fashioned, uncomfortable. First, intelligence: three students already spoke on air — Ella, Dylan and Kate. Listen to what they were ASKED, not only what they answered. Then take the mic.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The school radio is broadcasting about the day without uniform; at the end the school votes. First we listen to the report (Ella, Dylan, Kate), then we take the microphone ourselves and defend a position with adjectives.

crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 p.41 (8 descriptions ↔ photos A–H) as a race → ex.2 seven pairs of opposites in a Showdown → ex.3 questions in pairs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 as a race: Eight descriptions, eight photos. Two minutes. Key: 1 B · 2 D · 3 G · 4 F · 5 C · 6 E · 7 A · 8 H.
  2. ex.2 Kagan Showdown: I say an adjective — the boards go up with its opposite. Key: 1 c (smart–casual) · 2 f (thick–thin) · 3 g (long–short) · 4 a (baggy–tight) · 5 b (comfortable–uncomfortable) · 6 d (trendy–old-fashioned) · 7 e (expensive–cheap).
  3. Pronunciation straight away: comfortable — THREE syllables, tight — the gh is silent, expensive — the stress is on the second syllable.
  4. ex.3 Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: Do you prefer casual clothes or smart clothes? Do you like baggy jeans? — 8 swaps.
📺 On screen: SB p.41 ex.1–3 · mini-boards · question cards · timer
👦 Students do: they take the adjectives in pairs and put them straight into questions. Expected: opposites stick better than a list. Error: a jacket thick — I gesture “adjective first!”

Listening · dispatch from No Uniform Day
screen · desks

WHAT WE DO: two listenings to track 21: no.1 — which QUESTIONS Andy asks (the subskill) → no.2 — the facts, ex.4–6

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before the audio: Don't catch answers. Catch QUESTIONS. Write down every question Andy asks.
  2. Listening no.1 → checking the questions: What's your name? · Can you tell our listeners about what you're wearing today? · What do you think of No Uniform Day? · Do you like your uniform?
  3. Look at your questions. Which answers will be FACTS, which will be OPINIONS? — that is the whole subskill in one move.
  4. Listening no.2 with the support table: ex.4 (who likes the day — key: Ella yes · Dylan no · Kate yes) → ex.5 (who is wearing what — Ella: a long dress and boots · Dylan: jeans and a hoodie · Kate: trousers and a top) → ex.6 (key: 1 smart · 2 casual · 3 expensive · 4 uncomfortable and old-fashioned).
  5. If they are drowning — a third listening with a pause after each interview.
📺 On screen: audio track 21 (backup — I read the script in roles with a helper from the class) · the support table for the weaker ones
👦 Students do: they hold the order subskill → facts. Expected: everyone catches the questions, the facts come on the second listening. Error: Dylan is “against uniform”; in fact he is against the DAY and likes his uniform. I set that trap deliberately

WHAT WE DO: an interview relay: in 5 minutes take a micro-interview from 5 classmates on the formula “question + adjective”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Find Someone Who with a microphone (a marker pen will do instead): Find someone who is wearing something comfortable. Someone who thinks our uniform is old-fashioned. Someone who prefers casual clothes.
  2. The rule: write down the NAME + the adjective + the reason. Five lines in 5 minutes.
  3. Constraint: you may not write down the same person twice.
📺 On screen: “Find Someone Who” sheets · timer (backup — three questions on the board)
👦 Students do: they walk round and collect quotes. Expected: they gather the material for the broadcast themselves — and then use it in the product. Error: one-word answers — I insist on an adjective + a reason

PRODUCT · the school radio show
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: 60 seconds on air: the crew's position on No Uniform Day + 2 arguments with adjectives + a secret vote

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 6 minutes of preparation: Your show: an intro, one interview quote, two arguments, a closing line. Everyone owns a block.
  2. Sixty seconds on air. Judges: fluency · accuracy · adjectives used.
  3. I count the adjectives out loud after each show — that is the richness score.
  4. The vote is secret, with stickers: No Uniform Day: yes or no? You can't vote for your own crew's side. I put the result on the unit wall.
📺 On screen: 60-second timer · stickers · the rubric (backup — we vote with our eyes shut, hands up)
👦 Students do: they perform and vote. Expected: the argument taken from the audio decides the votes. Error: “It's bad” with no adjective — I take the microphone away (by the rule announced in the brief)

WB Unit 3 · points on the leaderboard
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can describe and evaluate clothes with 14 adjectives (and their opposites) and run a 60-second radio debate on school uniform, using arguments taken from a listening text.
  • Subsidiary. Subskill: listening to questions and answers — the interviewer's question as the key to the answer; ex.4–6 p.41 (who likes the day · who is wearing what · four adjectives); ex.7 the pros and cons of uniform.
  • Personal. The listening runs twice: the first time we catch Andy's QUESTIONS, the second time the facts. Do not change the order! The weaker ones get the support table for the second listening.
By the end, students can…
  • use 14 adjectives to describe clothes and name their opposites
  • catch the interviewer's questions, not only the answers
  • fill a who-wears-what table from a live report
  • defend a position on school uniform in 60 seconds

Pron. watch
from the Wordlist: comfortable /ˈkʌmftəb(ə)l/ (three syllables!) · expensive /ɪkˈspensɪv/ · casual /ˈkæʒuəl/ · old-fashioned /ˌəʊld ˈfæʃ(ə)nd/ · tight /taɪt/ (the gh is silent)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“a jacket thick” — is that possible? → no: the adjective goes before the item — a thick jacket.
Does smart mean clever? → about clothes it means neat and dressed up; the opposite is casual.
What is the first thing we catch in an interview? → the presenter's QUESTION: it tells you whether a fact or an opinion is coming.
Are cheap and expensive about quality? → about price; quality is a different word.

Assumptions: SB p.41, KTP pair 24. Some adjectives are familiar (long, short, cheap), new ones: baggy, casual, trendy, old-fashioned, tight, and smart in its “clothes” meaning.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They listen straight “for the facts” and miss the subskillI hold the two listenings firmly: no.1 — only Andy's questions (we write them down), no.2 — the facts. I check the questions BEFORE the second one.
The 14 adjectives blur into mushWe work in PAIRS of opposites (7 pairs), not as a list: a pair is learnt and tested as one unit.
The debate collapses into “uniform is rubbish”The broadcast rule: every opinion = an adjective + a reason. “It's bad” with no adjective does not get the microphone.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
