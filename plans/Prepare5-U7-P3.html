🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use seven fixed phrases with in (in detail, in fact, in general, in future, in advance, in particular, in the end) in speech, and write a 100-word online review of a product: informal, opinionated, linked, and ending with a clear recommendation.
What is genuinely hard
The phrases cannot be worked out and cannot be translated one-to-one — in fact does not mean фактически in most of its uses, in future is not the same as in the future, and in the end is not at the end. They are chunks. The writing is hard in a different way: at B1 a review collapses into a list of features, because the student knows the adjectives and not the CONNECTORS. Another advantage is that… is what turns a list into a text.
Typical error to expect
«in a fact», «in the general», «on advance» — the phrases with in are fixed chunks with no logic to reason from, and they are among the commonest distractors in the lexical section. They are learned whole or they are lost.
The other predictable loss is register: half the class will write a school essay about a camera. A review is spoken English on paper — contractions, exclamation, a person behind it.
Why the lesson is built this way
The phrases are taught in the only way fixed chunks can be — matched, then met in a connected text, then immediately spoken seven times in the extra-activity questions on p.136, which are built so that each question needs one phrase. That is fifteen minutes and it is enough. The writing half is a model-first lesson: the class reads one review, works out what makes it one (the highlighted words go into a three-column table), practises the negative half separately, decides what a recommendation sounds like, and only then writes. The model answer goes on the board AFTER they have written, never before.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Here is a camera you throw in the air. Thirty-six lenses fire at once and you get a photograph of everything around you. Today: seven phrases you cannot guess — and then you write the review that makes somebody buy it, or not.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: match the phrases to meanings, then meet them all inside one text

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: what these things do, then read the model and identify it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Then give the facts, ninety seconds, because they are what makes the lesson memorable: the three products on the page, in one sentence each, because the vocabulary comes out of them: the Panono is a ball camera with THIRTY-SIX lenses that fire at the same moment and are stitched into one panorama — you can hold it, mount it or throw it in the air, and it is expensive enough that you catch it · the Polaroid is an instant film camera whose photograph develops itself, once used for ID cards precisely because the image cannot be altered · the GoPro is waterproof to ten metres, worn on the body so the hands stay free, and some models take voice commands

WHAT WE DO: the partner's one required improvement, then the Teacher's Book model

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Swap notebooks. Kagan RallyCoach, three minutes: the partner ticks the four checklist lines and writes ONE sentence that could be better, with a reason. Not «good», not a smiley.
  2. Two minutes: the writers read their partner's comment and fix that one sentence. Only that one.
  3. Now the model goes up, and only now: the Teacher's Book model answer, about a hundred words, for the board AFTER they have written their own: «This camera is brilliant. It looks amazing, and it's small and very easy to use. Another good aspect is the quality of the videos and photos, which are stunning, even if you use it when you are skydiving… The only disadvantage is that it has a short battery life. Overall, it's a nicely made camera which works exceptionally well and I highly recommend it.»
  4. Thirty seconds on one question: What does this one do that yours doesn't? Take two answers and leave it there — the point is noticing, not rewriting.
  5. Collect the notebooks; these are marked, and the marking is against the same four lines the class has been looking at all lesson.

Target language
Lexis
in detailin factin generalin futurein advancein particularin the endawesomeamazingimpressivereallyannoyinglylimiteddisappointing
Structure
In general, I don't buy things online. · In fact, it was cheaper than the shop. · Another advantage is that it fits in your pocket. · The only disadvantage is that you need a special app. · Overall, it's definitely worth saving up for!
Form
the seven phrases are FIXED: no article except in the end, no preposition changes, no plural · review connectors go at the START of a sentence and take a comma: For a start, … · Plus, … · Annoyingly, … · Overall, …
Pron. watch
the eight social-media verbs are easy to say and hard to stress in a sentence: the class will put the accent on the pronoun (I TAGGED her) where English puts it on the new information · the phrases with in run together and the vowel of in reduces to a schwa: /ɪnfækt/, /ɪnəˈvɑːns/, /ɪnpəˈtɪkjələ/ — say them as one word or they sound like a list · in the passive, was and were are weak forms: /wəzˈteɪkən/, /wəˈʃəʊn/, which is why students do not hear the passive in the listening
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«In future, I'll book earlier.» Am I talking about tomorrow or about always, from now on? → always, from now on. That is why it is not in THE future.
«I didn't want to go, but in the end I went.» Does in the end mean at the end of the evening? → no, it means finally, after a lot of thinking.
A review and a school essay — which one has exclamation marks and contractions? → the review. Why? → because someone is speaking in it.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The phrases are learned as translations and misusedThey are never translated in this lesson. Each one is met inside a sentence, then produced inside a question that only fits that phrase. If a student asks for the Russian, give the situation instead.
The review turns into a list of featuresThe three-column table is the fix, and it is filled by the students from the model. Before they write, three connectors must be on their plan — For a start, Another advantage is that, The only disadvantage is that.
Half the class writes 60 words, half writes 200The word count is part of the task and is checked by the partner, not by you: the checklist's first line is «about 100 words — count them».
Peer feedback becomes «it's good»The checklist has four yes/no lines and one required improvement. The partner writes one sentence that could be better and says why. Nothing else counts as feedback.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
