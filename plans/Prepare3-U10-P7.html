🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that the vocabulary and both passives of Units 19–20 work without the book — and walk out with the nine study techniques that decide how much of it survives into next lesson's mock exam.
What is genuinely hard
Two passives that look the same and are one word apart, and a make / do list that has no rule in it. Both decay fast, and both are fixed by retrieval rather than by re-explanation.
Typical error to expect
My bike is stolen last year instead of was stolen — the right structure in the wrong tense, because the time expression was never read (SB p.113 ex.5) [high priority, A2/B1]
I make my homework / I do a mistake instead of do your homework and make a mistake. There is no rule to reason it out from; it is a list, and SB p.111 ex.5 is that list [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
Three parts, and the middle one is the reason the lesson is shaped like this. Thirty minutes of retrieval practice first — two stations and a game — because pulling the block out of memory is what fixes it and it shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, exactly which form still breaks. Then twelve minutes on the Life Skills spread «Taking exams», which is the one Life Skills page the course keeps and it is kept for exactly this position: everything on it is technique, technique is the only part of an exam result that can be improved in an afternoon, and next lesson is the mock. It also means the class walks into the mock briefed rather than surprised. Then the test itself, thirty minutes, nothing on it unseen. The review game is placed last of the three revision stages on purpose: a class that has just laughed sits a paper with a lower pulse than a class that has just been corrected.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Three things today. First we play the block back — everything you learnt about food and about lives that changed. Then twelve minutes on something that is not English at all: how to take an exam without losing marks to things that have nothing to do with your English. And then you show me the block. Thirty minutes, nothing on the paper you have not seen.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast round of the block's words, and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — block rewind. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade as a hot round: cards for the block's words — the cooking verbs, the eight ingredients, the make/do phrases and the twelve life changes. Two trades each. Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest: Everything you just said is on today's paper.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Three parts today: thirty minutes to remember, twelve minutes on how to take an exam, thirty minutes to show me the block.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, read the words out from a list and students give the phrase back
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the nerves dropping. Typical problem: they stumble on garlic and on start working or training

Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: put the test specification up in advance — transparency is what takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the seven parts of the test on the board: cooking verbs · ingredients and make/do · change and life changes · present passive in a text · past passive · find the mistake · the sounds.
  2. Look at the board. Have we done all of this in class? Yes or no? The class answers chorally — the answer is always yes.
  3. Say the honest thing about the paper, because it is worth saying: There is no official Cambridge test for these two units. I wrote this one, in the same shape as the real ones. Every item comes from a page you have open in front of you.
  4. Say the Review 5 boundary out loud: The book's review page today is for four units. Two of them were three weeks ago. We take only our half.
  5. The rule of the day: Your score goes in the register. Your five mistakes go in your notebook. Next lesson is the mock exam — that list IS your revision plan.
📺 On screen: the seven specification lines in chalk — the board is the medium and they stay up for the whole first half of the lesson, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the specification and answer chorally. Expect the honesty about the paper to raise the effort rather than lower it. Typical problem — somebody asks whether a test the teacher wrote «counts»; answer that it counts exactly as much as the others, and that the items come from the book either way

WHAT WE DO: the Units 19–20 items of Review 5 Vocabulary (SB p.118) plus a written spelling round

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, on the book page: Review 5 Vocabulary ex.2 — but only the FOOD half of the word box. Of the eight words (bowl, chapter, cover, knife, plate, shelf, spoon, title), ours are bowl, knife, plate and spoon; chapter, cover, shelf and title are Unit 18. The definitions key: 1 chapter · 2 bowl · 3 knife · 4 shelf · 5 plate · 6 title · 7 cover · 8 spoon — run items 2, 3, 5 and 8.
  2. Round 2: Vocabulary ex.3, items 3, 4, 6 and 7 — the life-change phrases. Ours are items 3, 4, 6 and 7. Key for the whole exercise: 1 found out · 2 'll give back · 3 took exams · 4 didn't change schools · 5 take out · 6 was born · 7 find part-time work · 8 Did you take back. Items 1, 2, 5 and 8 are the Unit 18 phrasal verbs — skip them.
  3. Round 3, books closed: Kagan Showdown — you say the word in Russian, the team writes it in English and raises the board on command. Put the traps in deliberately: garlic · potatoes · vegetables · married · biography · ingredients.
  4. Round 4, 90 seconds, and it is the one that matters most: MAKE or DO? You call the noun, the team calls the verb. the bed · a mistake · the shopping · your homework · a mess · the dishes.
  5. Every misspelling goes into a separate «watch out» column on the board — it stays there right up to the test.
📺 On screen: the Review 5 vocabulary exercises on the screen with the Units 17–18 items already crossed out (Backup: they are printed on SB p.118 — put a pencil cross against the book items of ex.2 and items 1, 2, 5 and 8 of ex.3 before the lesson, and run the rounds from the page)
👦 Students do: do the two book exercises, then spell and sort against the clock. Expect the oral rounds to be strong and the written round to drop. Typical error: vegtables and marryed — which is exactly why the «watch out» column is needed

WHAT WE DO: the Units 19–20 items of Review 5 Grammar (SB p.118) — the two passives side by side

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Grammar ex.1, items 3 and 4 only — and they are deliberately one of each. 3 The batteries are included in the price (present simple passive, Unit 19) · 4 This present was given to me by my father (past simple passive, Unit 20). Items 1, 2 and 5–8 are the Unit 17 reflexives and the Unit 18 conditional.
  2. Grammar ex.2, all six items, past passive: 1 was written · 2 was found · 3 were baked · 4 was opened · 5 wasn't made · 6 were sold
  3. Grammar ex.3, all ten gaps, present passive against present active — this is the single most useful five minutes of the revision half, because it is the only task where the choice has to be made. «What happens in an ice cream factory?»: 1 are mixed · 2 is cooked · 3 are added · 4 include · 5 put · 6 is frozen · 7 comes · 8 is put · 9 is sent · 10 are sold
  4. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the reason, not the answer — Can the subject do that by itself?
  5. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Gap six — number fours, boards up!
  6. Boil it down to two lines on the board: Now → is / are + third form. Then → was / were + third form. «By» only if it is news.
📺 On screen: the Review 5 grammar exercises on the screen, one item at a time, with the Units 17–18 items skipped (Backup: they are printed on SB p.118 — uncover them on the page with a ruler and keep the two-line summary in chalk on the board)
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with a reason, then report by number. Expect ex.2 to be clean and ex.3 to expose who is switching everything to the passive. Typical error: gap 4 (include) and gap 5 (put) made passive because the rest of the text is

Review game
groups of 3–4
Why: A game that hides revision inside a reason to speak, placed last on purpose: a class that has just laughed walks into a test with a lower pulse than a class that has just been corrected.

WHAT WE DO: the game «Crazy Sentences! (Review game Units 17–20, Cambridge, PHOTOCOPIABLE)» — three gaps per card, folded over and passed on

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in ninety seconds: Groups of three or more, one set of cards and one sheet of A4 per player. The cards go face down. One player picks a card and reads out the FIRST line only — «The cookies are made in a big ______,» — and everybody secretly writes a word at the top of their own sheet, folds the paper over so it cannot be seen, and passes it to the left. Then the second line is read, then the third. Each card has three gaps, so the sheets go round three times. Then everything is unfolded and read out, and the group decides which version is the funniest.
  2. ⚠️ Important, before you photocopy: ⚠️ The pack covers four units. Ours are six cards: 3, 4, 6, 10, 11 and 13. Card 3 «The cookies are made in a big ___ / explained the ___ / and they are filled with ___» is the present simple passive plus fill. Card 4 «Before I do the cleaning I always ___ / then I drive to the ___ / and eat a fried ___» is the do collocations plus fried. Card 6 «There's too much salt and pepper in my ___ / then she put her finger in the ___ / and said, Why don't you ___» is the ingredients. Card 10 «She hurt her ankle while she was ___ / then she was bitten by a ___ / and finally she made a big mistake with the ___» is the past passive plus make a mistake. Card 11 «This ___ really changed my life / It was given to me by ___ / while I was living in ___» is change plus the past passive. Card 13 «If I get married to that ___ / I'll be unhappy, said the woman with two ___ / I prefer to ___» is get married. Cards 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12 and 14 are Units 17–18 and were played in the block 9 review — pull them out before you photocopy.
  3. How to score it, if you want it scored: There is no scoring built in, and it does not need one. If you want it competitive, each group reads out one sentence at the end and you award points for originality, imagination, humour, good vocabulary, effort, spelling and grammar — the game book says explicitly to use your own judgement.
  4. Why it is placed here rather than earlier: The game is the only stage of the revision half where nobody can be wrong, and it is deliberately placed last: a class that has just laughed walks into a test with a lower pulse than a class that has just been corrected.
  5. Run four cards, not six. Each group reads out its best sentence at the end and the class votes on the funniest.
📺 On screen: one printed set of the six chosen cards per group and one sheet of A4 per student, run off in advance and cut along the dotted lines — paper is the medium here, so no backup is needed; with no printer, read the three lines of each card aloud yourself and the game works identically
👦 Students do: write, fold, pass and unfold. Expect the loudest eight minutes of the block and at least one sentence the class will quote for a fortnight. Typical error: a word that does not fit the gap grammatically — the group notices it when the sentence is read out, which is the correction

WHAT WE DO: SB p.116–117: the nine study techniques, and the briefing for next lesson's mock

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say why this is here, in one sentence: This is the only Life Skills spread kept in the course, and it is kept for one reason: it is the last lesson before the mock exam. Everything on it is technique, and technique is the only part of an exam result a student can improve in one afternoon.
  2. The box first, read together: The Life Skills box itself, three lines: when you have an exam you should organise your time, prepare what you need, and keep calm and don't worry. Exams make people nervous; preparing well and relaxing is what lets you do your best.
  3. ex.1, 90 seconds in pairs — five statements, true for you or not. I have a good memory. I find some exams difficult. I don't worry about exams. I can never finish exams in the time. I like doing exams.
  4. ex.2 and ex.3 together, 5 minutes: which of the six ideas does the text mention, and then the nine headings back into the nine tips. SB p.116 ex.2 — which of the six ideas the text actually mentions. Key (TB p.229): all of them except number 5, «talking to your teacher». Worth one extra question afterwards: how COULD talking to your teacher help? (they can explain what you still do not understand). SB p.117 ex.3 — put the nine headings back into the nine tips. Key (TB p.230): 1 c organise your study time · 2 g learn information in small blocks · 3 h draw pictures or create sentences · 4 a make mind maps · 5 e have a good breakfast · 6 f make sure you have breaks · 7 b get plenty of sleep · 8 i revise with a friend · 9 d exercise regularly. The pictures on the page show a, c, d and i.
  5. Kagan Think-Pair-Share on the only question that matters: Which THREE of these nine will you actually do before next lesson? Three, not nine.
  6. The checklist they take with them: The nine tips, in the order they appear on SB p.116, are the checklist the class carries into the mock exam next lesson: organise your study time · learn in small blocks · draw pictures or make sentences to remember things · make mind maps · have a good breakfast · take breaks and move every hour · sleep instead of messaging · revise with a friend by testing each other · exercise regularly.
  7. One line about next lesson so it is not a surprise: Next time you sit the listening paper and the reading paper of a real Cambridge practice test. Ninety minutes. It is not for a mark — it is to find out what to fix.
📺 On screen: the nine tips on the screen with the headings hidden (Backup: the whole spread is printed on SB p.116–117 — work from the paper book and write the nine headings on the board in a scrambled order for the class to place)
👦 Students do: match nine headings, then choose three to actually use. Expect «get plenty of sleep» and «revise with a friend» to be chosen most, and «make mind maps» least. Typical problem: they treat the page as filler — the sentence about next lesson is what stops that, so say it early rather than late

Test briefing

Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions for the paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go back to the «watch out» column on the board: the five or six words that were misspelt. Look at these for thirty seconds. Then I wipe them.
  2. Instructions: 30 minutes, seven parts, 53 marks; phones in the bag; finished early — turn the paper over and write out the nine study tips in your own words.
  3. Part 7 is done TOGETHER at the start: it is the pronunciation part and there is no recording, so you read the eight words aloud, twice each, and then everyone works alone.
  4. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  5. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; everything else in the room is switched off so there is nothing to look at
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect the «finished early» instruction to be taken seriously for once, because it is not a punishment. Typical problem: somebody asks whether the pronunciation part is «for a mark» — it is, eight of them

WHAT WE DO: the block 10 test: seven parts, 53 marks, 30 minutes — all of it written by us to the shape of the official Cambridge Unit tests

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. You have thirty minutes. Start.
  2. Part 7 (the sounds) comes first and you read it: eight words, twice each, in the printed order, 3 minutes. After that students work alone.
  3. Walk round the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  4. Write the time left in chalk: 15 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  5. Anyone who finishes early turns the paper over and writes out the nine study tips from SB p.116 in their own words.
  6. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling — and check that every passive on your paper has two words in it.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board and the eight pronunciation words read aloud by you (Backup: there is nothing to fail here — the paper needs no technology at all, and the only «audio» is your own voice; if your voice has gone, write the eight words on the board and let the class sort them by sight, marking part 7 out of four instead of eight)
👦 Students do: work in silence for 30 minutes. Expect parts 1–3 to be closed fast and parts 4 and 6 to take the time. Typical error: the missing be in part 4 — which is the thing the two-minute check at the end is aimed at

Test wrap-up

Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework, and the bridge into the mock exam

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip of paper, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the feedback slot.
  3. Homework: rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly once the papers come back; and bring the nine study tips from SB p.116 written out in your own words.
  4. Say the next step plainly, and say it warmly, because it is the last teaching lesson of the year: That was the last new grammar of Prepare 3. Next lesson is the mock exam — ninety minutes, two papers, and no mark goes in the register. It exists so that you find out what to fix while there is still time to fix it.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is already full, simply read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to be about the choice between the two passives most often — that is the line for the mock exam analysis

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
