🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): With new students you name the grammar openly: today's topic is the past of the verb be. Clarity beats intrigue. There is still a wrapper, though: we make labels for a museum of extinct animals — it gives every sentence a purpose and naturally calls for affirmatives, negatives and questions. The order is strict: table → model → written practice → mini-test with self-check → product read from notes.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today's topic is simple and very useful: the past of the verb 'be'. Two words — WAS and WERE — and you can talk about everything that already happened. We'll build the tables together, practise in writing, check yourselves against the key, and at the end each crew writes a museum label for one animal that is gone: what it WAS, where it lived, how many there WERE. You'll have the table in front of you the whole time.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the topic is stated openly: the past of the verb be. Two words — was and were — and you can talk about everything that has already happened. We build the table, practise in writing, check ourselves against the key, and at the end write a label for the museum of extinct animals.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use was/wasn't, were/weren't and there was/were in affirmative, negative and question forms, and write a 4-line museum label about an extinct animal.
  • Subsidiary. The ex.1 tables are completed together; ex.2 in writing; the word-order rule in ex.3 is given straight and shown on examples; ex.4 building questions with support; ex.5 matching; ex.6 the Fun Facts Forum text; Brain teaser ex.7 optional.
  • Personal. Name the grammar as the topic — new students need clarity. The table stays on screen all lesson. The mini-test with self-check sits in the middle, so the mistake is found privately and before the product.
By the end, students can…
  • choose was or were by the subject
  • make negatives with wasn't and weren't
  • use there was with one thing and there were with many
  • ask Was …? / Were there …? and answer Yes, it was. / No, there weren't.

Structure
Hunting was a serious problem. · The Tasmanian tiger wasn't a tiger. · Steller's sea cows were very big sea mammals. · There was less forest for the gorilla. · There were more than a million saigas. · There weren't any laws. · Were they dangerous? — No, they weren't. · Was the dodo a bird? — Yes, it was. · Were there any baiji dolphins in Australia? — No, there weren't. · What was the problem? · Why was there a big campaign? · How many saigas were there in 1990?
Form
in a Yes/No question the verb comes FIRST: Was the dodo a bird? (TB key ex.3: first) · in a Wh- question the verb comes AFTER the question word: What was the problem? (TB key: after) · in questions with there was/were the verb comes BEFORE there: Were there rhinos in Cameroon? (TB key: before) · there was + singular/uncountable · there were + plural
Pron. watch
in a sentence was is short /wəz/, in a short answer it is full /wɒz/: It was a bird — Yes, it WAS; weren't is said in one piece /wɜː(r)nt/, not as two syllables
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«There were a big campaign» — what is wrong? → one campaign: There WAS a big campaign.
«Was they dangerous?» — correct it. → Were they dangerous? — they always takes were.
«There was any laws?» — and here? → Were there any laws? — in a question the verb comes BEFORE there.

Assumptions: SB p.76, syllabus pair 48 (both halves are grammar). They know the present simple of be; what is new is the second form and there was/were.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They mix up was and wereA prompt poster on the wall: I/he/she/it → was · you/we/they → were. Before every task, check the subject with a finger (TB routine).
There was/were — the choice depends on what comes AFTERTwo boxes on the desks: “one” and “many”. You put the card in a box, then you say the sentence.
They build a question like a statementGive them the formula on cards: WAS / WERE — who/what — the rest. Physically move the cards around on the desk.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
