WHAT WE DO: a calm start: breathing, a mood check, the day's rule «nobody fails a show»

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Circle: Breathe in — four, out — four. Twice.
  2. Check: Fist to five — how ready do you feel? (fist = scared, 5 = raring to go). I note the «fists» — they get a warm eye on them.
  3. The day's rule: Today nobody fails. You walk on stage — you already win. The rubric is for growth, the nominations are for everyone.

👦 Students do: Students breathe, show their readiness with fingers. Expected: 2–3 fists — plan them into the middle of the wave (not first, not last).

WHAT WE DO: we build the stage, I hand out judging trios by draw, we drill the floor protocol for 60 seconds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stage: a table/corner, a «microphone», a timer on screen. A backstage corner for the next speaker.
  2. Drawing lots for judging trios (cards). Judges: rubric, one tick per line, defend your threes.
  3. The floor protocol — rehearsed on me: I walk out, bow — the room: silence → talk → Thank you! → applause → 1 compliment + 1 question from the frame.
  4. Floor frames on the board: I like the part about… · My question is…
📺 On screen: judging cards · floor frames on the board · timer on screen (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: Students take their seats: stage/floor/judges/backstage. Expected: after the rehearsal, the protocol holds by itself.

WHAT WE DO: wave 1 performances: talk 2–3 min → a compliment + a question from the floor → judges record it; the next one prepares backstage

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hosting: Next on stage — Sasha! Give it up! (or an MC volunteer hosts from a cue card).
  2. After every talk: 1 compliment (specific!) + 1 question from the floor; the speaker answers — that earns points too.
  3. Judges silently fill in the rubric; I make notes for the nominations.
  4. Timing is strict: at 3:00 the «wrap up» gesture. Between talks — 30 sec, no more.
📺 On screen: stage · timer · judging cards · my nomination notes
👦 Students do: Half perform, half work as the floor and judges — everyone's busy for all 28 minutes. Expected: 6–7 talks of 3–4 min each, with protocol. Typical error: a «good job» compliment — I ask for specifics: «WHAT part?»

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Find Someone Who: an interval with movement — find someone who… (based on wave 1's facts); releasing tension before wave 2

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Find Someone Who (mini-form, 3 lines): Find someone who asked a question… whose hook was a NUMBER… who spoke without notes. Get the signature — full sentences: ‘Did you ask a question?’ GO!
  2. Everyone walks around and asks questions at once; 3 signatures = sit down.
  3. To wave 2: You watched the best — now steal their tricks. Stage in two minutes.
📺 On screen: Find Someone Who mini-forms (3 lines)
👦 Students do: Students walk around, ask full questions, collect signatures — movement + language, nobody stands against the wall. Expected: the excitement of «we've seen how it's done».

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Timed RoundRobin (while the judges tally up) → the nomination ceremony: every performer gets their own (Best hook / Best facts / Best voice / Best pause…)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Judges hand in their cards; I take 2 min to tally up. The class meanwhile — ▶ Kagan · Timed RoundRobin (fours, 30 sec per voice): Round the table: my favourite talk was… because… Thirty seconds each, timer runs, everyone speaks!
  2. Ceremony: I announce the nomination → a drum roll on the desks → the name → applause. EVERYONE gets a nomination.
  3. I announce gold runs (9/9) separately: +5 to the leaderboard.
  4. Remember September? Could you do THIS in September? That's your year.
📺 On screen: nomination plaques/cards (asset) · judging cards · leaderboard
👦 Students do: Students discuss their favourite talks and receive nominations to a drum roll. Expected: the «Best pause» and «Best comeback» nominations amuse everyone and flatten the hierarchy.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, every learner has performed their TED-talk on stage (2–3 min) and worked as a judge/audience with questions in English.
  • Subsidiary. The audience is a working role: 1 compliment + 1 question in English after every talk; judging against the rubric is honest and kind; a nomination — for EVERY performer.
  • Personal. The season's headline day #1: hold the «big stage» atmosphere (lights, silence, applause on protocol) and strict timing — otherwise the second wave won't fit. No public criticism: feedback only through the rubric and the nominations.
By the end, students can…
  • perform a 2–3 min talk from keywords
  • ask a follow-up question politely
  • judge with the rubric fairly
  • give a compliment that names something specific

Target language
Lexis

Structure
Audience: I like the part about… · My question is… · Can you tell us more about…? Hosting: Next on stage — …! Give it up for…!
Form
a question from the floor = a full question (not «why?»); a compliment = something specific («I like your hook about dogs», not «good job»).
Pron. watch
applause — after «Thank you!», not before; questions — hand up, wait for the microphone (any object works as a «microphone»).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Forgot your words — is that a disaster?» → No: you look at your keywords, take a pause, carry on. A pause is style.
«Is a 1 from the judge hurtful?» → 1–3 is a growth scale, not a judgement of the person; and everyone gets a nomination.
«Can I ask in Russian?» → A question from the floor — only in English: the frame on the board will help.

Assumptions: Everyone rehearsed at home (homework from P2), the order is known from the tickets. Half the class performs in wave 1, half in wave 2; anyone who missed P2 performs to a simplified frame (hook + 2 facts).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Panic before going onThe order is known in advance; a «backstage corner»: the next speaker prepares there with their keyword card; 4-4 breathing with the class before the wave.
The room gets noisy between talksThe audience is a job: compliment+question are compulsory, judges are writing; the host (me or an MC volunteer) holds the protocol.
A 30-second talk («forgot everything»)I calmly pull out rescue questions: What's your topic? Fact one? — I pull them through in interview mode; there'll be a nomination regardless.
A 6-minute talkThe timer is visible to everyone; at 3:00 — the «wrap up» gesture; at 3:30 the host thanks them with applause. Announced in advance — not hurtful.
Judges inflate scores for friendsJudging trios are shuffled by draw; a judge justifies every «3» out loud in one sentence.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
