🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The do/go/play collocations come in not as a table but as «the language of a timetable»: if the verb is wrong, the programme is not accepted for printing. The radio interview with Ben the hockey player is built in as intelligence: his arguments about team sports are ready-made fuel for defending the programme. The subskill (general idea) is protected by the fact that after the first listening the questions are ONLY about the general idea — the details come on the second run.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“The Live Well Sports Centre is yours. One problem: Saturday is empty. Your crew designs the new Saturday programme — five activities, times, and a reason for each. But the verb has to be right: we do karate, we go swimming, we play hockey. A wrong verb and the leaflet goes back to the printer. Intelligence first: a hockey player on the radio has arguments you'll want. Listen, then build.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The sports centre is yours, but Saturday is empty: the crew builds the new Saturday programme — five activities, times and a reason for each. The verb has to be right, or the leaflet is not printed. The intelligence is an interview with a hockey player.

WB Unit 2 · points on the leaderboard
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can use do/go/play with 19 sports correctly, catch the general idea plus 4 details from a radio interview, and pitch a Saturday programme with reasons.
  • Subsidiary. The collocation rule is drawn out of the advert (ex.1–3), not handed over as a list; ex.4 sorting against the clock; subskill listening for the general idea (listen to the end, then choose); ex.7–8 the details.
  • Personal. The listening runs strictly in two passes: no.1 — the general idea only, no.2 — the details. I don't ask for a fact after the first listening (the TB rule).
By the end, students can…
  • choose do, go or play correctly for 19 activities
  • explain the rule (ball games / -ing / everything else)
  • catch the general idea of an interview before the details
  • pitch a five-activity programme with reasons

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«I do swimming» — why not? → swimming ends in -ing → go swimming.
«I play athletics» — and here? → athletics is not a ball game → do athletics.
What is the general idea of a recording? → what the conversation is about AS A WHOLE. The details we catch on the second run.

Assumptions: SB p.29, KTP pair 16. Many of the sports are international words; the difficulty is not in the words but in choosing the verb and in the speed.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They listen for facts straight away and the subskill is lostI hold the two listenings firmly: no.1 — the general idea only (three options on the board), no.2 — the details. I check the general idea BEFORE the second one.
19 collocations at once do not fitThree baskets (do · go · play) physically on the desks; each crew owns one basket and teaches the others.
The programme becomes a pile of «fun stuff»The filter: every activity gets one WHY line, at least one of them from Ben's interview. The judges take points off for «because it's fun».
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
