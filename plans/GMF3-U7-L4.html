Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name personal belongings and ask & answer past yes/no questions: “Was the (watch) on the (table)? — Yes, it was. / No, it wasn't. Were the (earrings) in the (cupboard)? — Yes, they were. / No, they weren't.”
  • Subsidiary. Say the memory chant (CD3 33); consolidate was/were from L2–L3 — now in QUESTIONS; discover the rule — short answers Yes, it was / No, it wasn't (Grammar clue, CD3 34); play a real class memory game; write & ask past questions (AB follow-up).
  • Personal. Insist on the it was / they were short answers (never bare “yes/no”); model the singular/plural split (watch→it, earrings→they); keep the memory game moving — objects change fast.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name 6 personal belongings;
  • ask “Was/Were … ?” about the past;
  • answer short: “Yes, it was. / No, they weren't.”

Structure
Was the (watch) on the (table)? — Yes, it was. / No, it wasn't.  ·  Were the (earrings) in the (cupboard)? — Yes, they were. / No, they weren't.
Form
Was + singular (watch, bracelet, umbrella) → Yes, it was / No, it wasn't. Were + plural (earrings, sunglasses, keys) → Yes, they were / No, they weren't.
Pron. watch
earrings /ˈɪərɪŋz/; bracelet /ˈbreɪslət/; sunglasses — stress SUN-glasses; umbrella /ʌmˈbrelə/; jewellery not needed but plural s /z/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Were the earrings…? — earrings: one thing or many?” → many → were → they.
“Was the watch on the table? — short answer?” → Yes, it was / No, it wasn't (not just “yes”!).

Assumptions: was/were statements are active from L2–L3; belongings watch/keys/umbrella likely known, earrings/bracelet/sunglasses newer. The move from statements to yes/no QUESTIONS + short answers is today's challenge. Readers can read short belonging words.

27–37′
🔍 Was it there? · info-gap
mingle → desks

Model first. Two “crime scene” grids (A & B) show belongings in different places. Model publicly: Was the bracelet on the sofa? — No, it wasn't. It was under the mat.

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade / Talk Partners info-gap. Each detective holds a belonging card + a grid. Music → mingle; off → pair: A Were the sunglasses in the bag? B answers Yes, they were / No, they weren't. Tick the grid, TRADE cards, new partner. 5–6 exchanges.

✔ Assess: circulate — is it “Yes, they were” (not “Yes, they did”)? Recast, note names.

💪 Support: card shows the full question text. ⭐ Extension: follow-up: Where were they? — They were in the drawer.

📓 Interactive notebook — GMF3_Unit7_Lesson4_Memory_Was_Were_A5
A5 glue-in note — 6 personal belongings (earrings · bracelet · sunglasses · watch · keys · umbrella) + a ONE THING / MORE THAN ONE panel (Was it…? — Yes, it was. · Were they…? — Yes, they were.)
  • Glue the note in; pupils sort the 6 belongings into ONE THING and MORE THAN ONE columns themselves.
  • Class chants the pair off the note: One thing — WAS IT? More than one — WERE THEY?
  • Teams build true questions about objects on the teacher's tray; a point only if was/were matches singular or plural.
  • Everyone circles the plural -s on earrings · sunglasses · keys — that is the visible signal for WERE.
When: ~5 min inside the 37–46′ “Make true questions · build” stage (desks, 2 teams)Prep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Glue sticks + coloured pencils. A tray of real objects makes the questions true.
