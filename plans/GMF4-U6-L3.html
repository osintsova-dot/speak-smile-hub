Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to follow, read and act out the story «The boat race» and talk about a machine problem: “What's wrong with the boat? Why isn't it moving? — We can make a battery out of lemons.”
  • Subsidiary. Value — recycling batteries (what to do with dead batteries); break Luke's code (→ BATTERY — a hook into the story); recycle is/are used for from L2 & preview made of for L4; story skills — predict → listen → read along → Read & correct → act out; give every child a role (Lily · Josh · Dad · Ravi).
  • Personal. Let the story breathe — big voices, pauses, prediction; the “made of” idea is NOTICED today, taught properly in L4 — don't over-correct. Keep the act-out carousel fast so no group waits long.
By the end, pupils can…
  • retell 2–3 things that happen in the story;
  • read their role's lines from the book;
  • read & correct false statements: “No, Ravi made the boat.”

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“The boat won't move — is it windy? has it got enough batteries?” → not windy · only 3 batteries (needs 4).
“How do they fix it? What's the battery made of?” → a battery made of lemons (+ coins, paperclips, wires).

Assumptions: is/are used for is active from L2; the alphabet-number code was used earlier in the year (recycled here). Readers follow the text with a finger. The story previews made of, taught in L4. The recycling value connects to everyday life.

Story · listen & read
carpet

Listen 1 — gist (CD3 10, books closed). Listen: why isn't the boat moving? → only three batteries, and it isn't windy!

Listen 2 — read along (books open pp.62–63). Finger under the text. Pause per frame: What's wrong with the boat? Why isn't it moving? … We can make a sail. … We can make a battery out of lemons. Dad can help.

Values (act. 4, whole class). The children have old, dead batteries. What do you do with batteries that don't work? Where can you recycle batteries? What else can you recycle? 3–4 hands, recast key words.

20–28′
Read & correct · comprehension
carpet → desks

Read and correct (act. 3, Talk Partners). Sentences: Josh made the boat. Ravi brings four batteries. The sail helps the boat move. The battery is made of oranges. The children win the race. Pairs correct the false ones in full: No, Ravi made the boat. Every pair speaks.

Who said it? (Showdown). Mini-whiteboards: you read a line — “We can make a battery out of lemons.” Who says it? — all write the name, Show me! 4 rounds.

✔ Assess: the boards are your check — who follows the story? The rest sit with you in the board game.

💪 Support: point to the right frame in the book. ⭐ Extension: add a second true fact about that moment.

28–40′
Act out
carpet, groups of 4

Model a frame. You + 3 pupils play the boat-won't-move frame with big drama: Why isn't it moving? — Oh no! We've only got three batteries!

Groups of 4 (Lily · Josh · Dad · Ravi). Rehearse 2 frames with books as scripts. Rotate roles once.

Showtime. Each group performs ONE frame (30–40 sec — fast carousel, no long waits). Audience task: shout the fix at the end (A battery made of lemons!).

💪 Support: shortest lines / the “Oh no!” reaction for the shyest child. ⭐ Extension: add an extra line from the frame.

40–52′
Break the code · what stopped?
desks, groups

Use the code (act. 1). Luke's message: Yesterday, the … in my watch stopped! Decode with the A=153 key: 177-153-783-783-245-715-947 → BATTERY! That's what the children need in the story!

▶ Code-breaker teams (RallyCoach). Each group decodes a short machine word (fridge · toaster · robot) with the key; partner A reads a number, B finds the letter, then swap. Everyone reads the word aloud.

🎮 Projector option: show the code key on screen; a rotating child spins to a number, the WHOLE class calls the letter.

ICQ: Number 177 — which letter? (→ B!)
