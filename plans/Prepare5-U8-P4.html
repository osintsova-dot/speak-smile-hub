🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use ten words of film (animated, appear, come out, direct, director, musical, performance, recording, role, soundtrack) in the right word class, and read an interview closely — matching questions to paragraphs, then answering six detail questions.
What is genuinely hard
Half of this set is one root in three classes — direct, director, directed; perform, performance — and the stress moves when the class does: diRECT but DIRector. Russian gives false friends here too: «play» for performance and «part» for role. And come out is a phrasal verb whose meaning has nothing to do with either word.
Typical error to expect
«I liked the play of the actor», «the film goes out» — performance, not play; come out, not go out; role, not part-of-the-actor. Cinema vocabulary is full of near-translations that are wrong by one word.
«I went to cinema», «she is doctor», «in city where I live» — Russian has no articles at all, so they are not forgotten, they were never there. Every English noun needs a decision: a, the or nothing — and the decision is made about a hundred times in a hundred-word email.
Why the lesson is built this way
The vocabulary arrives inside five sentences about real films and real people — Star Wars, Fantastic Beasts, The Lion King, a Bond director — which is what makes it worth reading at sixteen, and three of the sentences have to be matched to photographs, so the reading is purposeful before the matching exercise begins. The listening then puts three teenagers choosing films in front of them. The reading text is the lesson's argument: Evan Hara was sixteen and still at school when his feature film premiered, and the questions ask how he fitted it in — which is a more interesting question for this class than any comprehension task about cinema. And every quoted sentence in that interview is direct speech, sitting there waiting for tomorrow's grammar.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“He was sixteen, still at school, and his film premiered with a red carpet and paparazzi — in Plano, Texas. He says he has zero free time. Today: the English for how films are made, and the interview with him.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.3–4: meanings matched with the bare infinitive, then the choice items

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3 individually, four minutes: ten words, ten meanings. The instruction that matters: give verbs in the BARE INFINITIVE — direct, appear, come out. the ten meanings: 1 animated (films) · 2 recording · 3 appear · 4 role · 5 director · 6 performance · 7 soundtrack · 8 directed · 9 come out · 10 musical
  2. Every confirmed word goes on the wall, in its column, written by the student who answered, with its class named aloud.
  3. Two minutes on stress, clapped: DIRector against diRECT, PERformance against perFORM, REcording (the thing) against reCORDing (the action). This is the pronunciation the exam hears.
  4. ex.4, three minutes: five choose-the-word items, first one together. 1 appear · 2 comes out · 3 recording · 4 director · 5 performance At the check, ask what class of word the gap needed — that is why the wrong option is wrong.
  5. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade, three minutes: everybody takes one word from the wall and finds three partners. Say the meaning, the partner says the word and its class.
  6. Fast finishers write one more item in the style of ex.4; collect two and use them in the check.
📺 On screen: the three-column wall filling with words and stress marks — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: match ten words alone, build the wall, clap the stress, then quiz three partners standing

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: predict, then match four questions to four paragraphs in three minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 first, books open at the photo: how old is he, and where is he from? Take the guesses, then tell them — sixteen, and Plano, Texas. The gap between the guess and the answer is the hook.
  2. Set the task before the reading: four questions, four paragraphs, THREE minutes, and new vocabulary is ignored. This is skimming, and the time limit is what makes it skimming.

  5. Ninety seconds in pairs on the question the text really asks: He says he has zero free time. Is that a price worth paying?

Structure
A lot of new films come out at special times of the year. · Eddie Redmayne has the lead role. · Sam Mendes has directed many of the Bond films. · The Lion King has a great soundtrack.
Form
one root, three classes: direct (verb) → director (person) → directed (past) · perform → performance · record → recording · come out is a phrasal verb: the film COMES OUT in May · animated is an adjective and sits before the noun: an animated film.
Pron. watch
the film words move their stress when they change class and the class will keep it still: DIRector but diRECT · PERformance but perFORM · REcording (noun) against reCORDing (verb) · ANimated has four syllables and the stress on the first · and in reported speech the backshifted forms are unstressed and swallowed — he'd, they'd, she'd — which is why students do not hear the shift in the recording
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«The film comes out in May» — is it being released or leaving? → released. Can I say goes out? → no, and it is the commonest error in this set.
«Her performance was fantastic» — the play or her acting in it? → her acting. What is the Russian instinct here? → to say play, and it is wrong.
DIRector and diRECT — same word? → same root, different stress and different class. Say both, twice.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Nobody has seen the films in the photographsIt does not matter, and saying so aloud helps: the sentences are about what the WORDS mean, not about the films. The warmer list gives every student at least one film they can name.
Word class confusion: «he is a very good direct»The wall is built in three columns for exactly this reason, and the stress is marked on the board. At the check, every answer is given with its class named: director — person, noun.
The reading is long and the weaker half stallsThe first task is paragraph matching, which needs skimming, not decoding — set three minutes and mean it. The detail questions come afterwards, with the text open.
Fast finishers in the vocabulary stageThe Teacher's Book task: write one more item in the same style as exercise 4 for the class, using film vocabulary. Collect two and use them at the check.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
