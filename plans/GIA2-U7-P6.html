🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The lesson is doubled up in the book (Real-world speaking p.91 + Writing pp.92–93), and that serves one idea: first learn to argue without wrecking the conversation, then lay somebody else's life out in four written paragraphs. The argument gives the energy, the writing gives the structure. The writing subskill (writing in paragraphs) comes in through the Trudy Ederle model: one paragraph = one idea, and anything longer than 50–60 words gets split.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two rounds today. Round one: the argument. Carmel says there are lots of great players. Louis says there's only one — Salah. Nobody wins, and that's the point: you'll learn to say 'No way!' and 'You've got a point' so the conversation keeps going. Thirty seconds each, and you must disagree at least once. Round two: the life. Trudy Ederle swam thirty-four kilometres — and did fifty-six, because the sea moved her. Four paragraphs, one idea each. Argue first, write second.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: two rounds: first the argument (learning to say “No way!” and “You've got a point” so the conversation survives, with at least one disagreement), then the biography: Trudy Ederle, four paragraphs, one idea in each.

WHAT WE DO: a duel of opinions: 30 seconds per pair, at least one agreement and one disagreement, using only the phrases on the card

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The Key phrases card on the desk: What do you think of … ? · What about … ? · I (don't) think … · If you ask me, … · Yes, I think you're right about that. · You've got a point. · No way! · That's ridiculous.
  2. Topics to choose from (the TB allows you to drop football): the best singer · the best game · the best film this year · homework at weekends.
  3. The rule of the duel: Thirty seconds. At least one agreement and one disagreement. A Russian word and your partner takes the turn.
  4. Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: the outer circle moves round — a new opponent, a new topic.
📺 On screen: Key phrases cards · 30-second timer (Backup: write the eight phrases large on the board and leave them up to the end of the lesson)
👦 Students do: students argue round the circle, changing opponents. Expect: by the third round the phrases come automatically. Typical problem: “I am agree” — the partner catches it

🚀 Bonus level · not in the book
crews

WHAT WE DO: the level for our students: how to check facts and what “strong” disagreement sounds like

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Weak biography: 'She was good at swimming.' Useless. A biography gives DATES and NUMBERS.
  2. Upgrade frames: She broke eight world records. · It took her fourteen hours and thirty-four minutes. · Her time was two hours quicker than the men's record.
  3. The source rule (the TB requirement for ex.6): Check every fact on more than one webpage. One website is a rumour.
  4. The Culture note as fuel: the Channel swim is 32 km, but the distance drifts with the current; the fastest time is a little over 7 hours, the slowest almost 27; no wetsuit and no help of any kind, or it does not count.
  5. Shades of disagreement: 'No way!' is loud. 'That's ridiculous' is sharp. 'I don't think so' is safe. Choose on purpose.
📺 On screen: a slide with the upgrade frames (Backup: write the three frames and the three shades of disagreement on the board and leave them up to the end of the lesson)
👦 Students do: students rewrite their lines more precisely and choose a shade. Expect: the strong ones take “two hours quicker than” straight away

WHAT WE DO: two products: a 30-second argument in front of the class + a four-paragraph biography draft following the Skills boost

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1 (ex.5 Skills boost): THINK — your person. PREPARE — the dialogue with at least two agreements and two disagreements. PRACTISE — swap roles. PERFORM. Thirty seconds per pair in front of the class.
  2. Peer review ex.6 (the book's questions): Who do they talk about? Which Key phrases do they use? Do you agree with their opinions?
  3. Round 2 (ex.6 Writing Skills boost): THINK — a person from history you admire, checked on two websites. PREPARE — four short paragraphs: early life and family · how they got started · the most important things they did · later life. WRITE — start with '… was born in …'.
  4. CHECK from the book: interesting information · correct past simple · time expressions · logical paragraphs.
📺 On screen: Key phrases cards · 30-second timer · SB p.93 Skills boost (Backup: keep the four paragraph labels and the opening phrase “… was born in …” on the board for the whole stage)
👦 Students do: students argue in public, then write the draft. Expect: after the argument they write more willingly — the energy carries over. Typical problem: one long paragraph — send it back to the labels

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask for and give opinions, agree and disagree with 8 Key phrases, and draft a four-paragraph biography with past simple and past time expressions.
  • Subsidiary. p.91 ex.1 football vocabulary; ex.2–3 the Carmel and Louis video; ex.4 completing the dialogue; Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; ex.6 peer review · pp.92–93 ex.1–2 Trudy Ederle; ex.3 paragraphs; ex.4 time expressions; ex.5 about themselves; ex.6 their own biography; ex.7 peer review.
  • Personal. The TB fact-versus-opinion warm-up is compulsory: you argue ONLY about opinions. Make sure the disagreement stays in English and does not turn into a row in Russian.
By the end, students can…
  • ask for an opinion (What do you think of … ? What about … ?)
  • give an opinion (I think … · If you ask me, …)
  • agree and disagree without killing the conversation
  • organise a biography into four paragraphs with past time expressions

Structure
What do you think of Kanté? · If you ask me, Kanté is better than Salah. · You've got a point. · No way! · Trudy was born in New York in 1905. · 🚀 bonus: At the age of 12, she started training.
Form
Real-world grammar: Did you see the football last night? · He played well yesterday. Time expressions: in 1905 · at the age of 12 · when she was 19 · after her swim.
Pron. watch
English shows the strength of an opinion through STRESS (TB observation): the BEST player, I think, if you ask ME. Flat intonation = “I don't care”, and the argument dies.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“What do you think about him?” — “Yes.” Does that work? → no, an open question needs an opinion: I think he's …
“That's ridiculous” — is it rude? → strong, but acceptable between friends; with a stranger “I don't think so” is safer.
A 90-word paragraph — is that fine? → no, the TB asks you to split it: past 50–60 words the idea gets lost.

Assumptions: SB pp.91–93, syllabus pair 59. Did…? questions were fixed in P5 — they turn up directly in the dialogue (“Did you see the football last night?”). Football does not land with everyone: the TB explicitly allows you to switch it to singers and actors.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Half the class doesn't watch footballTB routine: pair those who follow it with those who don't; in their own dialogue the topic can switch to music, films or games — what matters is the phrases, not the football.
The disagreement collapses into a row in RussianThe rule of the round: you argue only with the phrases on the card. A Russian word = the turn goes to your opponent.
The biography turns into one long paragraphThe writing bar: four paragraphs, each with its own label in the margin (family · start · achievements · later life). A paragraph with no label does not count.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
