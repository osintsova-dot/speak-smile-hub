Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to follow, read and act out the story «The Moroccan adventure» and talk about travel experiences: “Have you ever been to a Moroccan market? — No, I haven't. Have you ever eaten dates?”
  • Subsidiary. Value — trying new things when you go to a different country (the children try dates, ride camels); break Luke's code (→ MOROCCO — the setting); recycle present perfect & been to from L2, meet been to a place / eaten / seen / ridden in context; story skills — predict → listen → read along → Read & answer → act out; preview the holiday activities for L4.
  • Personal. Let the story breathe — big voices, pauses, prediction; describing the camels (friendly · noisy · not comfortable) is play — enjoy it. Give every child a story role.
By the end, pupils can…
  • retell 2–3 things that happen on the trip;
  • read their role's lines from the book;
  • answer comprehension questions: “They go to the market.”

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“The children have never eaten dates — do they try them?” → yes! — trying new things.
“Whose snoring is the ‘terrible noise’ at the oasis?” → Josh's — not the camel!

Assumptions: Present perfect & been to are active from L2; the number code was learnt in the Starter (recycled in Before-you-read). Readers follow the text with a finger. The act-out routine is familiar from earlier units.

Read & answer · comprehension
carpet → desks

Ask and answer (act. 3, Talk Partners). Questions: Where do the children go first? What do they see at the market? Where do they go on the camels? What are the camels like? Who's making a noise? Pairs answer in full: They go to the market. Every pair speaks.

Who said it? (Showdown). Mini-whiteboards: you read a line — “My camel is really noisy.” Who says it? — all write the name, Show me! 4 rounds.

✔ Assess: the boards are your check — who follows the story? The rest sit with you in the board game.

💪 Support: point to the right frame in the book. ⭐ Extension: add a second true fact about that moment.

28–40′
Act out
carpet, groups of 4

Model a frame. You + 3 pupils play the camel-ride frame with big drama: My camel is very friendly! — My camel isn't very comfortable! — My camel is really noisy!

Groups of 4 (Lily · Josh · Ravi · a parent). Rehearse 2 frames with books as scripts. Rotate roles once.

Showtime. Each group performs ONE frame (30–40 sec — fast carousel, no long waits). Audience task: shout what the noise really was (“It's Josh snoring!”) at the end.

💪 Support: shortest lines / the snoring sound-effect for the shyest child. ⭐ Extension: add an extra line from the frame.

Break the code · travel words
desks, groups

Code-breaker teams (RallyCoach). Each group decodes a short travel word (camel · dates · oasis) with the number-key; partner A reads a number, B finds the letter, then swap. Everyone reads the word aloud.

🎮 Projector option: show the code machine on screen; a rotating child spins to a number, the WHOLE class calls the letter.

Adventure recall (RallyCoach). Pairs put 4 story-frame mini-cards in order & retell: First they go to the market… then they ride the camels… then they eat dates… then Josh snores!

ICQ: Which comes first — the market or the camels? (→ the market!)
