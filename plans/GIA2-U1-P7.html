🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The coursebook's WDYT project is turned into a startup pitch: the crew builds a dream school (the timetable = the product) and defends it in front of 'investors' (the class). The unit's super skill — making logical decisions — works as the filter on the pitch: the judges hammer them with 'why' questions. The Quick review is sewn into the preparation: revising the unit = preparing for the Pair 8 test, but under the flag of 'investor due diligence'.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Dream School Inc. Your crew founds a school. Product: the perfect timetable. Investors: this class. They fund ONE school only. Your pitch: 90 seconds, the timetable, and answers to the hardest question in business — WHY? 'Because it's fun' is not an answer. Think like founders. Build!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We found a dream school: the product is the perfect timetable, the investors are the class and they fund one school only. A 90-second pitch + answers to the 'why' questions.

THINK · advantages first

WHAT WE DO: Super skill: a pros/cons table for 6 candidate subjects BEFORE choosing; the Key phrases of discussion

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six candidate subjects. Advantages and disadvantages FIRST. Decide LAST.
  2. Phrases for the table: Wait a moment. · Yes, but don't forget… · I'd like to learn… — I count how often they are used.
  3. Kagan RoundRobin: round the circle — one advantage or disadvantage per turn.
  4. The argument itself runs on Kagan Talking Chips: everyone has 3 phrase cards, and each time you speak you put a card in the middle; out of cards means you listen. The crew decides, not the loudest voice.
📺 On screen: SB p.22 Super skills box · the table · Key phrase cards, 3 per student
👦 Students do: they argue using the phrases and decide after the table. Expected: the logic changes the initial choice of 1–2 crews

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can design an ideal-school timetable, justify each choice logically and pitch it in 90 seconds; the unit language is revised for the P8 test.
  • Subsidiary. Super skill: making logical decisions (pros/cons BEFORE the decision); the project Key phrases (Wait a moment · Yes, but don't forget…); Quick review p.21 run as stations.
  • Personal. The project is the weaker students' moment of glory: roles matched to their strengths (grid designer · voice · logic officer · defender). I never call the Quick review test preparation — it is 'investor due diligence'.
By the end, students can…
  • list advantages and disadvantages before deciding
  • use project Key phrases in a group discussion
  • pitch a timetable with logical reasons in 90 seconds
  • pass all Quick-review stations of Unit 1

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'Because it's fun' — is that an argument? → no: why does fun mean it is useful? Push them to the reason.
Do we write the pros and cons before or after the decision? → BEFORE. That is what a logical decision is.
Is the Quick review a test? → no, it is the check before the test: find your gaps today, not in Pair 8.

Assumptions: SB pp.21–23 (Quick review + Project), KTP pair 11. All the unit material has been covered; what is new is defending decisions logically.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The project eats the revisionA firm frame: the revision stations take 20 min IN THE MIDDLE of the pair, before Create — they are the 'due diligence on the company'.
The timetable becomes a pile of 'fun stuff'The logic filter: every subject on the grid has to answer WHY in one sentence, or the judges dock it.
Passengers in the crewRoles: grid designer · logic officer · voice · defender; the pitch collapses if one of the blocks stays silent.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
