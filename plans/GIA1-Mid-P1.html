Switchboard: which helper is in the question?
🎯 Switchboard · which helper?
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Kagan RoundRobin sorting BEFORE the stations: 10 questions by helper (am/is/are · have got · do/does · there) — everyone places a card in turn; anchor on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 10 mixed-up questions on cards: Where ___ you from? · ___ you got a pet? · What time ___ he get up? · ___ there any juice? · ___ she like music?…
  2. Sorting via RoundRobin: a pile in the centre; in turn you take the top one, read it aloud, put it in a column — the team confirms: Agree? — Agree! (nobody sorts for everyone else). 4 min; a choral check with the anchor: got-question → no do! · routine/like → do/does · describing → is/are · food on the table → there is/any.
  3. The anchor table stays on the board for the WHOLE lesson — it's the marathon's lifebuoy.
📺 On screen: 10-question slide · a 4-column anchor table (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they sort questions by helper — the main anti-confusion tool. Expected: 2–3 arguments exactly where the confusion is (have got vs does have). Typical error: Does she got — I point at the column.

stations · crews

WHAT WE DO: 5 vocabulary stations of 6 min with rotation: Starter codes · U1 map · U2 monster · U3 menu · U4 timetable; judge roles

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. S-Starter: a spelling code (decode 3 words + code 1) + numbers by ear from the judge. S-U1: a map: country→nationality + 2 sentences about the place (It's amazing/busy…). S-U2: a “monster”: a creature card → describe with have got + body/hair (It's got three green eyes…). S-U3: a café menu: sorting C/UC + an order with some/any. S-U4: a timetable: time in words + 3 routine sentences with a clean -s about “today's person”.
  2. Rotation on the whistle every 6 min; at the station — a task card + a face-down prompt (taking it = −1).
  3. Station judges — a rotation from the crews (the Timer role); I walk around and collect TYPICAL mistakes in my notebook for the debrief.
  4. Points: cleanliness of execution × no prompt used.
📺 On screen: 5 station cards · face-down prompts · timer/whistle
👦 Students do: they go through all 5 blocks of the half-year on their feet — movement + retrieval. Expected: U1–U2 are more forgotten — that's exactly the point, to expose it. Typical error: mixing has got/has — into the notebook, we'll go over it in the marathon.

Grammar marathon: 5 gates in a row
⚡ Grammar marathon · 5 gates
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: the grammar marathon via Kagan Showdown: 5 gates in a row (be → have got → some/any/there → present simple → mix); everyone writes every round, boards up on command

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A gate = 3 tasks on mini-boards, EVERYONE writes at the same time, on the command SHOWDOWN! boards up together: Gate be: She ___ from Italy · they ___n't happy · ___ you OK? Gate have got: a question+short answer+possessive 's. Gate food: some/any/there is-are in 3 gaps. Gate PS: aff+neg+question with 3rd person (watch/study traps). Gate MIX: 3 sentences where you have to CHOOSE the structure.
  2. A clean crew volley (all boards correct) = +2; the top mistakes — an instant 20-sec choral micro-drill.
  3. I hang up the test strategies as we go: Strategy 1: read the task TWICE. Strategy 2: easy first. Strategy 3: final check = hunt the -s.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · gate slides · a poster of the 3 strategies (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: everyone writes every round — nobody sits it out; the MIX gate exposes the confusion. Expected: be/have got clean, MIX — 60–70%. Typical error: Does she has — a choral fix: does + BARE verb.

Mini-mock · test rehearsal

WHAT WE DO: a 10-min mini-mock in test format (2 items from every part) + self-check against the key; we apply the strategies live

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out the mini-mock (ours, per the spec: 2 vocab + 3 grammar + 2 reading + 2 listening items I read aloud + a mini-writing of 2 sentences).
  2. Ten minutes. Use the three strategies — I'm watching for easy-first! Silence, timer.
  3. Self-check against the key on the slide; score privately.
  4. Everyone fills in a gap list: My 2 weakest parts: … · My fix plan for the weekend: …

👦 Students do: they rehearse the format solo, find their weak parts. Expected: panic is defused — the format is familiar; the gap lists are accurate. Typical error: a gap list that says “all fine” — I show them their own mock.

Product: the examiners' relay
🏁 PRODUCT · strategy relay
stage · crews

WHAT WE DO: finale: a “fix somebody else's mock” relay — crews get sheets with typical mistakes (my notebook from today!) and fix them at speed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand every crew a sheet from an “unknown student” with 8 mistakes — these are TYPICAL mistakes from today's stations/marathon (from my notebook, anonymised).
  2. You are the examiners now. Find and FIX all eight. Six minutes. Every fix needs the rule word: helper! plural! -s!
  3. A chain check: the crew names the mistake + the rule in one word (+1 for the fix, +1 for the rule).
  4. Finale: If you can CATCH the mistake, you won't WRITE it on Tuesday.
📺 On screen: sheets with 8 mistakes (I prepare them at break from today's notebook) · timer
👦 Students do: they hunt for mistakes in the role of examiners — retrieval through checking. Expected: they catch 7–8; they state the rule-word. Typical error: a fix with no rule — only half the point.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — forms are clean (1–3) · Richness — details, colours, numbers, connectors (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		error sheets

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners have recycled ALL Starter–U4 language through team stations and a grammar marathon, located their personal gaps and set test-taking strategies for the Mid-year test.
  • Subsidiary. 5 vocabulary stations (Starter/U1/U2/U3/U4) completed by every crew; the grammar marathon be→have got→C/UC→there is/are→present simple; test strategies (read the task twice · easy first · check -s) drilled on a mini-mock.
  • Personal. The last week before the test and the holidays: energy is high, concentration is already drifting. The marathon holds the pace better than “revising from the notebook”. Everyone's personal gap list is the main product of the day.
By the end, students can…
  • recall vocabulary sets of all 5 blocks at station speed
  • switch between be/have got/present simple without mixing
  • apply 3 test strategies deliberately
  • name their 2 personal gaps for the test

Target language
Lexis

Structure
the half-year in one list: I'm from… / She's got… / There is some… / Is there any…? / He gets up at… / Do you like…? — switching structures without mixing them.
Form
be (all forms); have got (all forms); possessive 's; countable/uncountable + some/any/a lot of; there is/there are; Present simple (aff/neg/questions + 3rd person -s) — the focus is on the “switchboard”: WHICH helper verb is in this question?
Pron. watch
a half-year accumulator: nationalities stress · /h/ in have · some /sʌm/ · -es /ɪz/ (watches) · time words (half /hɑːf/).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Have you got…? and Do you have lessons…? — which helper is where?” → have got = a got-question with no do; present simple = do/does.
“Is there any milk — why any?” → a question → any (some in a statement).
“Stuck on question 3 in the test — what do you do?” → easy first: skip it, come back at the end.

Assumptions: All the Starter–U4 language has been covered and unit-tested; mixing structures (has got vs does) is the main risk of the Mid-year test. The station format is familiar from the units. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling! numbers by ear! alphabet at speed!), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Pre-New-Year distractionNo long explanations: stations of 6–7 min, everything in motion, the timer is the boss.
has got + does confusionA switchboard station: sorting questions by helper (got? do? does? is?) — before the marathon.
The weaker ones drown at the stationsPrompts lie AT the station (face down): taking a prompt = minus 1 point, but it's legal — you still solved it.
“Why revise, I know it all”The mini-mock at the end of Lesson A answers that itself; a personal gap list is numbers, not an argument.
A queue at a stationThere are more stations than crews; rotation on the whistle, strictly round the circle.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
