🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Name ten adventure activities and five phrasal verbs with get, and talk about fixed plans using the present continuous for the future.
What is genuinely hard
Not the form — am/is/are + ‑ing has been theirs since block 1. The hard part is accepting that the SAME form now means Friday, not now. Russian marks the future with a separate verb, so the brain resists.
Typical error to expect
My brother play football every day instead of plays — the dropped ‑s in the third person singular [high priority, A2/B1]
Plus the block's own error: Taylor Swift sings at the stadium next Saturday — the present simple used for a fixed future event (SB p.43 ex.3, item 1).
Why the lesson is built this way
The recording does the teaching: they hear a whole week of plans (ex.2–3) BEFORE anyone names a tense, so when the grammar table arrives at 47′ the sentences in it are already familiar. Vocabulary comes first because the listening is made of those ten words. The phrasal verbs with get sit between the listening and the break: they are a small, closed set and they need a slot of their own, not a footnote. The productive stage is a real schedule with real questions, because a plan that nobody asks about does not need the future tense at all. During that stage you say nothing about errors: correcting kills the very thing you are measuring.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Ten activities and one strange tense. By the end of today you can plan a whole adventure week and ask anybody in this room what they are doing on Tuesday. One rule: if it is fixed, use ‑ing and say WHEN.”

door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a three-item rewind of the last block, and the bridge into Unit 7

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last block rewind. Three quick rounds on the board: One — say it with an adverb: he runs … (fast / quickly). Two — whose is it: the bag of Anna → Anna's bag. Three — one word in the gap: I'm not interested ___ football. (in).
  3. Count the score out loud: Hands up if you got all three.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the new unit: Books open, page forty-two. This unit is about the kind of holiday where you come home tired. Today: what you can do on one — and how to say what you are doing next Friday.
📺 On screen: the three prompts in chalk on the board — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: answer chorally, then one student per item explains. Expect the possessive to be the fastest and the preposition the slowest. Typical problem — interesting in: write both versions up and let the class choose

WHAT WE DO: name the language aim of the lesson and the standard an answer has to reach

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two sentences on the board, one under the other: I'm eating an apple. and I'm eating pizza with Masha on Saturday. Underline on Saturday in colour.
  2. Explain the aim in 40 seconds: the same three words am / is / are plus ‑ing do two jobs. Today we take the second job — plans that are already fixed. The time word is what tells you which job it is.
  3. Set the bar: Every plan you say today needs a WHEN in it. No when, no plan.

👦 Students do: read both sentences and one student says which is the future and why. Expect «because of Saturday» — accept it, that is the rule. Typical problem: they think the second sentence is wrong English

Vocabulary · holiday activities
pairs → class
Why: Meaning through pictures is instant; the mingle that follows is what turns recognition into production. Ten words said out loud twenty times beats ten words read once.

WHAT WE DO: the ten activities: ex.1 p.42 (track 51) plus a Quiz-Quiz-Trade mingle

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books open, pairs, 4 minutes on the matching. Concept-check as you go (TB p.81): Hiking — is that walking in a city or in the country?
  2. Play track 51 — check and choral repetition. Key: A horse riding · B camping · C kite surfing · D mountain biking · E diving · F sailing · G waterskiing · H paddle boarding · I zip wiring · J hiking.
  3. Fix the two-word stress with a clap: KITE surfing · MOUNTAIN biking · PADDLE boarding · ZIP wiring. Three choral repetitions each.
  4. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: every student holds a photo card or a word card and mingles: Quiz your partner. Coach if he's wrong. Trade cards. Find a new partner! 4 minutes, at least five trades.
  5. Round it off: Boards up — write the activity: 'You need a board and a lot of wind.'
📺 On screen: track 51 in the board app (Backup if the board is down: read the ten words aloud yourself and have the class repeat chorally; the Quiz-Quiz-Trade cards are printed in advance and need no technology at all)
👦 Students do: match, check, drill, then mingle with cards. Expect 7–8 out of 10 unaided. Typical errors: waterskiing confused with paddle boarding; the stress on mountain BIKING — fix it with the clap

WHAT WE DO: ex.2–3 p.42 (track 52): which two activities are NOT mentioned, then the whole week in a table

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the gist task BEFORE you play: Ten activities on the page. Two of them are NOT in the recording. Which two? Play track 52 once. Key: diving and waterskiing (the two activities Tara and Dan do NOT mention).
  2. Then ex.3 — the table. Warn them in advance (TB p.81): The days do NOT come in order. Listen for the day, then write the activity.
  3. Play track 52 again. Check in pairs first, then as a class. Key: Monday: hiking · Tuesday: sailing, horse riding · Wednesday: zip wiring · Thursday: mountain biking, paddle boarding · Friday: kite surfing.
  4. Two comprehension questions to finish (TB p.81): Where is the holiday? (in the Pyrenees) · Who is taking a musical instrument? (Tara, a guitar).
  5. Students who need support: TB p.81: pause the recording after each activity and ask «What is that activity?» and «When is it?» — the table gets filled a line at a time instead of all at once.
📺 On screen: track 52 in the board app, with the empty table on the screen so you can fill it in live (Backup: read the audioscript from TB p.291 yourself at natural speed — twice, exactly as the recording would be played — and draw the table in chalk)
👦 Students do: listen twice, fill the table, check in pairs. Expect Thursday (two activities) to be the one that is missed. Typical error: they write the activities down the column in the order they hear them

WHAT WE DO: ex.4–5 p.42: five phrasal verbs with get, then a personal question round

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Elicit first (TB p.81): What phrases do you already know with GET? Expect get up and get on.
  2. ex.4 in pairs, 3 minutes. Key: 1 getting to · 2 get back · 3 getting on · 4 getting up · 5 get lost.
  3. Meaning check with one question each: If I get lost, do I know where I am? (no) · If I get back at six, am I leaving or arriving home? (arriving).
  4. ex.5 as Kagan RallyCoach: partner A asks questions 1–3, partner B asks 4–5, and the listener's job is to catch a missing get phrase and coach it in.
📺 On screen: the five get phrases written in a column on the board so students can point at the one they need — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: complete the sentences, then interview each other. Expect real stories about getting lost. Typical error: I get up from the bus — the pair partner corrects it to get off or back to get on

47–58′
Grammar · discovery
pairs → class
Why: Guided discovery, not presentation: the form is already familiar as «now», so the useful work is noticing that the SAME form does the future. What they extract, they remember.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 p.43: pull the rule out of three example sentences from the recording

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the three examples on the board exactly as the book has them: We're getting on a bus at five o'clock in the morning! · I'm not taking my keyboard with me next week. · When are we going mountain biking and paddle boarding?
  2. Ask the TB p.82 question first: Are these about the past, the present or the future? (the future) Are they plans, or just thinking about it? (plans).
  3. Pairs complete the three rules in ex.1, 4 minutes. Key: 1 now and the future · 2 the future · 3 usually.
  4. Underline the time word in all three sentences in a second colour. State the rule in one line: Fixed plan + time word = am/is/are + ‑ing.
  5. One warning out loud (TB p.82): Do not confuse this with 'be going to'. Today we only use the ‑ing form.
📺 On screen: the three sentences and the three rules on the screen, marked up live in two colours (Backup: write the three sentences on the board before the lesson and underline them in coloured chalk as you go)
👦 Students do: derive the rules in pairs, then one student reads each rule out. Expect rule 3 («usually» a time word) to cause an argument — that is a good argument to have. Typical error: they read rule 1 as «only the future»

58–66′
Grammar · practice
solo → pairs
Why: Controlled practice with immediate peer correction — the error hunt makes them justify the choice, which is what transfers to speaking.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 p.43: write out Bella's whole weekend from her phone diary, including what she is NOT doing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model the first line together, exactly as the book does: She isn't cleaning her room at 2 pm on Sunday. Point at the crossed-out entries: Crossed out means NOT doing.
  2. Solo, 5 minutes, one sentence per diary entry — day and time in every sentence.
  3. Kagan RallyCoach to check: partner A reads Saturday, partner B reads Sunday; the listener checks that the day and the time are both there.
  4. Full key (TB p.82): On Saturday she's going diving with Gina at 10.30. She isn't going home for lunch at 12.30. She's riding her new bike to her grandpa's at 3 pm. She's catching the train to Anna's party at 6.30 pm. On Sunday she's going mountain biking with her mum at 9 am. She's doing her homework at 11 o'clock. She isn't cleaning her bedroom at 2 pm. She's watching the football game at 2 pm.
  5. Two TB p.82 checking questions to close it: Is Bella going mountain biking with her father? (no, with her mother) · How is she getting to Anna's party? (by train).
📺 On screen: Bella's phone diary on the screen so the whole class can look at the same entry (Backup: everyone works from the paper book and you copy the two crossed-out entries onto the board so they cannot be missed)
👦 Students do: write eight sentences, then read them to a partner. Expect the two negatives to be written as positives by two or three students. Typical error: the time is dropped — the partner's job is to demand it

66–72′
Pronunciation · sentence stress
class → pairs
Why: Six minutes on rhythm, right after the form is understood. Stress is what makes a plan sound like a plan; without it the sentence is grammatically right and unintelligible.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 then ex.4–5 p.43 (track 53): correct five sentences, then find the beat

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3 first — the error hunt, 3 minutes, Kagan Showdown: the team writes the corrected sentence on a mini-whiteboard and on Showdown! everyone reveals at once. Key: Taylor Swift is singing at the football stadium next Saturday. · We're meeting at 3 pm tomorrow, at the bus station. · I'm very excited that you're coming to visit next summer. · Are you bringing any money with you this evening? · I'm not visiting my grandparents next weekend..
  2. Then ex.4: students predict which words carry the stress, in pencil, before they hear anything.
  3. Play track 53. The pattern is simple and worth naming: the activity and the time word carry the stress; am / is / are, pronouns and prepositions do not — UNLESS the verb is negative, and then aren't / isn't takes a beat of its own.
  4. Key: 1 We're going kite surfing next week. · 2 Are you going mountain biking in the summer? · 3 They aren't buying a paddle board this weekend. · 4 He's going camping next month. · 5 Is she coming horse riding with us this evening? · 6 He isn't going hiking during the holidays.
  5. ex.5 in pairs: read the six sentences to each other, tapping the stressed words on the desk.
📺 On screen: track 53 in the board app, with the six sentences on the screen so you can underline the stresses live (Backup: say the six sentences yourself twice — once flat, once with the stress — and let the class tell you which one sounds English)
👦 Students do: correct, predict, then read aloud with the tapping. Expect them to over-stress the pronouns. Typical error in ex.3: item 4 is corrected to Do you bring…? instead of Are you bringing…?

72–84′
🎯 Speaking out · our adventure week
pairs → fours
Why: The productive outcome: they build a real schedule and have to ask each other about it, which forces the new tense at speed. Errors are logged silently — correcting now would kill fluency.

WHAT WE DO: ex.6–7 p.43: build a real adventure week and interview another pair about theirs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the frame: In pairs. Choose a place. Then one activity for every day, Monday to Friday. Write the day, the time and the activity. 6 minutes. Give them somewhere real to choose from (TB p.82): Kamchatka, Krabi, the Black Sea, Karelia.
  2. Then pairs meet pairs. Kagan Timed Pair Share: 90 seconds each way, questions only in the present continuous — What are you doing on Tuesday? Who are you going with? What time are you leaving?
  3. The listener's job: catch one sentence with no time word and say When? Nothing else.
  4. Second round with a new pair — the target is simply longer and without pauses.
  5. Log errors silently on your correction sheet; do not correct aloud — the feedback slot is the start of the next lesson.
  6. Finish with the whole class: each group says its best day in one sentence (On Monday morning, we're learning how to waterski.).
📺 On screen: the three model questions and a 90-second timer on the screen (Backup: questions on the board and you time it on the wall clock, calling «swap» yourself)
👦 Students do: plan a week, then interview and be interviewed. Expect the second round to be visibly faster. Typical error: What you do on Tuesday? — the missing auxiliary; note it, say nothing

Structure
We're getting on a bus at five o'clock in the morning. · I'm not taking my keyboard next week. · When are we going mountain biking? · What are you doing on Tuesday?
Form
am/is/are (+ not) + V-ing, with a future time word: next week · tomorrow · on Friday · at five o'clock. Questions: (question word) + am/is/are + subject + V-ing.
Pron. watch
sentence stress on the activity and the time word: We're going kite surfing next week. Word stress: ˈkite surfing · ˈmountain biking · ˈpaddle boarding · ˈzip wiring · ˈwaterskiing.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«We're getting on a bus at five o'clock.» Is the bus moving right now? → no — it is a plan for a fixed time.
«I'm not taking my keyboard next week.» Has the decision been made? → yes: it is arranged, not being considered.
«Taylor Swift sings at the stadium next Saturday» — what is wrong? → next Saturday is a fixed future event: is singing.
«What are you doing at the weekend?» — am I asking about now? → no: the time word decides.

Assumptions: SB p.42–43, syllabus lesson 22. The present continuous for NOW is secure after Unit 1. Of the ten activities they usually already know camping, diving, sailing and hiking; kite surfing, zip wiring and paddle boarding are new. Phrasal verbs with get are partly known — get up and get on are A1, get lost and get back are not.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“But this is present continuous — it means now!”Don't argue with it, show it. Two sentences on the board: I'm eating an apple. / I'm eating pizza with Masha on Saturday. Which apple am I eating right now? The time word is the whole answer.
Someone produces «going to» and the class asks about itOne sentence and move on: Both are correct English. Today we use the ‑ing one, because our plans have a time and a ticket. TB p.82 explicitly keeps going to out of this lesson.
Two-word activities said as one stress unitBeat it out with your hand: KITE surfing, MOUNTAIN biking, PADDLE boarding. Three choral repetitions with the clap, then individually.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
