🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use six prepositions and conjunctions (according to, because of, besides, despite, instead of, unless) with the right thing after them, and listen to an interview for attitude — the speaker's opinion, not their words.
What is genuinely hard
The six words are the same six the exam swaps for each other, and the choice is grammatical before it is stylistic: despite and because of take a NOUN or an -ing form, although and because take a whole clause. Russian makes no such split, so «despite it was raining» is produced by every group. The listening has a different difficulty: in Part 4 the printed option is deliberately NOT the spoken wording, so a student who listens for a matching word is being led straight to the distractor.
Typical error to expect
«despite it was raining», «although the rain» — despite and because of take a NOUN, although and because take a CLAUSE. Six words, one rule, and the Russian lexical section tests exactly this.
Choosing the option whose words you heard — in Listening Part 4 the correct option is almost always a PARAPHRASE, and the option containing the words from the recording is the trap. The Workbook exam tip says this in as many words.
Why the lesson is built this way
The six words are taught as a decision, not a list: one question — what comes after this word, a noun or a sentence? — asked on the page, then answered eight times in exam conditions in the Reading Part 6 open cloze that the Student's Book prints right underneath. That cloze is not an extra; it is the same six words with the training wheels off, and it is where the lesson's grammar lands. The listening half is built backwards from the trap: the class predicts, underlines key words in the OPTIONS, hears the interview, and then spends five minutes on the paraphrase column — what did she actually say that means this? The speaking is the same topic and the same structures, done in pairs with the Prepare to speak phrases ticked as they are used.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today you hear a woman who has been famous, talking about what it costs. And you will notice something: not one of the six right answers uses the words she says. Also today: six little words that decide whether the next thing you write is a noun or a sentence.”

Why: Vocabulary · six words, one decision

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: the meanings, the grammar behind them, then six sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start with the grammar question, not the meanings, because it is the one that pays. Read the six example sentences on the page and ask each time: What comes after the word in green — an -ing form, a noun phrase, or a whole clause? That is the Teacher's Book instruction and it is the lesson.
  2. ex.1, three minutes in pairs: the six words matched to their meanings. Answers: 1 f (according to = as said by someone), 2 e (because of = as a result of), 3 c (besides = in addition to), 4 b (despite = not prevented by), 5 a (instead of = in place of), 6 d (unless = except if).
  3. As each one is confirmed, the student who answered writes it in one of the two columns and says why. Unless is the one that causes an argument, and the argument is useful.
  4. Add the point the Teacher's Book flags later, now, while the columns are being built: instead of, besides and despite accept either a noun OR an -ing form. Write an example of each under the + NOUN column.
  5. ex.2 individually, four minutes: six gaps. Answers: 1 instead of, 2 According to, 3 despite, 4 besides, 5 because of, 6 Unless.
  6. Check by reading whole sentences aloud, and each time ask the column question again before confirming.
📺 On screen: the two columns filling up, written by students — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: answer the same grammatical question six times, then fill six gaps alone and justify each by column

Why: Reading Part 6 · the open cloze, in exam conditions

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: six gaps, one word each, and the lesson's grammar under pressure

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the format in one sentence before they start: Six gaps, one word in each, and the word is almost always a small one — a preposition, an article, a pronoun or a linker.
  2. The strategy, on the board, and it is two steps: read the WHOLE text first for meaning, then look at the words on BOTH sides of each gap. Ask the class the Teacher's Book question first — does the writer think it is easy for anyone to be a model? Answer: no.
  3. ex.3 individually, six minutes, silent, clock visible. Nothing on the desk but the page.
  4. Pairs compare, two minutes, and must agree before the check.
  5. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: 1 According, 2 of, 3 besides, 4 Because, 5 Despite, 6 unless. For every gap, one student says which COLUMN the answer belongs to and why the word on the other side of the gap proves it.
  6. Spend the last minute on gap 2, which is the one that catches everybody: instead OF a boring pair of jeans. The word is not the linker itself, it is the second half of it.
📺 On screen: the six answers and the column reasoning on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: work in silence against a clock, then justify every answer by the grammar column rather than by feel

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that drills the single decision of the lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two sides of the room: NOUN and SENTENCE.
  2. You read half a sentence and stop — Despite… · Although… · Because of… · Unless… · Instead of… · Besides… Everybody walks to the side that has to come next.
  3. The first person to arrive finishes the sentence out loud. Get it wrong and the other side gets to correct it — which is the point.
  4. Eight rounds. Then freeze and sit: Page one hundred and five. A woman who used to be famous is about to be interviewed, and six questions are waiting for you.

46–60′
Listening Part 4 · the interview
individual → class
Why: Listening Part 4 · the interview

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: track 70, twice, then the paraphrase column

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the format: One interview, six questions, three options each, and you will hear it twice. The questions follow the order of the recording.
  2. Track 70, first play, whole, no pausing. Pens down for the first thirty seconds so they hear the voice before they hunt.
  3. Ninety seconds to compare in pairs, then track 70 again — and this time pause after each answer if the class needs the support, which is exactly what the Teacher's Book suggests.
  4. Check: 1 B, 2 A, 3 B, 4 C, 5 B, 6 A. Check the predictions on the board against what she actually said.
  5. The paraphrase column — five minutes, and it is the reason this lesson exists. For each of the six, ask: What did she actually SAY that meant this? Write the spoken wording in the third column beside the printed option. Play thirty-second chunks again as needed.
  6. Make the rule visible at the end of the column: the correct option is a paraphrase; the option that repeats the words you heard is usually the trap.
  7. ex.4, three minutes, pairs then floor: what do you think of Pattie's advice, and why? Real opinions, one sentence each.
📺 On screen: track 70 in the board app, played twice, plus the paraphrase column filling in on the board. Backup: if the audio fails, read the interview from the audioscript (TB p.303) at natural speed, twice — the task and the paraphrase work survive intact
👦 Students do: listen twice, check against their own predictions, then rebuild the spoken wording behind each printed option

Why: 🎯 Speaking · discussing a topic, in pairs

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–5: the model discussion analysed, then Speaking Part 4 done properly

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1, three minutes: read the three discussion questions and make notes — can anyone famous really have a private life? should journalists leave celebrities alone? what about the children of famous people? Key words only, not sentences.
  2. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share, three minutes standing: mix, pair, share ONE of the three with a partner, then mix again. Two partners minimum. This is rehearsal, not performance.
  3. ex.2, track 71: two people discussing the same three questions. First listening — did they mention any of your ideas?
  4. ex.3, track 71 again, and the question is about the mechanics, not the content: does one speaker say more than the other, and HOW does each one involve the other? Answer: they contribute about equally, and they do it by asking questions and by commenting on what the other has just said. Say plainly that in a speaking exam this is assessed.
  5. ex.4, track 71 a third time with the Prepare to speak box open: tick the phrases you actually hear. Keeping the conversation going: What do you think? · Why do you say that? · But what about…? · Do you agree that…? · That's an interesting point. · I suppose so, although… · It depends, doesn't it? · I'm not sure, because… They use six of them: What do you think? · It depends, doesn't it? · That's an interesting point. · Do you agree that…? · I'm not sure, because… · But what about…?
  6. 🎯 ex.5 = Speaking Part 4, twelve minutes, in pairs: the four questions on the page — how much do you know about your favourite celebrity, what would you like to find out about your favourite film star, would you like to be famous, how would your life change if you were. Kagan Timed Pair Share for the first two, free discussion for the last two.
  7. Each pair keeps a tally of the Prepare to speak phrases they use. The target is four different ones each, and the tally is checked at the end — not the content.
  8. Delayed correction, three minutes: four sentences you heard, on the board, repaired by the class. Choose two with a preposition error, so the lesson closes where it opened.
📺 On screen: the Prepare to speak phrases on the board with a tally column. Backup: if track 71 fails, read the two speakers from the audioscript (TB p.303) with a strong student taking one part — the phrase-spotting task survives intact

Structure
Despite their odd appearance, the band are really nice guys. · She invited fans into her car because of the photographers. · I'm not interested in celebrity news unless it's about my favourite singer.
Form
according to / because of / besides / despite / instead of + NOUN or -ING · unless (and although, because) + SUBJECT + VERB · three of them (instead of, besides, despite) accept either a noun or an -ing form, and the Teacher's Book asks for that point explicitly.
Pron. watch
the four the Teacher's Book flags: annoyed /əˈnɔɪd/ · curious /ˈkjʊəriəs/ — three syllables, not two · professional /prəˈfeʃənəl/ · unexpected /ˌʌnɪkˈspektɪd/ with the stress on PEC. Add the one it does not flag: in have something done the stress falls on the PARTICIPLE — I had my hair CUT — which is how a listener hears that it was not you who did it
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Despite the rain» or «despite it was raining»? → the first. Despite takes a noun. If you want a sentence after it, you need although.
«Unless I get good grades, my parents won't let me go» — does unless mean if or if not? → if not. It is a negative word with no negative in it.
In Listening Part 4, you hear the exact words of option B. Is B right? → probably not. That is how the task is built.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 1–9 (listening for attitude and opinion) and tasks 32–38 (linkers and prepositions are tested there).

Listening Part 4 is an interview in which the printed option is never the spoken wording — the same machine as the Russian multiple-choice listening, where a candidate who hunts for a repeated word chooses the distractor on purpose planted for them. The preposition-and-conjunction set (according to, because of, besides, despite, instead of, unless) is the other half: these six are exactly the words the Russian lexical section swaps for each other, and the choice is grammatical, not stylistic — despite takes a noun, although takes a clause.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
