🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Consolidate the block's language — computer collocations, both phrasal-verb sets, the present simple passive, zero/first conditional and unless — and sit the official Cambridge Units 15–16 tests under calm exam conditions.
What is genuinely hard
Not the content — the retrieval: three weeks of language must come OUT of memory today, and spelling (password, wrap, participles) is where test marks quietly leak.
Typical error to expect
Under time pressure the old ghosts return: is call, if you'll, unless you don't, add up them. The briefing names all four aloud BEFORE the paper.
Why the lesson is built this way
A two-half lesson with a fixed shape the class already knows from blocks 5–7: 45 minutes of retrieval (two stations + the official List it! game with the thirteen U15–16 cards that block 7 set aside), then briefing, a real break, and the two official papers back to back. Everything in the papers has been done in class — the test measures learning, not surprise. Delayed feedback culture holds: errors from the stations are fixed in the briefing, not mid-game.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Three weeks: your digital life, your home lab, two tenses Russian doesn't have. First we play it all back — then you show it on paper. Everything in the test you have already done in this room.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a 90-second retrieval flood, and the day's map

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the block in one breath. Kagan RallyRobin: pairs alternate naming ANYTHING learned in Units 15–16 — a collocation, a verb, a rule — no repeats, 90 seconds.
  3. Frame the day: Half one — we play everything back. Half two — the official test. Nothing in it will surprise you. That's a promise with evidence.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): into the stations: Two stations, one game, one break, one paper. Go.

WHAT WE DO: List it! — the thirteen U15–16 cards from the official pack

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set up List it! (Review game Units 13–16, Cambridge, PHOTOCOPIABLE 2019): Teams of one to three; a pile of «List 5…» cards face down. A player draws a card and reads it out. ALL teams race to write five valid items; the first to five shouts «Listed!» and everyone stops. The fast team reads its list; other teams tick off items they also have. Scoring: 1 point for finishing first + 1 per item nobody else has + 1 if the whole list is spelled correctly — so spelling wins games.
  2. Today's deck: Block 7 played the eight U13–14 cards; TODAY'S legal deck is the thirteen U15–16 cards the last block set aside: things you switch on · things in a computer file · things people search for · passwords people use · things you tie · camera apps · things you shake · things you wrap · experiments · things people install · things people share online (×2) · things you stir. Six or seven cards at two minutes each fills the slot; keep the rest as tie-breakers.
  3. File: course folder → материалы → Review Games Worksheets → PREPARE_4_Review_game_Units_13-16.pdf. Sharper groups: the «list unlimited» version — as many items as possible in 60 seconds, one point per unique item.
📺 On screen: printed game cards — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: race, list, spell for the bonus point. The spelling point is the whole pedagogy: the test pays for spelling too, and everyone just felt why

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Game adrenaline crosses into the test halfThe break is REAL (five minutes, corridor, water) and the room is reset: desks apart, papers face down, volume to zero with the standard signal. Ritual beats speeches.
A student blanks on the passive mid-testThe briefing plants the recovery move: write the two-hands note (BE + participle) in the margin and rebuild from it. Margins are legal.
Early finishers fidgetThe rule announced with the paper: finished = check the trap list (spelling, if-side, unless) against YOUR OWN answers, then read quietly. Nobody leaves early.
Somebody is absent todayStandard make-up: they sit the same papers before the next lesson (both tests also exist as editable RTF); List it! owes them nothing.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
