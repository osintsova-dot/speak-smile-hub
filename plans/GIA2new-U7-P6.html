🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): These two lessons carry two things (Real-world speaking p.91 + Writing pp.92–93). For new students disagreeing is the scariest part, so all eight phrases lie on the desk on a card, and the dialogue is first completed from the book and read aloud, and only then moved onto their own topic. The writing goes through a ready model (Trudy Ederle) and a strict plan: four paragraphs, each with its own label.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two things today, both easier than they look. First: how to disagree in English without a fight. Eight phrases on a card — you don't have to invent anything, just pick one. 'No way!' 'You've got a point.' Second: a life story. Trudy Ederle swam thirty-four kilometres across the Channel — and did fifty-six, because the sea pushed her. Four paragraphs, one idea each, and I'll give you the plan. Nobody writes from nothing today.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: two jobs: first we learn to disagree off a card with eight ready-made phrases (nothing to invent), then we write a biography on the Trudy Ederle model — four paragraphs on a ready plan.

Opinion phrases: video + shadowing
Opinion phrases · video
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: the Key phrases card → the Carmel and Louis video → ex.4 completion → shadowing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The card goes on the desk and we read it together column by column: Asking: What do you think of … ? What about … ? · Giving: I (don't) think … If you ask me, … · Agreeing: Yes, I think you're right about that. You've got a point. · Disagreeing: No way! That's ridiculous.
  2. ex.2 first viewing, one task: Do they like the same players? (TB key: no — she likes Firmino, Mbappé and Kanté, he likes Mo Salah).
  3. ex.4: we close the first gap together at the board — I show how to find the phrase by the column on the card. Then Kagan RallyCoach: one pen per pair, the first puts the phrase in and reads the line aloud, the second holds the card and prompts the column (“that's Agreeing, look on the left”); after each gap they swap roles (TB key: 1 What about · 2 I think · 3 right about that · 4 If you ask me · 5 No way!).
  4. Shadowing: I read the lines and students repeat straight after the voice, copying the intonation. Then they read the dialogue aloud in pairs twice, swapping roles.
📺 On screen: video SB p.91 · Key phrases cards (Backup: I read the dialogue myself in parts from the board: “Did you see the football last night? — Yes, with the best player in the world. Mo Salah!” — and we shadow after me)
👦 Students do: they watch, complete and copy the intonation. Expect: “No way!” is remembered instantly — and we use that

WHAT WE DO: the opinion drafted in writing → a 60-second debate in pairs strictly off the card

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. In writing first: Write ONE opinion with a reason: 'I think … is the best … because …'
  2. Topics to choose from (the TB allows football to be swapped): the best singer · the best game · the best film · homework at weekends.
  3. A 60-second debate in pairs, strictly off the card: Ask, give, agree once, disagree once. Point at the phrase before you say it.
  4. Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: one circle moves on — new partner, same card.
📺 On screen: Key phrases cards · a 60-second timer (Backup: I write the eight phrases large on the board and don't rub them off before the end of these two lessons)
👦 Students do: they write an opinion, then debate off the card. Expect: by the third partner the phrases come without looking. Typical problem: “I am agree” — I point at the phrase on the card

WHAT WE DO: model BEFORE the task: we read the biography paragraph by paragraph, add the labels and collect the time expressions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: first I say the first title aloud and explain why it doesn't fit — that is the model of the reasoning. Then Kagan Showdown: everyone writes their letter on a mini-whiteboard and on the command Boards up! they show at the same time — nobody copies the fast one. Three titles. Which fits? (TB key: b — A sporting hero).
  2. We read paragraph by paragraph with stops, one ex.2 question after each (TB key: 1 in New York · 2 five · 3 no, swimming was not a popular sport for women · 4 eight world records and an Olympic gold · 5 the weather was terrible · 6 22 extra kilometres — 56 instead of 34 · 7 no, two hours faster than the men's record · 8 she taught deaf children to swim).
  3. The ex.3 paragraph labels in pencil in the margin (TB key: 1 d Trudy's family · 2 c Trudy's swimming talent · 3 e Trudy's Channel swim · 4 b What Trudy did later; the extra ones are a and f).
  4. ex.4: we write the time expressions on the board (TB key: 1 in 1905 · 2 at the age of 12 · 3 when she was 19 · 4 after her swim). ex.5 — three sentences about yourself on those frames.
📺 On screen: SB pp.92–93 ex.1–5 · mini-whiteboards (Backup: I write the four paragraph labels and the four time expressions on the board and students pencil them into the book)
👦 Students do: they read paragraph by paragraph, add the labels and write about themselves. Expect: 56 against 34 km makes an impression — that is the hook

Product: card debate + biography
PRODUCT · card debate + biography
solo → pairs

WHAT WE DO: the biography draft on the four-label plan + peer review on the book's questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A plan on paper for everyone: Paragraph 1 — early life and family. 2 — how they got started. 3 — the most important things they did. 4 — later life.
  2. Choosing the person: Someone from history you admire. Check the facts on TWO websites — one website is a rumour. (TB requirement)
  3. The opening frame: … was born in … . At the age of … , he/she … . When he/she was … , … . After … , … .
  4. We write for 12 minutes; I monitor and help individually (dates, verb forms — the P3 poster is on the wall).
  5. The book's CHECK: interesting information · correct past simple · time expressions · four paragraphs.
  6. Peer review ex.7: first I show on my own draft WHAT to say — “here is label 1, here is a date, here is what's missing”. Then Kagan Timed Pair Share: 90 seconds for the first to read their biography while the listener names one strength and one missing label; on the signal they swap.
📺 On screen: the four-label plan on paper · the P3 verb poster (Backup: I write the plan and the frame on the board if there are no printouts)
👦 Students do: they write to the plan and check each other. Expect: the plan holds the structure — there are almost no long paragraphs. Typical problem: “She borned” — corrected individually: was born

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask for and give an opinion, agree and disagree using 8 Key phrases from a card, and write a four-paragraph biography with past simple and past time expressions.
  • Subsidiary. p.91 ex.1 football vocabulary; ex.2–3 the video; ex.4 completing the dialogue and reading it aloud; Skills boost · pp.92–93 ex.1–2 Trudy Ederle; ex.3 paragraph labels; ex.4 time expressions; ex.5 about yourself; ex.6 their own biography.
  • Personal. The Key phrases card lies on every desk all lesson. The disagreement runs off the card only — I don't ask anyone to improvise. I change the football topic without a second thought; the TB explicitly allows it.
By the end, students can…
  • ask for an opinion (What do you think of … ?)
  • give an opinion with a reason (I think … because …)
  • agree and disagree with a phrase from the card
  • write a biography in four paragraphs with time expressions

Structure

Form
Real-world grammar: Did you see the football last night? · He played well yesterday. Time expressions: in 1905 · at the age of 12 · when she was 19 · after her swim.
Pron. watch
the opinion phrases are drilled by shadowing: I play the recording or read it myself and students repeat straight after the voice, copying the intonation. “No way!” goes up, “You've got a point” goes down.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“What do you think about him?” — “Yes.” Does that work? → no, an opinion is needed: I think he's …
“I am agree” — is that right? → no: I agree or I think so.
How many ideas are there in a paragraph? → one.

Assumptions: SB pp.91–93, syllabus pair 59. The Did…? questions were set in P5 — they come up directly in the dialogue. Not everybody follows football: the TB allows the topic to be changed to music or films.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They are afraid to disagree out loudWe disagree with the phrases from the card only — that takes the personal responsibility away: “it isn't me being rude, it is phrase number 7”.
Half the class knows nothing about footballTB technique: pair someone who knows with someone who doesn't; in their own dialogue the topic is free — singers, games, films.
The biography turns into one long paragraphThe plan with four labels is handed out on paper: family · start · achievements · later life. We write strictly by the labels.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
