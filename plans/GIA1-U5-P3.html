Structure
Can you fly a plane? — Yes, I can. / No, I can't. · What sport can you play? · The laptop is fast. The charger is easy to use.
Form
can + infinitive with NO to · no -s in the third person (he can play) · negative can't (= cannot) · question: (Wh-) + can + subject + infinitive.
Pron. watch
can in a statement is UNSTRESSED and weak /kən/ (I can speak French) · in a short answer — strong /kæn/ (Yes, I can) · can't is always strong and long /kɑːnt/ (TB pp.74–75 ABCD) · 1 keyboard · 2 screen · 3 cheap · 4 piece · 5 police · 6 people · 7 niece · 8 complete (the letters ey, ee, ea, ie, i, eo, e = /iː/)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He can to sing — what's wrong?” → there's no to after can.
“She cans dance?” → can never has an -s, neither on can nor on the verb after it.
“Yes, I can — strong or weak can?” → strong /kæn/: in a short answer it's stressed.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: can is already in speech, but written question accuracy and the missing to are not automatic yet. Tech nouns are partly familiar from life (laptop, screen); touchpad/charger are new. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (question word order with can / comparative spelling / possessive pronouns), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
