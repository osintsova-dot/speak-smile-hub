🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A pair made of two broadcasts: a spoken one (a clothes shop — a skill that works on a real trip) and a written one (a photo description for the “Selfie time” page). The “mystery shopper” storyline: first the agents go into the shop and have to buy an item without slipping into Russian once, then the editorial desk publishes their photo. because and so are served as an editorial standard: without them the text “falls apart into a list”.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Mystery shoppers, listen. Each of you gets money and a target: buy ONE item from these price tags — twenty, twenty-five, thirty, forty, sixty pounds. The shop assistant speaks English only. You ask the price, you ask for a size, you try it on, you buy it. Then — the second half: your photo goes to the Selfie desk, and the editor rejects any description without a because and a so. Wallets out. Go shopping!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Mystery shopper: everyone has to buy an item at a given price tag, and the shop assistant speaks English only; in the second half — the “Selfie time” editorial desk, where the editor rejects any description without because and so.

Challenge: a duel at the price tags
🔥 Challenge · price-tag duel
pairs

WHAT WE DO: the customer has a budget and a target; the assistant has the price tags; close the deal in 3 minutes with no Russian

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out cards: the customer gets a budget (£25 / £40 / £60) and a target (a sweater · some jeans · trainers · a jacket); the assistant gets the price tags from the p.43 slide.
  2. Three minutes per round. Close the deal — or walk out with 'I'm just looking, thanks.' Russian word = the deal dies.
  3. Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: the inner circle are the assistants, the outer one the customers; after each round the outer circle moves on by one — a new shop, a new deal.
  4. Two rounds with a change of roles; in the third round the assistant gets an awkward line (Sorry, we've only got a large) — the customer has to talk their way out of it.
  5. Constraint: at least 5 Key phrases per deal, counted by the observer next to them.
📺 On screen: budget and price-tag cards · timer (backup — the price tags on the board, I call the budgets out loud)
👦 Students do: they haggle for real. Expected: the third round with the awkward assistant is the best one: they talk their way out with phrases instead of going silent

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

PRODUCT · the selfie desk
solo → pairs

WHAT WE DO: THINK (2 photos, answers to questions a–f) → WRITE (two descriptions) → an editor's check → the wall

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: Choose two photos on your phone — or draw two. Answer questions a–f: where · why there · what you're wearing · who with · what they're doing · why you like it. NOTES, not sentences.
  2. WRITE, 8 minutes: Two descriptions. Photo present continuous, habits present simple, at least one because and one so. The support is the openers on the slide: In this photo, I'm… · This is a photo of me with… · I like this photo because…
  3. Editing: they swap texts and use the four-point ex.8 checklist (both tenses · because · so · is it clear what is in the photo). The editor underlines but does NOT rewrite.
  4. Publication: the descriptions go on the unit wall next to the photos. Selfie desk is live!
📺 On screen: the editor's checklist · the unit wall · timer (backup — instead of phones they draw two frames in their exercise books)
👦 Students do: they write in class off a fresh model, then fix each other. Expected: the editing catches 80% of the because/so errors before publication

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can run a full clothes-shopping dialogue with 10 Key phrases and write two photo descriptions using present continuous, present simple and the linkers because and so.
  • Subsidiary. The video with Archie (ex.2–5); sorting the phrases into assistant/customer; Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; the model descriptions A and B; the ex.3 table; the because/so rule (ex.4–6); peer review ex.8.
  • Personal. We write in class, not at home — I see the process. The because/so check is done by editor pairs, not by me. I get the dialogue on its feet through the assistant's gestures (pointing, checking the label, handing the item over) — from the video.
By the end, students can…
  • ask for an item, a price and a size in a shop
  • try something on and say what's wrong with it
  • write a photo description with the right tenses
  • connect ideas with because (reason) and so (result)

Structure
I'm looking for a sweater. · How much is it? · Can I try it on, please? · It's a bit small. What size is it? · Have you got this in a medium? · I'll take it.
Form
Real-world grammar: I'm looking for… (present continuous for an action right now) · because = reason, so = result
Pron. watch
the intonation of a polite request: Can I try it ON, please? ↗ · “How much is it?” — the stress falls on MUCH
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Are “I'm just looking” and “I'm looking for” the same? → no: the first refuses help, the second asks for it.
Is “It's a bit small” a complaint? → no, it is the normal polite formula in a shop.
“I like this photo so it's funny” — what is wrong? → because: that is a reason, not a result.
How many sentences in a photo description? → 4–6, but each about its own thing: where · what I'm wearing · why I like it.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
