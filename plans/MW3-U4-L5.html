Week 07.12–13.12 · Lesson 29🗣 Life skills · staying safe near wild animals~8 pupils · pairs + circlePH 23 (Magic E review · p.64–65) · Genki: Do You Like Animals?(13) · game: Mingle(4)
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, children will be better able to describe wild animals at a safari park — «What's that? — It's an elephant. It's got big ears.» — and to show the life skill of staying safe near wild animals: «Be careful! It's dangerous. Don't go near!»
  • Subsidiary. Watch Lucy, Alex & Auntie Vicky at the safari park (CD2 T10); guess-the-animal from descriptions; draw a wild animal you want to see & describe it in pairs (PB p.25); sing Genki «Do You Like Animals?» (13); Phonics Hero — Magic E review, speed-read p.64–65.
  • Personal. Speaking-focus lesson — never force a shy child; non-verbal (point, mime) counts. Model with the puppet first. Use PAIRS to maximise talking (Teacher's tip). Keep the safety line joyful, not scary: Be careful — but we're safe!
By the end, pupils can…
  • describe an animal — «It's grey. It's got big ears.»;
  • ask & answer «What's that?» in a pair;
  • say the safety words — «Be careful! Don't go near!»

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“The crocodile is dangerous — do we go near or stay back?” → stay back!
“It's got big ears and a big nose — is it an elephant or a snake?” → an elephant!

Assumptions: Animals & «It's got…» are secure from L1–L4; children can produce short descriptions. Today builds a fuller safari exchange + a safety life skill. Magic E reading is at review level (p.64–65). Support with the video, mime & pairs.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Shy — won't describe to a peerStart describing to the puppet, then you, then a partner; non-verbal is fine at first.
Safety idea sounds scaryKeep it playful — we're safe behind the fence!; big careful-face, not fear.
Same partner / clumpsMusic on = walk, music off = describe to the nearest friend (Mix-Pair).
Description fizzles outGive the 2-step frame with fingers: colour (1) · it's got… (2).
Off-task at the drawingDraw one animal, then straight to pairs: ask & answer about your animal (Teacher's tip).
Classroom management — signals
“Class? — Yes!” — you: “Class!” → pupils stop & echo “Yes!”
“WiFi” 🖐 — say “WiFi” + raise a spread hand → pupils silently mirror & tune in.
Volume control — raise/lower your arm = louder/quieter → settler.
“Give me five” — eyes · ears · mouth · hands · feet.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1… pencils down.”
“Freeze!” — statue, mid-movement reset.

9–17′
Phonics Hero 🔤 ⚡
carpet, moving

① Recognise / review. Phonics Hero lesson 23 · Magic E review — sounds Magic E: all — a-e · i-e · o-e · u-e. 🦝 Speaky shows each with its Jolly action (gesture technique); children do the action and NAME the sound — they already know all 26. We don't drill bare sounds: teacher voices, children gesture.

② Blend / build. Spot the magic e and read: cake · kite · nose · cube — blend any sticky one together. ▶ Word-swat

③ Speed reading. open Phonics Hero 2.0 (our workbook) p.64–65 — finger under each word, read the row FAST as a group (whole words, playful, celebrate speed — real reading from zero, GMF1-style).

Tricky words: read would · put together as whole words (we don't sound tricky words out).

17–26′
Curiosity input · safari-park video
carpet

Watch & describe. Lucy, Alex & Auntie Vicky are at the safari park with binoculars. Play the video (CD2 T10). Pause: What's that? It's grey. It's enormous! — It's an elephant! It's got big ears! Children repeat the descriptions.

Life skill — stay safe. The crocodile is in the water: Be careful, children. It's dangerous. Don't go near! Everyone does a big careful-face & steps back — safe behind the fence.

39–46′
Song + speak — Genki «Do You Like Animals?»

Meet & ask. Play Genki «Do You Like Animals?» (13): children mingle; on the pause they meet a friend and ask/answer Do you like tigers? — Yes! / No!

Add a description. new partner each pause; add a description for their favourite: I like giraffes. They've got long legs!

✔ Assess: who describes with It's got…? who uses the safety words? Note it — celebrate every attempt.
