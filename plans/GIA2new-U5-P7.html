🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The newcomers' first big project of the unit — strictly along the coursebook's steps (THINK→PLAN→CREATE→PRESENT), with the diagram, the phrase cards and the notes to the model project. The unit revision is built in as stations, framed as “checking the map before it goes to print” — test preparation without the word “test”. The presentation is the whole crew at the map, the speaker holding the sheet.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Next month someone your age moves to our town. They don't know where to eat, where to skate, where to go when everything is shut. Today your group makes the map that helps them. First we watch two students present their own town — look how they do it. Then we sort places into five groups, then four quick checks (you'll see, you know all of it), and finally your map goes on the wall. Real map, real English.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: in a month a teenager your age is moving here. Today your group makes the map that will help them. First we watch how students from an English town do it, then we sort places into five groups, then four quick checks — and the map goes on the wall.

Model: a map of the town of Poole
Model · the map of Poole
screen · crews

WHAT WE DO: the project video (Sophia and Liam) + Model project notes as a checklist

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 with the task set before watching: Write every place they name. Would you like to live there?
  2. Key from the video: Poole Park with the juice bar and the lake · Baiter Park with the skate park by the sea · The Lighthouse (cinema and arts centre) for a rainy day · Tower Park — cheap fast food.
  3. TB follow-up questions: Where is Poole? Where can you go if it rains? Where's a good place to eat if you don't want to spend much? We watch a second time to check — that second play IS the support.
  4. After the second play, Kagan Numbered Heads Together: the crew boils its answers down to one and checks that every member could say it (“point at it in your notes”), and the number I call answers. If one is not ready, the crew is not ready.
  5. Model project notes go on the board in simple words (TB p.81): Title. Names on the places. Only the MAIN roads. Small icons. Arrows if there's no space. Information boxes. Short sentences: 'There's a …'.
📺 On screen: the Project video on SB p.70 (backup — I read the video script from TB p.142 while the class draws the places on a sketch) · Model project SB p.71
👦 Students do: they watch with a task and end up with a ready checklist. Expect: they recognise the unit's familiar words in live speech — and that feels good

20–32′
Design · five categories
crews

WHAT WE DO: STEP 1–2: the ex.2–3 diagram → the bank of places → choosing the scope → Key phrases on cards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2 from the model map, we fill the diagram in together. TB key: places to eat — fast-food restaurants, juice bar · places to do exercise — country park, skate park, sports centre, park · entertainment — cinema, arts centre, museum · shopping — supermarket, department store · other — station.
  2. ex.3 — the bank of places on the slide (TB sample additions): market, food stalls, music venue, tennis courts, hospital, shopping centre, football stadium. We take at least four.
  3. STEP 2 ex.4: The whole town, or one district? (TB tip: I hand the districts out to different crews so they do not duplicate each other).
  4. Key phrases go on cards and we drill the pronunciation chorally: Don't forget … · What about somewhere for people who … ? · That's a good idea. · There aren't any places for … · Let's add something about transport.
  5. The discussion runs as Kagan Talking Chips: three cards each. Say a phrase — put a card in the middle. Out of cards — you listen and nod; while anyone still has cards left we do not start a new round. I talk one example through myself so the rule is clear.
  6. The Super skill rule with support: Name TWO kinds of people who need something different — and one place for each.
📺 On screen: SB pp.70–71 · the bank of places on the slide · Key phrases cards · Graphic organiser p.120 (Backup: the same written on the board before the lesson)
👦 Students do: they build the diagram with support. Expect: “other people” does not come at once — I help with the TB examples (sporty ones, ones with no money, creative ones, ones who find walking hard)

Four checks · revision stations
stations

WHAT WE DO: 4-minute stations with prompt sheets: food and the groups · places and compounds · a/an/some/any + there is/are · Is/Are there + How much/many

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four checks. Four minutes each. The posters on the wall help you!
  2. At every station there is a prompt card and the poster from that pair (the seven food groups, the station/centre builder, the three-colour poster, the fourth block with much/many).
  3. The first station runs as Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: I demonstrate the move myself with one card, then everyone holds a “word — group” card, quiz — answer — trade — move on. The answer is on the back and peeking is allowed.
  4. A personal “superpower list”: whatever did not work, I write down for myself.
📺 On screen: SB p.69 Quick review · the station cards · the class posters · timer
👦 Students do: they revise the unit with supports. Expect: they recognise their own materials and get through faster than they expect
📓 Interactive notebook — Get_Involved_A2_Unit5_Translation_Match_It_2up_A4 + Get_Involved_A2_Unit5_Translation_Translate_It_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to English A–L (food, countable and uncountable, there is/are, places in a town, How much / How many, directions), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the unit's exit line.
When: ~8 min at the start of the “Four checks · revision stations” stagePrep: 1 × A4 = 2 pupils (pre-cut, one of each sheet per pair). Glue sticks, pencils.

WHAT WE DO: STEP 4: presenting to another group (the speaker holds the sheet) + peer review + WDYT

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Getting ready to go up (TB tip): Who holds the map? Who points? Everyone says one sentence.
  2. The presentation frame: This is a map of our town. If you like sport, there's … . If it's cold, you can go to … . There aren't any … , but there are … .
  3. Peer review (ex.9): Write ONE thing you like about their map. Prepare ONE question.
  4. WDYT: So — what makes a town a good place to live in? We answer from our own map.
  5. Final reflection (SB p.71): is the map clear? did we allow for different people? which of the unit's language did we use?

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can sort places into five categories, make a simple map with information boxes and present it with a frame, and revise the whole unit at four stations.
  • Subsidiary. The Poole video as the model; the ex.2–3 diagram; the Super skills Key phrases on cards; Model project notes; Quick review p.69 as stations with prompt sheets; Final reflection; the WDYT question gets closed.
  • Personal. The project follows the coursebook's steps and I do not shorten it. At the stations there are prompt cards and the posters from the previous pairs. I call the gap list “the superpower list”; the word “test” is only used at the very end of the pair.
By the end, students can…
  • sort places into five categories
  • use project Key phrases to add ideas for other people
  • make a simple, clear map with information boxes
  • pass four revision stations

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The discussion collapses into RussianThe phrase cards are on the table; the rule: a decision only counts if it is made with a phrase off a card.
They do not know what to drawThe ex.2 diagram is filled in from the model map — it gives a ready list of categories and examples; plus a bank of 10 places on the slide.
The stations get squeezed out by the projectThe stations sit firmly in the middle of the pair, on a timer, with the posters and the cards — they are worth no fewer points than the map.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
