Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to recognise, blend & segment the /dʒ/ sound (spelled j and soft g) and to read and write a short club poster (question + information + instruction) about an after-school activity.
  • Subsidiary. Culture/CLIL — read about after-school activities in Ireland (Gaelic football · Irish dancing · hurling · tin whistle); recycle school subjects & Are you good at…? (L1–L4); literacy genre features (catchy question, exciting words, capital letters); 21C — open-mindedness & trying new things.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; run a stirrer→settler rhythm; make sure every child answers/moves in the phonics & culture games (mini-cards, whole-class movement), not just the pair at the board.
By the end, pupils can…
  • hear /dʒ/ and sort j- and soft-g words;
  • read a poster and find who / when / where / cost;
  • plan & write a club poster with a question, an instruction & capital letters.

Pron. watch
/dʒ/ (voiced, jaw drop) vs /tʃ/ (ch); hard g in go, big, gate ≠ soft g in gym, giraffe, orange.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“In gym, is the g a hard /g/ or a j sound?” → j sound /dʒ/.
“Do you play Gaelic football with your feet only?” → no → hands too.
“Does a good poster tell you WHEN and WHERE?” → yes.

Assumptions: pupils know school subjects/places, days of the week, the alphabet and can read/write short texts (L1–L4 done). They can decode single sounds but over-generalise “g always says j” — needs contrast. Teams: max 4 per team (12=3×4 · 10=3+3+4 · 9=3×3 · 8=2×4 · 7=3+4).

10–24′
Phonics /dʒ/ — “Sometimes ‘g’ says ‘j’”
board → desks

① Recognise (listen). Show the giraffe picture (PB p.13). Listen: /dʒ/… /dʒ/… giraffe. Hand on throat — Can you feel it buzz? That's a j sound. Model the line, class repeats twice: The giraffes are jumping and doing judo in the gym. 🦝 Speaky whispers: Look — g in “gym” says j!

② Blend. Segment then blend on fingers: /dʒ/–u–m–p → jump. /dʒ/–u–d–o → judo. Contrast pair for the CCQ: gym (soft) … go (hard). Sort 4 quick words into two hoops on the board: soft g/j vs hard g.

▶ Flyswatter (мухобойки) — /dʒ/ hunt. Board = ~10 word cards, half /dʒ/ (giraffe, gym, orange, jelly, juice, jeans), half distractors (go, big, gate, chips). PIES: every child holds a J card and a G-hard card. You say a word → whole class raises the right card → the two team racers swat the matching board card. Only a swat on a true /dʒ/ word wins a point; racers rotate every 2 words so all play.

📓 Interactive notebook — write & sort (desks, ~4 min). AB p.10: ex.1 Complete & say — fill the missing j/g in The g_iraffes are _umping and doing _udo in the g_ym; then ex.2 Find & circle the words, classify — sort the word-search words into a j column and a g column (juice, jelly, jeans, jump / giraffe, gym, orange, geography). ICQ: Which column for “orange”? → the g column, but it says j!
