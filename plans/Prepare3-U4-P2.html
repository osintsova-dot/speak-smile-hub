🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Name thirteen things you take on an adventure holiday, write down exactly the word, number, date or time you hear, and make, accept and refuse a suggestion.
What is genuinely hard
Writing down what you hear is a separate skill from understanding it. The recording gives more than one number, the name is spelled out, and one wrong digit destroys an otherwise perfect answer.
Typical error to expect
Numbers written out in letters (twelve fifty) where a figure was asked for, and dates in the Russian order. In the OGE the answer sheet takes figures — one letter or one digit wrong and the whole mark goes [exam report]
A word from the option was said in the recording → they tick it. A matching word is not a correct answer; meaning decides [exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson runs from reading to listening to speaking, and the kit vocabulary is the thread through all three: they read it on a leaflet, they hear it in a booking conversation, they argue about it in the final task. The two-minute «dates and numbers by ear» stage at 40′ exists because without it the note-taking listening simply fails — it is the cheapest eight marks in the whole block. The speaking phrases are met inside a dialogue and then sorted by JOB (suggest / agree / disagree / regret), because that is how they will be retrieved under pressure. The final stage is a genuine negotiation: two people, one programme, different wishes.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today you pack a bag and win an argument. First: what goes in the rucksack for a weekend in the mountains. Then: somebody reads you a phone number once — write it down perfectly. Then: your partner wants tennis and you want zip wiring. Talk them round.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, five present-continuous plans from last lesson, and the bridge into the kit list

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last lesson rewind. Five prompts on the board, students say the whole sentence: you / camping / next weekend → I'm going camping next weekend. Then: she / not / come / this evening · what / you / do / on Friday · we / meet / at 3 pm tomorrow · they / not / buy / a paddle board / this weekend.
  3. One rule repeated: Every sentence needs a WHEN.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the second spread: Books open, page forty-four. Same holiday — but now you have to pack for it. Get it wrong and you spend two days wet.
📺 On screen: the five prompts in chalk on the board — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: build the sentences out loud, in pairs first and then to the class. Expect the question form to be the weakest. Typical problem — What you doing on Friday?: put the missing are up in coloured chalk

Lesson frame

Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: name the language aim of the lesson and the standard an answer has to reach

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write one line on the board: 05371 255946.
  2. I will say this ONCE. Write it down. Say it fast and unhelpfully. Then show them the real number and let them count their own errors.
  3. Explain the aim in 40 seconds: today's language is the kit list, and today's SKILL is writing down exactly what you hear — a word, a number, a date, a time. That skill is worth six marks in the state exam and, more usefully, it is how you book anything by phone.
  4. Set the bar: One wrong letter, one wrong digit — the whole answer is wrong. That is the rule everywhere, not just here.
📺 On screen: the number in chalk, revealed after they have tried — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write down the number, then count their own mistakes. Expect most of the class to get it wrong, which is exactly the intended effect. Typical problem: they groan — answer that they will do it correctly by 58′

Reading · Brecon Beacons
solo → pairs
Why: A real leaflet with real consequences — what you pack decides whether the weekend works. Gist first, then detail: the CELTA order, and the source of the kit vocabulary.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3 p.44: the school leaflet about the adventure weekend, gist then six detail questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 first, books almost closed: Look at the photo only. What kind of place is this? What can you do here? Key: 1 mountains · 2 hiking and camping.
  2. Background, 30 seconds, not read out in full: TB p.85: the Brecon Beacons are a mountain range in South Wales, the highest peak Pen y Fan (886 m). The name comes from the tradition of lighting beacons (fires) on the tops to warn of danger. It is a National Park, and also the training ground of the UK armed forces because of the sudden weather.
  3. ex.2 — read the leaflet fast and check the two predictions. 90 seconds on the timer, and say so.
  4. ex.3 — six questions, 6 minutes. The evidence rule: No line from the text — no point. Finger on the line. Kagan RallyCoach: partner A answers 1–3, partner B answers 4–6, each pointing at the line.
  5. Key (TB p.85): 1 It can change very quickly: sunny one minute, raining or foggy and cold the next. · 2 On the kit list. · 3 Because each group is going to carry everything they need for camping and cooking. · 4 Because some students don't walk very much — so the walk is only 25 km over two days. · 5 One night (Saturday). · 6 By bus — it leaves the school at 6 am on Saturday and returns at 8 pm on Sunday.
📺 On screen: the leaflet on the screen so a line can be pointed out to everybody at once (Backup: everyone works from the paper book and you keep a running “who found the line” score on the board)
👦 Students do: predict, skim, then answer with evidence. Expect question 4 (why the walk is short) to be the one that is guessed rather than found. Typical error: they answer question 3 with «because it's heavy» without the line about carrying everything

Vocabulary · things to take
pairs → class
Why: The words are learnt through a decision («what do I need for rain?»), not through a list. A word chosen for a purpose is a word remembered.

WHAT WE DO: Vocabulary ex.1–2 p.44 (track 55): thirteen kit words, then sorted by what they are for

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs, 4 minutes: the kit list to photos A–L. Concept-check the hard ones as you go (TB p.85): You look at this if you are lost. (map and compass) · You energy — snacks.
  2. Play track 55 — check and choral repetition. Key: A backpack · B compass and map · C first-aid kit · D sleeping bag · E snacks · F sun cream · G tent · H torch · I trainers · J wash bag · K walking boots · L waterproof trousers and jacket.
  3. ex.2 — the same words sorted by purpose, in pairs, 4 minutes. This is the stage that fixes meaning: a word is chosen because it does a job. Possible answers (TB p.85): wet weather: waterproof trousers and jacket · sunny weather: sun hat, sun cream, sunglasses, T-shirts · cold weather: sweaters, warm hat, gloves · having fun: playing cards · eating and drinking: food, plate/bowl/mug/knife/fork/spoon, water bottle, snacks · sleeping: pyjamas, tent, sleeping bag · keeping clean: wash bag · not getting lost: map and compass, torch
  4. Collect the answers with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Cold weather — number twos, stand up and give me two things.
  5. Fast finishers: TB p.85: fast finishers underline every word on the kit list they do not know and ask the others. TB p.82: fast finishers write a reason for each corrected sentence — Taylor needs a stadium because she has a lot of fans.
📺 On screen: track 55 in the board app, with the twelve photos on the screen (Backup: work from the paper book and read the kit list aloud yourself, twice, for the class to repeat; the sorting task needs no technology at all)
👦 Students do: match, check, then sort by purpose. Expect wash bag and first-aid kit to be the two new ones. Typical error: waterproof trousers put under «cold weather» — accept it, then ask what makes you coldest

WHAT WE DO: the four Talking points questions from p.44 — a short, genuine opinion exchange

Why: The two things that break in every note-taking listening. Two minutes of calibration here saves the whole of the next stage.

WHAT WE DO: Listening ex.1–2 p.45: say dates out loud, then predict what kind of answer each gap needs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. Pairs write four dates of their own and dictate them to each other. The listener writes what they hear; then they compare papers. 3 minutes.

🎧 Listening out · five gaps
solo → pairs
Why: The productive listening outcome: predict the kind of answer, listen once to answer, listen again to check. The strategy, not the answers, is the point of the stage.

WHAT WE DO: Listening ex.3 p.45 (track 56): complete five gaps in a set of notes, played twice

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the rules before you play: First time you answer. Second time you check. One word, or a number, or a date, or a time — never more.
  2. Play track 56. Then a 30-second pause with no talking: Look at your five answers. Which one are you least sure about?
  3. Play track 56 again. Only now do they compare in pairs.
  4. Key: 1 28th July · 2 16 · 3 river · 4 345 · 5 05371 255946
  5. Where marks go, said out loud after the check: Where marks go: students write the first number they hear (the recording gives more than one), and they spell out numbers in letters and misspell them.
  6. The tip from the book, in one line: Exam tip, SB p.129: think about the kind of word BEFORE you listen; sometimes you hear two possible answers, so listen for which one is right; write numbers as figures, not as words, so the spelling cannot let you down.
📺 On screen: track 56 in the board app, with the information sheet on the screen (Backup: read the audioscript from TB p.292 yourself, twice, at natural speed and with no extra help — the notes are printed in the students' own book)
👦 Students do: listen twice, answer then check, then compare. Expect gap 4 (the number 345) and gap 5 (the phone number) to be the two that decide the score. Typical error: twelve fifty written out in letters

Speaking · suggest, agree, refuse
pairs
Why: The phrases are useless as a list and obvious in a dialogue, so they are met inside one — and sorted by JOB, which is how they will be retrieved.

WHAT WE DO: Speaking ex.1–3 p.45 (track 57): Laura and her dad plan a weekend; the phrases sorted by job

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 — read the dialogue and answer three questions, 3 minutes. Key: 1 They're going kayaking in the morning and horse riding in the afternoon. · 2 He's going waterskiing with Laura's mum. · 3 She's sleeping late..
  2. ex.2 — sort the highlighted phrases into four jobs. a make suggestions — Let's … · How about …? · I'd prefer … · Would you like to …? · Why don't you …? || b agree — That's a great idea! · I'd love to. · Good idea. || c disagree — I'm not that interested in … · I'd prefer to … || d express regret — It's a shame …
  3. The form check that saves the next stage (TB p.86): Note the forms after each phrase (TB p.86): How about + ‑ing · I'd prefer + to‑infinitive · Would you like + to‑infinitive · Why don't you + base form · I'd love + to‑infinitive · I'm not that interested in + ‑ing.
  4. Play track 57. Then pairs practise the dialogue aloud, swapping roles — twice.
  5. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with phrase cards: one side the phrase, the other side its job. 3 minutes, five trades each.
📺 On screen: track 57 in the board app with the dialogue on the screen, the phrases colour-coded by job (Backup: the dialogue is printed in the students' own book — read Dad's lines yourself and let the class read Laura's, then swap)
👦 Students do: read, sort, listen, act out, then mingle with the cards. Expect It's a shame to be the one nobody can place. Typical error: How about to go kayaking? — the ‑ing form is the whole point of the form check

🎯 Speaking out · plan the weekend
pairs → class
Why: A genuine negotiation: two people, one programme, different wishes. They cannot finish without using the phrases, which is the definition of a good speaking task.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4–5 p.45: the Golden Sands Beach programme — two people, one weekend, different wishes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. SB p.45 ex.4–5: the Golden Sands Beach programme — Saturday morning surfing OR waterskiing, afternoon climbing OR hiking, evening barbecue OR night walk and picnic; Sunday morning zip wiring OR mountain biking, afternoon tennis OR beach volleyball. Each partner circles what they want, then they have to agree on one plan.
  2. Add the constraint that makes it a real task: give each student a secret slip with TWO activities they must refuse politely. They may not say why.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share: 3 minutes to agree the whole weekend, then swap partners and do it again with the same slip.
  4. The standard: You may not say no. You must say no POLITELY and then suggest something else.
  5. Two pairs perform their negotiation to the class. The class listens for one thing only: did anybody change their mind?
  6. Log errors silently; the feedback slot is the start of the next lesson.
📺 On screen: the Golden Sands programme on the screen and a 3-minute timer (Backup: the programme is printed on SB p.45 — work from the book and time it on the wall clock)
👦 Students do: negotiate a full weekend twice, with different partners. Expect the second round to be faster and more polite. Typical error: I don't want instead of I'm not that interested in … — note it, say nothing

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«It's a shame about the waterskiing» — is he happy or sorry? → sorry: it expresses regret, not disagreement.
«How about mountain biking?» — what form comes next, biking or to bike? → ‑ing.
«Would you like to go horse riding?» — is this a question about the future or a suggestion? → both: it is the polite way to suggest.
In the notes task, gap 4 asks what NOT to wear. So am I listening for the right clothes or the wrong ones? → the wrong ones. Read the gap before you listen.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Everybody answers the notes task from the first listening and stopsMake the second play compulsory and give it a different job: Second time you are not answering. You are checking. Pen down until you disagree with yourself.
Numbers written out in wordsOne rule, said once and written on the board: If it is a number, write the number. It is in the book's own exam tip on SB p.129.
The final speaking collapses into “yes, ok”Give each partner a secret card with two activities they must NOT agree to. Now there is something to negotiate and the refusal phrases have a job.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 6–11 (listening: write in the word, the name or the number you hear — six marks).

This is the closest match in the whole block. The coursebook task is mechanically the same: five gaps, one word or a number or a date or a time in each. In the exam the answer goes on an answer sheet, so a misspelt word or a number written in letters loses the mark.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
