🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The three ways of talking about the future stand side by side for the first time, and for new students that risks turning into mush. So give them not three rules but one sorting question: I just think so (will) / I decided (going to) / we agreed (present continuous). The sorting comes BEFORE the form and is repeated all lesson. Students build the “three futures” sheet with their own hands and take it home — it is their support in the test as well.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Three ways to talk about the future, and today you get all three on one sheet. ‘I think I'll go to the beach’ — I just think so. ‘I'm going to go to the beach’ — I decided. ‘I'm going to the beach on Saturday at nine with Dasha’ — we agreed, and now somebody is waiting for me. One question sorts them all: did I think it, decide it, or agree it? At the end you write your own week — six lines. Sheets out.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: three ways of talking about the future on one sheet. will = I just think so. going to = I decided. present continuous = we agreed, there is a time and a second person. One question sorts them all. At the end we write our own week — six lines.

WHAT WE DO: build the sorting sheet by hand and practise on obvious examples

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone rules a sheet into three columns: I THINK (will) · I DECIDED (going to) · WE AGREED (present continuous).
  2. Say ten sentences out loud — students write them into the right column (the form does not matter yet, the meaning does): I think it'll rain. · We're meeting at the cinema at seven. · I'm going to learn guitar this summer. · She'll be a doctor one day. · They're flying to Sochi on Friday. · I'm going to tidy my room. · It won't be expensive. · We're having a barbecue on Saturday at my place. · He's going to look for a job. · Nobody will remember this.
  3. Check together: How do you know it's AGREED? A time. A place. Another person.
  4. Kagan Think-Pair-Share: compare the sheets in pairs.
📺 On screen: a sorting sheet for everyone · slide with the ten sentences (Backup: read the ten sentences out loud twice each)
👦 Students do: sort by meaning before any form. Expect: DECIDED and AGREED get mixed up — talk every disputed case through out loud

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 the rule → ex.2 five sentences with support → ex.3 about themselves → ex.4 questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 together: Finish the rule: subject + … (not) + going to + … . (TB key: be, verb (infinitive)). The formula on the screen: BE + going to + VERB.
  2. TB prompt before ex.2: work out the form of be first (am / is / are), then write. Do the first sentence together (TB key: 1 'm going to study · 2 isn't going to work · 3 are going to go · 4 's going to come · 5 'm not going to watch).
  3. ex.3 straight after it — the same sentences about themselves (TB sample answers: 2 My mum's going to work on Saturday. · 5 I'm going to watch TV.).
  4. ex.4 the question table (TB key: 1 Is · 2 isn't · 3 Are · 4 aren't). TB note: the negative short answer can be contracted two ways — No, she's not / No, she isn't.
  5. ex.5 building questions, the first one together (TB key: 1 Are you going to go on holiday this summer? · 2 Where are you going to go? · 3 Where are you going to stay? · 4 Are you going to go on any day trips?) → ex.6 in pairs AFTER the answers are written down.
  6. ex.6 runs as Kagan RallyRobin, and you model it on yourself first: take one student, ask them the first question, they answer and ask you the second — that is the whole move. Then in pairs strictly in turn: asked — answered — now the second one asks. The answers are already in the notebooks and may be read from there.
📺 On screen: SB p.102 ex.1–6 · the formula on screen · timer (Backup: write the formula BE + going to + VERB on the board and do not rub it off; the question table too)
👦 Students do: work out the rule and drill the form with support. Expect: “going to go” looks like a mistake to them — show that it is normal

WHAT WE DO: ex.7 the rule → -ing spelling → ex.8 Marco's calendar

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.7: Possible or definite? Read the three examples and choose. (TB key: definite). Talk it through: Time, place, another person — that's what makes it definite.
  2. The -ing spelling on the board in advance (the TB asks for it): have → having · get → getting · travel → travelling. Say them and write them down.
  3. ex.8 Marco's calendar — the first sentence is given in the book, do the second together, the rest in crews (TB key: On Tuesday, he's having a guitar lesson at half past five. · On Wednesday, he's getting a bus to the water park at eight o'clock. · On Thursday, he's going to the cinema with Izan at seven o'clock. · On Friday, he's staying with Adrian. · On Saturday, he's travelling to the mountains.)
  4. The crew closes the remaining days with Kagan RoundTable: one sheet per crew, one day per person. Start the round only after the second sentence has been written on the board together — the model stays in front of their eyes. You get the sheet, you write YOUR day in full (day + be + -ing + time) and pass it on; passing it on empty is not allowed. The three -ing words are on the board and looking at them is allowed.
  5. TB Extra activity as a run-up: Open your school timetable. What are you doing on Monday afternoon? — I'm studying French.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can form going to statements, negatives and questions, use the present continuous for arrangements, and write their own week in 6 lines with the right future in each.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the going to rule; ex.2–3 five sentences and rewriting them about themselves; ex.4 the question table; ex.5–6 questions about the summer; ex.7 the present continuous rule; ex.8 Marco's calendar; ex.9 Grammar round-up in part.
  • Personal. The topic is named out loud. Students build the “three futures” sheet by hand at the start and keep it on the desk to the end. The mini-test comes before the product — the mistake is found before the stage.
By the end, students can…
  • say a plan with going to (+, − and ?)
  • give a short answer with going to
  • turn a calendar into present continuous sentences
  • write my own week in 6 lines with the right future

Structure
I'm going to buy tickets tomorrow. · She's going to look after her sister. · They're going to try the new water slide. · I'm not going to have a holiday this year. · He isn't going to see his friends. · They aren't going to be home until late. · We're meeting at the bus station at 8:00. · A band is playing in the park on Saturday. · What are you doing this afternoon?
Form
subject + be (not) + going to + verb · question: (Question word) + be + subject + going to + verb · present continuous for the future = be + V-ing + a time/place. -ing spelling: have → having · get → getting · travel → travelling.
Pron. watch
in real speech going to sounds almost like “gonna”, and the stress falls on the second verb: I'm going to BUY tickets
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“She going to work” — what is missing? → be: She's going to work.
“We meeting at seven” — what is missing? → be: We're meeting at seven.
“I'm going to the cinema on Friday” — did I decide it or did we agree it? → there is a day, so we agreed.

Assumptions: SB p.102, syllabus pair 66. The present continuous for “now” has been familiar since Unit 3; what is new is the same present continuous about the future.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Three forms turning into mushThe “three futures” sheet with one sorting question: I think / I decided / we agreed. Sort BEFORE the form, all lesson long.
They forget the beA rule with a gesture: be is the ignition key. No be — the engine won't start. Check it with your hands before every hand-in.
-ing spellingThree words on the board in advance (the TB asks for it): have → having · get → getting · travel → travelling.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
