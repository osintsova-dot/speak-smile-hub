Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will have consolidated all of Unit 1 “Let's eat!” — 12 ingredients + 6 cooking verbs, quantifiers (a lot of / a few / a little), How much / How many, need to + verb, recipe sequencers (First/Next/Then/Finally) and the phonics i_e–ie — and shown it under pressure in the flagship Team Quiz with a reward game.
  • Subsidiary. Retrieval practice of the week's language (spaced, low-stakes); AB Review «Ready, Steady, Go!» (p.14); interactive-notebook translation (MATCH IT! + TRANSLATE IT!); honest self-assessment — readiness for the L7 test. Break Luke's code (→ HAWAII, a bridge to Unit 2).
  • Personal. Quiz = every child answers (Showdown boards), not captains only; keep the reward game short and joyful — it's the cherry, not the cake; both teams celebrate at the end.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name & spell unit words; sort countable ↔ uncountable;
  • ask & answer How much / How many + need to;
  • order recipe steps with First/Next/Then/Finally;
  • self-assess honestly with the traffic light.

Fast recall, all together (low-stakes). ① Flash the 12 ingredient cards — chorus + sort countable / uncountable with a hand signal (fingers = count, flat palm = mass); ② the 6 cooking verbs — a TPR action chain round the circle (stir → mix → chop → heat → pour → add); ③ two-question drill: point to a card → the class asks with How much…? OR How many…? and answers We need a little / a few…

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: Stand up if you need MANY of these… nuts! …flour? …biscuits? …sugar? — instant whole-class check, trick them with the uncountables. Every child moves every time.

9–16′
▶ Team Memo 🧘
tables, teams of 3–4

Memory match — pairs = picture ↔ word (or verb ↔ its action-picture). Give each small group its own face-down set of ~12 cards so many children flip at the same time (no one waits long).

The rule that hides the drill: to keep a matched pair, the flipper must say a full target sentence — not just the word: I need a few nuts. / I need a little cream. / First, chop the nuts. Team-mates chorus it back to verify; wrong or silent = cards go back down, next player's turn.

ICQ: Do you keep the pair for a word, or for a whole sentence? (→ a whole sentence). Most pairs at the table wins. 💪 support pupils get a sentence-starter strip; ⭐ fast finishers add How much / How many before the sentence.

2-up translation sheet (one A4 = 2 pupils). Two parts, do BOTH: MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences ↔ English A–L (some / a few / a little, How much / How many, need to + verb, the recipe sequencers); TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 written out in English from memory.
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — in class, pairs first, then check together.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back (start in class, finish at home).
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; mistakes are rewritten in full, not just crossed out.
  • Count your score out of 12 and write it in the margin — the unit's exit line.

28–35′
▶ Recipe Relay challenge ⚡
open floor, 2 teams

Card-grab relay — teams line up. Ingredient + verb cards are spread on the far wall/ledge. You call a clue — one runner per team races to grab the right card and brings it back saying the full sentence:

· You need a LITTLE of this — you can't count it! → runner grabs sugar / oil / cream → We need a little sugar.
· First you do this to the nuts… → chop → First, chop the nuts.
· You need a FEW of these… → nuts / raisins / biscuits → We need a few raisins.

The card only counts when the whole team chorus the sentence. Rotate the runner every round so every child runs. Fastest correct = a point (feeds into the quiz score). Reset with Freeze! if it gets loud.

 ingredient + verb wall cards · 2 team baskets · clue list
35–51′
🏆 TEAM QUIZ + reward ⚡🧘
desks, 2 teams

Flagship quiz (board or projector). Grid of tiles, interleaved on purpose — item types are mixed, never blocked, so pupils must decide which rule applies each time: picture → ingredient · verb → action · How much OR How many? · a few OR a little? · a need to sentence · order-the-recipe (First/Next/Then/Finally) · a phonics tile (i_e–ie: which spelling? rice · pie) · break-the-code.

Mechanics — Kagan Showdown: a tile opens → EVERY child writes/says the answer on their own board → Show me! → majority correct = team point → reward: one player takes a penalty kick / bowling shot (paper ball into a box). Rotate the shooter strictly — a different child each win.

🎮 Offline setup: card grid on the whiteboard + a real paper-ball penalty into a box/basket — one shot per win, 20 sec each.

ICQ: When do you show your board? (→ only on “Show me!”). Finale: count both scores in English together; Give me five!
