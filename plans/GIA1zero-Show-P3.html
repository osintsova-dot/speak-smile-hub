🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The small-group format is a shield against fear: 4–5 of their own + notes in hand. My job is to manage to HEAR everyone (I move between groups) and give each one personal praise. Nobody leaves without a success.

WHAT WE DO: seating for small groups, group hosts appointed, the smiley system handed out; a 60-sec protocol rehearsal

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

🏁 Wave 1 · performances in groups
groups

WHAT WE DO: wave 1: everyone in every group performs in turn; the audience gives smileys with compliments; I move around and listen

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start: Wave one — leaders, begin! Groups work IN PARALLEL — everyone performs at the same time in their own group.
  2. I move around: I listen to at least 2 performances in every group, noting down praise by name.
  3. A freeze — interview mode using their own frame; a smiley for courage.
  4. Hosts keep the protocol: announcement → talk → applause → smiley+compliment.
📺 On screen: groups · smileys · my notes
👦 Students do: everyone performs — in a safe group, with notes; the audience learns to praise specifically. Expected: 20 minutes is enough for all groups. Typical error: the compliment “good” — the frame: WHAT part?

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Find Someone Who: an interval mixer on performance facts — “find someone who talked about…”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: wave 2: NEW groups (mixed up) — a second run for everyone: a new audience = a new success; smileys again

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I mix the groups: New groups — new audience! Same talk, braver voice!
  2. A second run for everyone: the lines sound more confident now (the second time is always easier — I say this out loud).
  3. The audience: a smiley + a compliment for a NEW line (not the one from wave 1).

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, every learner has performed “My world” (60–90 sec, notes in hand) in a small group — and volunteers to the class too; everyone got smileys and a nomination.
  • Subsidiary. The room works in a friendly way: a smiley rating + 1 compliment from the frame; a second wave — a debrief and a “success party” (KTP); confidence is locked in with public praise for everyone.
  • Personal. The small-group format is a shield against fear: 4–5 of their own + notes in hand. My job is to manage to HEAR everyone (I move between groups) and give each one personal praise. Nobody leaves without a success.
By the end, students can…
  • perform their talk with notes in a small group
  • give a friendly compliment by frame
  • award smileys honestly and kindly
  • say what got better since P2

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Forgot a line?” → Look at your notes — they're in your hand FOR THIS. A pause is normal.
“Are smileys marks?” → They're a thank-you. Three smileys — three specific “thank you for…”s.
“Is the class stage compulsory?” → No: the group — compulsory, the class — by choice. Both stages are real.

Assumptions: Everyone has rehearsed (P2 + homework); groups and the order are known from P2. Those who missed P2 perform a short frame (3 lines) — that's a success too.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Freezes in the groupI come over, interview mode using their own frame: What's your name? What do you like? — the lines come out as a dialogue; a smiley for courage either way.
The group gigglesThe room rule on the board + the smiley system works for good: smileys are given by the AUDIENCE, an unkind viewer = no vote (won't be needed, but it's stated).
Strong learners get bored in the groupsThe group host role: announces the performers in English (Next — …! Give it up!) — mini-MC training.
Not everyone gets to the class stageAnd they shouldn't: the class stage is a bonus for volunteers. The group stage = a full performance, and I call it that.
Tears from nervesA quiet corner + water + “you'll go last in my group, I'm right here”; afterwards — double praise for pushing through.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
