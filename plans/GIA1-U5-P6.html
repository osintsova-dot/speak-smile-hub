🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Project and test in one pair — strict timing: every STEP has its own timer, the test is exactly 30 minutes. The revenge quiz puts the full stop not on the grade, but on “what you fixed”.

“Two things today. First you build a robot that solves a real problem — with roles and timers. Then the test: thirty minutes, every format you've already met.” “What did you learn about technology in this unit? What did you enjoy most?” “Is the plan for something that already exists?”

STEP 2 PLAN · problem + roles
crews of 3

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share: threes with roles: choose a problem (ex.4) + drill the Key phrases Creativity (ex.5); a strict timer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Threes (TB p.81 — the project really is in groups of three). Roles on cards: Problem-owner · Robot-designer · Presenter — but all three speak.
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A talks for their timed turn, B is an active listener (counts pauses on fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. Ex.4: Think of a problem. Only the problem for now — no robot yet. Four minutes. Reinforcement (TB p.81): a bank of problems on the board — litter in the street · cleaning your room · homework · losing your phone or keys · learning English · doing sport (TB p.81 Reinforcement).
  4. We fill in THE PROBLEM using the 4 questions (Who/What/Where/When) — as in ex.2.
  5. Ex.5 — Key phrases Creativity, we drill the intonation in chorus, then in threes: What do you think? · Let's listen to (Jon). · Why don't we (+ verb)? · Don't forget … · That's a good idea. · That's great!.
  6. Work rule (Super skills box): Listen to everyone. Use your imagination. Your idea must SOLVE the problem.
📺 On screen: role cards · the problem bank on the board · SB p.70 Super skills box · timer
👦 Students do: they choose a problem in threes, drill the Key phrases. Expected: 2–3 strong everyday problems (lost keys, cleaning). Typical error: a fantasy problem (“a teleporter”) — I bring them back to “real problem in YOUR life”.

Step 3: we put together four slides
STEP 3 CREATE · four slides
crews of 3

WHAT WE DO: we design the robot and put together 4 slides using How to p.120; language targets on the board; a timer per slide

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.6: Design a robot that solves YOUR problem. Five minutes for the drawing — no more.
  2. Ex.7 — four slides (SB p.70): Slide 1: explain the problem · Slide 2: introduce your robot · Slide 3: more information — the robot's abilities, comparing it with other solutions · Slide 4: say how it solves the problem. 2 minutes per slide, I announce the transitions.
  3. Language targets on the board (what scores points): 3+ ‘can’ · 2+ comparatives with than · 1 possessive pronoun · problem→solution link.
  4. I remind them (TB p.82): Notes on slides, not sentences. The details live in your voice.
  5. We assign who talks on which slide (TB p.82) and rehearse one run-through in the three.

👦 Students do: they draw, label with arrows, assign slides and rehearse. Expected: 4 slides ready for every three in 14 minutes. Typical error: paragraph text on a slide — I point at the model p.71.

WHAT WE DO: presentations by the threes + Peer review on 3 questions ex.8; judging by the axes TB p.82

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Each three — 2 minutes (all three speak). Judging axes (TB p.82): Layout (a large drawing + labelling arrows to details) · Content (little text on the slide, details — spoken) · Language (notes, not full sentences; can for abilities + comparatives for comparison) · Visuals (clear without explanations) — TB p.82.
  2. Listeners are never idle: everyone has a card with three Peer review questions (ex.8): 1 Is the robot a good solution? 2 Ask a question about the robot. 3 What differences are there between your robots?
  3. After every three — ONE question from the floor is compulsory (that also scores for the asker).
  4. The teacher keeps the language score: a tick for every target (can / comparative / possessive / problem-solution).
  5. 🚀 bonus superlative award: Comparatives compare two. To crown ONE you need the superlative — not in this unit, but you're getting it: the MOST useful robot, the FASTEST robot. I hand out 3 awards with the formulas from the slide.

The Unit 5 test — 30 minutes
Unit 5 Test (30 minutes exactly)
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: the test by the specification below: Vocabulary · Grammar · Listening · Writing; quiet, no prompts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I start the timer for 30 minutes, visible to everyone.
  2. I read the Listening part myself twice (or play the track) at the 12th minute of the test — announced in advance.
  3. I circulate in silence; I only answer questions about the task instructions, not the content.
  4. 5 minutes before the end: Five minutes. Check your comparatives and your spelling.
  5. I collect the papers; whoever finishes early does Fast finishers: WB Self-evaluation p.43 (honest ticks).
📺 On screen: test papers (higher) · a 30-min timer · audio for part 3 (Backup: I read it myself)
👦 Students do: they write the test solo in silence; those who finish early do self-evaluation. Expected: 25–30 minutes is enough for everyone. Typical error: they skip Writing for lack of time — a warning at the 20th minute: “Writing is ten points, don't drop it”.

WHAT WE DO: an express revenge: 4 items from the test in teams (points for what's corrected) + a re-vote on the unit question

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Revenge quiz: I put up 4 “hot” items (a can-question · a comparative with doubling · a possessive pronoun · a spelling-trap word). Crews answer in a volley on their boards — the point is for what's CORRECTED.
  2. Everyone names out loud one thing they'll nail (I write it on the “wall of goals”).
  3. WDYT?-re-vote (TB p.81): Does technology make the world better? Vote again — same walls. I compare with pair 1's score: Four people changed their mind. Who? Why? — 2 votes of 20 sec.
  4. HW: WB SEL pp.76–77 + Super skills p.42 + a portion of Exam trainer + “top-2 mistakes”.
  5. Teaser: Unit 6 — art, rooms and a tense that says what's happening RIGHT NOW.
📺 On screen: the revenge-quiz slide (Backup: the 4 items written on the board) · the voting walls (the same as pair 1) · the wall of goals
👦 Students do: they bat back the revenge on their boards, physically vote, name their goals. Expected: 3–5 people changed their mind — the best possible ending to the unit. Typical error: discussing the “grade” instead of the errors — I keep the focus: “points tomorrow, errors now”.

Unit 5 test — specification (DRAFT until the official Macmillan A1+ pack)
PartContents & marking
Part 1 · Vocabulary (14 pts)A) 6 items: ability collocations — ___ a ball (catch), ___ a plane (fly), ___ a language (speak), ___ a bike (repair), ___ numbers (add), ___ stories (tell). B) 4 items: label the tech nouns from a picture (touchpad · charger · screen · speaker — traps: keyboard/touchpad). C) 4 items: adjective opposites (cheap ↔ ___ · heavy ↔ ___ · difficult ↔ ___ · fast ↔ ___).
Part 2 · Grammar (18 pts)A) 6 gaps: can / can't affirmative & negative (Robots ___ get tired. · He ___ sing, but he ___ play the piano.). B) 4 items: order the words → a Can-question and a Wh-question + a true short answer (you / fly / can / a plane ?). C) 5 items: comparative forms across all five rules (big → ___ · easy → ___ · expensive → ___ · good → ___ · light → ___) + a than trap. D) 3 items: possessive pronouns (Is this your tablet? — No, it isn't ___ .).
Part 3 · Listening (8 pts)A short talk about two gadgets (in the style of SB p.65 ex.6–7; teacher-read twice if no track): 4 items — complete a table “You can …” with ticks + 4 items — true/false about price and size. Mirrors Subskill: completing a table.
Part 4 · Writing (10 pts)An advert (30+ words) for an object you want to sell. Marked for: can/can't + what you can do with it (3) · at least two comparatives with than (3) · adjectives + price/condition (2) · spelling of unit words (2) — mirrors SB p.69 ex.8 Skills boost and Subskill: Spelling.

Total 50 pts · pass focus: not the number but the personal error log. Built strictly from Quick review SB p.69 — every format was met in class during the unit. The two lines: 🚀 higher (Wh-questions with can in writing, all five comparative rules, more spelling traps) · 🐣 standard (Yes/No questions, comparatives by rules 1, 3 and 4, only familiar traps).

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
