🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The compound nouns come in not as a table but as a kit list: if an item is named wrongly (or written as two words instead of one), the store 'doesn't issue it'. Brett the guide's briefing is built in as a real briefing before setting off: the team takes the park rules out of it, rules they will need tomorrow — and at the same time they hear must / mustn't / can't in live speech for the first time (groundwork for P5). The subskill (using pictures before listening) is held in place by the teams naming everything they see and predicting the words before the audio.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Outward Bound Yosemite. You're going. One week, a five-hour walk in the mountains, and one backpack each — not a suitcase, a backpack. The store gives you an item only if you name it correctly in English: sleeping bag, water bottle, penknife, sun cream. And before we go, the guide has a briefing — there's a bear in this park who comes for toothpaste. Listen well. Ten items, sixty seconds to defend them.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We are being taken on an expedition to Yosemite: a week, a five-hour walk in the mountains, one backpack. The store hands you an item only if you name it correctly. Before we set off there is the guide's briefing (there is a bear there that likes toothpaste). At the end you defend your ten items.

PRODUCT · the packing pitch
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: building a list of 10 items (+ a WHY for each) → a 60-second pitch → a closed vote on 'which backpack makes it to the end of the hike'

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes to build it: Exactly ten items. One why-line each. At least one comparison and at least one rule from the guide.
  2. Sixty seconds to sell your backpack. Then the vote: sticky dots, you can't vote for your own.
  3. The judges hold the rubric + a separate 'wrong spelling' flag (a point off straight away).
  4. One list wins — we put it on the unit wall as the 'official kit'.
📺 On screen: list form · stickers · timer · the rubric
👦 Students do: they build, defend and vote. Expect: the 'knife or cream' argument is settled by the argument, not by taste

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can name 14 compound nouns correctly (one word vs two words), catch 6 park rules from a guide's briefing, and defend a 10-item packing list with reasons.
  • Subsidiary. The Outward Bound leaflet (ex.1–3) as the way in; the ex.4 table (one word / two words) is worked out by sorting; ex.5 the labels for photos A–K; the subskill using pictures to help you understand; ex.7–8 (track 27) in two passes; ex.9 the pros and cons of a trip like this.
  • Personal. The listening runs strictly in two passes: no.1 — the order of the pictures, no.2 — the details of the rules. Before the audio we always say the pictures out loud (the subskill rule). I do NOT announce the modal verbs from the briefing as a topic — they will surface in P5.
By the end, students can…
  • name 14 things to take on a trip and write them correctly
  • say which compounds are one word and which are two
  • use pictures to predict what a recording is about
  • defend a 10-item packing list with reasons

Form
some compounds are written as ONE word: backpack, guidebook, hairbrush, penknife, suitcase, toothbrush, toothpaste · others as TWO: beach towel, phrase book, sleeping bag, sun cream, wash bag, water bottle, waterproof jacket · there is no 'by ear' rule — this has to be memorised and checked against the list in Quick review p.57 (track 29)
Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'back pack' as two words — is that allowed? → no: backpack is one word; sleeping bag, on the other hand, is two.
penknife — do we say the k? → no, /ˈpenˌnaɪf/: the k is silent.
Why look at the pictures BEFORE listening? → so you name the objects and predict the words — then you recognise them in the stream (the subskill of the unit).

Assumptions: SB p.53, KTP pair 33 (the KTP says p.54 — a +1 shift, the real page is 53). Half the items are recognisable from the picture; the difficulty is in writing 'one word / two words' and in the speed.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
