🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that two units work without the book: 22 outdoor and shop words, twelve irregular participles, the present perfect in all the forms met, and correct spelling.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the two invisible things. The third form of the verb, which is a list and behaves like one under time pressure; and the position of just, yet and already, which survives orally and collapses on paper.
Typical error to expect
swimmed, breaked, eated, flied, growed — the regular ‑ed ending put on an irregular verb. In the exam a misspelt form scores nothing [high priority, A2/B1]
I have already not finished · I have finished already it — the word order: just and already go after have and before the main verb, yet goes at the end [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is retrieval practice, not re-teaching: the two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it and what shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which forms still break. ⚠️ Unlike block 6, there is NO Review page in the coursebook for these two units — Review 3 covers Units 9–12 and Review 4 comes only after Unit 16 — so the stations are built from the unit spreads themselves and from the photocopiable worksheets, exactly as in blocks 3 and 5. The game is placed between the stations and the briefing on purpose, and this block's game earns its place twice over: four of its thirteen playable cards are present perfect drills wearing a vocabulary costume — list five things you have never bought. The briefing comes after the stations because only by then do you know which five words belong in the «watch out» column.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. There is no revision page in your book for these two units, so we make one. First forty-five minutes we play the block back. Then forty-five minutes you show it. Half the paper is the real Cambridge one and half is mine. Your score is not the point: your own list of five mistakes is.”

WHAT WE DO: «List it!» — the Cambridge review game for Units 13–16

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Any number of teams of one to three. The cards are cut up and put face down in a pile. A player picks up a card and reads it out to their team — List 5 places you can look for fossils. All the teams race to write down five items. When a team has five they shout Listed! and everybody else stops. You check discreetly that the five are valid; if they are not, you say Keep writing and the game goes on. The winning team then reads its five out and the other teams tick off anything they also had.
  2. One point for being first with five valid items · one bonus point for every item that appears on nobody else's list · one extra point if all five are spelt correctly. Keep the score on a single sheet of paper. Students will argue about whether an answer counts — the teacher's page says that is part of the game and good, genuine communication. Be a referee, not a dictionary.
  3. The pack covers Units 13–16, so play only the cards whose answer belongs to our two units. Unit 13: List 5 places to try rock climbing · List 5 places you can look for fossils · List 5 places humans have never visited · List 5 things you have never seen · List 5 activities you enjoy with friends. Unit 14: List 5 types of shop · List 5 units of measurement · List 5 things that come in pairs «a pair of …» · List 5 things that come in slices «a slice of …» · List 5 things you can buy in a newsagent's · List 5 things you can buy in a supermarket · List 5 places where you can get a sandwich · List 5 things you have never bought. The rest (things that help you learn a language, typical spelling mistakes, good places to listen to music, activities on a smartphone, things you can ride, things you can do online, things that make people feel glad, good places to read books) belongs to Units 15–16 and has not been taught yet — leave those cards out of the pile.
  4. Notice what four of the thirteen cards are: things you have never seen · things you have never bought · places humans have never visited. The game is a present perfect drill wearing a vocabulary costume, which is exactly what this block needs the day before its test.
  5. Choose one variation before you start: Three variations are printed. Use Longer list (up to ten items) only with a strong group; List unlimited (as many as you can in a fixed time) is the fairest when the group is mixed, because nobody is knocked out by speed alone. Whole class — you simply read the cards out and everybody works alone — is the version to use if the class is under fifteen and you are short of time.
  6. Nine minutes on a visible timer. Keep the score on one sheet where everybody can see it.
📺 On screen: the timer and the running score on the board (Backup: the cards are photocopied and cut up in advance from PREPARE_3_Review Games_Units 13-16.pdf and the game is entirely paper — nothing here can fail)
👦 Students do: race to list five, then defend the list. Expect the argument about what counts to be the best English of the stage, and expect five things you have never bought to be the card that produces the most laughter. Typical problem: one team writes in Russian to be fast — that list scores nothing, and say so before the first card

WHAT WE DO: the block 7 paper — Unit test 13 (official) plus parts 6–10 for Unit 14

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pens down. Paper over. You have thirty-five minutes. Start with part one. Timer on the board.
  2. Run part 5 at about the 15-minute mark: the eight past participles from TG3-U13_01…03, each item played twice, with the class working in silence.
  3. Run part 10 at about the 30-minute mark: read your six sentences aloud, twice each, half as questions and half as statements. Do not exaggerate the intonation — that is the test.
  4. Circulate once, silently, at the halfway point. Answer no content questions at all — point at the instruction line instead.
  5. Collect the papers face down as they finish. No comparing answers, no talking, even after the last paper is in.
📺 On screen: the timer on the board and the audio cued in order (Backup if the sound fails: say the eight past participles yourself — they are single words and your voice is a legitimate substitute. Write on the papers that part 5 was run live)
👦 Students do: work in silence for thirty-five minutes. Expect part 1 (the ten collocations) to be the fastest and part 4 (questions and short answers) to be the slowest. Typical problem: they leave part 5 blank because the audio is coming later — say clearly at the start that parts 5 and 10 are done together and out of order

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
There is no book page to revise from and it showsSay it and turn it into an advantage: we make our own revision page. The two stations ARE that page, and the worksheets in the course folder supply the exercises.
Someone finishes the paper in 25 minutesThe rule: hand it in, turn the paper over and write five sentences about your own life in the present perfect — three with never and two with already. It is not a punishment and it is the language of the block.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
