🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Sit the publisher's End of Year test — fifteen sections, one hundred marks, covering the vocabulary and grammar of all ten units plus listening, reading, an essay and a speaking task — and finish the course with twenty minutes that are worth remembering.
What is genuinely hard
Not the English: everything on this paper has been taught and tested once already. The difficulty is coverage and clock. Ten units of vocabulary are spelled from memory in the first three sections; the grammar half asks for word order, error correction and an open cloze with no options anywhere; and at the end of it, when concentration is gone, there is a 140–190-word essay worth ten marks. The students who lose most are the ones who spend twenty-five minutes polishing the vocabulary sections and then write four sentences of essay.
Typical error to expect
An essay that answers the two given notes and forgets the third point. Part 1 gives two notes and a blank line marked «your own idea», and the blank line is worth as much as the other two. It is the single most common way to lose Content marks in this paper.
Why the lesson is built this way
Ten minutes of express revision aimed only at the seven grammar lines the paper actually tests, then the speaking section — done first, in pairs, while everybody is fresh and while it can be done simultaneously rather than one pair at a time. Then a four-minute briefing that includes standing up and shaking out, because the next fifty-six minutes are sitting. Then the paper, with the audio played in the middle of it at a moment you announce in advance. The last four minutes belong to the year, not to the test.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One hundred marks, and every one of them is something we did this year. Two warnings, both honest: the vocabulary half is spelled from memory, not chosen from options, and the essay at the end is worth ten marks — which means it needs fifteen minutes, not the four you have left after checking exercise 3 for the third time. Speaking first, while you are fresh. Then the paper. Then we finish the year properly.”

WHAT WE DO: the seven sections of the paper written up, with the two band scales read aloud

8–18′
Express review · seven grammar lines and five word sets
teams of four, mini-whiteboards
Why: Express review · seven grammar lines and five word sets

WHAT WE DO: ten minutes of retrieval aimed only at what the paper actually asks for

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six minutes of grammar, one line at a time, everybody writing on a mini-whiteboard and all boards up together — Kagan Showdown.
  2. One prompt per line: Join with a non-defining clause: This artist has been very successful. He was born in 1950. · Make it passive: They showed the royal wedding all over the world. · Report it: I'm going to be a bit late, he said. · Wish: I stayed out in the sun too long. · Indirect: Is William at school today? · Impersonal passive: They say the sportsman is out of shape. · Verb pattern: Mum reminded me ____ careful.
  3. Four minutes of vocabulary, five sets at speed: technology and the cloud · natural disasters · films and music · health and the body · the news. Two prompts each, spelling counts.
  4. Do not explain anything at length here. If a board comes up wrong, put the right version on the main board and move on — there are ten minutes and seven lines.
  5. Boards down, mini-whiteboards collected, desks cleared for the speaking.
📺 On screen: the prompts are read aloud, not displayed; wrong answers are corrected on the main board in one line each
👦 Students do: produce twelve grammar sentences and ten vocabulary items on their own board, all shown simultaneously
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text). The passive, reported speech, conditionals and relative clauses are four named lines of the Russian codifier, and the Showdown prompts already make the class produce all four from nothing. The only difference in the Russian paper is that they arrive inside a connected text.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: run two of the seven grammar prompts as a four-gap mini-text on the board with the verbs in capitals instead of as separate sentences. Same content, exam packaging, and it costs no extra minutes.

WHAT WE DO: exercise 15 of the paper: arguments for and against, then a presentation to the partner, marked on the printed scale

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The statement is on the board: All young people should have a full-time education until they are 21.
  2. Four minutes, individually and in silence. Three arguments for, three against, then your own opinion in one sentence. Notes, not sentences — and say that the notes are not marked.
  3. Four minutes, in pairs. Kagan Timed Pair Share: two minutes each way, and the partner listening has one job — to say afterwards which side was argued better and why.
  4. You circulate with the printed band scale and mark six students properly, then six more while the written paper is running and the room is silent. Marking twelve people in four minutes is not possible and pretending otherwise helps nobody.
  5. One rule that decides the band: Both sides before your opinion. A presentation with one side in it is half a presentation.
  6. Notes are collected with the paper at the end — they are not marked, but they stop anybody claiming they had no time to prepare.
📺 On screen: the statement written on the board and left there — no other material is needed
👦 Students do: prepare in silence for four minutes, then speak for two minutes and evaluate a partner's argument

whole class
Why: Test briefing · four minutes, then fifty-six of silence

WHAT WE DO: the conditions, the order of work, the fifteen-minute essay rule, and thirty seconds of standing up

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands behind their chair and shakes out for thirty seconds — hands, shoulders, neck. Say why: You are about to sit still for nearly an hour. Do it now.
  2. Sit. Then the conditions, said once: Fifty-six minutes. Fourteen exercises. Silent, individual, no dictionaries.
  3. The order rule, which is the one that saves marks: Do the paper in any order you like, but START THE ESSAY WHEN FIFTEEN MINUTES ARE LEFT, whatever else is unfinished. I will announce it.
  4. The recording: at half past, both playings, whatever anybody is doing. Written on the board since minute five, so nobody is surprised.
  5. Early finishers: Go back to the essay and add your own third argument. Nobody sits doing nothing and nobody leaves.
  6. Papers out, names on, start together.
📺 On screen: the board holds only the mark distribution and the audio time. Backup: if the audio machine fails, the printed audioscript is on your desk — read the talk aloud twice at natural speed and announce that you are doing so
👦 Students do: stand and reset physically, hear the order rule, and begin the paper together

WHAT WE DO: the End of Year test, sat whole, with the recording played in the middle at the announced minute

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Vocabulary 1–3 (20 marks), Grammar 4–7 (25), Use of English 8 (8), Listening 9–10 (12), Reading 11–13 (15), Writing 14 (10). The specification table in this plan lists every section.
  2. At minute thirty of the paper, exactly as announced: Pens down for the recording. Play the school trip talk twice, with the pause between playings, then let them go on. Exercises 9 and 10 use the same recording, so they read both before it starts.
  3. Announce three times only: the recording, the fifteen-minute essay mark, and five minutes to go. Nothing else.

  5. For a student stuck on an item, one instruction only, quietly: Leave it, go on, come back.
  6. Pens down at eighty-six minutes and collect everything, including the speaking notes. Nothing is taken home.

WHAT WE DO: the year closed properly — no marks, no post-mortem, one sentence each and one from you

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. No discussion of the answers. Say it and mean it: The papers are marked by me and they come back with your report. Today is finished.
  2. Round the room, one sentence each, no repeats: One thing from this year you will still have in September. Twelve students, ninety seconds, and it is the right last thing they say in this room.
  3. Then your sentence, and prepare it in advance rather than improvising: what this group did well this year, named specifically — the unit they were best at, the day the discussion would not stop, the student who changed most.
  4. The two summer lines, said lightly and not set as homework: keep the Workbook, because pages 94 to 103 are a complete practice paper with the summaries beside them; and read or watch something in English that nobody set you.
  5. Phones back at the door, one at a time. Say goodbye to each of them by name.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students have completed the End of Year test — the speaking section in pairs and the written paper of fourteen sections — under test conditions, and have one honest number out of a hundred to start September with.
  • Subsidiary. Allocating time across a long paper; producing an argued essay under time pressure; presenting an opinion to a partner from notes.
  • Personal. Keep four minutes at the end whatever happens, and do not spend them on the test. A course that finishes with «pens down, see you in September» finishes badly, and this is the last lesson these students have with this book.
By the end, students can…
  • produce the vocabulary of ten units from memory, spelled correctly
  • handle word order, error correction and an open cloze with no options given
  • write a for-and-against essay of 140–190 words in fifteen minutes
  • present an argued opinion to a partner from notes made in four minutes

Structure
the seven grammar lines the paper tests, revised as meaning in the first ten minutes: relative clauses · the conditionals, including the mixed one · the passive and the impersonal passive · reported speech · wish and if only · indirect questions and question tags · verb patterns
Form
revision only; nothing new is presented on the last day of a course. The full list of what the paper asks for is in the specification table of this plan — read it before the lesson and aim the ten minutes of review at the three lines your group is weakest on.
Pron. watch
assessed once, in the speaking section, and only at the level of intelligibility — the printed band scale says «pronunciation mostly clear». Do not correct pronunciation during the assessed speaking; note it and let it go.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
The essay is worth ten marks out of a hundred. How many minutes does it need? → about fifteen. How many do most people leave for it? → four, and that is the mistake this briefing exists to stop.
Exercise 5 gives seven sentences and says four contain a mistake. What do you do with the other three? → tick them. A blank line scores nothing even if the sentence really is correct.
In the speaking task you have to give arguments for AND against before your own opinion. Can you skip the side you disagree with? → no: the band scale rewards a full contribution, and one side is half a contribution.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Somebody writes four sentences of essay because time ran outThat is what the briefing at 26–30 is for: the essay is done when fifteen minutes are left, and you announce the fifteen-minute mark out loud. Anybody who has not started by then is told to start immediately, even mid-exercise.
The audio interrupts students who are in the middle of the grammarAnnounce the exact minute in the briefing — The recording starts at half past, whatever you are doing — and write it on the board. Then keep to it. An unannounced recording costs the room five minutes of resettling.
A strong student finishes the Standard paper in thirty-five minutesGive the Higher paper to those students from the start, not as a reward afterwards. If somebody still finishes early, the instruction is fixed: Go back to the essay and add your own third argument. That is where the marks are.
The lesson ends with a bell and no endingProtect the last four minutes as if they were part of the test. Collect at eighty-six minutes exactly, whatever is unfinished — an unfinished section costs a student two marks, and a course that ends without a closing costs more than that.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
