🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): A project for new students has to be doable: so the four-slide template is handed out straight away, the content comes from the biography draft they already have from P6, and no more than five short lines are allowed per slide. The Super skill of the unit (using someone else's feedback) is practised through a circle of kind feedback with ready phrases on a card — nobody is made to criticise “in their own words”.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Last project of the unit — and half of it is already written. Your biography from last lesson becomes four slides. Slide one: who they were. Slide two: early life. Slide three: what they did. Slide four: why they're a hero — and their own words. Katherine Johnson's last slide is one sentence: 'Girls are capable of doing everything men are capable of doing.' Five short lines per slide, no more, and a picture on every one. Then we help each other make it better.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the project is already half done: the biography draft from the last two lessons turns into four slides — who they were, early life, what they did, why they are a hero and their own words. No more than five short lines per slide and a picture on each. Then — the circle of kind feedback, off the card.

Building the slides from the template
pairs · devices/paper

WHAT WE DO: STEP 2 PLAN + STEP 3 CREATE: moving the P6 draft into the four-slide template

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 the list of heroes is already on the board (the heroes of the unit + the categories). ex.5: Choose YOURS — you can keep the person from your biography.
  2. The template is handed out on paper: four frames, each with a heading and a space for a picture.
  3. The five-line rule: Five short lines per slide. Not sentences — notes. '1918 – born in West Virginia.' I cut the first sentence of a draft down to one line myself at the board — out loud, crossing words out.
  4. After that we cut through Kagan RallyCoach: one pen per pair — the first crosses out what is extra in their own sentence and reads what is left, the second checks it against the five-line rule and says “short enough” or “cut more”, then they swap.
  5. We read How to… tips p.121 together: a picture or a question at the start · a heading on every slide · a picture on every slide · simple facts only · speaking notes kept separately · rehearse it.
  6. I monitor and help individually: dates, verb forms (the P3 poster is on the wall).
📺 On screen: laptops/phones · the paper template · SB p.121 (Backup: if there is no technology or no internet, we make the slides on four sheets of A4 with markers; the criteria and the marking are the same)
👦 Students do: they move the draft into the template. Expect: there is one difficulty — cutting sentences down to lines; I help them cross words out

Circle of kind feedback
pairs ↔ pairs

WHAT WE DO: ex.7–8 Super skills: two phrases each — praise and a suggestion — and one change

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The Super skills card on the desk: I really like the part about … · How about including … ? · Why don't you … ? · This part is really clear. · What do you think about … ? · I didn't really understand …
  2. The rule of the circle (TB requirement): Say TWO things: one you like, one idea to make it better. Always with a suggestion.
  3. Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: two minutes per pair, then move on. Two rounds.
  4. A compulsory action: Change at least ONE thing. Write down what you changed — you'll say it out loud.
📺 On screen: Super skills cards · a 2-minute timer (Backup: I write the six phrases large on the board and don't rub them off before the end of these two lessons)
👦 Students do: they praise and suggest off the card and make a change. Expect: the changes are simple — take text out, add a date or a picture

WHAT WE DO: STEP 4 PRESENT: presenting to the class (ex.9) + peer review (ex.10) + the vote

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rehearsing the quote: Read the last line three times. Slower and quieter than the rest.
  2. Before they present: Read the Peer review questions first, so you know what to listen for.
  3. The presentation, 60–90 seconds: Slide one — who. Slide two — early life. Slide three — what they did. Slide four — why, and the quote. You may hold your notes.
  4. Peer review ex.10 (the book's questions): 1 Is the presentation clear and interesting? 2 Who is the most important hero from the past? — for the second one I keep a tally on the board (TB technique).
  5. One line of recognition out loud for every student: Four slides, a picture on each, and a quote. That's a real presentation.
📺 On screen: projector/printed slides · timer (Backup: the paper slides are held up to the class by hand and I keep the vote as a tally on the board)
👦 Students do: they present with the page in hand and vote. Expect: nervous, but the page and the template hold them. Typical problem: reading the slide word for word — I remind them about the speaking notes

WHAT WE DO: a run through Quick review p.93 + a 10-item mini-test on the whole unit, checked against the key

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A run through Quick review p.93 out loud: Regular and irregular verbs. Negative: didn't + infinitive. Questions: Did you … ? Short answers. Time expressions: five years ago, in 2010, when I was eight, at the age of nine.
  2. A 10-item mini-test: 3 jobs + 3 adjectives + 2 verb forms + 2 questions. Six minutes. Posters covered.
  3. Key on screen afterwards: Mark your own. Circle every slip — that's your revision list for the test.
  4. Score to the team: the pair's points go into the team total.
📺 On screen: SB p.93 Quick review · mini-test sheet · key on screen (Backup: I read the key aloud item by item and the Quick review is open in the paper book)
👦 Students do: they run the review, write the mini-test and mark themselves. Expect: the circled mistakes are exactly the revision plan for P8

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can build and present a four-slide presentation about a hero from the past and give and use friendly feedback with the Super skills phrases.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the Katherine Johnson video; ex.2–3 analysing the Model project; ex.4–5 choosing a hero; ex.6 building it on the How to… tips p.121; ex.7–8 Super skills and rehearsal; ex.9–10 presenting and peer review; Quick review p.93; Final reflection.
  • Personal. I make sure feedback comes ONLY off the card and always with a suggestion for how to fix it. A paper version of the slides counts exactly as much as a digital one — technology must not decide who managed it.
By the end, students can…
  • choose a hero and say why they matter
  • put short notes on a slide instead of sentences
  • say one helpful thing about someone else's work
  • change my own work after feedback

Structure
She was a mathematician. · She was born in 1918. · She started work at NASA in 1953. · I really like the part about … · How about including … ?
Form
the language of a slide is short lines, not sentences: “1918 – born in West Virginia”, “went to university – maths and French”. The speech while presenting is full sentences in the past simple.
Pron. watch
the quote on the last slide is read more slowly and more quietly — that is the line we rehearse, three times each.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
How many lines on a slide? → no more than five, each on a new line.
“I didn't like it” — is that feedback? → no, you need a phrase from the card and a suggestion.
“She was born in 1918” — on the slide or in the speech? → on the slide “1918 – born”, in the speech a full sentence.

Assumptions: SB pp.93–95, syllabus pair 60. The biography drafts from P6 are in their hands — that is the content of the project. The internet can let the technology down: I prepare the paper version in advance.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“I don't know a single hero from the past”The list is ready on the board in advance: the heroes of the unit itself (Trudy Ederle, Katherine Johnson, Yash Gupta, Florence Nightingale, Ada Lovelace) + the categories of science, sport and music. Plus the person from the P6 draft.
The slide turns into a paragraphA hard bar: five short lines, a picture is compulsory. I show the Model project as the standard and measure against it.
Feedback = silenceThe Super skills card with six phrases lies on the desk; everybody must say exactly two things: one piece of praise and one suggestion.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
