🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The last lesson of the course. For new students its main job is not to «revise the skills» but to let them see their own growth: they joined this course from outside, often with the feeling «I am the weakest one here», and the year must not end on that feeling. That is why the second page of the book of the year is called «what I CAN DO» and is filled in not with words but with proofs: I listened to a podcast and answered five questions · I performed a dialogue · I wrote a text · I spoke for a minute in the game. Every block is built the same predictable way: model → written draft → speaking with the sheet in hand → self-check. The board game on p.115 comes at the end not as a reward but as the safest speaking of the year: in a group, square by square, with no stage. And we finish not with a mark but with the display of their work and «I can…».
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Last lesson. Page two: WHAT I CAN DO. Four proofs today. One — you listen to a real podcast and answer exam questions. Two — you play a real situation with a partner, with your notes in your hand. Three — you write, with a plan and a model. Four — the board game: twenty-one squares, the whole year, and you speak English at the table. No marks. At the end you'll hold two pages that say exactly what you know and what you can do. Open page 112.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the last lesson: we build the second page — «what I can do». Four proofs: I listen to a podcast and answer the questions · I perform a dialogue from a model · I write a text to a plan · I speak in the board game. No marks.

WHAT WE DO: p.112 ex.1–4: talking about podcasts → key words written down first → track 57 twice

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs, 2 minutes, with a frame on the board: I listen to music in/on/at … · I use … · I sometimes listen to … podcasts.
  2. ex.2 — the key step for new students: three expected words for each of the eight podcasts. Write them. This is the skill: key words BEFORE you listen.
  3. Track 57, first time (ex.3): recognise five programmes out of eight and write down the order. Key: 1 Good cooking! · 2 Future world · 3 News update · 4 Teen talk · 5 Natural science.
  4. Track 57, second time (ex.4) — with pauses after every extract so they have time to write the detail down. Key: 1 butter, sugar, eggs, chocolate · 2 on the Moon / on Mars · 3 a thunderstorm and heavy rain · 4 they post photos of clothes on social media and people rent the clothes for money · 5 the tortoise was 185 years old.
  5. Checking in pairs before the whole-class key, through Kagan RallyCoach. First model the move with yourself and one student: the first one says their answer and the word they heard it by; the second is the coach, holds the key and says «yes» or gives a hint. Then they swap roles on the next extract.
  6. After that a self-check → sticker.
  7. 🟢🟡🔴 Self-check stickers: after every mini-test the student sticks a sticker in the margin themselves: 🟢 got it · 🟡 so-so · 🔴 didn't get it. This is NOT a mark — it is my map of who to go to on the next task. Say this out loud every single time.
📺 On screen: SB p.112 ex.1–4 · track 57 · the ex.1 frame on the board (Backup with no sound: read the five extracts aloud yourself from the audioscript in the Teacher's Book p. 137 — they are short, 4–6 lines each, and this works just as well as the recording)
👦 Students do: write the expected words, listen twice, check in pairs. Expect: after ex.2 recognition jumps — students notice for themselves that it is the preparation working, not their ear. Mistake: they write the details in Russian — accept it today, but flag it: in the final work the English word is needed

WHAT WE DO: p.113 ex.1–4: the six situations of the year → the Key phrases table → add one of your own to each → two dialogues

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 from the photos, 2 minutes: three situations in the pictures — asking about a course (Unit 2) · asking for directions (Unit 5) · buying clothes (Unit 3). Name the remaining situations of the year yourself and write them on the board (checking answers U1 · suggestions U4 · showing interest U6 · opinion U7 · arranging to meet U8) — expecting a new student to recall all eight on their own is unrealistic, and there is no need to.
  2. ex.2: six Key phrases across six columns. Students first copy the table into their notebooks (a TB tip — leave room for additions), then fill it in. Key: information — What time does it start? · clothes — How much is it? · suggestions — Why don't we take a picnic? · directions — Is there an underground station near here? · opinion — What do you think of hip-hop music? · arranging to meet — Are you free on Saturday?
  3. Take the table in through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: say the first column yourself as a model, then the crew checks its six lines and makes sure every member knows the answer — and you call a number, and that person answers. Number twos — column four. Which phrase? Notebooks stay open for this: in this line reading is allowed.
  4. ex.3 with support: let them flick back to the Real-world speaking pages (the TB advises exactly that). The bar is honest: ONE more phrase in every column. One is enough.
  5. ex.4 in pairs, 3 minutes: two dialogues, choose the right option. Key: 1 find out · 2 on · 3 sign up · 4 try · 5 size · 6 take. Then read those dialogues aloud in parts — they are a ready-made model for the next block.

👦 Students do: copy the table, sort the phrases, add one of their own. Expect: the «directions» column is the emptiest — only a couple of usable phrases are in memory, and that is normal. Mistake: «How much cost it?» — write the right version on the board silently, next to the crossed-out one

Dialogue from a model (write → rehearse → perform)
Dialogue from a model
pairs

WHAT WE DO: p.113 ex.5: their own dialogue following Skills boost — write → rehearse → perform in front of one neighbouring pair (not in front of the class)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the situations yourself so no time goes on choosing: A — arrange something for the weekend · B — buy a present for a friend · C — talk about a new club. Send them to the right page of the year: A → SB p.103 · B → SB p.55 · C → SB p.91.
  2. THINK/PREPARE, 5 minutes: Write the whole dialogue. At least FOUR Key phrases. Underline them. The model dialogues in ex.4 stay open on the page — that is the support.
  3. PRACTISE, 3 minutes: they rehearse in twos, then swap roles (a TB tip — swap roles before performing so both roles have been in their mouths).
  4. PERFORM: they perform NOT in front of the class but in front of one neighbouring pair, with the sheet in hand. Two pairs sit facing each other and each performs its own dialogue.
  5. ex.6 Peer review on three points, in writing in the margin: which situation? which phrases did you hear? what did you like most? The third point is compulsory.
  6. Write it into the «I spoke» box on page 2: I played a dialogue with ___ Key phrases.
  7. Read the Exam tip before they perform: Take a deep breath before you start. Calm beats fast.
📺 On screen: SB p.113 ex.5–6 + Skills boost · the model dialogues in ex.4 left open · a timer (Backup: if the number of pairs is odd, take a student as your own partner — that is fine and it takes the tension out)
👦 Students do: write the dialogue, rehearse, perform in front of one pair. Expect: EVERYBODY performs, because the audience is two people, not twenty — that is the whole point of this format in this line. Mistake: they read off the sheet in a monotone — ask them to underline three words in their own text that have to be said louder

Writing · analysis and draft
pairs → solo

WHAT WE DO: p.114 ex.1–4: five text types → the six language questions of the year → correcting five sentences → their own 80–100-word text to a plan

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in 2 minutes: five texts ↔ five types. Key: 1 C a blog · 2 D an invitation · 3 A a letter · 4 B instructions · 5 E a forum post.
  2. ex.2 in pairs, 4 minutes — six questions, each about one unit of the year. Allow and ask them to flick back: Look it up. Looking it up is the skill. The order of events (U6) · capital letters (U1) · and/but/or (U2) · adjectives for places (U5) · also/too (U4) · because/so (U3).
  3. ex.3 together on the board, 2 minutes — five sentences with a mistake. Key: also after the verb · Mondays with a capital letter · interesting without -s · quiet OR busy · a small town in Spain last August.
  4. Their own writing (ex.4), the bar honestly lowered: 80–100 words instead of 120, and this is announced out loud. 2 minutes for a three-paragraph plan (what goes in each), then 5 minutes of writing — a draft, finished at home.
  5. A choice of task: A a blog about the last holidays · B a letter to a friend about summer plans · C a reply to a post «tell us about your town». The opening sentences of each option go on the board as a frame.
  6. CHECK is compulsory, 1 minute on three points: tenses · linkers · capital letters.
  7. Write it into the «I wrote» box on page 2.
📺 On screen: SB p.114 ex.1–4 + Skills boost · the frame of opening sentences on the board · the three CHECK points on the board (Backup: the model texts A–E are right there in the book — keep those open, no projector needed)
👦 Students do: sort out the text types, correct the sentences, write a draft to the plan. Expect: most of them choose option B; the three-paragraph plan genuinely saves the structure if you give it its own two minutes. Mistake: they write it as one lump — ask them to physically put three ticks in the margin, one per paragraph

70–80′
The review board game
REVIEW GAME
groups of 3–5

WHAT WE DO: p.115: a 21-square board game — the safest speaking of the year, in a group and square by square

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the rules TOGETHER before the dice are handed out (a TB requirement): throw → move → do the task on the square. Check understanding of the blue «Miss a turn» and the green «Go back to START» separately.
  2. The TB tip that is compulsory for new students: Before we start — look at ALL the squares. Think what you'd say for each one. Two minutes. Without this step half of them freeze on the very first square.
  3. The Key phrases of the game — on a card on every table: You start. · Whose turn is it? · What's the question? · It's your/my turn. The rule: Russian at the table = miss a turn.
  4. The one-minute squares (3 · 10 · 14 · 18 · 20) — notes in hand are allowed, that is this line. Notes in your hand are fine. Silence is not.
  5. Walk round and listen without correcting: write down three phrases that came out well and read them out at the end of the lesson.
  6. Write it into the «I played» box on page 2: I spoke English for ___ squares.
📺 On screen: SB p.115 · a dice per group · counters (coins/erasers) · the Key phrases card on the table · a way of timing a minute (Backup with no dice: an app on the captain's phone or 6 slips of paper in a cup; the board itself is printed in the book)
👦 Students do: play in groups of 3–5 and speak English with their notes in hand. Expect: the one-minute squares about birthdays and summer plans go best; the listing squares («how many animals can you name») sag. Mistake: they slide into Russian when it gets hard — the miss-a-turn rule holds that without you if it was announced BEFORE the game

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can show four skills with support: answer multiple-choice questions on a podcast, perform a prepared real-world dialogue, write a guided text with a plan, and speak for one minute in the review game.
  • Subsidiary. Listening SB p.112: track 57 (five extracts, key words written down beforehand) and track 58 (the e-sports podcast, 5 questions a–c, questions read BEFORE listening). Speaking SB p.113: the six situations of the year, the Key phrases table, their own dialogue through THINK–PREPARE–PRACTISE–PERFORM with the sheet in hand. Writing SB p.114: five text types, the six language questions of the year, correcting five sentences, a text to a plan. Board game SB p.115.
  • Personal. For new students the last lesson of the year has to end on a plus: out loud today you talk only about what worked, and everything that needs fixing goes in the margin of their work. Twice in the lesson repeat that there are no marks and that the final work comes separately. Everything spoken happens only after a written draft, with no exceptions, even in the last lesson.
By the end, students can…
  • answer 5 multiple-choice questions on a podcast after reading the questions first
  • sort the Key phrases of six real-world situations and use four of them in a dialogue
  • write a guided text with a three-paragraph plan and check it
  • speak for one minute on a familiar topic in a group game
  • fill in a ‘What I can do’ page with real proofs, not with rules

Structure
the six situations of the year (information · clothes · suggestions · directions · opinion · arranging to meet) + a written text of three paragraphs to a plan
Form
the linkers we check in the writing: and — a similar idea · but — a different one · or — an alternative · because — a reason · so — a result · also — after be and before other verbs · too — at the end of the sentence. Capital letters: languages, days of the week, months, names, cities, countries, nationalities, I
Pron. watch
the ’ll contraction (SB p.117): I’ll · you’ll · we’ll — drill it before the dialogue, because new students pronounce will in full and sound unnatural
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Why read the questions BEFORE listening? → so you know what to catch; otherwise you listen to everything and hear nothing.
«also» and «too» — where do they go? → also after be and before other verbs; too at the end.
«because» or «so»? → because gives the reason, so gives the result.
«Mondays» with a capital letter — why? → in English the days of the week always take a capital, like names.

Assumptions: SB pp.112–115, syllabus pair 71 (lessons 139–140, May) — the LAST lesson of the course. Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«I won't be able to perform» — in the last lesson stage fright is higher than usualThere is no stage today. The dialogue is performed at the desk in front of one neighbouring pair, with the sheet in hand. The display of work is two prepared sentences that may be read out. This was announced in the previous lesson, so nobody arrives anxious.
Listening frightens them: «I don't understand anything»The order is strict: read the questions → underline the key words → listen the first time for the general idea → listen the second time with a pause after every answer. The second run with pauses is compulsory, it is not a luxury.
A 120-word piece of writing paralyses a new studentLower the bar honestly and out loud: 80–100 words today and a compulsory three-paragraph plan. The full 120 words come in the final work, and we are working towards it. Plus a model text in front of them (SB p.114, texts A–E) and a frame of opening sentences on the board.
They slide into Russian in the board gameThe Key phrases of the game stay up on the board, and the rule (miss a turn for Russian at the table) is announced before the dice are handed out, not as you go. Plus a card with the four phrases straight onto every group's table.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
