🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Sit a complete A2-level written paper — the whole Listening paper and the whole Reading section — under real conditions, and come out of it with a map of what holds and what does not.
What is genuinely hard
Not the questions. The eighty unbroken minutes: holding attention through a second and a third text when the first one was hard, and keeping the transfer discipline in the listening — numbers as figures, spelling checked, no box left empty.
Typical error to expect
A word from the option was said in the recording → they tick it. A matching word is not a correct answer; meaning decides [exam report]
A question left blank. There is no penalty for a wrong answer in either exam, so an empty box is a mark given away for nothing [exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The arithmetic is the whole design problem: the Reading and Writing paper is sixty minutes and the Listening paper is thirty, which is ninety — the entire lesson, with not one minute left for handing out, briefing, settling or collecting. So the paper is cut, and it is cut where cutting costs least. The Listening runs complete and first, because its clock belongs to the recording rather than to the class: everybody starts and stops together, which settles the room, and it spends the freshest thirty minutes on the section where a lapse of attention cannot be recovered by going back. Then the reading, thirty questions in silence. Then Writing Part 6 only. Writing Part 7 — the story from three pictures — goes home, because it is the one part of this paper with no counterpart at all in the Russian exam and the only one that can be marked at home without distorting the diagnosis: a story written calmly still shows the past tense, the linkers and the spelling. And the Speaking paper is not here at all — it runs eight to ten minutes per pair with an examiner opposite, so a group of ten needs forty to fifty minutes of one-to-one time. It goes into lesson 71, where the rest of the room has a structure that survives half the class being busy. All of this is said out loud to the students: children trust a teacher who tells them which parts are for the exam and which are for the language.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today is the whole paper, and I am going to tell you exactly what we are doing and why. Listening first, all of it. Then the reading, thirty questions, forty minutes, silence. Then one short email. The story from pictures goes home — it is the one thing on this paper that can be written calmly without spoiling anything. This is not a mark for your report. It is a map: at the end of it you will know what holds and what we fix. Two rules for the whole day — check before you hand it in, and never leave a box empty.”

door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls back the one thing that is needed today rather than everything that was ever taught.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, thirty seconds of the exam checklist from last lesson, and the bridge into the paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and leave the desk empty. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the checklist, not a lesson. Kagan Timed Pair Share, thirty seconds each way: Name three things from the exam page we did last week. Then tell your partner one thing you actually did last night.
  3. Take three of the nine back from the room. For your own reference the nine are: Organise your study time · learn information in small blocks · draw pictures or make sentences to remember things · make mind maps · have a good breakfast · make sure you have breaks · get plenty of sleep · revise with a friend · exercise regularly.
  4. Then close it and put up the only two that are for inside the room today: The two that matter inside the room today are the ones from the Life Skills listening: check your work before you hand it in, and never leave a question blank. Both are on the board for the whole paper.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Desks empty, phones in the bag at the front. Today you sit a whole paper — and I will tell you exactly what we are doing and what we are not.
📺 On screen: the two in-room rules in chalk, large, at the top of the board where they stay for the whole lesson — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: recall three study-skills points and hear the two rules for today. Expect «get plenty of sleep» first and honest laughter about last night. Typical problem: somebody wants to discuss revision technique — one sentence, then stop, the paper needs the minutes

5–8′
Exam briefing
board
Why: Three minutes spent on conditions and rules buys eighty minutes of quiet. Everything that could interrupt the paper — where the clock is, what to do when you finish, what happens if you cannot answer — is settled before the first page is turned.

WHAT WE DO: the shape of the day, the conditions, and the two rules — three minutes, then silence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The shape of the day in four lines on the board, so that nothing later is a surprise: Listening — all of it, thirty minutes. Break, two minutes. Reading — thirty questions, thirty-nine minutes. One email, nine minutes.
  2. Say the two honest sentences about what is missing. First: Writing Part 7 — the 35-word story from three pictures — goes home. It is the one part of this paper with no counterpart at all in the Russian exam, and it is the only part that can be marked at home without distorting the diagnosis: a story written calmly still shows the past tense, the linking words and the spelling. Everything that has to be seen under pressure stays in the room. Second: The Speaking paper is not in this lesson and cannot be. It runs 8 to 10 minutes per PAIR with an examiner sitting opposite; a group of ten needs forty to fifty minutes of one-to-one time, which no whole-class lesson can absorb. It goes into lesson 71, where the rest of the class has a structure that survives half the room being busy.
  3. Conditions, in one breath: Desks apart, phones in the bag at the front, nothing on the walls that can be read, a visible clock and the minutes remaining written on the board rather than announced out loud. Questions about content get one answer only: Read the instruction again.
  4. The two rules go up next to the shape of the day and stay there: check before you hand it in; never leave a box empty. Nobody loses a mark for a wrong answer. Everybody loses a mark for an empty one.
  5. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment the room is silent until the break.
📺 On screen: the four-line shape of the day and the two rules in chalk, and they stay up for the whole lesson — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the shape of the day and put their phones away. Expect one question about the register — answer it once and move on. Typical problem: a student who wants to know the mark scheme; there isn't one today, and saying so plainly settles it

Listening paper · all five parts
solo, silent mode
Why: The listening goes first because it is the only section whose clock belongs to the recording: everyone starts and stops together, which settles the room, and the freshest thirty minutes go to the section where a lapse of attention cannot be recovered.

WHAT WE DO: the complete Listening paper: 5 parts, 25 questions, every piece played twice

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out the question paper: PREPARE_Listening QP_Test.pdf — 5 parts, 25 questions, printed on the cover as «approximately 35 minutes (including 6 minutes' transfer time)». Every piece is heard twice and every question carries one mark. Part 1: five short dialogues with picture options. Part 2: a five-gap form — one word or a number or a date or a time. Part 3: one long dialogue, five three-option questions. Part 4: five separate short extracts. Part 5: matching five items to eight options A–H.
  2. Ninety seconds to read Part 1 before anything plays. Look at the pictures. Say the difference between them in your head, in English.
  3. Play the five files in order, end to end: five files, one per part: PREPARE_Listening_Part 01.mp3 … Part 05.mp3. Played end to end they run about 32 minutes, and the pauses for reading and checking are already inside the recordings — you do not add your own. The pauses for reading and checking are inside the recordings — do not add your own and do not stop between parts.
  4. One reminder before Part 2 only, because it is the part that bleeds marks: One word, or a number, or a date, or a time. Write numbers as figures.
  5. Walk the room in silence. To any question about content the answer is only Read the instruction again.
  6. At the end, thirty seconds: Check every box has something in it.
📺 On screen: the five audio files PREPARE_Listening_Part 01…05.mp3 played from the whiteboard app (Backup: PREPARE_Audioscript_Test.pdf is printed and on your desk — read every part aloud yourself, twice, at natural speed, with no extra help and no pauses that are not in the script; the question paper is in the students' hands either way)
👦 Students do: listen twice per part and answer on the question paper. Expect Part 2 (the form) and Part 5 (the matching) to be the two that separate the group. Typical error: a number written out as a word and then misspelt — which is why the reminder goes in before Part 2 and nowhere else

Break · papers face down
standing
Why: Two minutes of standing between two papers. Attention is a resource and the reading paper is about to ask for forty minutes of it in one unbroken block.

WHAT WE DO: two minutes of movement between the two papers, and no comparing of answers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Papers face down on the desk. Stand up. Two minutes.
  2. Kagan Stand Up–Hand Up–Pair Up with one rule: One sentence to the person you meet, in English, about anything except this paper.
  3. Back on a clap, pens down, and the reading paper goes out face down.
📺 On screen: instructions by voice, nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: stand, move and say one unrelated English sentence. Expect the ban on comparing answers to hold badly — which is exactly why the break is two minutes and not five

Reading paper · Parts 1–5
solo, silent mode
Why: Thirty questions of reading in silence, at their own pace, with nobody helping. This is the part of the day that cannot be simulated in a ten-minute drill, and it is the part that tells you most about who is ready for Prepare 4.

WHAT WE DO: thirty reading questions in silence: five parts, 39 minutes on the clock

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand the paper out face down, then: PREPARE_R&W QP_Test.pdf — Reading and Writing, 7 parts, 32 questions, 1 hour. Parts 1–5 are 30 reading marks (three-option questions on short real-world texts · matching seven questions to three short texts · three-option questions on one long text · a six-gap multiple-choice cloze · a six-gap open cloze where you write one word). Part 6 is a 25-word email, part 7 a 35-word story from three pictures.
  2. Turn the paper. Parts one to five only. Do not start part six. Thirty-nine minutes. Begin.
  3. Write the minutes remaining on the board — 20 · 10 · 5 — in chalk, never out loud.
  4. The official paper allows sixty minutes for all seven parts. Thirty-nine for the thirty reading questions is slightly tighter than the real thing, and that is a deliberate consequence of fitting two papers into one lesson. Note who does not finish; that is data, not a failure, and it goes into next lesson's log.
  5. At 5 minutes: Five minutes. Go back to part five and check every gap. One word only.
📺 On screen: nothing is displayed and nothing is playing — the printed paper is the only medium in the room, and the minutes remaining go on the board in chalk, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: work in silence for thirty-nine minutes. Expect Part 4 (the multiple-choice cloze) to be the slowest and Part 5 (the open cloze) to be the one that is rushed. Typical error: the option that repeats a word from the text is chosen in Part 3 — this is the trap the whole year has been named

79–88′
Writing Part 6 · the email
solo, silent mode
Why: Twenty-five words under a clock, after eighty minutes of paper. It is deliberately at the end: writing when you are tired is exactly the condition the real thing is written in, and the three content points are either counted or lost.

WHAT WE DO: the 25-word email — nine minutes, three content points, counted before and after

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Part six now. Nine minutes. Twenty-five words or more.
  2. Before anybody writes, thirty seconds out loud: Find the three questions in the email. Number them in the margin — one, two, three.
  3. The official paper gives about twenty minutes to parts 6 and 7 together, so nine for the email alone is honest. Say so if asked.
  4. At two minutes: Two minutes. Tick your three numbers. Did you answer all three?
  5. Nothing else is corrected out loud. The point of this stage is the counting habit, not the language.
📺 On screen: nothing displayed and nothing playing — the paper in front of them is the medium, so no backup is needed; the three-point reminder goes on the board in chalk
👦 Students do: number the three questions, write about thirty to fifty words, tick them off. Expect two of the three answered by a third of the group. Typical error: the third point dropped when the clock gets close — which is exactly the error the tick removes

Papers in · one line
circle
Why: The paper closes with a sentence written by the student, not with a comment from the teacher. It also gives you, before you have marked anything, the group's own map of where it hurt.

WHAT WE DO: papers collected, one written sentence each, and the homework named

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect both papers in silence, in one pile, without a word of comment on anybody's work.
  2. One line on a slip of paper, not out loud: Which part was hardest? One sentence, in English. Collect the slips — before you have marked anything, that is the group's own map.
  3. Kagan Round Robin, one sentence each, thirty seconds for the whole circle: One word about how that felt. Go.
  4. Homework named and nothing else: the three pictures on the last page of the reading paper, 35 words or more, past tense, three linking words. That page stays with you. The rest comes to me.
  5. Last line of the lesson, and mean it: Next lesson is the one that makes today worth doing.
📺 On screen: the two homework instructions in chalk on the board, copied into diaries — the board is the medium here, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hand in, write one line and take the picture page home. Expect «the listening, part five» most often. Typical problem: somebody asks for their score on the spot — the answer is that nobody has one yet, including you

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students have sat a complete Listening paper and a complete Reading section under exam conditions and know, from their own experience, what eighty minutes of paper feels like.
  • Subsidiary. The two habits that carry the most marks in both exams: checking work before handing it in, and never leaving a question blank.
  • Personal. Say the honest thing about the cuts, once, at the start. This is a diagnosis and not a verdict, and a class that knows the difference works better in the room and worries less outside it.
By the end, students can…
  • hold attention through a complete listening paper played twice
  • answer thirty reading questions in silence at their own pace
  • write a 25-word email answering all three content points under a clock
  • say which section was hardest, and why, in one written sentence

Structure
You will hear each piece twice. · Write one word or a number or a date or a time. · Write 25 words or more. · For each question, choose the correct answer.
Form
The rubric language of the paper itself — the four instructions above are the only new language of the lesson, and they are read out and translated once before anything starts.
Pron. watch
Nothing is drilled today. What matters is that the two rules are said slowly enough to be remembered: check before you hand it in, never leave a box empty.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«You will hear each piece twice.» So do I have to get it the first time? → no. First time you answer, second time you check.
«Write one word or a number or a date or a time.» Are two words allowed? → no. One only, and a number is written as a figure.
«Write 25 words or more.» Is 24 enough? → no. Is 40 a problem? → no: «or more» means more is fine.
There is no penalty for a wrong answer in either exam. So what is the worst thing you can do with a question you cannot answer? → leave it empty.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The recording plays and the sound is wrong, or there is no sound at allThe audioscript is printed: PREPARE_Audioscript_Test.pdf. Read each part aloud twice, at natural speed, with no extra help and no pauses that are not in the script. It is slower to prepare and it works. Have it printed and on the desk before the lesson, not found during it.
Somebody finishes the reading twenty minutes earlyNobody leaves and nobody starts the writing early. The instruction is fixed and it is on the board: Go back to Part 5 and check every gap. One word only. Part 5 is the open cloze and it is where the checking pays.
«Is this going in the register?»Answer it once, plainly, before the papers go out: No. This one is a map. The test that goes in the register is next week, and this lesson is how we get ready for it. Then do not return to the subject.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the OGE — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: The whole written paper — in the Russian exam that is tasks 1–35: listening 1–11, reading 12–19, grammar and vocabulary 20–34, the email 35. 53 marks, 120 minutes, one sitting.

What today gives a student who will sit that exam in two years is not the format — it is the experience of holding attention for eighty minutes of paper without anybody helping. That is the part nobody can teach in ten-minute drills, and it is the part that decides the Russian paper too. The formats differ; the stamina does not.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
