🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that two units work without the book: 41 words, adverbs of comparison, the apostrophe, the pronoun set, and correct spelling.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the two invisible things. The apostrophe cannot be heard, so it is the first thing to disappear under time pressure; and the adjective/adverb line survives orally and collapses on paper.
Typical error to expect
My friends name is Ben — the missing apostrophe, and my parent's car for two parents. In 2026 the apostrophe counts as PUNCTUATION in the writing paper, not as grammar [exam report]
You speak English really good instead of well — an adjective put where an adverb is needed [high priority, A2/B1]. The coursebook flags this one itself on SB p.33 ex.5.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is retrieval practice, not re-teaching: the two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it and what shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which forms still break. ⚠️ Unlike block 2, there is no Review page in the coursebook for these two units — Review 1 covers Units 1–4 and Review 2 comes after Unit 8 — so the stations are built from the unit spreads themselves and from the photocopiable worksheets. The game sits between the stations and the briefing on purpose: it is the settler that turns revision into a reason to listen, and this block's game is a listening game, which is a useful last rehearsal before a paper with a dictated part in it. The briefing comes after the stations because only by then do you know which five words belong in the «watch out» column.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. There is no revision page in your book for these two units — so we make one. First forty-five minutes we play the block back. Then forty-five minutes you show it. Your score is not the point: your own list of five mistakes is.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast round of the block's words and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last lesson rewind. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade as a hot round: cards for all 41 words of the block, two trades each. Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest: Everything you just said is in today's test.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, read the words out from a list and students write them in pairs
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety dropping. Typical problem: they stumble on chemistry and technology — exactly the two that go into the «watch out» column later

Lesson frame

Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: put the test specification up in advance — transparency is what takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the nine parts of the test on the board, one line each: school subjects · take · materials · adjectives for objects · comparative adverbs · correcting the adverb · possession · pronunciation by ear · a short description.
  2. Look at the board. Have we done all of this in class? Yes or no? The class answers chorally — the answer is always yes.
  3. Be honest about the missing page: There is no Review page in your book for these two units. So today we make one.
  4. The rule of the day: Your score goes in the register. Your five mistakes go in your notebook. The second one matters more.
📺 On screen: the nine specification lines in chalk — the board is the medium and it stays up for the whole first half of the lesson, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the specification and answer chorally. Expect visible relief. Typical problem — they do not believe «we have done all of it»: ask them to name the lesson for each line

Review station 1 · words
teams
Why: Retrieval practice rather than re-teaching: they pull the block's vocabulary out of memory in short bursts, which is what makes it stay.

WHAT WE DO: the block's vocabulary: 41 words, against the clock, orally and then in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, oral, 4 minutes: you read a definition or show a picture, the team says the word. Twelve items, mixed from both units — subjects, take-phrases, materials, adjectives.
  2. Round 2, written, 6 minutes: Kagan Showdown — you say the word, the team writes it in English and reveals on command. Spelling counts.
  3. Put the traps into round 2 deliberately: chemistry · geography · technology · colourful · jewellery · leather.
  4. Every misspelling goes into a «watch out» column on the board and stays there right up to the test.
  5. Two minutes at the end on the one thing a word list cannot test: three objects described in two adjectives each, in the right order.
📺 On screen: the pictures and definitions on the screen (Backup: word cards in your hand and the definitions read from a sheet; the «watch out» column goes on the board either way)
👦 Students do: say the words, then write them on mini-whiteboards. Expect almost everything orally and a visible drop in the written round. Typical error: chemestry — which is exactly why the column is needed
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare3_Block3_Units5-6_Match_2up_A4 + Prepare3_Block3_Units5-6_Translate_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — Russian sentences to be joined to their English pairs (school subjects, comparative adverbs, the seven routes of take, materials, possession and object description), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same sentences written out in English from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score in the margin — that is the block's exit line.

Review station 2 · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar — and it shows you, twenty minutes before the test, exactly which form still breaks.

WHAT WE DO: the block's grammar: the adverbs and the apostrophe, ten mixed sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Give ten mixed sentences — five on comparative and superlative adverbs, five on possession — modelled on SB p.33 ex.4–5 and SB p.37 ex.7–8. The Grammar worksheets for Units 5 and 6 in the course folder supply them ready-made, with keys.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the REASON, not the answer — Adjective or adverb? One owner or more?
  3. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Sentence seven — number twos, boards up!
  4. Boil every explanation down to two lines that stay on the board until the briefing: ‑ly = HOW you do it · apostrophe before the s = one owner.
📺 On screen: the ten sentences one at a time on the screen (Backup: write them on the board before the lesson and uncover them one by one with a sheet of paper)
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with an explanation, then report by number. Expect the adverbs to be confident and the apostrophes to wobble. Typical error: my parent's car for two parents — the two-colour pen trick from Unit 6 fixes it in ten seconds

32–40′
Review game
two teams
Why: A game that hides revision inside a reason to listen; nerves before a test drop when the last thing they did was laugh.

WHAT WE DO: the game «What's the word? (Review game Units 5–8, Cambridge, PHOTOCOPIABLE 2019)» — one team reads, the other finds the word

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Two teams sit facing each other, each with its own half of the sheet — and neither may see the other's half. Team B reads out a sentence and says «blank» where the gap is, WITHOUT saying the capitalised word at the end, which is the answer. Team A picks the word from the box on its own sheet. Right first time — 3 points; second attempt — 2; third — 1; then nothing. There is one extra word in each box that is never used.
  2. ⚠️ Which sentences are ours: ⚠️ The sheet covers Units 5–8, so play only the sentences whose answer belongs to our two units: 5 I love wearing ___ socks (COLOURFUL) · 9 My ___ teacher taught us about the body (SCIENCE) · 10 Your jacket is ___ (LOVELY) · 12 This bin is for paper and that bin is for ___ (PLASTIC) · 13 My new jumper is made of ___ (COTTON) · 14 I can't swim but I love ___ (PE) · 18 My sister's new backpack is ___ (HEAVY) · 19 I like old houses because they're ___ (PRETTY) · 21 My grandma gave me a scarf made of ___ (WOOL) · 22 Those boots are ___ (LEATHER) · 27 I love learning about ___ (HISTORY) · 32 I like toys that are made of ___ (WOOD) — plus the unused word GLASS on sheet B, which is worth pointing out. The rest (camping, hiking, sleeping bag, torch, compass) is Units 7–8 and has not been taught yet — skip it.
  3. Variations worth using: Two variations printed on the teacher's page, and both are useful: No crossing out — players may not cross off the words they have used and have to remember them; Not in the box — the team must complete the sentence with a word that is NOT in the box, and the class decides whether it is acceptable. The second one turns a vocabulary game into real thinking.
  4. Set the bar: You may not show your sheet. You may only speak — and they may only listen.
  5. This game is a listening game, which is exactly the right last rehearsal before a paper whose part 8 is dictated.
📺 On screen: one printed A/B sheet per pair of teams, run off and cut along the dotted line in advance — paper is the medium here, so no backup is needed; with no printer, read twelve of the sentences aloud yourself and write the word box on the board
👦 Students do: read out sentences and guess the missing word. Expect scoring arguments — the three-two-one scale settles them. Typical error: they say the capitalised answer out loud by accident; that round simply does not count

40–45′
Test briefing
class
Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions for the test

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go back to the «watch out» column: the words that were misspelt. Look at these words for thirty seconds. Then I close them.
  2. Instructions: 45 minutes, nine parts, 80 marks; phones in the bag; finished early — part 9, the short description. Part 8 is done TOGETHER at the start: you say the words and the sentences, so nobody can be late for it.
  3. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  4. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; everything else in the room is switched off
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect silence to settle in 20 seconds if the flat hand goes up before you start talking

Block test
solo, silent mode
Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats. Everything in the paper has been done in class, so the test measures learning, not surprise.

WHAT WE DO: the block 3 test: nine parts, 80 marks, 38 minutes (the first 5 are the part 8 dictation)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. You have thirty-eight minutes. Start.
  2. Walk round the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  3. Write the time left in chalk: 20 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  4. Part 8 (pronunciation) comes first and runs to your voice — 5 minutes: six words for the stress patterns, then four sentences for the weak forms. After that students work on their own.
  5. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your apostrophes.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; nothing at all is displayed in the room while the test is running
👦 Students do: work in silence for 38 minutes. Expect parts 1–4 to be closed fast and part 7 (possession) to slow them down. Typical error: they skip part 9, taking it for an optional extra — which is why it is on the specification on the board

85–90′
Test wrap-up
circle
Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip of paper, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the feedback lesson.
  3. Homework: rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly once the papers come back; for now — the Vocabulary or Grammar worksheet Unit 5–6 if anything is still open.
  4. Teaser: Next lesson we open page one hundred and twenty-five — six gaps, one word each, and then an email where the only thing that matters is whether you answered the questions.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is full, read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to be about the apostrophe most often — that is the plan for the next feedback clinic

Block 3 test — specification (DRAFT: the official pack is not in the course folder yet)
PartContent and marks
Part 1 · Vocabulary: school subjects12 definitions or pictures → the subject (biology … physics) — modelled on Vocabulary Standard Unit 5, task 1. ⚠️ ICT and PE are written in capitals. 12 marks.
Part 2 · Vocabulary: collocations with take7 gapped sentences: take an exam / a photo / a train / medicine / an umbrella / a subject / the second turning — SB p.35 ex.2 and ex.4. 7 marks.
Part 3 · Vocabulary: materials10 items → the material (cotton, glass, gold, leather, metal, paper, plastic, silver, wood, wool), plus 4 items in which the material has to be used as an adjective (a wooden box). 14 marks.
Part 4 · Vocabulary: adjectives for describing objects10 items: match colourful, hard, heavy, large, little, lovely, old, pretty, round, small, smooth, soft to descriptions, and put three of them in the right order before a noun (SB p.39). 10 marks.
Part 5 · Grammar: comparative and superlative adverbs8 sentences, put the adverb into the right form — Grammar Standard Unit 5, tasks 1–3. Two items are the irregular well – better – the best. 8 marks.
Part 6 · Grammar: correcting the adverb6 sentences to correct (You speak English really good · I did it more quicker · He runs fastest in our class) — SB p.33 ex.5. 6 marks.
Part 7 · Grammar: possession8 items: 's or s' (4), and determiner or pronoun — my / mine, their / theirs (4) — Grammar Standard Unit 6 + SB p.37 ex.3, ex.7, ex.8. 8 marks.
Part 8 · Pronunciation (by ear — as in the official Unit test)6 words to be sorted into the three stress patterns 0o / 0oo / o0oo as you say them (SB p.33, track 38) + 4 sentences to be repeated and marked for the weak a and of (SB p.37, track 44). 10 marks.
Part 9 · Writing (mini)About 35 words on the SB p.39 model: describe one object you own — where it came from, what it looks like — with at least two pairs of adjectives in the right order. 5 marks for content.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
There is no book page to revise from and it showsSay it and turn it into an advantage: we make our own revision page. The two stations ARE that page, and the worksheets in the course folder supply the exercises.
Someone finishes the test in 25 minutesThe rule: hand it in, turn the paper over and describe one object in the room in three adjectives in the right order. That is part 9 and it carries marks.
After the test they want to compare answers out loudCollect the papers in silence; the analysis is next lesson. Today, once a paper is in, the only thing left is personal reflection.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
