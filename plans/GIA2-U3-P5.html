🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The contrast of the two tenses is hidden inside an interrogation: a suspect has an “ordinary day” (present simple) and a “right now” (present continuous), and the alibi falls apart exactly where he confuses one with the other. Time expressions become evidence: at the moment against every day. Stative verbs are served as “words you cannot put into motion”, not as a list of exceptions. The Grammar round-up about the sock millionaire is the investigator's final check.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“The Alibi Room is open. Every suspect tells you two stories: what they USUALLY do — and what they are doing RIGHT NOW. Both can be true. But if the time expression doesn't match the tense, the alibi breaks. Your job: ask, listen, catch the contradiction. Two clocks on the wall, agents — the everyday clock and the right-now clock. Don't mix them up.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The interrogation room: every suspect has two stories — what they usually do and what they are doing right now; the alibi breaks where the time does not match the form. The job is to catch the contradiction.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: the time-expression sorter into two trays against the clock + 8-second flips “usually ↔ now”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ten time expressions, two trays: EVERYDAY clock or RIGHT-NOW clock. Two minutes — crew record?
  2. Key for ex.3: ex.2 throws up at the moment · on Saturdays · today · every day. Into the table — present simple: every day, every Monday, sometimes, usually · present continuous: now, right now, this week, today.
  3. Careful — 'today' can live in BOTH trays. Why? (today as a stretch of time vs today as a moment) — a great argument, I give it 1 minute.
  4. 8-second flips: I give a sentence with one time expression — the crews flip it into the other tense with another expression.
📺 On screen: time-expression cards · two trays (backup — two columns on the board) · timer
👦 Students do: they sort with their hands and argue about today. Expected: the argument about today sticks better than the rule. Error: they put this week into the simple — I take them back to the meaning “this stretch of time, it's still running”

PRODUCT · the Alibi Room
pairs → stage

WHAT WE DO: the interrogation: the suspect answers about “usually” and “right now”, the investigators catch 2 contradictions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 5 minutes of preparation: everyone writes themselves a cover story — 3 “usually” lines and 3 “right now” lines, and ONE hidden contradiction (for example: “I never wear a hat” + “I'm wearing my favourite hat”).
  2. The investigators' questions come from ex.5. Key for ex.5: 1 What do you do in the evenings? · 2 What are other people in your family doing now? · 3 What do you usually wear to go out? · 4 What is your teacher wearing today? · 5 What language do you speak at home?
  3. Sixty seconds per suspect. Investigators: ask in BOTH tenses. Find the contradiction — and say it out loud: 'But you said you never wear a hat!'
  4. The report in writing, 4 lines: He usually…, but right now he… The judges count the accuracy of the form and the contradictions found.
📺 On screen: cover-story cards · 60-second timer · the report checklist
👦 Students do: they interrogate and write the report. Expected: they hunt the contradiction eagerly, and for its sake they watch the form of their questions themselves. Error: “What you doing?” — the question burns, next investigator

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can choose between present simple and present continuous by the time expression, avoid stative verbs in the continuous, and run a 60-second interrogation catching two contradictions.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the contrast table and the two rules (guided discovery); the stative verbs box; ex.2 choosing the form; ex.3 sorting the time expressions; ex.4 four mini-dialogues; ex.5–6 five questions in pairs; Grammar round-up ex.7.
  • Personal. I don't read the rule out — the students formulate it from the table. Every written run ends with a self-check against the key before a neighbour sees the error.
By the end, students can…
  • choose the tense from the time expression
  • explain why some verbs never take the continuous
  • ask questions in both tenses in one conversation
  • catch a contradiction between 'usually' and 'right now'

Structure
We usually wear a school uniform. / We are wearing normal clothes today. · He doesn't play football on Wednesdays. / He isn't playing football today. · Do you have lunch at school? — Yes, I do. / Are you having lunch? — Yes, I am. · What does he always do? / What is he doing now?
Form
present continuous — an action in progress RIGHT NOW · present simple — routine and habit · never in the continuous: be, have got, like, love, want (and know, need)
Pron. watch
the weak “do you” /djʊ/ in present simple questions against the clear “are you” in the continuous — an audible difference
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I'm wanting new trainers” — why not? → want does not go in the continuous: I want new trainers.
“What are you doing every Saturday?” — what is wrong? → every Saturday = routine, you need the present simple: What do you do every Saturday?
You hear at the moment — which tense do you take? → present continuous.
“She is having a bike.” → have GOT for possession never goes in the continuous: She's got a bike.

Assumptions: SB p.42, KTP pair 25. Both tenses are familiar separately from A1+ and from P3 — what is new is the CHOICE under pressure and the stative verbs.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They put the continuous everywhere because the unit is about itThe evidence rule: first find the time expression, then choose the form. No expression — ask “now or in general?”.
I'm wanting / She's having a bikeThe stative box on the wall + a separate trap station: 5 sentences where the continuous is banned.
The class gets tired of choosing formsI alternate: writing → boards up → oral interrogation → writing. No single run longer than 6 minutes.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
