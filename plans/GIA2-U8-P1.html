🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The three lexical sets of the unit (types of holiday · places to stay · facilities) are not introduced as a list but as goods: the crew builds a holiday offer and has to sell it to the class in 60 seconds. The video “Cool summer holidays” gives a live model of the genre — the vlogger asks viewers “Where are you going to go this year?” and shows three photos sent in. The going to form is in every line of the video, but it is NOT announced as the topic of the lesson — that is the work of Pair 5. The unit's WDYT question (“What's your idea of a good holiday?”) opens here and stays live until the project in Pair 7.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Travel desk, open. Look at the website on page 96: seven offers, seven photos, seven dates. Ten nights in August — ‘Have fun in the sun’. Two nights in April — ‘The world's best rides’. Someone wrote those words and someone bought those holidays. Today your crew builds ONE offer: the type of holiday, the place you stay, the facilities, the dates. Sixty seconds to sell it to this room. And the room votes with money it doesn't have — so make it good. Desks are your travel agency.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: p.96 is a website with seven holiday offers. The crew builds its own: type of holiday, where you stay, which facilities, the dates. 60 seconds to sell it to the class. The class votes on the offer it would buy.

⚡ Base sprint · seven offers
crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 p.96 (seven types of holiday from photos, vocabulary track 52) + ex.3 six riddles about places to stay (vocabulary track 53) through Showdown + ex.4 against the clock

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: Seven photos, seven offers. The captions help: 'Sleep under the stars' — which one is that? Three minutes — GO. (key, TB: 1 go to the beach · 2 go skiing · 3 go camping · 4 go on a city break · 5 go on a walking holiday · 6 go to a theme park resort · 7 go on a water sports holiday).
  2. Three columns on the board straight away: go · go ON · go TO. Students put each phrase into a column themselves — the preposition is now part of the word.
  3. ex.3 as riddles: 'If you stay here, you don't sleep in a bed or in a building.' Boards up! (key, TB: 1 hotel · 2 B & B · 3 campsite · 4 country cottage · 5 apartment · 6 youth hostel).
  4. Run the riddles as Kagan Showdown: you read the riddle, everyone writes their own answer on a mini-whiteboard in silence and keeps it face down; on Showdown! the whole crew raises the boards at the same moment. Nobody may shout out early, so all six think, not just the fastest one.
  5. Pronunciation drill (the TB insists): apartment /əˈpɑː(r)tmənt/ · B & B /ˌbiː ən ˈbiː/ · campsite /ˈkæmpˌsaɪt/ · country cottage /ˌkʌntri ˈkɒtɪdʒ/ · hotel /həʊˈtel/ · youth hostel /ˈjuːθ hɒst(ə)l/.
  6. ex.4 against the clock: For each holiday type — one or more places to stay. More than one answer works. (key, TB: beach — apartment, hotel · skiing — apartment, hotel · camping — campsite · city break — hotel, B & B, apartment · walking holiday — youth hostel, campsite, B & B, country cottage · theme park resort — hotel, apartment · water sports — hotel, apartment).
📺 On screen: SB p.96 ex.1, 3, 4 · mini-whiteboards · timer
👦 Students do: they close three tasks as a team. Expect: the riddles land fast and get a laugh. Problem: they mix up B & B and hotel — sort it out with the TB note (a B & B is somebody's house and breakfast is always included; in a hotel it depends on the deal)

🔥 Challenge · dream holiday duel
pairs → crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.6 seven facility icons (vocabulary track 54) against the clock + ex.7 four descriptions + the “my place is better” duel through RallyRobin, 8 seconds per turn

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.6: Seven icons, seven facilities, ninety seconds. GO. (key, TB: 1 garden · 2 bike hire · 3 gym · 4 games room · 5 swimming pool · 6 picnic area · 7 car park).
  2. ex.7 against the clock — two gaps in each description (key, TB: 1 campsite, swimming pool · 2 hotel, garden · 3 apartment, bike hire · 4 youth hostel, games room).
  3. The duel in pairs: Name your place and ONE facility — eight seconds. 'A youth hostel with a games room.' Your turn! The constraint: repeat somebody else's facility and you lose your turn.
  4. The format of the duel is Kagan RallyRobin: strictly turn by turn, no “let me think”. The partner waits for their eight seconds and takes the turn. Both partners talk an equal amount — hiding behind the stronger one is not an option.
  5. Fast finishers (TB task): Invent one more facility and draw its icon — free Wi-Fi? guided tours? a restaurant?
📺 On screen: SB p.97 ex.6–7 · 8-second timer
👦 Students do: they race through the icons and duel. Expect: they argue about what matters more — the pool or the Wi-Fi; 30 seconds of arguing is fine

Product: the 60-second offer
PRODUCT · the 60-second offer
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second offer: type of holiday + place to stay + two facilities + who it is for; the class votes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes to prepare: Keywords only, no scripts. Everyone owns one block: the type and the dates · the place you stay · two facilities · who it's perfect for.
  2. The agency rule: Every word in English. A Russian word = the sale is off.
  3. Sixty seconds per crew. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness — cards up!
  4. The class votes once all the offers are in: You have one holiday to buy. Which crew gets your money — and WHY? One reason each.
  5. A challenge from the class after each performance: You said 'great facilities'. Name two. Now.
📺 On screen: 60-second timer · judges' cards · the rubric
👦 Students do: they perform in their roles, the judges use the rubric, the class votes. Expect: the bar holds the format together. Problem: reading off the page — the judges take off fluency

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can name 7 types of holiday, 6 places to stay and 7 facilities and sell a holiday offer in 60 seconds (type + place + facilities + why).
  • Subsidiary. The gate exposes the real ceiling of their vocabulary; ex.1 (vocabulary track 52), ex.3 the accommodation riddles (vocabulary track 53), ex.6 the facility icons (vocabulary track 54) and ex.7 are all closed against the clock; Video skills is watched like producers; going to is heard in context with no topic announced.
  • Personal. Announce neither going to nor will. Log errors like “I go to Turkey next week” and “we will to go” silently — they are the material for the error clinics in Pairs 3 and 5.
By the end, students can…
  • name seven types of holiday and match them to photos and dates
  • say where people stay on holiday and why (six places)
  • read the facility icons and describe what a place offers
  • sell a holiday offer in 60 seconds

Structure
We go to the beach in August. · There's a swimming pool and bike hire. · We usually stay in an apartment at the beach. · 🚀 bonus: You'll love it because … · It's perfect for people who …
Form
every type of holiday lives with the verb go, but with different prepositions: go camping / go skiing (no preposition) · go ON a city break, a walking holiday, a water sports holiday · go TO the beach, TO a theme park resort. A wrong preposition = a wrong word.
Pron. watch
from the Wordlist: apartment /əˈpɑː(r)tmənt/ · campsite /ˈkæmpˌsaɪt/ · country cottage /ˌkʌntri ˈkɒtɪdʒ/ · hotel /həʊˈtel/ · youth hostel /ˈjuːθ ˌhɒst(ə)l/ (TB: these words look like the Russian ones but sound different — drilling is compulsory)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“B & B” — how do we say it? → /ˌbiː ən ˈbiː/, “bee-and-bee”, and it is somebody's house, not a hotel (ABCD note in the TB).
“go on the beach” — can we say that? → no: go TO the beach. The preposition is part of the word.
“You know your holiday words. Can you write 12 of them in 90 seconds?” → the gate.

Assumptions: SB pp.96–97, syllabus pair 62. Beach, hotel, camping are familiar from A1+; new are B & B, youth hostel, country cottage, bike hire, picnic area, games room, theme park resort.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“We know everything about holidays” from the doorDon't argue — put up the gate: not recognition but WRITING against 90 seconds, spelling included. The result is private, the rematch comes at the end of the pair.
The offer turns into a list of wordsThe agency rule: four compulsory blocks — type · place to stay · two facilities · why it is for you. Judges take a point off for “just a list”.
The prepositions in holiday types driftThree columns on the board (go · go on · go to) stay up all lesson; students put every new phrase into a column themselves.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
