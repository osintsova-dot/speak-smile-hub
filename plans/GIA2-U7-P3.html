🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): This is where our students' line works hardest: irregular verbs are NOT announced as the topic of the lesson. Students get the role of an eyewitness reporting live on what happened yesterday — and they discover that eight verbs break if you don't know them. The book gives exactly this material: the affirmative/negative table (ex.2), the eight forms (ex.3), flipping the text about the pilot (ex.5), the text about Emma Watson (ex.8). The P1–P2 error clinic finally opens: every “She's lawyer” and “they was” you have been logging silently goes here.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Live in three… two… You are an eyewitness. Something happened yesterday and you are the only one who saw it. Ninety seconds on air: what you saw, what you heard, what you did — and what you did NOT do. Here's the catch: eight verbs in this story do not play by the rules. Say 'goed', 'taked', 'didn't woke' and the broadcast is cut. Your crew has one lesson to get the verbs bulletproof. Camera — rolling!”
The gist, if you need to restate it: everyone is an eyewitness: 90 seconds live on what happened yesterday (what you saw, what you heard, what you did and what you did NOT do). The catch: eight verbs break if you don't know the form — “goed” and “didn't woke” cut the broadcast.

PRODUCT · the eyewitness report
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a 90-second report: what you saw · what you heard · what you did · what you did NOT do — five broken verbs minimum

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes of preparation: Keywords only. Everyone owns one block: what you SAW · what you HEARD · what you DID · what you DIDN'T do.
  2. The rule of the broadcast: Five irregular verbs minimum, and at least two negatives. Break a form — I cut the broadcast and the next crew takes over.
  3. A model on the board: I woke up early. I saw water in the street. I rang my friend. We took the boat. I didn't see the police — they came later.
  4. Ninety seconds per crew. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness — cards up!
📺 On screen: 90-second timer · judges' cards · the rubric (Backup: show the “broadcast cut” with your hand and a clap — no technology needed)
👦 Students do: students perform in their roles and catch each other's forms. Expect: the bar holds. Typical problem: “I didn't saw” — the broadcast is cut and the crew passes the turn

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can use eight irregular verbs in the affirmative and negative past simple (began · came · gave · rang · saw · spent · took · woke up) and hold a 90-second eyewitness report without breaking the forms.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1–2 the table; ex.3 the eight forms checked against pp.126–127; ex.4 four sentences; ex.5 flipping the text about the pilot (Harrison Ford); ex.6–7 about themselves; ex.8 Emma Watson; ex.9 Brain teaser; the memorising routines from the TB.
  • Personal. The word “grammar” is never said in this lesson. Say “forms”, “the rules of the broadcast”, “the verbs that break”. Open the P1–P2 clinic here: work through what you logged, with no names.
By the end, students can…
  • give the past form of eight irregular verbs without thinking
  • build a negative with didn't + infinitive (didn't wake up, not didn't woke)
  • flip a sentence from affirmative to negative and back
  • hold a 90-second eyewitness report in the past

Structure
Thomas woke up and saw houses under water. · He didn't wake up late. · They didn't have electricity. · She spent time in Zambia. · 🚀 bonus: Her career didn't stop after Harry Potter.
Form
affirmative — the past form of the verb itself (woke up, saw, had); negative — didn't + the infinitive (didn't wake up, didn't see, didn't have). The past lives EITHER in the verb OR in didn't — never in both.
Pron. watch
rhyme chains for memorising: began–ran–ran · came–became · rang–sang–swam · took–shook · spent–sent–went. The stress is always on the verb, not on didn't.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He didn't woke up” — what is wrong? → after didn't comes the infinitive: didn't wake up.
“She goed” — correct it. → went, go breaks.
“decided” — does it break? → no, an ordinary -ed.

Assumptions: SB p.88, syllabus pair 56. The past simple of regular verbs is familiar from A1+ and Unit 6 (was/were). Irregular forms are partly recognised but not actively produced — and that is exactly what the gate exposes.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“We've done this” — and then they break the formsA 90-second gate: not recognition but WRITING the forms. The private score and the rematch at the end of the lesson show the real level with no argument.
didn't + a past form (“didn't saw”)The switch gesture: one hand on didn't — “the past is already here”, the other hand opens — “the verb is clean”. Repeat the gesture all lesson instead of a spoken correction.
The list on pp.126–127 scares them by its sizeTB routine: start with the frequent core only (be, come, do, get, give, go, have, know, say, see, think), then take them in groups. Show that the eight verbs of this lesson are already inside the core.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
