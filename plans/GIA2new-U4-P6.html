🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Two real-life things in one pair: the phrases you use to propose a plan and to object politely, and their first written instructions. For new students the dialogue is built through shadowing the video (repeating after a voice takes the shyness away) and a written draft, and the instructions come on a strict staircase: two groups of things → three paragraphs → a checklist. too and also I anchor in the body as early as the warm-up.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today you learn the phrases that work in real life: Why don't we…? How about…? OK, fine. Really? That's too early. First we watch Kirsty and Sam plan their day out, then we do it — our own planning floor right here. And then your first instructions: what goes in the suitcase, what goes in your hand luggage, and why. Step by step. The model shows the way.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The phrases people really use to agree on things: we watch how Kirsty and Sam do it, then we hold our own planning meeting. Then the first instructions: what goes in the suitcase, what goes in the hand luggage and why. Step by step, with a model.

WHAT WE DO: we write our own mini-dialogue on the ex.4 model → the planning meeting: agree with a partner, then swap

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pairs write a dialogue on the model (their own version of the outing: the ice rink, the cinema, the park) — 6 minutes, phrase cards on the desks.
  2. Everyone gets a card with one constraint: You've got 500 roubles · You're busy until 11:00 · You hate picnics · You haven't got a waterproof jacket.
  3. Now plan for real: where, what time, what you wear, what you eat. You must agree at the end.
  4. The constraint is soft: if a phrase is forgotten, pick up the card, that is fine. Change partners after 5 minutes.
📺 On screen: phrase cards · constraint cards · timer
👦 Students do: they write first and speak after — the fear is gone. Expected: with a card in hand everybody speaks

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2 p.56, the order of the paragraphs and the «what and why» table → ex.3–4 too and also

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. ex.2, the table. TB key: top — water bottle and waterproof jacket, «because you use these things during the journey»; bottom — sleeping bag, clothes and cooking equipment, «because the sleeping bag is big but light», «because you don't need these things during the day».

  4. Kagan Think-Pair-Share before ex.4: Where does 'also' go — before or after the verb? Think — pair — share.
  5. ex.4 in writing, the first two sentences we do together (TB technique). TB key: 1 …waterproof trousers too · 2 I also like going on zip lines · 3 …to see my grandparents too · 4 It's also very heavy · 5 I also take photos with it · 6 …a hat and sun cream too.

WHAT WE DO: the staircase: two columns of things → compare with a partner → three sentences → three paragraphs → checklist → swap

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1 (ex.5 THINK): Beach holiday. Hand luggage and suitcase — three or four things in each column. (TB options: hand luggage — snack, water bottle, book, tickets, money; suitcase — T-shirts, sandals, sun hat, beach towel, wash bag).
  2. Step 2 through Kagan RallyRobin: first I show a turn on my own column («I say snack — you say sun hat»), then in pairs, one item per turn, back and forth — Is anything missing? Whatever you haven't got and your partner has, you add to your own list.
  3. Step 3: three sentences from ready-made openings: It's important to … · It's a good idea to … · Your hand luggage is a good place for …
  4. Step 4 (PREPARE + WRITE): three paragraphs — the introduction (a ready-made line from the coursebook) · the suitcase · the hand luggage.
  5. Step 5 (CHECK + ex.6): the checklist on their own (three paragraphs? too and also in place? is there a modal or a comparison?) → swap with a partner. The instructions go up on the unit wall!

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can make and answer suggestions with 6 Key phrases in a short dialogue and write a 3-paragraph set of instructions using too and also correctly.
  • Subsidiary. The Kirsty and Sam video + shadowing; ex.3–4, the phrases and the gaps; the model «How to pack for a camping trip» (ex.1–2); the subskill too / also (ex.3–4); ex.5, their own instructions on the staircase; ex.6, peer review.
  • Personal. The writing happens in class with my support; at home they only copy it out neatly. To the board only volunteers, and only with a finished dialogue.
By the end, students can…
  • make a suggestion in three different ways
  • agree and disagree politely
  • write instructions in three paragraphs
  • put too and also in the right position

Target language
Lexis
We should (go by train).Let's (get the bus).Why don't we (take a picnic)?How about (getting the 9:00 bus)?OK, fine.Good idea.Really? That's too early.I'm not sure. I don't like (picnics).
Structure
Why don't we take a picnic? · How about getting the 9:00 bus? · We should go by train. · OK, fine. / Good idea. / Really? That's too early. · It's important to put your phone in your hand luggage.
Form
too — at the end of the sentence, usually after a list · also — after the verb be and BEFORE all other verbs ('Our friends also came', not 'Our friends came also')
Pron. watch
should — the l is silent: /ʃʊd/ · Let's is said as one piece, /lets/ · polite falling intonation: How about the 9:00 bus? ↘
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Why don't we take a picnic?» — is that a reproach? → no, it is a suggestion: «let's…».
«I like also…» — is that allowed? → no: I also like… (also goes before the main verb).
How many paragraphs are there in the instructions? → three: introduction, suitcase, hand luggage.

Assumptions: SB pp.55–57, KTP pair 35 (the KTP says pp.56–58 and a different writing genre — in fact it is Real-world speaking p.55 + Writing pp.56–57, the genre is INSTRUCTIONS, the subskill is too/also). The dialogue and the model text are simple and short.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They are shy about acting out the dialogueFirst shadowing the video in chorus, then the pair writes its own dialogue, then acts it out at the desk; to the board only volunteers.
They agree to everythingThe partner's card carries one hard constraint (no money / busy until 11:00) — so they have to object, and the phrase for that is right in front of their eyes.
«I don't know what to write» in the instructionsThe staircase: two columns of things → three paragraphs → a checklist; the introduction I hand them as a ready-made line from the coursebook.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
