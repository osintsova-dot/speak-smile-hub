🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The blog about a 13-year-old world champion is read not «because it was set», but as a scout's dossier: the crew decides whether that life is worth its price, and has to back every answer with a line. The unit subskill (reading for specific information) is built into the rule: first we underline the key words in the question, then we hunt for them — otherwise the answer does not count. The Critical thinking from the book becomes the finale of the mission, not an add-on.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“New file: Brighton Zeuner. Thirteen years old. World champion skateboarder. Competes against women twice her age. And in the evening — homework, music, YouTube, like everyone in this room. Your crew is a talent radar: read her day, find the price she pays, and answer one question in sixty seconds — is that life worth it? Every answer needs a LINE from the text. No line, no point.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: A file on a 13-year-old world skateboarding champion: read her day, find the price of that life, and at the end answer in 60 seconds — is it worth it or not? Every answer is backed by a line from the text.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can find specific information in a blog by key words (underline → search → quote), use 6 new words in context, and deliver a 60-second verdict on the price of being a champion at 13.
  • Subsidiary. Identifying the genre (ex.3) and the best summary (ex.4); ex.5–6 against the clock; Word work (superstar · trains · amazing · junior · champion · nervous); Critical thinking Understand → Evaluate → Create with a role answer «as Brighton».
  • Personal. The strong readers' speed drowns out the weaker ones — I hold the rule «an answer only counts with an evidence line». Adverb position errors I collect silently for P3.
By the end, students can…
  • identify key words in a question before reading
  • find and quote the line that proves an answer
  • use six new words (superstar, trains, amazing, junior, champion, nervous)
  • give a 60-second verdict with two pieces of evidence

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«I think she's 14» — does that count? → no: only with a line from the text.
junior — does that mean «younger brother»? → in sport: competitions for young people, not for adults. And Brighton does NOT compete in those.
Why underline the words in the question BEFORE reading? → so that you hunt for them (or their synonyms) in the text instead of re-reading everything.

Assumptions: SB pp.26–27, KTP pair 14. They read confidently, but «key words first» is a new discipline. All 6 Word work items are in the official Wordlist.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
