🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Systematise the unit — three present forms, the two perfects, gerunds and infinitives, and the whole word list of school life and higher education — and then demonstrate it under test conditions: the book's own Test yourself, six exercises, 40 points.
What is genuinely hard
Not the content — the transfer. Students who can do each exercise when it is labelled «present perfect» fall apart when six exercises sit on one page and nothing is labelled. The second difficulty is spelling: a right answer written wrongly scores nothing, and half the vocabulary of this unit is long (accommodation, assessment, qualifications).
Typical error to expect
«I am studying English for five years» — Russian uses the present for an action that started in the past and is still going on; English uses the present perfect continuous: I have been studying English for five years. This is the single most common B2 error in Russian written work.
«make homework», «make a mistake in the exam» for «do badly» — Russian has one verb (делать) where English has two, and the split is not logical: you DO a course, an assignment, an exam, your best; you MAKE a decision, a mistake, progress, an effort. It is learned as pairs, not as a rule.
«I suggest to go», «I managed finishing», «I avoid to eat» — the pattern belongs to the verb, not to the meaning, and Russian gives no clue at all: suggest + -ing, manage + to, avoid + -ing, decide + to.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first forty-five minutes are revision that looks nothing like a test: the Check it page is walked through as a set of questions the students answer, not as a page they read, and the vocabulary comes back through Showdown, where everybody writes at the same time. Five minutes before the paper, the two ghosts are named aloud — the missing «been» and the wrong collocation — because naming them is worth more than another exercise. The test itself is thirty-five quiet minutes, and the first exercise is self-checked immediately afterwards, while the answers are still warm.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Everything you have met in three weeks is on two pages of your book, and in forty minutes you will show me you own it. First we put it all in one place — then forty points, quietly, and you will know your score before you go home.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every student says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls the previous lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, error logs collected, and one mistake said aloud before the revision starts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — what is the best thing that has happened this week? Any content counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — the error logs come in. Collect them by name at the door and look at each for five seconds. That is enough to know who has been working.
  3. 1 minute — one line each, aloud. Think-Pair-Share: read your partner ONE line from your log — the mistake and the correction. No comment, no discussion.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Page sixteen. Everything from three weeks is on this page — and in forty minutes it is on page seventeen, with your name on it.
📺 On screen: nothing on screen — paper only, and the paper is theirs. Backup: if a student has no log, they write one line now, from memory.
👦 Students do: hand over the error log and read one line of it to a partner

10–22′
Check it · the three presents and the two perfects
desks
Why: A reference page that is read is forgotten; the same page answered from memory and then checked is revision that actually moves something.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.16: the reference page used as questions, not as reading

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books at p.16. Cover the page with your hand. I ask, you answer, then you uncover and check.
  2. Six questions, fast: When do we use will for the present? (repeated or habitual behaviour) · Which verbs cannot take the continuous? · What does the present perfect continuous emphasise? (process and duration) · When can we NOT use the continuous? (a very short action: «I've broken the window», not «I've been breaking») · for or since with «last week»? · What does «I've painted my bedroom» tell us? (it's finished).
  3. Then a two-minute pair drill, RallyCoach: A gives a time expression, B produces a full sentence with the right tense. Swap after a minute.
  4. Board the three sentences from the reference that the test will echo: I've been studying in this school for more than five months. I've seen that film three times. My sister and I are always fighting.
📺 On screen: the SB p.16 reference on the board app if you have it — but covered, revealed one box at a time. Backup: the page is in their hands; use the cover-and-answer routine.
👦 Students do: answer six questions with the page covered, then drill tense choice in pairs

22–32′
Check it · gerunds and infinitives
desks
Why: The hardest exercise in the paper is a technique as much as a grammar point, and the technique takes four minutes to teach.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.16 table + a fast transformation drill

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Same routine, page covered. Five questions: after a preposition? (-ing) · after «too», «enough», «the first»? (to) · after admit, avoid, consider, risk, suggest? (-ing) · after want, learn, agree, decide, expect, hope, promise? (to) · gerund or infinitive as the subject of a sentence? (gerund).
  2. Then teach the transformation mechanism explicitly, because exercise 3 of the test is transformations and nobody has been taught how they work: Read sentence one. Look at the word given. Decide the pattern that word needs. Then count: two to five words, and you never change the word given.
  3. Model one on the board: Don't do that because there's a chance you'll fail. (risk) → Don't do that, you risk failing.
  4. Three more, done in pairs on paper: go riding · can't stand getting up · consider joining.
📺 On screen: the transformation model written on the board and left there. Backup: not needed.
👦 Students do: answer five rule questions from memory, then do four transformations in pairs
SB p.16 · board
32–40′
Vocabulary Showdown
desks in fours
Why: Showdown gives every student the same number of attempts as the fastest one in the group — which is the whole point of the structure and the opposite of a quiz.

WHAT WE DO: the twenty-nine words of the unit, everybody writing at once

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. «SHOWDOWN» on the unit word list (SB p.16): each team has the twenty-nine words face down; the leader turns one over, everyone writes a definition or a collocation ALONE, then «Showdown!» and all four cards go up at once. One point for a correct definition, two for a correct sentence with it.
  2. Two rounds of six words each. The leader rotates every round, so four different students run the game.
  3. Board the six words the teams got wrong most often — those are the ones that will be in the paper twenty minutes later, and you say so.
  4. Finish with sixty seconds of do/make: you call a noun, the whole class calls the verb. Twenty items, no thinking time.
📺 On screen: the unit word list open at SB p.16. Backup: if there are no word cards, the leader simply reads a word from the list on p.16.
👦 Students do: write a definition or a collocation alone, then show it simultaneously with the team
word cards or SB p.16 · mini-whiteboards or paper

Move break · shake it out
open floor
Why: Physical state before a test matters more than one more exercise: they arrive at the paper awake and not wound up.

WHAT WE DO: three minutes standing before a thirty-five minute paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand, push the chairs in, two rounds of the do/make wall: left wall DO, right wall MAKE, ten nouns called fast.
  2. Then thirty seconds of nothing: stand, shoulders down, look out of the window. Say it plainly: Sit down when you are ready to write. Not before.
📺 On screen: two wall labels. Backup: hands up left / right.
👦 Students do: walk to a wall ten times, then stand still for thirty seconds

48–83′
Test yourself · 40 points
desks
Why: A short, well-signposted paper on familiar material is an honest measure of the unit; the timing and the finisher rule keep the room quiet to the last minute.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.17: six exercises, 35 minutes, silence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Thirty-five minutes. Grammar first, vocabulary second, and if you are stuck, move on and come back. Write the finishing time on the board.
  2. Walk the room twice in the first five minutes, then sit down. A teacher circling for thirty-five minutes raises the temperature of the room.
  3. At the halfway mark write 17 minutes left on the board without saying anything.
  4. Fast finishers: the rule they were given — go back to the definitions in Vocabulary 2 and write a better one for each of the seven words.
  5. Collect at 83 minutes exactly, in silence.
📺 On screen: the finishing time and the halfway warning on the board. Backup: a wall clock and your own watch if the board is needed for anything else.
👦 Students do: sit the paper alone in silence for thirty-five minutes

WHAT WE DO: three minutes: they leave knowing something, not wondering

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I've lived here for ten years» and «I've been living here for ten years» — is one of them wrong? → no, both work; the second stresses the process. That honesty prevents panic in the test.
«He's been reading that book» — has he finished it? → probably not.
ICQ before the paper: how long? → thirty-five minutes. Can you go back to exercise 1 at the end? → yes, if you have time.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«We didn't revise» on the day of the testThe revision was the homework and the first half of today. Say that once, calmly, and start. The test is 40 points of what they have done four times each.
A student panics at exercise 3 (transformations)Teach the mechanism in the revision half: cover the second sentence, read the first, decide the pattern, then count the words. Two to five words means the given word is never changed.
Fast finishers hand in after twenty minutesThey do not leave the paper. The rule is announced beforehand: when you finish, go back to the vocabulary definitions and write a second, better one for each.
Someone was absent for two lessons of this unitGive them the photocopiable Standard test from the course folder next week instead — do not let them sit today's paper unprepared.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The unit test is built the way that exam block is built: produce the form, spell it correctly, choose the right partner word. A present perfect continuous written as a present perfect scores nothing; «make homework» scores nothing. The revision half of the lesson names both traps aloud before the paper is turned over.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
