🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Put the whole of Unit 4 in one place — the grammar reference in their own words, the word list in their mouths — and then find out what is still missing while there is still a night to fix it.
What is genuinely hard
Not the content: the transfer. A student who can do each exercise when it is labelled falls apart when six exercises sit on one page with no labels. The second difficulty is honesty — a practice paper only works if nobody hides the score from themselves.
Typical error to expect
«more happier», «more easier», «the most biggest» — a double comparative. English marks the degree ONCE: either -er / -est or more / the most, never both. It is the single most common comparative mistake in Russian classrooms because Russian marks it twice quite happily (более лучший).
«She is best student in class» — the superlative needs the, and Russian has no articles to remind anyone. Same root cause as «I am student» and «I play guitar».
«creativeness», «lazyness», «inventer» — the stem changes when the suffix goes on (creativity, laziness, inventor). This is a spelling mark in tasks 26–31 and it is learned by writing, not by recognising.
«Yesterday I could buy the tickets» for one single successful occasion — English needs was able to / managed to / succeeded in. Could is general ability in the past, not one achieved event.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is revision that looks nothing like a test: the Check it page is answered, not read, and the vocabulary comes back through games where everybody writes at once. Then the room turns quiet for a timed run of the book's own Test yourself — for practice, with the marks belonging to nobody — and the students mark their own papers and write the reason for every error. The lesson ends on the real test: seven sections, a hundred points, the camera and the choice of version, all named a day early so that the paper measures the language and not the surprise.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Everything from this unit, on your desk, in one lesson — and one run against the clock that costs you nothing. Tomorrow it counts; today it only tells you where to look.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting at the door, then a standing retrieval round on the whole unit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what is the best thing that happened to you this week? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — rewind, everything. Quiz-Quiz-Trade with mixed cards from the whole unit: one side an adjective, an idiom or a base word, the other side the answer. Two rounds, standing.
  3. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Forty-five minutes to get everything back, then the test. Page 54.
📺 On screen: the mixed card set handed out at the door. Backup: no cards — run it from the vocabulary lists on SB p.54, partner A covers the word and defines it.
👦 Students do: stand, quiz, trade, move — three minutes of continuous retrieval.

5–18′
Grammar reference · the showdown
teams of four
Why: Showdown gives simultaneous individual accountability — the opposite of a hands-up review, where the same three students answer and the rest revise nothing.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.54 Grammar reference turned into a team retrieval game rather than a reading task

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books at p.54, two minutes of silent reading of the Grammar reference — nothing else, and time it.
  2. Books closed. Showdown in teams of four: you read a prompt, every student writes the answer on a mini whiteboard, the team captain says Showdown! and all four boards go up together.
  3. Fifteen prompts, in this order — comparatives first, then articles, then ability: (1) the opposite of «more expensive». (2) big + comparative. (3) happy + superlative. (4) «Dogs are ___ clever ___ cats». (5) Correct: «She is best student». (6) Article: «___ Sun». (7) Article: «___ scientists say ___ animals have complex brains». (8) Article: «He's ___ scientist». (9) Yesterday I ___ (can) finish the race. (10) He succeeded ___ finding the temple. (11) He managed ___ move the statue. (12) Next year I'll ___ drive. (13) I love ___ able to swim. (14) far / much / slightly + what? (15) «The harder you work, the ___ you get».
  4. One point per correct board, and the team only scores if ALL FOUR are right — that is what makes them coach each other between rounds.
  5. Do not explain during the game. Note the two prompts that failed most and re-teach those two only, at the end, in ninety seconds.
📺 On screen: the prompts read aloud one at a time, and the running score on the board. Backup: if there are no mini whiteboards, students write on a torn sheet and raise it — identical effect.
👦 Students do: read, then write every answer individually and show it simultaneously; coach team-mates between rounds.

18–28′
Vocabulary revision · the running dictation
open floor
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies. Running dictation also forces the exact spelling of the compound adjectives, which is a test mark.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.54 vocabulary lists revised on the feet: definitions on the wall, words in the notebook

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before the lesson, print or write eight definitions and stick them on the walls around the room (from the unit: broad-minded, resourceful, impetuous, tactless, resilient; a live wire, a wallflower, a big mouth).
  2. Pairs: one runner, one writer. The runner walks (walks!) to a definition, memorises it, comes back and dictates; the writer writes the DEFINITION and the WORD. Swap roles after four definitions.
  3. Six minutes, then two minutes of checking against p.54 in pairs.
  4. Finish standing with a fast whole-class round: you say the definition, the class says the word in chorus. Twelve seconds per item, no hands up.
📺 On screen: eight printed definitions on the walls, prepared in advance. Backup: write them on eight pieces of paper during the previous stage — five minutes of your time, and it is reusable all year.
👦 Students do: walk, memorise, dictate, write, swap — every student both speaks and writes.
📓 Interactive notebook — 09_Unit_4_Review_Matching_1 + 10_Unit_4_Review_Matching_2 + 11_Unit_4_Review_Translate_1 + 12_Unit_4_Review_Translate_2 + 13_Unit_4_Review_Translate_3
five A5 sheets — MATCHING 1/2 and 2/2 (Russian sentences joined to their English pairs: the chunks and structures of the whole unit) and TRANSLATE 1/3, 2/3, 3/3 (the same sentences written out in English in full, with a SELF-CHECK strip: whole chunks · verb form · tense)
  • Glue MATCHING 1/2 and 2/2 in and join the pairs in pencil — alone first, then with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE 1/3 in and write the English without looking back at the matching.
  • Self-check against the matched sheets; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • TRANSLATE 2/3 and 3/3 are the homework half — same routine, same self-check.

28–40′
Suffix relay + the last three ghosts
teams → desks
Why: Retrieval under mild competitive pressure is the closest thing to test conditions that is still enjoyable — and the three ghosts are the three marks the class would otherwise donate.

WHAT WE DO: the word-formation half of the test, drilled to automaticity, and the three named error patterns

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Suffix relay, six minutes. Teams of four, one board pen per team, one column per team on the board. You call a base word (create · lazy · shy · concentrate · invent · free · improve · aware · psychology · relax); the first student runs up, writes the noun, hands the pen to the next.
  2. A wrong spelling is not a point. That is the whole pedagogical content of the game and they will argue about it, which is exactly what you want.
  3. Then sit down and name the three ghosts — write them on the board and leave them there through the test: (1) once only: NOT «more happier». (2) the + superlative: NOT «she is best». (3) one occasion in the past: «managed to», NOT «could».
  4. Ninety seconds of silent personal revision: look at your own notebook page from Lesson 3 and fix anything that now looks wrong.
📺 On screen: the team columns and, after the game, the three ghosts. Backup: the relay works on paper at the desks with one sheet per team if the board is small.
👦 Students do: run, write, argue about spelling, then revise their own notebook page in silence.
board pens · notebooks
40–49′
Doubt clinic · their questions, not yours
desks
Why: The revision so far was ours. This eight minutes is theirs, and an anonymous slip is the only way the weakest student in the room asks the question that has been in the way for two weeks.

WHAT WE DO: the last honest questions before the paper — asked by the students

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand every student a slip. Write one question about this unit you still cannot answer. Anonymous. One sentence. Ninety seconds, everybody writes — a student who has no question writes the hardest question they CAN answer, for the class.
  2. Collect, shuffle, read aloud. Answer the first three yourself, fast. Then hand the rest to the class: Numbered Heads Together — the group agrees the answer, one number answers.
  3. Anything nobody can answer goes on the board under the heading ASK BEFORE THE TEST and is answered in the first two minutes of the next lesson, before the papers go out.
  4. Do not turn this into a new lesson. If a question needs twelve minutes of teaching, say so honestly: it is not on the paper in that form, and here is the one line that covers what is.
📺 On screen: the slips, then the board list ASK BEFORE THE TEST. Backup: none needed — paper and voice only.
👦 Students do: write one anonymous question, then answer other people's questions in groups
slips of paper, board
49–69′
🎯 Test yourself · against the clock, for practice
desks
Why: The first time they meet the unit under time pressure must not be the day of the real test. Everything that goes wrong here is still repairable — and it is repaired by them, tonight.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.55 — the book's own Test yourself page, done to the clock — done to time, but the mark belongs to nobody

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say what this is, once, and mean it: This is practice. I am not writing these marks down. What you find out in the next 20 minutes is what you revise tonight.
  2. Clock it on the board. Same rule as the real paper: no book, no phone, no talking, and you may go back at the end if you have time.
  3. Monitor with a notebook, not a pen over their shoulders. Write down which exercise the room slows on — that is the section you re-teach in the last minutes, not the one you had planned to.
  4. Two minutes before the end, warn them once. Students who finish early do NOT sit and wait: they turn the page over and write three sentences of their own using the unit's grammar.
📺 On screen: the timer on the board, big enough to see from the back. Backup: a phone stopwatch face up on your desk.
👦 Students do: work in silence to the clock, then stop when the time stops

69–78′
Self-check and the error map
desks → pairs
Why: A score without a reason teaches nothing. Writing WHY the answer was wrong is the whole point of the practice run, and doing it while the paper is warm is worth three exercises done cold.

WHAT WE DO: keys go to the students; every wrong answer becomes a line to work on

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Answers on the board, or the key page open on the projector. Students mark their own paper — not a partner's. This is the one time where nobody else sees the score.
  2. Every wrong answer gets one line in the error log: what the answer was, what the right one is, and why — in their own words. «Careless» is not a reason and does not count.
  3. RallyCoach in pairs: take one error each and explain it to your partner. If you cannot explain it, it goes on the ASK BEFORE THE TEST list on the board.
  4. Take a show of hands by section — grammar, vocabulary, transformations — and write the three weakest on the board. That list IS tonight's homework, personalised, without you writing twelve different tasks.
📺 On screen: the answer key, then the three weakest sections on the board. Backup: if the projector fails, read the key aloud twice, slowly, and let them mark as you read.
👦 Students do: mark their own paper, write the reason for every error, then explain one error to a partner
answer key, error logs
78–86′
The test explained · format, level, camera
board
Why: Format panic costs more marks than gaps in the language. A student who knows the order, the totals and the camera walks in measuring the unit, not the room.

WHAT WE DO: exactly what happens next lesson, so nobody is surprised by the paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Put the shape of the test on the board and leave it there: Listening 12 · Vocabulary 20 · Grammar 25 · Use of English 8 · Reading 15 · Writing 10 · Speaking 10 = 100. Ninety minutes, the whole lesson.
  2. Name the order and the reason: listening first, while the ears are fresh; writing last; and speaking taken in pairs in the corner while the room writes — the only honest way to fit five skills into one lesson.
  3. Say the camera out loud now, not on the day: Your speaking answer is filmed. The mark is for the test, the video is for December — so that you can hear for yourself how much better you have got. Nobody watches it but you and me.
  4. The choice of version. Standard or Higher, same seven sections, same hundred points, harder wording. Ask them to decide tonight, not at the door: Which version measures you honestly? Anyone who cannot decide gets Standard and may swap in the first five minutes.
  5. Last line, and say it plainly: Bring a pen that works and the sleep of somebody who has already done the revision.
📺 On screen: the seven sections and the totals, written up and left on the board. Backup: none — this is board and voice.
👦 Students do: copy the seven sections into the notebook and decide their version
board

Why: Self-assessment against the aim they heard at the start, so the lesson closes the loop it opened. Homework comes from their own workbook, not from a photocopy.

WHAT WE DO: self-check against today's aim, then the homework said once and written on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Self-check (60 seconds): One thing from this unit you now trust yourself with, and one you'll revise this weekend. On the exit slip.
  2. Homework: Workbook p.37 — the article, 140–190 words, four paragraphs, at least three techniques from the Writing bank. This is the marked piece of the unit.
  3. Next lesson: no new grammar. Exam success — the four tasks the Cambridge and the Russian exams both use, and your project group.
📺 On screen: the homework word count and the four-paragraph plan on the board. Backup: a photo of Workbook p.37 in the class chat.
👦 Students do: write two lines on a slip and hand it in at the door.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«We didn't revise» on the day of the testSay it once, calmly: the revision was the homework, and the whole of today. Then hand out the error map — it names three sections each, which is a night's work, not a fortnight's.
A student treats the practice run as a real test and panicsSay the sentence before the timer starts and again at the halfway point: nobody is writing these marks down. If a student freezes anyway, let them work from the book for the second half — the diagnosis matters more than the conditions.
Fast finishers hand the practice paper in after eight minutesNobody hands anything in — it is their own page. The rule is announced beforehand: finish early, turn the page over and write three sentences of your own with the unit's grammar. Those get checked in the self-marking stage.
The doubt clinic turns into a full re-teach of one grammar pointAnswer at the level of the paper, not of the grammar book: one line, one example, and the reference page number. Anything bigger goes on the ASK BEFORE THE TEST list and gets two minutes before the papers go out.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 and 26–31 — the grammar and word-formation block, thirteen marks of the written paper.

The unit test is built exactly the way the exam block is: produce the form, spell it right. A superlative without the, a noun suffix attached to the wrong stem or a missing article scores nothing at all, which is why the briefing names all three ghosts aloud before the papers go face down.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
