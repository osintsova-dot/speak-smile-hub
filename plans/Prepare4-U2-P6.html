🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Pull four units back out of memory on the book's own Review 1 spread (SB p.30–31): the U3 collocations, the Olympics cloze, the mixed-tense conversations and the comparatives — plus a ten-sentence grammar auction across Units 1–4 and a personal revision list to take home.
What is genuinely hard
Nothing here is new — that is the trap: three-week-old language FEELS known until it has to be produced. The collocations (play/do/go, leave/get/have) fade first, and mixed-tense items need the choosing step (fact? right now? finished? in progress?) said out loud again.
Typical error to expect
make sport · go to jogging · play swimming — the do/go/play collocation guessed from Russian «заниматься» [high priority, A2/B1]
more better · more easier · the most happiest — two comparative markers glued onto one adjective [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The syllabus gives the Review 1 spread its own lesson and names the format: team spread work, a grammar auction, the unit videos, reflection. The spread is split honestly with block 1: its U1–U2 tasks were played in lesson 8, so today runs ONLY today's share (Vocabulary ex.3–4, the grammar leftovers) — nothing is done twice. The auction sentences are the teacher's own ten, covering all four units with keys in this plan, because the TB key for the spread itself is not in our course notes (see the keys table — check the printed TB before class). The lesson ends on reflection because that is what the review is FOR: a named list of what to revise beats an unmarked feeling of «we repeated everything».
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Four units. Fourteen lessons. One hour to prove it stuck. Today the book looks back at everything since September — in teams, with an auction and real money on the table (fake money). You leave with a list of exactly three things to polish before block three. Deal?”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a four-topic card sweep, and the bridge into the review spread

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the four-unit sweep. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with a mixed card set (describing words + clothes + life events + sports), two trades each: Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap: Every card in your hands is from THIS year. Today the book checks what stayed.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the spread: Pages thirty and thirty-one. Review one — four units, one spread. In teams.
📺 On screen: the mixed card set from lessons past — the cards are the medium, so no backup is needed; if sets are missing, call definitions and pairs answer chorally
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room across four topics. Expect the U1 adjectives to be the dustiest — note it for the carousel

Lesson frame

Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: name the shape of the hour and the one rule of review

  2. The one rule: Today nobody learns anything new — we PROVE what we already own. Points mean nothing; your list at the end means everything.
  3. Teams of three or four, mixed by you (one strong voice per team), team names in ten seconds.

👦 Students do: hear the shape, form teams. Expect the «points mean nothing» line to relax exactly the students who need it

8–20′
Team review · collocations
teams
Why: The spread's collocation sort is run as a team relay because collocations are exactly what fades first — pulling play/do/go and leave/get/have back out of memory is the point of a review lesson.

WHAT WE DO: spread Vocabulary ex.3: the play/do/go + leave/get/have sort

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3 on the spread — the U3/U4 collocation sort. Teams solve it in their books, 4 minutes. ⚠️ No verified key in the course notes: run the check as a PROOF round — Kagan Numbered Heads Together, item two — number threes, and tell us WHY — and pencil the confirmed answers into your copy.
  2. Extend beyond the book while the engine is warm — the machine chant: gymnastics? — DO! · school at eighteen? — LEAVE! · a degree? — GET! · children? — HAVE! Twelve fast calls.
  3. Any collocation that wobbles goes into the «my list» corner of the board — students will harvest it at 82′.
📺 On screen: the spread is in every book on p.30 — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed; the wobble corner grows in chalk
👦 Students do: sort, prove, chant. Expect leave/get/have to be shakier than play/do/go — it had less air time. Typical error: make a degree back from the dead — chant it out

20–32′
Team review · the Olympics cloze
teams
Why: A four-option cloze on Units 3–4 lexis, read the careful way: the words around the gap vote. Teams must UNDERLINE the deciding words before an answer counts — the habit matters more than the score.

WHAT WE DO: spread Vocabulary ex.4: «Life after the Olympics», A/B/C/D

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame the habit before the text: Four options per gap. The words AROUND the gap vote — you count the votes. Underline the deciding words or the answer doesn't count.
  2. Teams solve the cloze on p.31, 6 minutes. ⚠️ No verified key in the course notes — run the check as a defended round: a team names the answer AND its deciding words; rival teams may challenge; the text settles every dispute. Pencil the confirmed key into your copy (and check the printed TB before class if you can).
  3. Debrief one gap deliberately: which wrong option LOOKED nice, and which neighbouring word killed it.
📺 On screen: the cloze is in every book on p.31 — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read carefully, underline, defend. Expect disputes — they are the lesson: a defended answer is revision squared. Typical problem: options chosen because they «sound sporty» — the underline rule blocks it

Move break
standing
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: the four-unit compass

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up. Four corners: U1 people · U2 clothes · U3 life · U4 sport. I say a word — you walk to its unit. Miserable! Second-hand! Retire! Squash! Eight calls, arguments allowed in one sentence.
  2. Last call lands them back with their teams.
📺 On screen: instructions by voice, nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: walk the words to their units. Expect a fight over miserable (people or life?) — one sentence settles it

34–46′
Spread grammar in teams
teams
Why: The spread's grammar items that block 1 honestly left for today — mixed tenses and comparatives, solved with a reason out loud, because the explanation is what shows whether the form survived three weeks.

WHAT WE DO: the grammar leftovers block 1 saved for today

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the split honestly: Half of this grammar box you did in October — block one owns it. Today: the rest.
  2. Grammar ex.1, items 3 and 5 only (the mixed-tense conversations). Verified key: Grammar ex.1 item 3: is Tom being, don't know, says · item 5: were, got, didn't own (checked in block 1's page-by-page notes).
  3. Grammar ex.2 (the comparatives set) + ex.3 item 3 + ex.4 items 3–4. ⚠️ No verified key in the notes — same defended check-round as the cloze; the reasons are the point: fact? → present simple · in progress then? → past continuous · comparing two? → -er + than.
  4. Kagan RallyCoach inside teams: solver speaks, coach demands the reason before writing.
📺 On screen: the spread is in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed; reasons boiled onto the board as they land
👦 Students do: solve the leftovers with reasons out loud. Expect item 3 of ex.1 (is Tom being…) to raise eyebrows — being + adjective is behaviour, a nice bonus fact, not a test item. Typical error: comparative items double-marked

46–60′
Grammar auction
teams
Why: The syllabus asks for a grammar auction and it earns its slot: bidding forces every team to COMMIT to correct/incorrect and argue for it — engagement does the drilling, and the ten sentences cover all four units.

WHAT WE DO: ten sentences, four units, fake money — commit and defend

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: every team gets £100. I read a sentence; teams bid for the right to judge it — correct or incorrect, and if incorrect — fix it. Right judgement + right fix = the pot. Wrong = the bid is gone and the sentence re-opens.
  2. The ten sentences (read them with a straight face, one at a time): I am not understanding this exercise. · We didn't heared the bell. · She is more taller than her brother. · My grandad retired when he was sixty. · It was the most happiest day of my life. · I was watch TV at eight pm yesterday. · We go jogging every morning before school. · He plays athletics for the school team. · Were she sleeping at midnight? · My phone isn't as newer as yours..
  3. Full key in the keys table below — two of the ten are correct as they stand; the straight face matters.
  4. Bank the scores; the winning team names the rule that earned them the most money.
📺 On screen: the auction bank in chalk: teams, budgets, pots — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; play money optional and glorious
👦 Students do: bid, judge, fix, defend. Expect the two correct sentences to cause beautiful panic — that hesitation is four units of grammar being weighed at once. Typical error: fixing what is not broken (grandad retired…) — celebrate the team that leaves it alone

WHAT WE DO: the unit videos as thirty-second retrieval bursts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The syllabus sends the About-you videos here. Confirmed in the app: video 01 «In fashion» (U2) and video 03 (U3 — learning to walk, talk, swim). ⚠️ Check the app's list for Units 1 and 4 before class; a missing video's slot is filled with 60 seconds of choral About-you questions from that unit's opening page.

70–82′
🎯 Speaking out · four units about me
carousel pairs
Why: The productive outcome of the whole block of blocks: one carousel where every station speaks the language of a different unit — describing, comparing, telling. Personalisation is what retrieval sticks to.

WHAT WE DO: one carousel, four stations — each speaks one unit's language

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four prompts on the board, one per unit: U1 — describe someone in your family (looks + character). · U2 — what were you wearing last Saturday, and is your style smart or casual? · U3 — the best age to leave home, with a because. · U4 — a match or event you remember: what happened while you were watching?
  2. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share ×4: mix, new partner, 90 seconds each way on station 1; mix again for station 2 — through all four.
  3. The listener's job rotates too: hunt ONE thing (U1 — an adjective pair; U2 — a past simple; U3 — a comparison; U4 — a was/were line) and report the best find.
  4. You walk and log SILENTLY — this log opens block 3.
📺 On screen: the four prompts in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: speak all four units in twelve minutes. Expect audible warm-up by station 3 — say so. Typical error: the U4 story sliding into present tense — logged, not corrected

82–88′
Reflection · my revision list
solo
Why: The syllabus closes Review 1 with reflection, and rightly: students who NAME what is solid and what is shaky revise the right things at home — and hand you the map for block 3.

WHAT WE DO: three lines that make the review worth having

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The format on the board: MY LIST — three lines. Each line = a topic + where it lives. Model one honestly: too/enough — WB p.14 bottom.
  2. Students harvest from the lesson's wobble corner, the auction, their own ears. 3 minutes, silence.
  3. Pair check, 60 seconds: read your list to your partner — the partner asks Which one first?
  4. Collect nothing: the list goes home in the notebook — it IS the homework.
📺 On screen: the list format and the wobble corner in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write three honest lines, rank them. Expect «everything is fine» from two students — send them to the wobble corner for inspiration

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
