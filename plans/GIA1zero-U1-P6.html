WHAT WE DO: Showdown as a rewind: high-fives at the door; a lightning rewind of P5: the 3 message moves + fixing capitals; a bridge to the “final boss”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door — high-fives + one word: project mood? “Excited!” “Sleepy…”
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everyone writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they all lift them together — I see every answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. Rewind of P5 — in chorus: The three message moves? — Start! Why! Ask to write!
  4. One “crime” on mini-boards: i'm from tokyo → Boards UP! +1 for clean.
  5. Bridge: You've met the world, questioned it, written to it… Today — the FINAL BOSS of Unit 1.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · the crime line on the board
👦 Students do: give high-fives, finish off the moves in chorus, fix capitals on the boards. Expected: I'm from Tokyo. Typical error: fixing only Tokyo — “TWO crimes in this line!”

WHAT WE DO: threes choose 5 sections — negotiating ONLY with phrases from the book; 3 name options for the country

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out Key phrases tent cards to the desks (What's a good name/flag? · I like your idea · What about…? · That's a good idea! · No, that's not very original).
  2. TB Reinforcement (p.29): first EVERYONE chooses sections alone for 60 sec — then a discussion: the quiet ones already have an opinion ready.
  3. Task (ex.2–3, 5): Choose YOUR five sections. Decide ONLY with the Key phrases! I walk round, award teamwork points for phrases I hear.
  4. The creativity rule: THREE name options minimum, then vote. Don't marry the first idea!
📺 On screen: Key phrases tent cards on the desks · a slide-menu of sections (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: choose sections solo first, then bargain using phrases from the tent card, generate 3 names and vote. Expected: “That's a good idea!” heard with no prompting. Typical error: deciding in Russian — the decision “doesn't count”, we replay it in English in 20 sec.

WHAT WE DO: write the section texts (2–3 sentences with be) and glue them onto the A2 sheet; a sync check bell in the middle

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I start a 22-min countdown: CREATE! Texts: two-three sentences per section. Visuals. Glue! TB: a draft first, read each other's sections, then the final copy.
  2. At minute 11 I ring a bell: Sync check! Fact file done? Flag drawn? Who's behind — help them!

WHAT WE DO: 3 min rehearsal, presentations of ~2 min each; the audience-judges answer 2 questions aloud; my teacher's check — the TB 4 axes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I give 3 min for rehearsal: Everyone speaks three-plus sentences. Toes to the audience. A teammate holds the poster!
  2. I call up the threes in turn (~2 min per three).
  3. After each one — judging questions for the room (ex.10): How is it different from our real country? Do you want to visit — why? The audience answers aloud.
  4. Points on 3 scales: content 1–3 · everyone spoke 1–3 · creativity 1–3. (For myself — the TB frame Layout/Content/Language/Visuals: notes in my notebook for feedback.) Applause — the protocol.
📺 On screen: posters · peer-review cards for the audience · a points board with the 3 scales
👦 Students do: rehearse, present (each their own sections), the audience listens with a task and answers. Expected: everyone says 3+ sentences. Typical error: mumbling into the poster — the “toes to the room” gesture, a teammate holds the poster.

Revision games: a warm-up for the test
Revision games (test warm-up)
stations · teams

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: three quick games across the whole unit instead of dry review; everyone takes their weak round into homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I announce: Test warm-up — three games, everything you know!
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. Game 1 (4′) — a spelling relay: teams chain-write nationalities for my countries, one letter — one player.
  4. Game 2 (4′) — the Opposite Slam finale: an opposite as a sentence: The city is old! — No, it's modern!
  5. Game 3 (4′) — “ask & grab” from P4: short answers on cards + 2 “fix the mistake” from today's CREATE (anonymous!).
  6. I sum up: Your weakest game = tonight's focus. WB page 11 is your test map. Thursday — calm and sharp. Homework on the slide.
📺 On screen: my list of countries · a ball · answer cards · 2 CREATE mistake slips · the homework slide (Backup: I write the homework on the board and they copy it into their diaries.)
👦 Students do: play three short rounds as whole teams at once, fix their own project mistakes, find their weak spot. Expected: confidence — “we can do all this”. Typical error: panic about the test — “the format's familiar, one page: WB 11”.

two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to English A–L (countries and nationalities, be in all three forms, place adjectives, the keypal message), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 written out in English from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the unit's exit line.

Workbook U1 · notebook
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, trios design and present a poster of an invented country (5 sections, strict roles, timed steps) recycling all Unit 1 language — and warm up for the test with revision games.
  • Subsidiary. Project cycle THINK→PLAN→CREATE→PRESENT with the Calobia video model; Graphic-organiser warmer (p.118); Super skill Creativity + group Key phrases; Peer review; Final reflection (TB guide: yellow/red smiley → “what I'll do differently”); revision games instead of dry review.
  • Personal. You're the keeper of time, not the designer: strict roles, loud timers for every STEP, we praise the process. Presentation success = everyone said 3+ sentences. The project now takes a WHOLE pair — honest time for Create, no rushing.
By the end, students can…
  • agree ideas with Key phrases
  • own one poster role and deliver it on time
  • present their section in 3+ sentences with be
  • play the unit revision games with confidence

“One person draws everything?” → No: three roles, everyone owns sections.
“Is ‘No, that's not very original’ rude?” → No — a polite team phrase from the book.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
