WHAT WE DO: Kagan Showdown warm-up: everyone writes at once — 3 quick recall shots across Unit 1; a bridge to the “final mission”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door — a high-five + one word: how do you feel about a project today? “Excited!” “Nervous…” — I take both, no pressure.
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time on a mini-board, boards face down; on No peeking… Boards up! they lift them together — I see every answer, nobody sits it out, nobody shouts first.
  3. Three retrieval shots, ~40 sec each (spaced recall of the whole unit): ① One country + its nationality. ② A true be sentence about you: I'm… ③ One adjective for a place. Boards up ×3, praise the variety.
  4. Bridge: You've named the world, questioned it, written to a keypal… Today — the FINAL MISSION of Unit 1: invent your OWN country.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · the 3 recall prompts on the board (Backup: prompts written on the board before class.)
👦 Students do: high-five, write all three recalls on their own board, lift together. Expected: Spain–Spanish, I'm from Norilsk, busy/quiet. Typical error: “I'm Russian” vs “I'm from Russia” — I recast both as correct, no red pen.

Hook · brief + trios

WHAT WE DO: Graphic organiser p.118 in pairs; I announce the project and its reward map, and form balanced trios myself

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pairs, 2 min, Project planner p.118 (Graphic organiser): What did you LEARN about countries? What did you enjoy most? — 2–3 answers aloud.
  2. I read the TASK from p.22: Design a new country and make a poster to introduce it. Learning outcomes on screen: ① a poster about a country · ② use your imagination · ③ use language from the unit.
  3. I form the trios MYSELF, balancing a confident speaker + a quiet one + an “artist” in each; I read out the line-ups so no one is left choosing.
  4. Reward map (friendly, no “winner”): points for content, for everyone speaking, for creativity — every trio can score full marks.
📺 On screen: SB p.118 planner · the WDYT / TASK slide · the trio list · points board (Backup: outcomes + trio list on the board before class, or on one printed card per trio.)
👦 Students do: recall the unit in pairs, sit into their trios, hear the reward map. Expected: “can it be a country from a game?” → Invent your OWN — even better.

WHAT WE DO: we watch students present Calobia — where is it? how many sections? which do you like?

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Task BEFORE the video (SB ex.1): Watch the students present their poster. Where is Calobia? Play. (Answer: a small island in the Pacific Ocean, ~3,000 km from New Zealand.)

  4. No video → I open p.23 as a “frozen presentation” and voice the intro myself (script TB p.138).

STEP 1 THINK: choose 5 sections + name
THINK · sections + name
desks · trios

WHAT WE DO: trios pick 5 poster sections and 3 name options, negotiating ONLY with the Super-skills Key phrases

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I put a Key-phrases tent card on each desk (from the Creativity box, p.22): What's a good name/flag? · I like that idea. · What about…? · That's a good idea! · That's great! · No, that's not very original.
  2. Think-first scaffold: EVERYONE chooses their five favourite sections alone for 60 sec (from the ex.2 menu: language, currency, flag, description, cities, family life, famous people, food, sport, school life, population, music, unusual info) — the quiet ones arrive with an opinion ready.
  3. Task (ex.5): Now agree on FIVE sections for your trio. Decide ONLY with the Key phrases — I'm listening for them. I walk round awarding teamwork points for phrases I hear.
  4. Creativity rule (ex.3 “Don't choose the first idea”): THREE name options, then vote. Don't marry the first name!
📺 On screen: Key-phrases tent cards on desks · the sections menu (Backup: menu on the board before class, or one printed card per trio.)
👦 Students do: choose solo first, then bargain with the tent-card phrases, generate 3 names and vote. Expected: “That's a good idea!” with no prompting. Typical error: deciding in Russian — the decision “doesn't count”, we re-run it in English in 20 sec.

WHAT WE DO: role cards (flag+facts / map+cities / language+currency); everyone writes KEY-WORD notes for their own section

STEP 3 CREATE (part 1): section texts
CREATE · be-sentences from frames
desks · trios

WHAT WE DO: each writes 2–3 be-sentences for their section from the frame bank — draft, then swap and read

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I put a sentence-frame strip on every desk (EAL scaffold — the frames carry the be grammar so thinking goes to the ideas): ___ is a small/big island/country. · It's in the ___ Ocean. · The capital is ___. · Our flag is ___ and ___. · (Colour) is for the ___. · The people are ___. · It isn't ___, but it's ___.
  2. CREATE — part one! Two or three sentences for YOUR section. Use the frames — fill the gaps. 8-min draft.
  3. Buddy-read: Swap with a teammate — read theirs, tick one thing you like. This is the first peer check, low-stakes.
  4. I circulate with a notebook and quietly log 2 typical be-errors for the revision quiz — I do NOT correct on the spot (keeps the flow, feeds retrieval later).
📺 On screen: frame strips on desks · the Calobia model as an example (Backup: frames written on the board.)
👦 Students do: fill the frames into real sentences about their section, swap and tick. Expected: “It's a small island. It's very beautiful.” Typical error: “It very beautiful” — the frame shows the missing 's; I circle it silently on the strip.
💪 Support: give the weakest writers a half-done frame (first word filled). ⭐ Stretch: add one negative — “It isn't cold, but it's…”.		frame strips · SB p.23

WHAT WE DO: 3-min rehearsal, ~2-min presentations; audience judges on a friendly 3-criterion rubric + the 2 book questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 3-min rehearsal: Everyone says three-plus sentences. Toes to the audience. A teammate holds the poster.
  2. I hand the audience a 3-criterion 🐣 rubric card (criterion-referenced, mirrors the book's Final reflection — friendly wording, ticks not scores):
    ① Task — Is the poster clear, with info about the country? 🐣 / 🐣🐣 / 🐣🐣🐣
    ② Imagination — Are the ideas original and fun? 🐣 / 🐣🐣 / 🐣🐣🐣
    ③ Language — Did they use be and unit words? 🐣 / 🐣🐣 / 🐣🐣🐣
  3. Trios present in turn (~2 min each). After each, the room answers the book's ex.10 questions aloud: ① How is the new country different from ours? ② Do you want to visit it — why?
  4. I keep my own copy of the rubric for feedback; every trio hears at least one specific praise + one “next time” tip.
📺 On screen: posters at the front · the rubric card for each audience member · applause protocol (Backup: rubric drawn on the board; audience shows 🐣-fingers 1/2/3.)
👦 Students do: rehearse, present their own sections, then judge the next trio on the 3 criteria and answer the 2 questions. Expected: everyone says 3+ sentences. Typical error: mumbling into the poster — the “toes to the room” gesture, teammate holds the poster.
⭐ Stretch: a presenter takes one unscripted question from the audience.		posters · rubric cards · SB p.23

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. 📺 On screen: mini-boards · my 2 anonymous poster errors · homework slide (Backup: homework written on the board to copy into diaries.)

two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to English A–L (countries and nationalities, be in all three forms, place adjectives, the keypal message), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 written out in English from memory. Both parts are used — Match It in class, Translate It as the written self-test.
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the unit's exit line.

Workbook U1 (pp.10–11) · notebook
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can design and present a poster about an invented country, speaking 3+ scaffolded be-sentences each and using Unit 1 vocabulary, then revise the whole unit confidently before the test.
  • Subsidiary. Speaking/negotiation with the Creativity Key phrases; writing 2–3 be-sentences from a frame bank; peer review on a 3-criterion rubric; retrieval + interleaved revision of countries/nationalities, place adjectives and be (all forms).
  • Personal. The unit's celebration, not an exam rehearsal. FOUNDATION scaffolding throughout: frame strips carry the grammar, roles guarantee everyone speaks, the rubric is 🐣-friendly, revision is a calm Showdown — pressure stays low.
By the end, students can…
  • negotiate a group choice with Key phrases (I like that idea / What about…?)
  • write 2–3 be-sentences describing a country from a frame
  • present their own poster sections to the class (3+ sentences)
  • judge a poster on Task · Imagination · Language
  • recall & correct Unit 1 be, countries and place adjectives

Target language
Lexis
countries + nationality adjectivescapital · currency · language · population · flag · continentplace adjectives: beautiful · busy · quiet · modern · boring · amazingKey phrases: What's a good name? · I like that idea. · What about…? · That's a good idea! · No, that's not very original.
Structure
be (all forms): It's a small island. · It isn't cold, but it's hot. · The capital is Beachtown. · Our flag is dark blue and yellow. · Is it beautiful? — Yes, it is.
Form
am/is/are (+); isn't/aren't (−); Is/Are…? + short answers (Yes, it is / No, it isn't); adjective after be, never plural (two beautiful cities ✓, not “beautifuls”).
Pron. watch
contraction it's /ɪts/ vs its; rising Is it…?↗ in the audience questions; sentence stress on the new adjective.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“It very beautiful.” → What's missing? → It 's very beautiful (be after It).
“Yes, it's.” as a short answer → No: the full form — Yes, it is.
“We decided in Russian — does it count?” → No: the deal is real only in English (Key phrases).

Assumptions: countries/nationalities, place adjectives and be (aff/neg/questions) all taught across P1–P5; the keypal writing (P5) modelled sentence-building. A scaffolded speaking format is familiar (P4 “Meeting new people”). New today = the project genre (poster + presentation) and working in a trio with roles.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». Never shout over noise.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; “radio whisper” during rehearsal.
«Boards up!» — Showdown rounds: everyone commits, all boards rise together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-project.
English-first — Russian ideas are welcome; POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
