🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Say what a thing is made of and whose it is — ten materials used as adjectives, the apostrophe, and the difference between my and mine.
What is genuinely hard
The apostrophe is a single mark that carries the whole meaning and cannot be heard at all: my brother's dog and my brothers' dog sound identical. And Russian has no mine / yours / theirs at all — it has one word for both jobs, so the two English sets get mixed.
Typical error to expect
My friends name is Ben — the missing apostrophe, and my parent's car for two parents. In 2026 the apostrophe counts as PUNCTUATION in the writing paper, not as grammar [exam report]
This is mine book / The book is my — determiner and pronoun swapped [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The grammar is discovered from two pictures rather than from a rule, because one dog and two brothers settle in three seconds what a paragraph cannot. Vocabulary goes first and is spent immediately: the ten materials are met as nouns and used as adjectives within the same stage, because the useful unit is a wooden box, not wood. The listening sits between the vocabulary and the grammar and does double duty — it recycles the materials in somebody else's mouth and it is where the two grammar example sentences come from, so the grammar arrives already heard. Pronunciation is deliberately late, at 66′: a friend of mine is only worth drilling once the phrase means something, and clapping on the strong words is also the stirrer this point in the lesson needs. The productive stage uses real objects belonging to real relatives, because possession is the one grammar point that is impossible to practise honestly on invented material.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One mark, and the whole meaning changes. My brother's dog — one brother. My brothers' dog — two. You cannot hear the difference, you can only write it. Today: what your things are made of, and whose they really are.”

Why: Ten materials on ten photographs, then straight into using them as adjectives — because the useful thing is not the word but the phrase a wooden box.

WHAT WE DO: ten materials on ten photographs, then straight into using them as adjectives (ex.1–5 p.36, track 42)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start with objects, not with the book (TB p.69): put five things on your desk, give them one minute to look, cover them and see how many objects AND materials they can name.
  2. ex.1 — match the ten materials to photos A–J, in pairs, 3 minutes. Play track 42 to check and repeat. Key: A plastic · B cotton · C gold · D wool · E metal · F leather · G glass · H wood · I paper · J silver
  3. ex.2 — the construction goes on the board: … is / are made of … Pairs, 3 minutes. Key: A The headphones are made of plastic. · B The T-shirts are made of cotton. · C The ring is made of gold. · D The gloves are made of wool. · E The coin is made of metal. · F The jacket is made of leather. · G The bowl is made of glass. · H The box is made of wood. · I The cat is made of paper. · J The necklace is made of silver.
  4. ex.3–4, and this is the point of the whole stage: TB p.69: all of these words can be both a noun and an adjective — except wood, which becomes wooden. That single exception is the whole point of ex.3 and ex.4. Keys: wood is a noun; wooden is an adjective. And the pair worth adding (TB p.69): gold is the material, golden is the colour or something associated with gold. || A They're plastic headphones. · B They're cotton T-shirts. · C It's a gold ring. · D They're wool gloves. · E It's a metal coin. · F It's a leather jacket. · G It's a glass bowl. · H It's a wooden box. · I It's a paper cat. · J It's a silver necklace.
  5. ex.5 as a game (TB p.69): look round the room carefully, close your eyes, and describe what is in it with your eyes still shut — materials only. Then open and check.
  6. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with material cards, 2 minutes.
📺 On screen: track 42 in the whiteboard app with the ten photographs on the screen (Backup if the board is down: read the ten materials aloud twice and use the photographs in the paper book; five real objects on your desk cover the rest of the stage)
👦 Students do: match, then build phrases, then describe the room blind. Expect the eyes-shut game to be the moment the words stick. Typical error: glassen bowl — the exception is wooden and only wooden

Grammar · discovery · possession
pairs → class
Why: The apostrophe is one mark that carries the entire meaning, and it is invisible in speech. The pictures do what an explanation cannot: one dog, two brothers.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–5 p.37: two pictures, one apostrophe, and the table of pronouns

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 — the two pictures, and the one question that teaches it (TB p.70): Is there one boy or more than one? Match the sentences. Key: 1 B · 2 A
  2. ex.2 — now they say the rule, you do not: Sentence 1 has 's because there is one person. Sentence 2 has s' because there is more than one person. Send one student to the board to write both forms.
  3. Mixed ability (TB p.70): TB p.70: some students cannot see the difference between 's and s' because they sound identical — give them two different-coloured pens and have them mark the two forms in different colours.
  4. ex.3 — four sentences, 3 minutes. Key: 1 sisters' · 2 brother's · 3 children's · 4 dogs'
  5. ex.4, and here the second half of the grammar starts. Take a pen from a student and ask Is this my pen or yours? Elicit It's not your pen, it's mine. Write it up and name the two jobs: determiners go BEFORE a noun, pronouns stand INSTEAD of one. Key: 1 They're not mine (pronoun), they're my sister's (determiner). · 2 Oh and what are theirs (pronoun)? Your dad's, for example (determiner).
  6. ex.5 — complete the table, 3 minutes. Key: Pronouns: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs
  7. Close the eyes game from TB p.70: describe something on somebody's desk and ask whose it is — I can see a blue jacket. Is it Silvia's?
📺 On screen: the two pictures and the determiner/pronoun table on the screen, filled in live (Backup: draw the two-column table in chalk and use two real students at the front instead of the pictures — one boy, two boys — it works better than the screen)
👦 Students do: match, state the rule, fill the table. Expect the apostrophe rule to be stated correctly and then forgotten in ex.8. Typical error: childrens' — an irregular plural takes 's like a singular

Pronunciation · weak forms
class → pairs
Why: a and of are the two shortest words in English and the two that make speech sound English. Clapping on the strong words teaches the rhythm without a single rule.

WHAT WE DO: ex.9 p.37 (track 44): how a and of almost disappear

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. SB p.37 ex.9 (track 44): four sentences in which a and of are said weakly — /ə/ and /əv/. They are shorter and quieter than everything around them.
  2. The four lines: She's a friend of mine. · He's a friend of hers. · They're friends of ours. · Is he a friend of yours?
  3. TB p.70: play it, then have students repeat CLAPPING on the strong words only — the weak ones fall into the gaps by themselves. Then they invent three sentences with the same rhythm: Mike's a friend of ours. · That's a dream of mine. · The girls are fans of theirs.
  4. Two minutes in pairs: say your three sentences to each other with the clapping, then without it.
📺 On screen: track 44 in the whiteboard app with the four sentences on the screen (Backup: say the four sentences yourself, twice — once slowly and wrongly, with a and of stressed, once properly — and let the class hear which one sounds English)
👦 Students do: repeat with clapping, then invent three sentences of their own. Expect the rhythm to arrive through the hands rather than through the ears. Typical error: they stress of because it is a whole word — the clapping fixes it without explanation

WHAT WE DO: ex.10 p.37: three things that belong to different people in your family

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the frame with the five questions from the page: What are they? What are they made of? Who do they belong to? Where did they come from? Why are they special?
  2. Model it yourself first with one real object, as TB p.70 suggests: A box of paints. Is it your daughter's? No, it's my wife's. She likes painting.

WB Unit 6 (p.24–27)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can say what an object is made of using the material as an adjective, and say whose it is with 's, s' and the possessive pronouns.
  • Subsidiary. Listening for detail and completing a three-column table (track 43); the weak forms of a and of (track 44); speaking about three objects in their own family.
  • Personal. Do not explain the apostrophe — ask «one boy or more than one?» about the two pictures and let them say it. And keep the determiner/pronoun table on the board until the end of the lesson.
By the end, students can…
  • name ten materials and use them as adjectives (a wooden box)
  • write 's or s' correctly for one owner and for several
  • choose between my and mine, their and theirs
  • say a friend of mine with the right rhythm

Structure
The box is made of wood. It's a wooden box. · It's my brother's dog. · They're not mine, they're my sister's. · He's a friend of theirs.
Form
's = one owner · s' = more than one · irregular plurals take 's (children's) · determiners my/your/his/her/our/their before a noun · pronouns mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs instead of one.
Pron. watch
the weak forms /ə/ and /əv/ in a friend of mine — both words almost disappear; jewellery of the unit: silver /ˈsɪlvə/, leather /ˈleðə/ (with the voiced th), wool /wʊl/ — short, not /wuːl/.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«It's my brothers' dog» — how many brothers? → more than one. The apostrophe after the s is doing all the work.
«This is mine book» — what's wrong? → mine stands instead of a noun; before a noun you need my. This is my book / This book is mine.
«a friend of my» — correct it. → a friend of mine. The of-construction always takes the pronoun.

Assumptions: SB p.36–37, syllabus lesson 18. The possessive 's is known from earlier years in its simplest form; the plural s' and the pronoun set are new. The materials are almost all new as English words, though the objects are everyday. Students were asked last lesson to bring one small object they own — if they have forgotten, the classroom is full of objects and the lesson does not depend on it.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
