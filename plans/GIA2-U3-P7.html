🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The coursebook's WDYT project is turned into a museum: a crew makes a mini-book about a traditional costume as an exhibit, and the class decides what goes in the main window. The unit's super skill — creativity — works as a hard rule: THREE layout ideas BEFORE you choose, or the exhibit is not accepted. The Quick review is sewn into the middle of the pair as “the stock-take before opening”: revising the unit = getting ready for the P8 test, but under the museum's flag.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“The Costume Museum opens tonight. Each crew builds ONE exhibit: a mini-book about a traditional costume — a region, the clothes for men and for women, and when people wear them. Museum rule: THREE layout ideas before you choose one. 'It's fine' is not a decision. And before the doors open — inventory: four checks of everything this unit taught you. Curators — to work!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We are opening a costume museum: every crew makes a mini-book exhibit about traditional clothes; the museum rule is three layout ideas before you choose; before the opening there is a stock-take (unit revision at stations).

WHAT WE DO: the video about making a mini-book (ex.1) → working through the model page (ex.2) → Key phrases + three layouts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1, the video: What do you need to make a mini-book? Key: coloured card, paper, coloured pens and pencils, scissors, glue, a ruler, a rubber, photos, a stapler.
  2. ex.2, the model page (Bolivia): Which of these things can you see? Key: EVERYTHING is there except the information about the history of the region.

WHAT WE DO: choosing a region/country + RoundTable notes on the three questions + finding or drawing an image

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Choose a region — of our country or any other. Then three questions: Where is it? What do men and women wear? When do they wear it?
  2. Notes through Kagan RoundTable: one planner sheet per crew goes round the circle, everyone writes in their fact and passes it on — the sheet never stays with anyone longer than thirty seconds. Two circles, and the planner is filled by the whole crew, not by one “person who writes”.
  3. I suggest options if they stall: Buryatia · Tatarstan · the Russian North · Scotland · India · Japan · Mexico · Peru.
  4. The image: a photo from the prepared folder OR a drawing — both are worth the same points.
  5. The rule for notes: Notes, not sentences. Sentences come in CREATE.
📺 On screen: the planner sheet (Project planner SB p.119) · a folder of printed photos (backup — they draw them themselves)
👦 Students do: they collect the facts and the picture. Expected: an argument about choosing the region — I hold it to a 2-minute limit

Stock-take · Quick review at stations
stations

WHAT WE DO: 4 stations of 4.5 minutes: vocabulary · Present continuous · Present simple vs continuous · because/so and the Key phrases

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before a museum opens, everything gets counted. Four inspections, four and a half minutes each.
  2. Station 1 — vocabulary: 21 items + 14 adjectives, a blitz and the opposites (Quick review p.45).
  3. Station 2 — Present continuous: the tables, -ing spelling, questions and short answers.
  4. Station 3 — Present simple vs continuous: time expressions into two trays + the stative traps.
  5. Station 4 — because/so and the shop phrases: 6 sentences + a mini-dialogue.
  6. Each station finishes with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: the crew checks the station's answers and makes sure anyone could explain them; I call a number and that person answers. If they can't — the station is not closed and the crew goes back for a minute.
  7. A personal gap list: Write down what YOU missed. That's your test-prep list — not everything, just yours.
📺 On screen: SB p.45 Quick review · station cards · timer (backup — the stations run row by row and I pass the cards round myself)
👦 Students do: they go through the stations and log their gaps honestly. Expected: the gap lists differ — that is the whole point. Error: they copy from a neighbour — I remind them the list is for them, not for me

two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to English A–L (clothes, present continuous, simple against continuous, buying an item, because and so), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the unit's exit line.

WHAT WE DO: 90-second presentations + peer review ex.8 + a secret vote on “what goes in the main window”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Doors open! Ninety seconds per exhibit: the region, the clothes, when people wear them, and the photo caption.
  2. Peer review from ex.8 p.47 through Kagan RallyRobin: pairs swap books and give one observation per turn, in turn. What NEW thing did you learn? Does the mini-book have the region AND the clothes? Is it attractive — why?
  3. The vote: everyone gets 2 stickers, you may not vote for your own exhibit. The winner goes into the main window (the centre of the unit wall).
  4. Final reflection p.47 in crews: the task · the super skill · the language — with examples.

👦 Students do: the whole crew presents and they look honestly at other people's work. Expected: the peer review exposes that “pretty” and “clear” are not the same thing

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can research and present a traditional costume in a mini-book, using present simple for facts and present continuous for the photo captions; the unit language is revised at four stations for the P8 test.
  • Subsidiary. Super skill: creativity (three ideas before the choice, the Key phrases for discussing); working through the model page (Bolivia) and the video about making a mini-book; Quick review p.45 at stations; Final reflection p.47.
  • Personal. The project is the moment of glory for the ones who are weaker at the language: roles are matched to strengths (layout designer · researcher · writer · voice). I never call the Quick review test preparation — it is “the stock-take”.
By the end, students can…
  • propose three layout ideas before deciding
  • use project Key phrases to agree and disagree in a crew
  • write facts in present simple and photo captions in present continuous
  • pass all four Quick-review stations of Unit 3

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“It's fine, let's take this one” — is that a decision? → no: three options first, then the choice.
Which tense is the project model written in? → the facts in the present simple, and the photo caption (“men are playing instruments”) in the present continuous.
Are the stations a test? → no, they are a stock-take: find your own gaps today, not in P8.

Assumptions: SB pp.45–47 (Quick review + Project + Model project), KTP pair 27. All the unit material has been covered; what is new is the research and defending the layout.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The project eats the revisionA hard frame: the stations take 18 minutes IN THE MIDDLE of the pair, before CREATE — they are “the museum stock-take”.
The mini-book = pictures with no textThe exhibit checklist from ex.2 p.46: a title · the region · a description of the clothes · when they are worn · an attractive picture. Five ticks — otherwise it is not accepted into the window.
Passengers in the crewRoles: researcher · layout designer · writer · voice; at the presentation a silent block sinks the exhibit.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
