🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A live feed frightens a new student with its layout: timestamps, reverse order, a lot of short chunks. So the way in has three steps: the words first (6 of them, with pictures), then “what kind of text is this at all” (a safe question — you can reason rather than read), and only then the reading — in chunks, one per crew. The product is modest and reachable: one post of your own for the feed, on a frame.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at the photos. A green man. A man with orange hair and blue tights. Hundreds of people. This is Comic Con — a festival where fans dress up as their heroes. And here is a strange text about it: it has TIMES, and it reads from the BOTTOM. Today we learn to read it — and at the end, each of you writes ONE post for our own feed. Four lines, with a frame. Ready?”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Comic Con is a festival where fans dress up as their heroes. The text about it is unusual: it has times and it reads from the bottom up. We learn to read it, and at the end everyone writes their own post on a frame.

WHAT WE DO: subskill: working out the text type from the layout, BEFORE reading (ex.2–3)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The subskill rule goes on the board: Look at the LAYOUT, the title, the pictures. Are there times? days? prices? comments?
  2. Practice on three examples from the slide: a menu · a chat message · a feed with timestamps. What type? And WHAT shows it?
  3. Now the book. ex.2: What event is this? What happens there? Who goes? Key: a festival for comic fans · they dress up as heroes, play video games, watch the artists · fans, young people, people who love comics and films.
  4. ex.3: What type of text? Where do you START reading? Key: a live feed (a feed, tweets) · people read it on a computer and on a phone (b and c) · start at the bottom. We draw the bottom-to-top arrow in pencil in the book.
📺 On screen: the slide with the three sample texts · SB p.38 ex.2–3 (Backup: the same written on the board before the lesson)
👦 Students do: they reason about the text type without reading it — you can hardly get it wrong, so there is no fear. Expect: the bottom-to-top arrow clears up the main confusion

WHAT WE DO: the text in 3 chunks by crew → 2 facts per chunk → we build the feed together on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Split through Kagan Jigsaw: crew 1 — the 11:25 and 12:01 posts · crew 2 — 12:15 and 12:32 · crew 3 — 1:54, 2:47 and 3:31. I work through one post on the board myself — there is the model for how to find two facts — and only then do the crews take their chunks.
  2. Your crew owns these posts. Find TWO facts. You will teach the class. Word bank and glossary — open. Only your crew knows your chunk: without you the class cannot put the whole day together.
  3. Assembly: the crews report in time order and I build the timeline of the day on the board (11:25 → 3:31).
  4. Track 20 (ex.4) against the timeline: Match each message with a photo. Key: A 3:31 · B 2:47 · C 12:15 · D 12:32 · E 11:25 · F 1:54 · G 12:01.
📺 On screen: SB pp.38–39 (the text in 3 chunks) · the timeline board · audio track 20 (backup — I read the posts out loud in time order)
👦 Students do: they own their chunk and listen to the others. Expect: the assembly gives the whole picture without the fear of the length. Error: retelling in Russian — I accept it but ask for one sentence in English

WHAT WE DO: written, on the frame: one post for our shared feed (4 lines) + read it to your partner

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. We choose the event as a class: break time at school · a match · a concert · the first snow.
  2. The frame on screen: [time] I'm at … . People are … . Someone is wearing … . It's … ! The verb bank next to it: coming, playing, buying, wearing, waiting, talking.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can identify a text type before reading, read a live feed with support, answer True/False with the proof line, and write one live-feed post of their own using a frame.
  • Subsidiary. The unit subskill: identifying the text type; Word work (6 words with pictures); ex.7 (the words have been swapped round); Critical thinking in a simplified form.
  • Personal. I cut the text into 3 chunks — one per crew, then we put it back together. All of it at once is frightening. Vocabulary BEFORE the reading, not during it.
By the end, students can…
  • say what type a text is and what shows it
  • read a short part of a live feed and retell two facts
  • find the proof line for a True/False answer
  • write one live-feed post with a frame

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
hero — is that the hero of a book? → here: someone you admire, an idol.
costume — is that a two-piece suit? → no: an outfit that makes you look like another character.
Why is a feed read from the bottom? → because a new post goes on top, so the very first one is at the bottom.
“True, I think so” — does that count? → you need the line from the text.

Assumptions: SB pp.38–39, KTP pair 22. They read more slowly than “ours” — the text in chunks, tasks with frames, and I say the keys out loud.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The length and the layout frighten themThe text in 3 chunks, one per crew; each crew owns its own and tells the others about it. We draw the bottom-to-top arrow in pencil in the book itself.
New words stall the readingWord work BEFORE the text, with pictures; a mini-glossary in the margin of the notebook.
“I don't know what to write” in the productThe post frame + a verb bank on the slide (is/are + coming, playing, buying, wearing, waiting).
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
