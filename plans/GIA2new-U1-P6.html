🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Two real things in one pair: the living phrases of teamwork (they can be carried into any lesson and into life) and the first big piece of written work. For newcomers the blog post is built on a strict staircase: 9 questions → notes → 3 paragraphs on Altin's skeleton → checklist. Capital letters are a rule with a bodily anchor (stand up / sit down) right from the stirrer.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today you learn the phrases that real teams use: What have you got for number two? Are you sure? Yes, me too! First — a quiz and a team check, like a real classroom crew. Then — your first blog post: three paragraphs about YOUR school, on the class wall. Don't worry: we build it step by step, and Altin's post shows the way.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The phrases of a real team check + the first blog post about your own school, for the classroom wall; we build it step by step, with Altin's model leading the way.

Quiz + team check
solo → pairs

WHAT WE DO: THINK: the quiz done individually (guessing is allowed!) → checking with the Key phrases ONLY → a dialogue from the model; the frame is Think-Pair-Share, and Talking Chips holds the phrases

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: Five minutes, your answers. Guessing is fine — checking is the point!
  2. Kagan Think-Pair-Share: first your own sheet in silence (THINK) — nobody peeks, then the check in pairs (PAIR) and only after that on air for the class (SHARE). Everyone arrives in the pair with their own answer — which means there is something to talk about.
  3. The check: Only Key phrases. What have you got for number one?
  4. I act out the check of one item with a helper as a model, then Kagan Talking Chips: 4 chips per person, and a chip goes down on the desk for every Key phrase spoken; when the chips run out, you listen. The quiet one has to speak, the loud one has to stop.
  5. Pairs build a dialogue on the ex.4 model using their own answers; the phrase cards are on the desks.
📺 On screen: SB p.19 quiz · phrase cards
👦 Students do: they check each other for real. Expect: the guessed answers make the checking fun

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Are you sure?» — is that an argument? → no, it is a normal check inside a team.
«i'm from norilsk» — how many corrections? → two: I'm and Norilsk.
How many sentences in a paragraph? → 2–4, all about one thing.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They are shy about acting out the dialogueFirst chorally behind the screen (shadowing the video), then in pairs at the desk, and only volunteers go to the board.
«I don't know what to write» about the postThe 9 questions on p.20 give them all the material; notes before sentences.
Capitals done «by eye»A 6-point checklist + the «stand up / sit down» stirrer gives a bodily anchor.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
