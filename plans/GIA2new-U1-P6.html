🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Speaking and writing sit in one pair because they are the same unit content in two channels. For newcomers the risk is the freeze — so both halves run on a heavy→light staircase: for speaking, full script → frames on cards → own words with the cards face-down; for writing, Altin's model → sentence frames per paragraph → own words → frames removed for the confident. Capital letters are not a lecture but a rule with a body anchor (stand up / sit down) and a 7-point checklist. Everything is written and rehearsed in class, with support — at home they only copy the clean version out.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today two real skills. First, the phrases a real team uses to check answers — What have you got for number two? Are you sure? Yes, me too! We watch Carmel and Lukas, then you check a quiz the same way. Second, your first blog post: three short paragraphs about YOUR school, and they go up on our wall for the whole class to read. Don't worry about a blank page — Altin's post shows the way and you build it step by step.”
Если классу нужна опора, короче: фразы командной проверки ответов + первый блог-пост про свою школу на стену; строим по шагам, Altin ведёт за руку.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–4 p.19: video → tick the phrases → shadow chorally → fill the gaps (the FULL SCRIPT is in their hand)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 lead-in: Four things students do in class — playing a game, doing an exam, checking answers, asking about homework. In which do you work TOGETHER?

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 Skills boost — the quiz solo (THINK), then check it as a team, fading the support step by step

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK (solo). Five minutes, the geography quiz, your own answers. Guessing is fine — the CHECK is the real point. (Quiz: 1 city not in Canada — Montreal/Seattle/Toronto? · 2 Antarctic — north or south? · 3 next planet after Earth? · 4 capital of Turkey — Ankara or Istanbul? · 5 which continent is Mount Kilimanjaro in?)
  2. 💪 HEAVY. Read the ex.4 model dialogue in pairs in roles, script in hand — the words are given, they only voice them.
  3. MEDIUM. Now check the quiz for real, but the phrases come off the notebook card, not the full script. Kagan Talking Chips: 4 chips each, a chip goes down for every Key phrase you use; when your chips are gone you listen. The quiet one has to speak, the loud one has to stop.
  4. ⭐ LIGHT. Turn the cards face-down. Re-check two answers from memory — the frames are in your head now, not on the desk. Compulsory: disagree at least once (Are you sure? I've got…) and ask again.
  5. ICQ before you start: Is “Are you sure?” an argument? (→ no, it is a normal team check).
Support fade:full script→frames on card→card face-down

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–6 p.20 — take Altin's post apart into a 3-paragraph skeleton and pull out the capital-letter rules

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name the genre out loud: This is a blog post — a short, friendly description of your school for readers in other countries. Not a story: each paragraph is ONE topic.
  2. ex.1–2: read Altin's post (Tirana, Albania). Does he answer all nine questions? Find the answers to questions 1–9 inside the text.
  3. ex.3 the skeleton — match the three headings, then write them on the board as the frame everyone will reuse: ¶1 the school (place · size · students · teachers · uniform) · ¶2 subjects (favourites + what else + computers) · ¶3 the school day & free time (start/finish · lunch · after school · sports).
  4. ex.4 quick personal hook: Is Altin's school similar to yours or different? Why? (one sentence each — feeds their own post).
  5. Subskill — capital letters. ex.5 the subjects line (art · English · French · geography · ICT · maths · PE): Which get a capital? Why? Two rules fall out — languages get a capital (English, French); ordinary subjects don't (art, maths, geography), but abbreviations do (ICT, PE).
  6. ex.6 build the checklist. TB key — capital for: a “I” · b start of a sentence · d days of the week · f months · g names & surnames · h people's titles · i cities, countries & nationalities. NOT c “all nouns”. Put the 7-point checklist on the board — it stays up for the whole writing block.
  7. We Do. I take two capitals from Altin apart out loud against the checklist (the model). Then Kagan RallyCoach: on a mini de-capitalised sentence, A names each capital + the reason from the list, B is the coach — confirms (Yes — day of the week!) or points back to the list; swap for the next sentence. Both always working, neither goes quiet.
📺 On screen: SB p.20 · the 3-paragraph skeleton · the 7-point capitals checklist (Backup: print the checklist + one de-capitalised sentence per pair for RallyCoach)
👦 Students do: derive the paragraph skeleton and the capital rules from the model, then coach each other. Expect: nationalities/languages (English, Albanian) are the classic slip — the checklist catches them

WHAT WE DO: the staircase — 9 questions → NOTES → 3 paragraphs on sentence frames → own words → capitals patrol

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1 — NOTES (not sentences). Answer the nine questions from the book — key words only, no full sentences yet. Word bank on the screen (subjects · numbers · times · sports).
  2. Step 2 — frames (medium support). Write three paragraphs on Altin's skeleton, using the sentence frames on the slide: ¶1 “Our school is in… It's a (big/small) school with about … students. We('ve got / haven't got) a uniform.” · ¶2 “My favourite subjects are… We also study… I (love/don't) use computers…” · ¶3 “We start school at… and finish at… I have lunch… After school, I…”
  3. ⭐ Frames away (stretch). Confident writers cover the frames and write in their own words; add one extra sentence Altin didn't have. 💪 Support: weaker writers keep the frames and just swap in their own facts — that already counts as a full post.
  4. Step 3 — the capitals patrol. Self-check against the 7-point checklist first, then swap notebooks: the partner ticks the CHECK list and circles any missed capital in pencil (does not correct it — the writer fixes it).
  5. Clean posts go up on the unit wall.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can check answers in pairs using the Key phrases (ask · answer · agree · disagree · “I don't know”) and write a three-paragraph blog post about their own school following Altin's model, with capital letters checked against a 7-point list.

Structure
Real-world grammar: I think it's… / Do you think it's…? · blog frames: Our school is in… · We('ve got / haven't got) a uniform. · My favourite subjects are… · We start school at… and finish at…
Form
Capital letters — 7 positions: I · start of sentence · days · months · names & surnames · titles · cities/countries/nationalities (+ abbreviations ICT, PE). NOT every noun. · a blog post = 3 paragraphs, one topic each.
Pron. watch
checking-back intonation rises: Are you SURE? ↗ · agreement falls: Yes, me too. ↘
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Are you sure?” — is that an argument? → no, it is a normal check inside a team.
“i'm from norilsk” — how many corrections? → two: I'm and Norilsk.
“I love maths and English” — which word gets a capital and which doesn't? → English (a language), not maths (an ordinary subject).
How many sentences in a paragraph? → 2–4, all about one topic.

Assumptions: SB pp.19–21, WB pp.10–11, KTP pair 10. Present simple (aff/neg, Yes/No, Wh-) and school-subject vocabulary are secure from P1–P5. The geography quiz needs no prior knowledge — guessing is allowed, the checking is the target. New here: the Key phrases and the capital-letter rules. Note: this is NOT the review pair — Match It / Translate It come in P7.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — everyone commits together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
