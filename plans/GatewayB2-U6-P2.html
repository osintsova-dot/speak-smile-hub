🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Choose and produce the zero, first and second conditional according to what the situation actually is, use unless, as long as, provided/providing (that) and in case with their real meanings, and use seven idioms of health and illness as fixed strings.
What is genuinely hard
The forms are known and the choice is not. A B2 group can recite «if + past simple, would + infinitive» and still write «If it will rain» three lines later, because Russian puts the future in both halves. The connectors are worse: in case is not if, and «в случае если» maps onto if so cleanly that the mistake is never heard. And unless is a double negative machine — «unless you don't hurry» is the sentence that will appear.
Typical error to expect
«If it will rain, we will stay at home» — the single most persistent conditional error in Russian classrooms. Russian puts the future in both halves, English forbids it after if: the if-clause takes the present simple and lets the main clause carry will. It is one rule, it is tested every year, and it survives in students who can name all four conditionals. «In case you are late, call me» meaning «if you are late» — in case is not if. It means «because this might happen, prepare now»: Take an umbrella in case it rains. Russian «в случае если» maps onto if, so the whole group imports the wrong meaning and the sentence still sounds grammatical, which is why it is never self-corrected.
Why the lesson is built this way
Guided discovery, not a rule on a slide: the three conditionals are already on the page in sentences the class met yesterday (boiling water, surviving an apocalypse), so ex.1a–1c asks them to name what they are already reading. Practice climbs — recognise, complete, correct, question — and the questions in ex.4 are personal, so the free stage is not an extra activity but the same exercise turned outwards. The connectors get their own meaning test (if / only if / because maybe) before any gap-fill. The idioms then arrive inside one text about a racing driver who fractured a knee, blacked out at 380 kph and pulled through — which is exactly the context that makes them memorable.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Matthew Anderson has crashed a car at 380 kilometres an hour, blacked out, fractured a knee and pulled through — twice. He says he feels on top of the world. Four of those words are idioms and you will own them by the end of the lesson. Before that: the difference between «if», «unless» and «in case», which is worth more marks than anything else on this page.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.1a–1c: the three conditionals named from sentences the class already understands

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1a as a whole class, three minutes: three sentences from yesterday's text — boiling water, surviving an apocalypse, looking after this world. Which conditional is each one? Answers: 1 zero, 2 second, 3 first.
  2. ex.1b, two minutes in pairs: match the three rules to the three types. Answers: a second, b first, c zero. Insist on the WORDS in the rules — «generally true», «possible», «imaginary» — because those three words are the whole decision.
  3. ex.1c on the board, four minutes. Write the three skeletons with gaps and elicit the forms: zero — if + present simple, present simple · first — if + present simple, will + infinitive · second — if + past simple, would + infinitive.
  4. Two Language notes from the Teacher's Book, said once and written up: the two halves can swap places, and the comma only appears when the if-clause comes first; and were is used instead of was in the second conditional — If I were you, I would buy a new jacket.
  5. Then ask each student to write ONE more ending for each of the three sentences in ex.1a. Two minutes. Take three aloud.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that recycles ex.5 and the three conditionals at speed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: the class mills, you clap, everybody pairs with the nearest person.
  2. You call one of the questions from ex.4 — Which country would you live in if you were free to choose anywhere? — and both partners answer it in a full conditional sentence. Fifteen seconds each.
  3. Clap, mix, new partner, next question. Five rounds, five partners, ten answers per student, nobody sitting.
  4. Last round is a chain instead: you start If I get home early tonight … and five students in a row continue, each one using the previous student's main clause as the new if-clause.
  5. Freeze and sit: Now the four words that can replace «if» — and one of them does not mean «if» at all.
📺 On screen: the questions are called out, not shown — nothing to prepare and nothing to fail
👦 Students do: answer five conditional questions with five different partners, then build a five-link chain standing

WHAT WE DO: ex.6–7: the meanings decided first, the gap-fill second

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.6, four minutes: four sentences from the page matched to three meanings. Answers: a = unless (if … not, except if) · b = provided/providing (that) and as long as (if, only if) · c = in case (because maybe).
  2. Then the finger test, ninety seconds, and every student answers every time: you read a sentence, they show one finger for «if», two for «only if», three for «because maybe». Ten sentences at speed.
  3. Make the in case point unforgettable with two sentences on the board: Take an umbrella IF it rains — you are standing in the rain deciding. Take an umbrella IN CASE it rains — the sky is clear and you take it anyway. Ask which one is sensible.
  4. ex.7, four minutes, individually then checked in pairs: eight alternatives. Answers: 1 as long as · 2 unless · 3 as long as · 4 unless · 5 in case · 6 providing · 7 in case · 8 as long as. For each one the student says the MEANING, not the word.
  5. Every confirmed connector goes into the fourth column of the wall with one true example written by its owner.
📺 On screen: the two umbrella sentences stay on the board for the rest of the lesson — chalk only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: match four connectors to three meanings, answer ten meaning questions with their fingers, then choose eight alternatives

WHAT WE DO: ex.8 with the Exam tip: eight second sentences, two to five words, the given word unchanged

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the Exam tip on p.73 WITH them, not to them: do not add or change words that are not necessary, and do not change the meaning of the original.
  2. Put the three rules of the format on the board and leave them: Two to five words · the given word never changes · a contraction counts as two words.
  3. Model item 1 aloud, thinking out loud so they hear the process: what does the original mean, which structure carries that meaning, does the given word usually take a partner. Answer: «if you do not take».
  4. ex.8, six minutes, individually, clock visible. The rule of the day: count the words on your fingers before you write them.
  5. Check with Kagan Showdown — pairs write each answer, hold it up on the signal, and the class sees every answer at once: 1 if you do not take · 2 as long as you listen · 3 unless you enjoy · 4 provided (that) you speak · 5 if my marks were good · 6 in case this one runs · 7 to class as long as · 8 would complain if I was/were.
📺 On screen: the three format rules written on the board and left there for the rest of the unit — chalk only
👦 Students do: read the Exam tip, work eight transformations alone against the clock, then show every answer simultaneously

WHAT WE DO: Developing vocabulary ex.1–2: the Matthew Anderson text, then good health or bad

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame the text in one line: An Australian television presenter who also drives racing cars. He has crashed at three hundred and eighty kilometres an hour — and he is fine.
  2. ex.1, four minutes, individually: seven gaps in the text with the words about, back, out, picture, pull, shape, world. Weaker students get the Teacher's Book support written up as pairs of options — a shape/picture, b about/out, c back/pull, d about/out, e back/out, f picture/shape, g world/shape.
  3. Check and, for each one, elicit the meaning rather than giving it: the picture of health — look very healthy · up and about — active again after being ill · pull through — survive something serious · black out — lose consciousness · back on your feet — recovered · keep in shape — stay fit · on top of the world — feel great.
  4. ex.2, three minutes in pairs: six more idioms sorted into good health and bad health. Answers: good — full of beans; bad — come down with, on your last legs, out of shape, under the weather.

Then the connected-speech drill from the Teacher's Book, ninety seconds: write up◡and◡about, blacked◡out, back◡on◡his feet, keep◡in shape, full◡of◡beans, out◡of shape with the link marks and drill them. Ask them to spot the pattern — a consonant meeting a vowel — and note that of nearly always weakens to /əv/.
  6. Finish with the scored spelling round, books shut: one point per complete idiom, minus one for a missing article or preposition.

Form
zero: if + present simple, present simple · first: if + present simple, will + infinitive · second: if + past simple, would + infinitive · in a QUESTION it is the other verb that inverts, never the one after if · comma only when the if-clause comes first · unless = if … not / except if · as long as, provided/providing (that) = if, only if · in case = because maybe.
Pron. watch
the contraction is the whole difficulty in speech: I'd, you'd, we'd are heard as a single sound and written as two different words · WOULD in a full form is stressed only when you contradict somebody · and the idioms live on connected speech — up◡and◡about, blacked◡out, back◡on◡his feet, full◡of◡beans, out◡of shape, with of reduced to /əv/, which is why students never catch them.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«If you heat water to one hundred, it boils.» Does it happen every time? → yes, always → zero conditional. «If I heated water to one hundred, it would boil.» Same meaning? → yes, and the book accepts both here — that is the exception, not the rule.
«Take an umbrella in case it rains.» Is it raining now? → maybe not. Do I take the umbrella now or later? → NOW, because it might rain later. So in case is not if.
«You won't finish unless you hurry.» Rewrite it with if. → if you do NOT hurry. So how many negatives can unless carry? → one, and it is already inside the word.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«If it will rain …» survives every explanationDo not explain it again. Make it audible: any sentence with will after if gets one silent hand raised by the whole class, and the speaker repairs it themselves. Three catches and the group starts self-monitoring. Add it to the wall as a crossed-out sentence.
in case used to mean ifThe meaning test comes before any gap-fill, and it is physical: you read a sentence, the class shows one finger for «if», two for «only if», three for «because maybe». Ten sentences, thirty seconds, and every student answers every time.
Key-word transformations over the word limitSay the three rules of the format out loud and put them on the board: two to five words, the given word never changes, contractions count as two. Then have them count the words with a finger before writing. The Exam tip on p.73 says the same thing — read it with them, do not paraphrase it.
Idioms half-rememberedThey go on the wall as complete strings with the article and the preposition — the picture of health, full of beans, on your last legs, out of shape, under the weather — and the spelling round at the end of the stage is scored: one point per idiom, minus one for a missing word. Name lesson 6, where the test asks for exactly these seven.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
