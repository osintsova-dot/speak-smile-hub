🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): For new students the mission is simple and «about me»: learn to name the weather and talk about your own four seasons. That gives a safe product (I talk about myself, inside a frame, with a sheet in my hand) and at the same time feeds in the 27 words of the set. The map of extreme weather in the USA on pp.48–49 is not a task, it is a hook: the numbers are clear without translation and start an argument straight away.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at this map. Seattle: clouds for 220 days a year. New Hampshire: fog for 300 days. Death Valley: six centimetres of rain in a WHOLE year. Today two things: we learn to name every kind of weather in English — and each of you writes your own four seasons, six lines. At the end you present with your notes in your hand. This is not a test. Let's start with MY winter…” (and I show the model straight away)
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We look at the map of extreme weather in the USA, learn to name the weather in English and build our own four seasons — six lines. At the end we present with the sheet in our hand.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 p.49, the noun → adjective table + three traps for the poster + track 24 (five sounds)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Page 49. Six words. Which are nouns, which are adjectives? And what letter do we add? TB key: Noun — ice, wind, frost · Adjective — sunny, cloudy, foggy; we add -y.
  2. Three traps for the poster (TB tip): fog → foGGy · sun → suNNy · ice → icy (the e goes away).
  3. Track 24 — five sounds, teams write them on mini-whiteboards. TB key: 1 wet/rain · 2 wind/windy · 3 thunderstorm · 4 snow/frost/freezing · 5 icy/ice, freezing.
  4. ex.5 quickly: How do you say these words in Russian? — we say the pairs out loud so the words settle.

WHAT WE DO: the product: 6 lines about their own four seasons + one number; the speaker holds the sheet

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 27 weather and season words and describe the weather in their own region in 6 lines with numbers.
  • Subsidiary. The three baskets in ex.1 with support; the map words in ex.3 through definitions; the table in ex.4 (noun → adjective) as a rule for the poster; track 24 (five sounds); ex.7, the text about New York and Phoenix; Video skills p.49 with a task set before watching.
  • Personal. I never call on anyone without a draft. Every speaking task comes AFTER written fixing. The word bank stays on screen all lesson. This is the first pair after the holidays — the pace is gentle, but with no empty gaps.
By the end, students can…
  • say and write 27 weather and season words
  • sort them into seasons / temperature / weather
  • build the adjective from the noun (sun → sunny)
  • describe my four seasons in 6 sentences with numbers

Structure
In winter it's cold and there's a lot of snow. · In summer it's warm and sunny. · It rains in August. · We have snow for about six months.
Form
noun → adjective with -y: cloud → cloudy, wind → windy; in fog and sun the consonant doubles (foggy, sunny); in ice the -e drops (icy).
Pron. watch
from the Wordlist: autumn /ˈɔːtəm/ (the n is silent) · freezing /ˈfriːzɪŋ/ · showers /ˈʃaʊə(r)z/ · thunderstorms /ˈθʌndə(r)ˌstɔː(r)mz/ · icy /ˈaɪsi/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«It's sun» or «It's sunny»? → It's sunny: after it's we need an adjective.
«freezing» — is that «cold» or «very, very cold»? → very, very: below zero.
«showers» — is that the shower you wash in? → in a forecast it means short bursts of rain.

Assumptions: SB pp.48–49, KTP pair 30. Some of the words are familiar (hot, cold, rain, snow, sunny); new ones are freezing, showers, thunderstorms, frost/frosty, fog/foggy, wet, dry, cool.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Fear of speaking in front of people they don't knowRule of the pair: first we write, then we read it to a partner, and we go to the board with the sheet in our hands.
27 words at once do not fitI give them in three portions by basket (seasons · temperature · weather), with a gesture for each; recognition first (point at it), production second.
Noun and adjective get mixed upA two-column NOUN / ADJECTIVE poster stays up all lesson; when there is an error I point at the column, I do not say the word.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
