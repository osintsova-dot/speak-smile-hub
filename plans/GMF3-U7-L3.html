Week 08.03–14.03 · Lesson 50✨ NEW · Where was it? It was in… (past in a story)~10 pupils · groups of 4Pupil's Book pp.74–75 · AB p.72
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to follow, read and act out the story «The missing watch» and talk about the past mystery: “Where was Mum's watch? — It was in a magpie's nest in the castle.”
  • Subsidiary. Break Luke's code again («Have you got a watch, too?») — recycles the alphabet code; Values — looking after your personal belongings; story skills: predict → listen → read along → sequence the events → act out; consolidate was/were from L2 in context.
  • Personal. Let the story breathe — big detective voices, pauses, prediction; every child gets a story role; keep the sequencing hands-on. Don't over-correct was/were — notice and recast.
By the end, pupils can…
  • retell 2–3 events of the watch mystery in order;
  • read their role's lines from the book;
  • answer “Where was/were…?” about the story with was/were.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“The watch was MISSING — is it here or has it gone?” → it's gone; we can't find it.
“A magpie took it — why?” → magpies love SHINY things; the watch is shiny.

Assumptions: was/were are active from L2; household objects & prepositions from L1; the alphabet code was used across the year. First act-out of the term for this cast — routine known. Readers follow the text with a finger.

Listen 1 — gist (CD3 30, books closed). Listen: WHO finds the watch? WHERE was it? Check ideas on fingers/hands.

Listen 2 — read along (books open pp.74–75). Finger under the text. Pause per frame: Where was the watch? — It was in the living room, but it isn't there now! Look at the shiny things in the nest! Raise a “was/were” card each time it sounds.

Values (act. 4). Mum was worried. How can you look after YOUR things — your watch, your keys, your phone? 3–4 hands, Russian OK, recast key words.

20–30′
Sequence & correct · comprehension
carpet → desks

Order the events (groups of 4). 6 story pictures per group — race to put them in order; then check against pp.74–75. What happens first? — Mum can't find her watch. Groups narrate their order with was/were.

Read and correct (Talk Partners). Statements: The watch was under the sofa… true or false? Pairs correct the false ones fully: No, it wasn't. It was in the magpie's nest. 5 statements — every pair speaks.

✔ Assess: listen in on the correcting — is it “No, it WASN'T” + the true place? Note who needs the anchor cards.

💪 Support: point to the right frame in the book. ⭐ Extension: add a second true fact about that frame.

30–40′
Act out
carpet, groups of 4

Model a frame. You + 3 pupils play the nest-discovery frame with big drama: Wow! Look at all the shiny things! My watch! Where was it?

Groups of 4 (Lily · Mum · Ravi · Dad). Rehearse 2 frames with books as scripts. Rotate roles once.

Showtime. Each group performs ONE frame (30–40 sec — fast carousel, no long waits). Audience task: shout WHERE the watch was at the end (in the magpie's nest!).

💪 Support: shortest lines / the “Wow, shiny things!” chorus for the shyest child. ⭐ Extension: add an extra line from the frame.

40–52′
🔍 Where is it? · search game
carpet → desks

▶ Hide & ask — Where was it? Before class, “the magpie” hid a shiny object card in the room. Detectives ask past questions to narrow it down: Was it behind the bookcase? Was it under the mat? You answer No, it wasn't / Yes, it was! Whole class calls the questions together — the finder becomes the magpie next round.

🎮 Board option: a room picture with 6 hiding spots numbered; a rotating child taps a spot as the WHOLE class asks the was/were question.

ICQ: Do you ask “Is it…?” or “Was it…?” — it's a PAST mystery! (→ Was it…?)
