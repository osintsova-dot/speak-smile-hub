🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the shopping set (charge, discount, exchange, online shopping, promotion, purchase, receipt, refund, send something back, serve, service, spend) to talk about how you shop, survive a gapped-text exam task, and choose determiners correctly: much money but many websites, several games, plenty of shops.
What is genuinely hard
Two different problems in one lesson. Six of the shopping words are both noun and verb (a charge / to charge, a refund / to refund), so the form has to be decided every time. And determiners break where Russian gives no warning: one word covers much and many, and a double negative feels natural («we don't eat no special food»).
Typical error to expect
«How much shops are there?» — much never meets a countable noun. Russian has one word for both, so the choice has to be made consciously every single time: count it or weigh it?
«We don't eat no special food» — one negative per sentence in English. The book's own Grammar ex.4 plants this trap, and the Teacher's Book asks you to name it out loud.
Why the lesson is built this way
First lesson of the year with the book open, so the shape is built to earn attention fast. The words arrive through five photographs of real places and five teenagers describing where they shop — meaning first, list never. They go straight back out in a survey the class actually cares about. The reading sells itself (Olivia and her friends spending a Saturday in town) and ends in the book's own Reading Part 4 task, taught with a stated method rather than a hope. Grammar comes last and comes from the text they have just read: eight sentences from the article, sorted into countable and uncountable, then the table built on the board and used.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Olivia is sixteen. On Saturday she and her friends drink coffee, look at trainers they can't afford yet, and wait three weeks for the sale. Today: the words English uses for all of that — and why you can have plenty of shops but not much money.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a 90-second money rewind, and the bridge into Unit 1

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what's the best thing about today so far? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer counts as long as it is in English.

  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the unit: Books open, page ten. Unit one — Going shopping. First: five places, and what you can buy in each.

WHAT WE DO: About you + ex.1–3: five photos, five teenagers, thirteen words in context

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. About you as a quick pair exchange: Where do you usually go shopping? Which is your favourite shop? Which is better: going to shops or shopping online? Take three answers only — the lesson is long.
  2. ex.1 in pairs, 90 seconds: what can you buy in each of the five places? Accept lists, push for precision: Plants and vegetables outside, on tables — so what kind of place is that?
  3. ex.2, track 01: play once, whole. Students match Daisy, Sean, Alec, Rose and Iris to the photos. Before the second play, use the Teacher's Book trick — write the first syllables on the board (fav… mon… clo… dep… we…) and let the class predict the words they are about to hear.
  4. ex.3 with the book: mixed-ability pairs, first item done together as a class. Then play the track again, pausing after each speaker for pairs to check.
  5. Check meaning fast, not slowly: Which of these words can be a noun AND a verb? (charge, discount, exchange, purchase, refund, shop). Drill receipt, purchase and promotion chorally — three repetitions each, no more.
📺 On screen: the photos on screen if you have the digital book, otherwise the printed page. Backup: if the audio fails, read the five speaker texts from the audioscript, TB p.284, at natural speed — the task works identically

Move break · count it or weigh it
standing, two walls
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that plants the grammar before it is taught

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two signs on opposite walls: I COUNT IT and I WEIGH IT.
  2. Call out the Teacher's Book warmer list one word at a time — teens, friends, make-up, food, games, hair, websites, discounts, money, shops — and everyone walks to a wall.
  3. Do not correct. Ask one student on each side for a reason, then move on. The class sorts itself out by word four.
  4. Freeze them where they stand and say the punchline: That wall takes MANY. That wall takes MUCH. Sit down — we're doing it properly at the end of the lesson.
📺 On screen: two A4 signs on the walls — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: walk, choose, justify. The physical choice makes the grammar concrete twenty minutes before the rule appears

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 = B1 Reading Part 4: five sentences removed, eight offered, three extra

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the exam instructions with them and check with two true/false questions: There are five gaps — true. There are five sentences to choose from — false, there are eight.
  2. Do gap 1 together, out loud, with the method visible on the board: 1 read the sentence before · 2 read the sentence after · 3 find the linking word · 4 choose. In sentence B, there points back to the coffee shop, and online shopping points forward to finding fashions on Pinterest.
  3. Pairs do gaps 2–5. Monitor and ask the useful question rather than giving answers: Which word in your sentence points back to something?
  4. Check as a class. For every answer, the pair must name the link, not just the letter. Then ask about the three leftovers: Why doesn't A fit anywhere?

Structure
They don't charge you as much money. · There are plenty of second-hand websites. · I don't usually have any money left. · A lot of games cost more than we can afford. · How many pairs of trainers have you got?
Form
determiners before the noun: much / a little + uncountable · many / several / a few + countable · a lot of, plenty of, some, no with both; any in questions and negatives; ONE negative per English sentence.
Pron. watch
receipt /rɪˈsiːt/ — the p is silent and it is the word most often mispronounced in this unit · purchase /ˈpɜːtʃəs/ (not «pur-CHASE») · exchange /ɪksˈtʃeɪndʒ/ · discount is a noun with the stress on the FIRST syllable /ˈdɪskaʊnt/ and a verb with the stress on the second
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«money» — can I count it? → no, so it takes much, not many. Test question for every noun today: do I count it or weigh it?
«plenty of shops» — is that enough shops or more than enough? → more than enough. And several? → some, but not a lot.
«We don't eat no special food» — how many negatives can an English sentence carry? → one. So: we don't eat ANY special food.

Assumptions: SB p.10–11 + p.12 (top), syllabus lesson 4 — the first lesson with the book after three introduction lessons, so the class knows each other and the year's map. Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The thirteen words blur into each otherThe wall is two columns for a reason: THINGS YOU DO on the left, THINGS YOU GET on the right. Every wrong answer gets the same three-second treatment — walk to the wall, point, say the pair aloud (a receipt is the paper, a refund is the money).
The gapped text becomes guessingThe method is said out loud and stays on the board: read the sentence BEFORE the gap, read the sentence AFTER it, find the word that links them (there, they, that way), THEN choose. Do gap 1 together as a demonstration, including one wrong option eliminated in public.
Fast finishers in the readingThe Teacher's Book extension: find cool and stuff in the article and write your own definition for each; compare with another fast finisher and decide which definition is better before checking a dictionary.
Somebody produces «It costs much money»Accept the meaning, fix the form on the spot with the class question: Positive or negative? Much and many live most happily in negatives and questions; in a positive sentence English prefers a lot of.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The shopping field is one of the exam's steady lexical suppliers (refund, receipt, discount, charge, service), and every one of those words is tested as a collocation rather than as a translation. The gapped-text task done today teaches the exact habit the cloze pays for: read the sentence BEFORE and AFTER the gap, then decide. Determiners are the quiet other half — much/many/plenty of are grammar-cloze items in tasks 19–25.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
