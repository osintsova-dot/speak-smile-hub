Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to talk about shopping habits using the target lexis in chunks, and to choose determiners (much/many · some/any/no · a lot of/plenty of) correctly in context.
  • Subsidiary — reading. Read “Shopping: Teen Trends” for gist then detail; deal with a Reading Part 4 gapped-text task using cohesion/reference clues (exam strategy).
  • Subsidiary — speaking. Personalise the topic in Think-Pair-Share & a short Talking-Points discussion — the everyday-topic register tested in ОГЭ/ЕГЭ speaking.
  • Personal. Keep TTT low; set a clear task + ICQ before each stage; run a guided-discovery grammar stage (elicit, don't lecture).
By the end, students can…
  • use ~13 shopping words + 6 reading chunks accurately in a sentence;
  • read a B1 text for gist and detail and complete a gapped-text exam task;
  • select the right determiner for countable / uncountable nouns and avoid the classic exam traps (a piece of advice, information).

Grammar
Determiners. Countable pl.: many · several · some/any · a lot of/plenty of. Uncountable: much · some · any/no · a lot of/plenty of.
Meaning
some in positives · any in questions & negatives · no = not any · much lives in ?/− while a lot of is the neutral positive.
Pron. watch
/tʃ/ in charge · exchange; connected “a lot of” /əˈlɒtəv/; weak some /səm/; stress on discount (n.) vs discount is not used — noun stress front.
Useful frames
You can get a discount on… · It's good value for money. · I usually spend … on … · Have you ever sent something back?
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I bought three shirts — countable or uncountable? Many or much?” → countable → many.
“Do we normally use much in a positive sentence?” → no → questions/negatives; positive → a lot of.
“A shop reduces the price by 20% — a discount or a refund?” → discount.
“You return an item and get your money back — refund or exchange?” → refund (exchange = swap for another item).

Assumptions: students met Past Simple / Present Perfect & basic countable–uncountable in Prepare 4; they can read a B1 text with support; the class contract & pair/group routines are set (intro weeks 1–3). Groups: pairs for TPS & Quiz-Quiz-Trade; groups of 3–4 for Talking Points.

refund vs exchange mixed upGesture: refund = money back to your hand; exchange = swap item ↔ item.
much overused in positives (“I have much money”)Drill a lot of / plenty of as the neutral positive; flag much for ?/−.
Reading Part 4: pick a sentence by one keyword onlyModel reading before & after the gap — pronouns, linkers, this/that reference; re-read for sense.

Prepare for the Exam · Reading Part 4
pairs

Exam strategy (think-aloud). SB ex 2 — five sentences removed, three extra distractors (Reading Part 4). Model gap 1 aloud: First I read the whole text for sense, ignoring the gaps. Now — what comes before this gap? “Some people…” — so the missing sentence probably continues that idea. I look for pronouns and linkers: “this way”, “they also”. Option B says “While they're there…” — does it link? Let me re-read to check it makes sense. (from reading-comprehension-strategy-selector: gist-before-gaps + cohesion/reference cues.)

▶ Sage-N-Scribe pairs finish gaps 2–5: one reasons aloud (“It links back to trainers…”), one writes the letter + why. Swap roles each gap. Whole-class check; each answer justified by a clue word, not a guess. Signpost: full task & tips on SB p. 124.

📓 Interactive notebook — chunk bank for the exam text. Glue in 03_U1_Reading_WB_Words_Chunks_A5 (“The Biggest Mall”: mall · area · districts · section · luxury + spend time doing · designer shops · look in the shop windows · get lost · ask for directions · plenty of things to see and do). This primes the Workbook Reading Part 4 set for homework. Watch the chunk: spend time DOING, never spend time to do.

Grammar · Determiners
seated · guided discovery

Guided discovery (I don't tell — they notice). SB ex 1 — eight examples taken from the text (some friends · any money left · no brand new stuff · much money · many teens · plenty of second-hand websites · several new games · a lot of games). Look at the noun after each word. Countable or uncountable?

SB ex 2 — complete the table (Positive / Negative / Questions × Countable / Uncountable). Elicit the rules: some in positives, any in ?/−, much for uncountable ?/−, a lot of = neutral positive. Add the audio-3 conversation examples (ex 3).

CCQ check (see box): countable→many; much in positive? no. Flag exam traps: a piece of advice, some information.

Controlled practice. SB ex 4 (choose the correct word ×6) — individually, peer-check. Then SB ex 5 (complete the conversation with the box words) in pairs — read it aloud when done.

📓 Interactive notebook — Determiners: choose the right one. Glue in 04_U1_Grammar_Determiners_A5: two columns (countable / uncountable) to colour, the meaning box, and the exam traps highlighted. Task: sort ten nouns from the Shopping sheet into the two columns; write three sentences — one with some, one with any, one with no. Grammar reference: SB p. 147.
