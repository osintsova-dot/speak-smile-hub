🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The magazine article is read not 'because it was set', but as two files in an argument: the crew decides where survival is harder — in Mawsynram (rain every day) or in Oymyakon (-50°C), and has to back every argument with a line from the text. The subskill of the unit (understanding the main idea) is built into the gate: 60 seconds for the whole text with the tasks covered — and only then the details. Critical thinking (Remember → Analyse → Evaluate) becomes the finale of the mission, not an add-on.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two files on the table. Mawsynram, India: the wettest place on earth, rain every single day, bridges made of living tree roots. Oymyakon, Siberia: minus fifty, dark for twenty-one hours, phones freeze in your hand. Both are real, both have schools, both have kids your age. Your crew answers ONE question in sixty seconds: which is harder to live in? Every argument needs a LINE from the text. No line, no point.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Two files: Mawsynram, where it rains every day, and Oymyakon, where it is -50 and phones freeze. The crew decides where life is harder and defends the verdict in 60 seconds. Every argument is backed by a line from the text.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1, the factfiles are closed with one sentence (they were in the brief) + ex.2, five words by place → ex.4 (track 25), six questions through RallyCoach, every answer with its line

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

TB key: 1 It rains every day in the monsoon season · 2 They put bamboo umbrellas over their heads · 3 Because the rain destroys normal bridges · 4 They freeze · 5 They eat meat because vegetables don't grow there · 6 When temperatures are lower than -52°C.

WHAT WE DO: Critical thinking Remember → Analyse → Evaluate + upgrade frames for concession and comparison

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Remember (LOT): Two lists — what is difficult in Mawsynram, what is difficult in Oymyakon. TB prompt: think about the consequences that the text does not state directly (a downpour at night → no sleep; no vegetables → no vitamins).
  2. Analyse (HOT): Order your lists: most difficult at the top. TB tip: some problems are temporary or rare — that is an argument too.
  3. Upgrade frames: Even though it rains every day, people still… · Unlike Mawsynram, Oymyakon…
  4. Two upgrade lines in your verdict = richness points.

👦 Students do: they argue for real and enrich the language. Expect: the strong ones take even though, the rest get a prompt card from me

PRODUCT · the survival verdict
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second verdict: two facts from the text + two arguments from Critical thinking + a comparison + the answer 'where life is harder'

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes to prepare: Keywords only. Everyone owns a block: fact 1 · fact 2 · the worst problem · the verdict.
  2. Sixty seconds. Judges: fluency · accuracy · evidence. Proof from the text or it didn't happen!
  3. A challenge from the floor after each one: But the text says schools only close below minus fifty-two — and you still call that the harder place?
  4. I add the Culture note at the end as a bonus fact (TB): Oymyakon has about 500 inhabitants and three hours of daylight in winter; in Mawsynram machinery is not started until October in the rainy season.
📺 On screen: 60-second timer · the rubric · Culture note
👦 Students do: they perform with evidence. Expect: the verdicts differ — and that is the best possible result

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can get the main idea of a magazine article in 60 seconds and then find specific facts with evidence lines, use 6 new words in context, and deliver a 60-second verdict on which extreme place is harder to live in.
  • Subsidiary. The factfiles and the vocabulary before the text (ex.1–2); ex.3 the main idea; ex.4 (track 25) six questions; ex.5 M / O / Both with the line; Word work (noise · destroys · grass · average · last · roof); Critical thinking Remember → Analyse → Evaluate.
  • Personal. I hold the order of the subskill: FAST first and 'what is this about at all', the details come on the second pass. An answer counts only with the evidence line. Comparative-form errors I log silently for P3.
By the end, students can…
  • get the main idea of a text in 60 seconds
  • find and quote the line that proves an answer
  • use six new words (noise, destroys, grass, average, last, roof)
  • give a 60-second verdict with two pieces of evidence

Structure
It rains every day in the monsoon season. · People can only stay outside for a minute or two. · The text says 'the nearest city is two days away' — that's why Oymyakon is harder.
Form
quoting: The text says + the line; the answer to 'where exactly' is the paragraph number or the first word of the line.
Pron. watch
from the Wordlist: average /ˈæv(ə)rɪdʒ/ · noise /nɔɪz/ · grass /ɡrɑːs/ · roof /ruːf/ · destroys /dɪˈstrɔɪz/ · last /lɑːst/ (TB tip: same vowels — noise = destroys, grass = last)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'I think it's colder there' — does that count? → no: only with a line from the text.
average — does it mean 'so-so'? → no: the typical amount, the mean of them all: average rainfall 11,871 mm.
Why is the first pass only 60 seconds? → to catch the GENERAL idea; the details come on the second and third pass (the subskill of the unit).

Assumptions: SB pp.50–51, KTP pair 31. They read confidently, but 'fast first, details later' is a new discipline. All 6 Word work words are in the official Wordlist.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They drown in the text from the first lineThe gate limits them physically: 60 seconds, the book closes, we write only 'what it is about'. Details are allowed only from the second pass.
They skim and then argue 'I'm right'The evidence-line rule: the argument is settled only by a line from the text, with a finger on that line.
The verdict turns into 'it's awful there / it's worse there'The verdict rubric: 2 facts from the text + 1 argument from Critical thinking + one comparison.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
