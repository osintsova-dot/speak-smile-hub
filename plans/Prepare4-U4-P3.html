🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Walk through the whole airport in eleven words (check-in desk → security check → departure gate → passport control → baggage hall → customs) and talk about the future with be going to (intentions, predictions) vs the present continuous (fixed arrangements).
What is genuinely hard
The airport words are long, low-frequency and full of silent letters (queue!, sign) — they only stick as a SEQUENCE, not a list. The grammar trap is subtler: Russian «я собираюсь» and «я еду завтра» both exist, but students reach for will as the only future — the arrangement/intention decision has never been conscious.
Typical error to expect
I will visit my grandma tomorrow for a fixed arrangement — will grabbed as the only future; and I'm go to visit / I going to visit — be dropped from be going to [high priority, A2/B1]
becouse · tomorow · alot · wich — the most frequent misspellings; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam In this unit that means que · bordering pass · custums.
Why the lesson is built this way
Vocabulary comes first and is taught as a JOURNEY, not a list — the ordering task (ex.2–3) is the memory architecture, and the move break walks the room through the same airport. The Tokyo reading does double duty: careful reading with a planted trap (the hedgehog café) AND the quarry the grammar is mined from — Olivia's blog is written in going-to and present-continuous future, sentence by sentence. The discovery stage extracts the three rules from sentences already read; the practice runs at three depths; the productive stage is real next-weekend plans, because the arrangement/intention split only makes sense on the student's own diary.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“On Saturday a taxi is picking Olivia up at 8 am — she's off to Tokyo: cat cafés, manga hotels, vending machines that sell umbrellas. By the end of today you can walk through any airport in the world naming every step — and talk about your own next weekend the way English actually does it: what's ARRANGED, what you INTEND, what you can see coming.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast phrasal-verb round, and the bridge from friendship to travel

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.

  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the unit: Books open, page forty-six. Unit eight — Going away.

Vocabulary · the airport line
pairs → solo
Why: Eleven words arrive from photos, then get ORDERED into the real airport journey — sequence is the memory hook: check-in → security → gate → (land) → passport control → baggage hall → customs.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: eleven words ↔ photos A–K; ex.2–3 (track 35): order the journey

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Drill the sound traps by ear FIRST, books closed, three choral repetitions: baggage /ˈbæɡɪdʒ/ · boarding /ˈbɔːdɪŋ/ · customs /ˈkʌstəmz/ · departure /dɪˈpɑːtʃə/ · queue /kjuː/ (one sound, four silent letters!) · security /sɪˈkjʊərəti/ · sign /saɪn/ (silent g). Show queue's spelling last, as the joke it is.
  2. ex.1 in pairs: match the eleven words to photos A–K — 3 minutes. Key: A baggage hall · B baggage · C security check · D check-in desk · E boarding pass · F passport control · G passport · H queue · I departure gate · J customs · K sign.
  3. ex.2 in pairs: number the photos in the order of a real journey, then TELL it: You arrive at the airport and go to the check-in desk. You show…
  4. ex.3, play track 35 to check the order. Key: the airport order (track 35): 1 check-in desk → boarding pass · 2 security check · 3 departure gate · then after landing: 4 passport control · 5 baggage hall — collect your baggage · 6 customs · 7 walk out into Arrivals.
  5. Fill the journey line on the board as the order settles — seven boxes, the class dictates.
📺 On screen: track 35 in the whiteboard app (Backup: read the airport walk-through from the audioscript, TB p.267 — the checking pass works identically by voice)
👦 Students do: drill, match, order, retell. Expect baggage hall vs baggage and sign vs departure gate to be the photo fights — the fights are the learning. Typical error: controls of passport — word order flips

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 complete + ex.5: ticket vs boarding pass, security vs customs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 solo, 3 minutes, pair check. Key: 1 passport · 2 queue · 3 signs; boarding pass / passport (either order) · 4 baggage.
  2. ex.5 as Kagan RallyCoach: partner A explains distinction 1 (ticket vs boarding pass), B coaches with the text; swap for 2.   3. Concept-check cold: They open your bag BEFORE you fly — security or customs? They open it AFTER you land?
  4. Question 3 to the class: Best and worst things about air travel? — two answers, thirty seconds.
📺 On screen: the journey line stays in chalk with SECURITY and CUSTOMS boxed at its two ends — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: complete, explain, get checked. Expect security/customs to feel identical until the before/after anchor lands — that anchor is the stage. Typical error: customs check used for the pre-flight scan

Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: walk the room through the airport

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Map the room out loud: The door is the check-in desk. The bookshelf — security check. My desk — the departure gate. The window — passport control. The back wall — baggage hall, then customs.
  2. Stand up. Walk your journey and SAY each stop as you pass it. Fly quietly between gate and passport control. Go!
  3. Land them: Welcome to Tokyo. Sit down.
📺 On screen: instructions by voice, the mapped room is the medium — nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: walk the sequence, naming every stop. Expect giggles at the flying — the sillier the flight, the harder the sequence sticks

  5. Colour with TB background: TB background: Roppongi — a rich district with nightlife; Japanese internet cafés = manga + a night cheaper than a hotel; Harajuku — the centre of youth fashion.
  6. Talking point if the pace allows: Would you like to visit Tokyo? What would you see and do? · What sightseeing would you recommend to visitors to your town?

Grammar · future discovery

Why: The three rules are extracted from the blog sentences the class has already read. The decision «arrangement or intention?» is the whole lesson — the diary warmer makes it visible before it gets a name.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the three rules, mined from Olivia's blog

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Warmer, books closed: TB warmer: a week's diary page on the board (Mon–Sun: doctor, cinema, basketball, a wedding). What am I doing on Tuesday? — students pull the present continuous out of the diary themselves.
  2. ex.1 — the heart of the lesson: three sentences FROM the blog, three rules to match. Pairs get 3 minutes. Key: rules: a present continuous (arrangements, specific time) · b be going to (intentions) · c be going to (predictions); match: 1–b · 2–a · 3–c.
  3. Fill the two board columns with the blog sentences: ARRANGED (We're leaving on Saturday · A taxi is picking us up at 8) | INTENDED/PREDICTED (We're going to visit a cat café · You're going to see a lot of people).
  4. Box the skeleton: BE + GOING TO + VERB — three parts, none optional.
  5. Concept checks from the plan header — all three, cold-calling by name.
  6. Signpost: Grammar reference page 145 — tonight, two minutes, if you want the rules in a table.
📺 On screen: the two columns fill up in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: extract the rules and place the sentences into the columns. Expect «is picking us up» to be called «about now» by someone — perfect: the taxi is BOOKED, that is the whole discovery. Typical error: will offered for every column
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit8_Grammar_Future_Plans_A5
A5 grammar card — TWO WAYS TO TALK ABOUT PLANS: BE GOING TO = an intention or plan in your head, and a prediction from evidence (am/is/are + going to + BASE VERB: I'm going to visit London. · Look at those clouds! It's going to rain.) against PRESENT CONTINUOUS = a fixed arrangement with a time, place or person (am/is/are + verb-ing + future time: We're flying on Friday. · I'm meeting Ana at six.), with the DECISION CLUE (idea → going to · arranged + WHEN? → present continuous), the negative and the question
  • Glue the card in and underline the future time in every arranged example.
  • Ask the decision question before every sentence: is it agreed, or just an idea?
  • Write two plans of your own: one intention, one real arrangement.
  • The prediction from evidence is going to as well — mark that example.
When: ~5 min at the start of the “Grammar · future discovery” stagePrep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Scissors, glue sticks, pencils.

🎯 Speaking out · next weekend
solo → pairs → class
Why: The productive outcome: real plans for next weekend, arrangements in the continuous and intentions with going to — personal, timed, logged.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: real plans — arrangements in continuous, intentions with going to

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two minutes solo: notes on next weekend — TWO real arrangements (booked, agreed, timed) and TWO intentions (no date yet). The box on p.48 feeds ideas (go shopping, a gig, hang out, a match…).
  2. Model the split out loud: I'm meeting my friends on Saturday at six — arranged. I'm going to buy new trainers — some time, intended.
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share, 90 seconds each way: tell your weekend; the listener hunts — which plans are ARRANGED? — and reports one of each.
  4. Swap partners once (move one seat right) and repeat faster.
  5. Four students report a partner's plan to the class; the class votes columns: arranged or intended?
  6. You log future-form errors silently — they open lesson 4.
📺 On screen: the two columns and the dream-destination corner stay in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: plan, tell, hunt the arrangements, report. Expect proud arrangements (a real match, a real trip) — ride them: real diaries are the best grammar. Typical error: I will meet — out-ask it: is it arranged?

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class

Structure

Form
present continuous = arrangement + specific time; be going to = intention (maybe no date) or a prediction you can see coming; the airport sequence: check-in → security → gate → fly → passport control → baggage hall → customs → arrivals.
Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«A taxi is picking us up at 8 am» — is the taxi booked? → yes: arranged, specific time → present continuous.
«I'm going to learn Japanese one day» — is there a date? → no: an intention → be going to.
«The sky is dark. It's going to rain» — do I KNOW the future? → no, but I can see the evidence → be going to for predictions.

Assumptions: SB p.46–48 (top), syllabus lesson 22. Present continuous as a FORM is solid (Unit 1); using it for the future is effectively new as conscious knowledge. Be going to was met in Prepare 3. Of the eleven airport words the class usually owns passport and maybe baggage as a loan-shape; queue is known by ear, never by spelling. Unit 7 phrasal verbs are one lesson old — the opening recycles them.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
