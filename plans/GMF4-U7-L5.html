Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /ɔɪ/ sound spelt oy and oi (Joy oils the joints on Roy's toy robot), talk about transport in Hong Kong, and read & plan an acrostic poem — three mini-blocks, one “future city” thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that oy and oi both spell /ɔɪ/ (recognise → say → sort); culture — transport in Hong Kong (trams · the Star Ferry · junk boats · the underground/MTR) + a video, 21C: making predictions from what you know; literacy — text type an acrostic poem (first letters spell a word, e.g. FUTURE), plan & write one.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (chant/action bridges); the Joy-oils-the-joints tongue-twister is play; cap each block hard so the acrostic actually gets planned.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the /ɔɪ/ twister and sort oy / oi words;
  • name 2–3 Hong Kong transport types and compare with ours;
  • find how an acrostic poem works and plan one with a partner.

Structure
Joy oils the joints on Roy's toy robot.  ·  You can travel by (junk boat) in Hong Kong.  ·  acrostic: Far away… Underground trains will… There will be…
Form
/ɔɪ/ = oy (Joy, Roy, toy, boy) usually at the END · oi (oils, joints, coin, soil) usually in the MIDDLE. Acrostic: each line starts with a letter of the title word (top to bottom).
Pron. watch
/ɔɪ/ clean in Joy/oils/joints/Roy/toy; Hong Kong /ˌhɒŋ ˈkɒŋ/; ferry /ˈferi/; junk /dʒʌŋk/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“toy and coin — same /ɔɪ/ sound? Same letters?” → same sound, different spelling (oy vs oi)!
“An acrostic — do the FIRST letters spell a word?” → yes — read them top to bottom.

Assumptions: Places, transport & will/won't are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from earlier GMF4 phonics is enough to enjoy the oy / oi noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short acrostic lines with support.

Block 1 · Phonics /ɔɪ/ (oy · oi)
carpet → desks

Listen, read and say (act. 1, CD3 34). PB p.77: Joy oils the joints on Roy's toy robot. Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort the /ɔɪ/ spellings. Hoops/columns: oy (toy, Roy, boy, Joy) · oi (oil, coin, soil, joints). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card.

Notice the rule. Toy and coin — same sound /ɔɪ/, different letters! “oy” is often at the end; “oi” is often in the middle. (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more /ɔɪ/ word to a hoop (enjoy, point).

✔ Assess: who still can't hear /ɔɪ/? Note them — extra RallyRead next unit.

Block 3 · Literacy · an acrostic poem
carpet → desks, pairs

Read an acrostic poem (PB p.78, act. 1–3, CD3 36). Which words do you think will be in a poem about the future? Read the FIRST letter of each line — what word do they spell? Read/listen; discover the acrostic spells a word (e.g. FUTURE).

▶ Acrostic scavenger hunt. Race in pairs on AB p.75: Order the lines. What's the title of the poem? First hands up read the title aloud — everyone scans the first letters.

Writing tip (PB p.79). An acrostic poem: each line starts with a letter of the title word — read top to bottom! Make a mind-map of future words together (robots · flying cars · the moon).

▶ Plan an acrostic (Cooperative learning, AB p.76 format). Pairs choose a short title word (CITY · ROBOT · SPACE) & make notes for each line. 2 pairs share one line aloud.

💪 Support: fill the plan template with word/verb support. ⭐ Extension: write the full acrostic with a line for every letter.
