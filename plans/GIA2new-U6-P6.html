🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): Two very practical things in one lesson: how to react when a friend is telling you something (this rescues any conversation in English), and how to write an email about a trip. Both follow the same shape: model → shadowing / taking the sample apart → writing it down → saying it out loud. New students need the Key phrases especially: they are short, easy to learn, and they make a conversation come alive instantly.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two very useful things today. One: what to SAY when a friend tells you something. 'What was it like?' 'No way!' 'That's so funny!' Short phrases — but they keep a conversation alive, and you can use them tomorrow. Two: how to write an email about a trip, step by step: First of all, Then, After that, Next, Finally. We'll watch a real dialogue, repeat it together, write our own — and you'll have the phrases on the screen the whole time.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: two useful things: what to say when a friend is telling you something (short phrases that bring a conversation to life), and how to write an email about a trip, step by step. We watch a real dialogue, repeat after it, write our own — and the phrases stay on screen the whole time.

WHAT WE DO: Skills boost: THINK (notes about a place) → PREPARE (the dialogue in writing) → PRACTISE → PERFORM from the page

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: A place you visited. Three notes: where, what you saw, one funny or surprising thing. (they can reuse the place from the P5 homework)
  2. Say your own three notes aloud first as a model, then Kagan Timed Pair Share: partner A speaks from their notes for exactly 45 seconds, partner B only listens and at the end says one Key phrase; on the signal they swap. Notes in front of them, silence is not allowed — but reading is.
  3. PREPARE: write the whole dialogue in pairs on the Megan and Lukas skeleton — at least three Key phrases. The skeleton is on screen.
  4. PRACTISE: read it in pairs twice, the partner gives feedback on the voice (the TB asks for this) and you decide who plays which role.
  5. PERFORM: act it from the page for the next pair, then two pairs act for the class.
  6. Peer review ex.6: Which Key phrases did they use? Did the voice sound interested?

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can respond with 6 Key phrases and the right intonation in a short dialogue, and write a 5–6-line email about a trip with five sequencing words.
  • Subsidiary. The video (ex.1–3) + shadowing on track 40; Skills boost with the notes ready; Josef's email as the model (ex.1–3); the sequencing words subskill (ex.4); ex.5 the theme-park plan turned into a text on the model; peer review.
  • Personal. Write the dialogue out in full and only then act it (no improvising for new students). Drill the intonation through shadowing: repeat after the recording with the same voice, not “correctly from a grammar point of view”.
By the end, students can…
  • ask What was it like? and Was it fun?
  • respond with No way! / You're kidding! / That's so funny! / Sounds boring!
  • put events in order with First of all, Then, After that, Next, Finally
  • write an email about a trip in 5–6 lines

Structure
How was your weekend? · What was it like? · It was brilliant! · There were loads of animals. · No way! · Sounds boring!
Form
First of all · Then · After that · Next · Finally (Jozef's email uses exactly these five). TB adds: after that, then and next can go in any order; first of all and finally go only at the beginning and at the end. Put a comma after the linker.
Pron. watch
TB: compare HOW Lukas says «No way!» and «You're kidding!» (warm, impressed, excited) and HOW Megan says «Sounds boring!» (disappointed, sympathetic). The phrase does not work without the intonation.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“That's so funny!” said in a flat voice — will it work? → no, they will hear indifference. The voice matters more than the words.
Can Finally go in the middle? → no: first of all at the start, finally at the end.
Is there a comma after First of all? → yes (a TB reminder).

Assumptions: SB pp.79–81, syllabus pair 51. The past simple was drilled in P3 and P5 — here it is a working tool. All the skeletons (the gapped dialogue, the model email) are in the coursebook.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The reactions sound roboticShadowing on track 40: repeat after the recording, copying the voice. Compare Lukas's “No way!” with Megan's “Sounds boring!” (TB routine).
They are afraid to act the dialogue outWrite the dialogue in full, rehearse it in pairs from the page, and act it from the page too. No improvising required.
The email turns into a listThe five linkers are compulsory and stand in a column on the board; ex.5 (the theme-park plan) is drilled exactly as “list → text”.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
