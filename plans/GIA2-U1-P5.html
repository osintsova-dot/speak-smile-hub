Get Involved! A2 · Unit 1 “School life” · Pair 5 — Grammar 2: Yes/No questions & object pronouns
A2 teens (~12–14 y.o., confident readers) · 90 min · Grammar lesson (Guided discovery + PPP) · SB p.18
28.09–04.10 · October · Unit 1, in-unit pair 5 (global pair 9)✨ NEW GRAMMAR~12 pupils · 3 teams of 4Line GIA2 (continuing A2)
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to form and answer present-simple Yes/No questions (Do/Does + subject + base verb; short answers) and use object pronouns (me, you, him, her, it, us, them) — in the context of asking about school life.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle school subjects, daily routines & present simple aff./neg. (previous pairs) as the content of the questions; fluency in a personalised pair interview; accuracy sub-focus: base verb after does and short-answer form.
  • Personal. Model the form on the board before eliciting; use a hinge check before freer practice; keep TTT low, ICQ every task; nominate widely; hold pairs/teams accountable (PIES).
By the end, learners can…
  • make correct Do/Does …? questions and give true short answers;
  • keep the main verb in the base form after does;
  • replace a noun object with the right object pronoun;
  • interview a partner about their school and report back accurately.
Target language
Grammar
Yes/No Qs: Do + I/you/we/they + base? · Does + he/she/it + base?  ·  Short answers: Yes, I do. / No, it doesn't.  ·  Object pronouns: meyouhimheritusthem
Structure
Do you have history lessons? — Yes, we do. · Does your school start later? — No, it doesn't. · We like him a lot. · I can't imagine studying it.
Form
Do/Does carries tense & person → main verb stays base (Does she start?, not starts). Object pronoun replaces the noun object, after the verb / preposition.
Pron. watch
weak forms Do you /djə/, Does he /dəzi/; rising intonation on Yes/No questions; stress the auxiliary in the answer — Yes, I DO. / No, it DOESN'T.
Recycled lexis
in context: school subjects (history, art, science, maths, French), uniform, swimming pool, homework, daily routines — the topic the questions are about.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Hinge: Which is right — Does she watch TV? or Does she watches TV? → watch (base after does).
“We like Mr Flynn → we like ___?” → him.
“Yes, I do like it or just Yes, I do?” → Yes, I do.
“Do you / Does he — which subject takes does?” → he/she/it.

Assumptions: present simple aff./neg. is known (pair 3, WB p.5); school subjects (pair 4) and daily routines (pair 1) are recent. Groups: team = max 4 (12=3×4 · 10=3+3+4 · 9=3×3 · 8=2×4 · 7=3+4).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Keeping the -s after does (Does she watches?)Hinge check before freer stage; red cross the wrong form on the notebook card; “does already has the -s”.
Over-long short answer (Yes, I do like it)Drill the two-word answer only; hand-chop after do / doesn't.
Wrong object pronoun / subject form (I love he)Subject↔object table on the board; stand-up “Point & Pronoun” drill so the swap is physical.
Do vs Does mix-upSort by subject: he/she/it → Does, everything else → Do; colour-code.
L1 creeps into the pair interviewQuestion frames stay on the board; “English only” signal; feed the phrase, don't translate.
Classroom management — signals
“1–2–3, eyes on me” — class replies “1–2, eyes on you” → stop & look.
Silent hand-up — raise a hand, learners mirror & finish talking → quiet.
Volume dial — hand high/low = louder/quieter.
“English only” — tap the board frame when L1 creeps in.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1 — pens down.”
“Freeze!” — stop mid-task, reset before the next stage.

0–8′
Lead-in · retrieval
seats → pairs

Retrieval warm-up (recycle → today). Board a mini-grid of things they've just studied: uniform · maths · history · a swimming pool · lots of homework. Which of these does OUR school have? Learners react — this reactivates school subjects and present simple from the last pairs and seeds today's questions.

Set the theme. Today you become school reporters — you're going to interview each other about school. But first, how do we ASK a question in the present simple?

▶ Quick pair-share: Tell your partner ONE thing you want to ask a new classmate about their school. Take two ideas in English; if a well-formed question comes up, put it on the board as the target.

 board grid · SB p.18 open
8–20′
Guided discovery · Yes/No questions
board / seats

Examples first (SB p.18 ex.1). Read the three examples aloud: Do you have history lessons? — Yes, we do. · Does your school start later? — No, it doesn't. Think-aloud the pattern: Do or Does comes first, then the person, then the plain verb — no ‑s on the main verb.

Complete the table (ex.1). Learners copy & complete the Yes/No + short-answers table (Do → I/you/we/they; Does → he/she/it). Elicit the gaps, board the finished table, highlight the base verb.

Hinge check (before practice). Fingers 1 or 2: 1) Does she watch TV?   2) Does she watches TV? → all should show 1. Re-teach if not.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down (short-answer drill, everyone moves): teacher asks quick questions — Do you like pizza? Does your school have a pool? Do we have English now? — learners stand for “Yes, I/we do,” crouch for “No, … doesn't/don't,” saying the short answer aloud.

seats → pair-check

SB p.18 ex.2 — match. Match the questions with the short answers. Individually, 90 sec, then pair-check.

SB p.18 ex.3 — write questions. Write questions with Do or Does. (you / start school / 8 o'clock? → Do you start school at eight o'clock?) Monitor for the base verb; board 2–3 for whole-class correction.

ICQ: After ‘does’, do we add ‑s to the next verb? (→ no).

SB p.18 ex.4 becomes a mingle. ▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade: each learner gets a card with a school prompt (study French · wear a uniform · have a pool · finish at 3:30 · like Mondays).

Stand up, hand up, pair up! A turns the prompt into a question — Do you study French? — B gives a true short answer — Yes, I do. / No, I don't. — then swap roles, trade cards, new partner. 4–5 trades.

44–56′
Presentation · object pronouns
board → standing

Notice (SB p.18). Show the two sentences: We present them. · I can't imagine studying it. What does ‘them’ / ‘it’ mean here? → they replace a noun so we don't repeat it.

SB p.18 ex.5 — complete the table. Subject → Object: I→me, you→you, he→him, she→her, it→it, we→us, they→them. Board it; drill chorally.

▶ Point & Pronoun (physical, everyone): teacher points — Look at Sara and Tom! class replies …them!; Look at me! → …you!; The board → …it! Fast round the room.

SB p.18 ex.6 — replace. Replace the words in bold with an object pronoun. (we like Mr Flynn → we like him.) Pairs, then check. CCQ: ‘I love PE’ → I love ___? (it).

56–64′
📓 Interactive notebook
seats (hands-on)

Glue-in A5 grammar card — “ASK + POINT BACK”. The card carries Do/Does + subject + base verb with short answers and the reminder after DOES → base verb, plus the object-pronoun strip (I→me · you→you · he→him · she→her · it→it · we→us · they→them) on live examples (Mr Flynn is great. We like him.).

① Glue the card in. ② In red pen, cross out Does she watches…? — write the fix. ③ Build four Do/Does questions about your partner. ④ In each answer, swap the noun for a pronoun from the bottom strip. The card stays open for the interview next.

64–76′
Freer production · School interview
pairs → report

Personalised interview. ▶ RallyRobin → Timed Pair Share: using their own four questions (+ the SB frames), partners interview each other about their real school day & subjects. Ask, give a true short answer, then a detail.

Stretch frame on the board: Do you …? — Yes, I do. I … it because … / No, I don't. I prefer …

Report back with pronouns. 3–4 learners report one thing about their partner — forcing the pronoun: She likes maths — she studies it every day. I sit with them at lunch.
