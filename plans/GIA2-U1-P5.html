🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Yes/No questions and object pronouns are hidden inside a detective story: 'the interview room', where the secret only cracks open under correctly built questions. Do/Does under the pressure of a game, and the object pronouns as 'the name-replacement code' in the report. Again the grammar is not the topic — the interrogation is.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“The Interview Room. One of you holds a secret card — a person, a school, a day. The rest have twenty questions. But the Room accepts only PERFECT questions: Do you…? Does she…? A broken question burns. Twenty questions, one secret, zero mercy. Interrogators — ready?”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: One student holds a secret card; the others crack the secret with Do/Does questions. A broken question burns. Twenty questions, one secret.

WHAT WE DO: the game itself: secret cards, 20 questions per crew round the circle (RoundRobin), a broken question burns

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Secret cards out. Twenty questions per crew. A burned question is GONE — count them.

pairs

WHAT WE DO: ex.6 p.17: replacing the highlighted names with pronouns through RallyCoach + the 'a name only once' rule

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Protocol code: a name appears ONCE. After that — him, her, it, us, them.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach: ex.6 goes item by item — the first student makes the replacement out loud and explains it ('Mr Flynn is a man, so him'), the second follows with a pencil and says code accepted or sends it back for another think; on the next item the roles swap.
  3. ex.6 in pairs, key in chorus: PE → it · Mr Flynn → him · science subjects → them · Sara → her · our class → us.
  4. Lightning round: I love my dog. → I love HIM… or IT? Discuss!
📺 On screen: SB p.17 ex.6
👦 Students do: they replace by the code. Expected: the dog=him/it argument — an excellent moment for a CCQ

PRODUCT · interview + protocol
pairs → stage

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second Timed Pair Share interview (5+ clean questions) → a written report with pronouns → 3 reports read out

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The interview: Sixty seconds. Five clean questions minimum. Notes only.
  2. Kagan Timed Pair Share: A interrogates for exactly 60 seconds (B answers and does not stall), then on the whistle B gets 60 seconds. Equal time, so both of them have material for the report.
  3. A 4-line report: I asked Dasha about her day. She gets up at seven. I asked HER about maths — she loves IT.
  4. The code: a name only once! The judges count breaches of the code.
📺 On screen: timer · report checklist
👦 Students do: they interview and write to the code. Expected: the pronouns from the game carry over into the writing

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can fire grammatically clean Yes/No questions at speed, answer with short forms, and replace names with object pronouns in a report.
  • Subsidiary. The Do/Does table is built out of the examples; ex.6 p.17, replacing with object pronouns; the interview report as a written product.
  • Personal. I watch the question intonation (rising) — I drill it in chorus. I give the object pronouns as 'the code of the report': using a name twice is unprofessional.
By the end, students can…
  • build clean Do/Does questions at speed
  • answer with short forms (Yes, she does / No, they don't)
  • replace names with me/him/her/it/us/them
  • run a 60-second interview and report it

Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'Does she likes PE?' — why not? → does already marks the third person, the verb stays bare.
'I like she' — what has to change? → her: after a verb we use the object pronoun.
The secret was cracked with 'Does she likes PE?' — does it count? → no, the question burned: like.

Assumptions: SB p.17, KTP pair 9. Do/Does is familiar from A1+, and so are the object pronouns; the load is in the speed and the accuracy under the pressure of the game.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The questions collapse into 'Is it…?'The secret cards are built around verbs (day/habits/subjects) — you cannot crack them with be-questions.
Does she likes — the classicThe rule of the Room: a broken question burns WITHOUT an answer. The burned questions go to the clinic.
Names instead of pronouns in the reportThe code of the report: a name may be written once, after that — only a pronoun. The judges count the breaches.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
