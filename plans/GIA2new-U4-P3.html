🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): With new students grammar CAN be announced as the topic — they need clarity, not camouflage. But it is delivered through discovery: they build the «two things / three and more» rule themselves out of four familiar sentences from the P2 text. After that, lots of short written runs with self-check against a key, and the Spelling rules poster from p.57 on the wall: confidence grows from «I got it right», not from an explanation.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two words change everything. THAN and THE. Siberia is colder THAN India — that's two things. This is THE coldest town in the world — that's everything in the world. Today you get the full map: how to build the forms, how to spell them (hot → hoTTer, dry → driest), and the two words that don't follow the rules — good and bad. By the end you write three comparisons about your own region, and they'll all be correct.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Two words decide everything: than (two things) and the …est (three and more). We go through the rule, the spelling and the two exceptions (good, bad), and then write three correct comparisons about our own region.

Discovery · two things or three
pairs → board

WHAT WE DO: four sentences from the P2 text on cards → sorting into «two things / three and more» (ex.1) → the rule (ex.2) → the gesture

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. We lay the sentence cards from the article out in two piles: Which sentences compare TWO things? Which compare everything? TB key ex.1: two — colder than, wetter than; three+ — the coldest, the wettest.
  2. Now finish the rules. TB key ex.2: 1 than · 2 the.
  3. I introduce the gesture for the whole unit as a model: I show it myself — two fingers → THAN, an open palm → THE …EST — and the class repeats it in chorus with the gesture. After that it becomes Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols: I name a pair of things, everyone shows the right gesture at the same time, and I can see every single student, not just the one answering.
  4. A poster for the wall: TWO things → …er THAN · THREE+ → THE …est.
📺 On screen: sentence cards · SB p.52 ex.1–2 · the poster
👦 Students do: they discover the rule with their hands. Expected: the gesture holds the choice more reliably than a wording does

WHAT WE DO: we go through Spelling rules p.57 point by point → ex.3, the table of forms with self-check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The Spelling rules p.57 poster point by point, an example in chorus for each: cold → colder · white → whiter · hot → hoTTer · sunny → sunnIER · beautiful → MORE beautiful · good → better · bad → worse.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4, six sentences with self-check → ex.5, Brazil and Ecuador in pairs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 in writing, 4 minutes. TB key: 1 hotter · 2 windier · 3 colder · 4 better · 5 more dangerous · 6 warmer. I write the comparative on the board — spelling matters more than speed (TB tip).

WB Unit 4 · the «TWO → than / THREE+ → the …est» and Spelling rules posters stay up in the classroom until the end of the unit
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can choose between comparative + than and the + superlative and spell both forms correctly, checked by themselves against keys.
  • Subsidiary. Guided discovery from the P2 text (ex.1–2); ex.3, the table of forms with the Spelling rules p.57 as support; ex.4–5, written runs with self-check; the world records quiz ex.6–7 (track 26); the text about Dubai ex.8; a mini-test with self-check.
  • Personal. The pace is half that of the «ours» line — and that is normal. Every stage ends with self-check against a key: the error is found privately, before anyone else sees it. The Brain teaser (ex.9) I give as a bonus, not as compulsory.
By the end, students can…
  • choose than or the …est
  • spell hotter, driest, the wettest correctly
  • use good → better → best and bad → worse → worst
  • write three correct comparisons about my region

Structure
Siberia is colder than India. · India is wetter than Siberia. · This is the coldest town in the world. · May and June are the wettest months.
Form
comparing TWO things → comparative adjective + than: colder than, wetter than · comparing THREE or more → the + superlative adjective: the coldest, the wettest · good → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst
Pron. watch
than is unstressed, it sounds like /ðən/ · the -est ending is short: the coldest /ˈkəʊldɪst/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'India is more hot than the UK' — what's wrong? → hot is short: hotter than.
'the hotest place' — and here? → hot doubles the consonant: the hottest.
'Siberia is colder that India' — correct it. → colder THAN India.

Assumptions: SB p.52 + Spelling rules p.57, KTP pair 32. For some of the students both the form itself and the spelling are genuinely new; I build on sentences from a text they have already read.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They have heard the rule but do not apply itI do not explain first — they sort the four sentences and word the rule themselves; I confirm it and put the poster up.
Spelling of the forms (hoter, dryest)The Spelling rules p.57 poster on the wall; before every written run, 10 seconds of «everyone look at the poster».
Fear of making mistakes in writingEvery written run comes with self-check against a key, before anyone else's eyes see it.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
