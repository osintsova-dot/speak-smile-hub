WHAT WE DO: 2 minutes: rewrite a letter that's lost 9 capitals; from the fixes we derive 7 rules

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I display the letter in lower case: hi! my name's leo. i'm from porto, in portugal. i speak portuguese and english. my favourite day is friday…
  2. I say: Rewrite it. Find all NINE capital crimes. You've written English for four years — this is proofreader speed. Two minutes!
  3. Key, self-count, score in the margin.
  4. From the fixes we derive 7 rules, I put up a poster (start · I · countries/nationalities · cities · languages · days · names). TB bonus: + islands (Honshu) and Mr/Mrs West.
📺 On screen: slide of the crime letter → key · the 7-rules poster on the board (stays up) (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: rewrite at speed, count the “crimes” they found. Expected: 7–9 out of 9. Typical error: friday stays lower case — rule №6 is born from this fix.

🏁 Quick review · Pre-flight check
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: a team quiz, 5 rounds over the whole of page 21: the last big points before the project and the test

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I announce: PRE-FLIGHT CHECK — five rounds, everything from this unit. This page (21) IS the map of your test. Streaks ×2 after three clean answers!
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift them together — I see everyone's answer, no hiding.
  3. R1 spelling: 6 countries → nationality in writing on mini-boards (audio 15 or myself). R2 opposites: 6 flashes (audio 16). R3 be-flips: statement → negative within 8 sec. R4 question scrambles: 3 of them + a true short answer. R5 capitals: 2 “crimes”.
  4. Live scoring, intercepts on misses, I announce streaks in a commentator's voice.
  5. Finale: Whatever round was weakest — that's your WB page 11 tonight… wait, that's Pair 6. Tonight: pages 10. Weak round = your focus.
📺 On screen: round slides · audio 15–16 · mini-boards · leaderboard (Backup: no track — I read the script aloud at listening speed; everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: fly through 5 rounds with all crews at once (mini-boards), find their weak round. Expected: loud, exciting; the weak link is spelling. Typical error: “we know this” — streaks and intercepts keep the grip.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write a 6–8 sentence keypal message with flawless capitals and one beyond-the-book structure — and pass the unit «pre-flight check» (Quick review p.21) as a team quiz.
  • Subsidiary. Capitals subskill as a speed-fix gate (7 rules); the three message moves stolen from the models; writing cycle in timed portions with an upgrade pass (“longer and sharper”); peer review as judging; write-back vote; Quick review = last big points before project & test.
  • Personal. Writing gets a real audience (the write-back market) — guard the portions with timers, never 15 minutes of silence. The Quick review quiz at the end reframes revision as a race, not a worksheet.
By the end, students can…
  • fix capitals at speed (7 rules)
  • use the three message moves (start · why · ask to write)
  • write a message that wins write-back votes
  • clear all five Quick-review rounds with their crew

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“i'm from spain — how many crimes?” → two: I + Spain.
“A message without the ask-to-write move — does it get answers?” → Rarely. Move 3 sells it.
“Is the Quick review page new material?” → No — it's the MAP of the unit test.

Assumptions: All be forms + Key phrases active (P3–P4). Capitals «known» but break at speed — the gate proves it. Keypal format is motivating; the market vote gives writing a real reader. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
