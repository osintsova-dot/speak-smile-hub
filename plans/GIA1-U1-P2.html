Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read an informational text for gist and detail (“The Netherlands — a good place to be a teenager”), using the sub-skill identifying different types of text to predict content before reading.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle be (affirmative/negative) in the reading context; build topic vocabulary + word-work items (great, diet, bike, subjects, education, currency); develop critical thinking (Remember → Evaluate: rank what makes a good place to live) with 3+ sentence arguments; fluency personalisation about their own country.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk lean; set a clear time pressure on every reading task so teens skim/scan instead of reading every word; use a hinge question before moving on; English-only signal during discussion.
By the end, pupils can…
  • predict what a text is about from its title, pictures & layout;
  • find specific facts fast (match, true/false, correct);
  • define 6 word-work items and use them about their own country;
  • rank and justify what makes a place good to live (Evaluate).

Reading sub-skill
Identifying different types of text — before you read, look at titles / pictures / whether you see full sentences or just words & numbers, to guess what information the text gives.
Thinking
Remember (find one thing similar & one different to your country) → Evaluate (order what matters for a happy life).
Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Hinge (text type): “A box with Name / Population / Capital / Currency — is that a story, an opinion, or quick facts?” → quick facts (a fact file).
“Do I read every word to find one number?” → No — I scan for it.
“Dutch = the people / language of…?” → the Netherlands.

Assumptions: countries & nationality adjectives are fresh from P1; be aff/neg known from GMF4 (re-entry) — here it is only recognised in context, not taught. Teens read fluently. Teams — max 4; split by size (12=3×4 · 10=3+3+4 · 9=3×3 · 8=2×4 · 7=3+4).

Lead-in · WDYT hook
board → pairs

Hook. On the board: Where is the best place in the world to be a teenager? Why? Show 3 country photos (Netherlands, Norway, Japan) with no names.

▶ Timed Pair Share (30 sec each): partner A gives one place + one reason, then B. Frame on board: I think … is a good place to be a teenager because …

Quick hands-up vote; take 2–3 reasons. Today we read about the country teenagers voted number one — let's find out why.

Pre-reading · text types
seats, teams

Sub-skill — identifying different types of text. Open SB p.14. ▶ Ex 1 teams match words 1–5 (money · city transport · food · a popular sport · school) to photos A–E — 60-sec race, first team to signal reads out.

Look at the four texts on page 15, but don't read them yet. What TYPE is each one? ▶ Ex 2 (a general info / b unusual info / c one teenager's life / d teenage life in general). Point to clues: Title? Pictures? Full sentences, or just words and numbers?

Hinge check (all show fingers): The box with Name, Population, Capital, Currency — is it (1) a story, (2) an opinion, or (3) quick facts? → 3, a fact file. If a team is unsure, re-model before moving on.

Reading 1 · gist race
seats → audio

Read for gist — against the clock. ▶ Ex 3: Read the four texts QUICKLY — 90 seconds. Don't stop on hard words. Which piece of information from exercise 1 is NOT in the texts? (→ a popular sport). Buzzer stops them.

Read & listen — match. ▶ Ex 4 (audio 10): play the recording; learners match sentence halves 1–5 with a–e (Amsterdam is d · Dutch people love c · Thomas's parents are from e · Most Dutch teens are b · Life at home and school is good a). Pair-check, then whole-class.

Board line to fix the trap: country = the Netherlands · people & language = Dutch.

24–36′
Reading 2 · fact hunt
teams

Read for detail. ▶ Ex 5 — Numbered Heads Together (teams of 4, each pupil numbered 1–4): 7 true/false statements. Decide together — true or false — and CORRECT every false one with a full sentence. You have 5 minutes. Key: 1 F (13) · 2 F (900 km) · 3 T · 4 T · 5 F (Dutch food is good for you) · 6 F (25% is under sea level) · 7 F (it isn't difficult).

Check with accountability. Call a random number: Number 3s — stand. Statement 5: true or false? Correct it. That corrected sentence must use is / isn't / are / aren't — spotlight the recycled be.

📓 Interactive notebook — Text-type detective + place word-bank (hand-made note, no printed sheet this pair)
Reading pairs carry no catalog A5 takeaway (those are Voc/Gram/Speaking/Writing/Translation). Learners build a two-part notebook page they reuse in P3.
  • Draw a 4-box grid: label each Netherlands text by TYPE (fact file · profile “Meet Thomas” · infographic · fun facts) + one clue that gave it away.
  • Start a PLACE WORD-BANK strip: copy the 6 bold/word-work items with a mini-definition — it feeds the “Describe a place” card in P3.
When: ~5 min woven into the fact-hunt check (seats). Prep: ordinary notebooks, pencils, coloured pen. No cutting.

36–45′
Word work · game round
stand up, mingle

Word work (SB Ex 6). First, teams match the 6 definitions to the bold words in the text (very good → great · everything about school & learning → education/subjects · the food people eat → diet · informal word for bicycle → bike · things you study → subjects · money a country uses → currency).

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade — one card each (definition on front, word on back). Stand up, hand up, pair up. A reads the definition, B says the word, swap, then trade cards and find a new partner. 4–5 rounds.

ICQ: Do you read the WORD or the DEFINITION to your partner? (→ the definition).

Speaking · your country
pairs → teams

Personalisation (SB Ex 7). ▶ RallyRobin in pairs — take turns asking & answering the 6 questions about YOUR country: What's the currency? Name three things in a good diet. What's your favourite school subject? Are bikes popular? How old are children when they start school? What's a great place to go with friends in your city?

60–75′
Critical thinking · Evaluate
teams → wall

Remember (SB CT 1). Quick pair task: Find one thing in the text that is the SAME as life in your country, and one thing that is DIFFERENT. Share one each.

Evaluate (SB CT 2) — Diamond ranking. Teams order 8 cards for To be happy, what things are most important?: education · family · free time · food · health · money · people · the weather. Arrange top-to-bottom; agree as a team.

Argue with frames (on board, tiered): We think … is more important than … because … · … matters most because without it you can't … Rule: at least 3 sentences and the word because. Talk move when checking: Why do you think that? Does everyone agree?

 8 factor cards ×3 sets · sentence-frame strip on board
75–85′
Challenge · mini-debate
teams present

Challenge discussion — where would you live? Each team takes a stance: Is the Netherlands really the best place to be a teenager, or is your city better? Give us 3 reasons.

Teams have 4 min to prepare, then 1 spokesperson presents (rotate the role). The listening teams must ask one follow-up question each: Why…? Is it …? What about …?

Vote for the most convincing case. Reinforce place adjectives + be in their reasons.
