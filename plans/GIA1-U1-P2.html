WHAT WE DO: Showdown as a review: a word about the weekend + “battery level”; blitz-rewind of P1: 5 nationalities on mini-boards + a volunteer's micro-pitch

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: One word about your weekend! “Battery” 0–100% on fingers.
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift them together — I see everyone's answer, no hiding.
  3. Rewind of P1: 5 flash countries → nationality in writing on mini-boards (echo of the gate — spelling!), Boards UP! +1 for a clean one.
  4. Volunteer: Your №1 place — one sentence, twenty seconds, no pause! +2.
  5. Bridge: Producers, promotion: today you're DETECTIVES. A magazine made a bold claim — somebody has to check it.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · my list of 5 countries (Brazil · the Netherlands · Turkey · China · New Zealand) — this IS the TB warmer for the reading lesson (country→nationality, finishing on the Netherlands, TB p.20)
👦 Students do: write nationalities and lift together, listen to the micro-pitch. Expected: spelling better than at gate P1. Typical error: Dutch forgotten — “the Netherlands → ?” in chorus.

WHAT WE DO: I announce the mission: verify the claim “the Netherlands is №1 for teenagers”; a verdict only with evidence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Slide MISSION “COUNTRY DETECTIVES”.
  2. I say: A magazine claims: the Netherlands is the №1 country to be a teenager. Your agency fact-checks it TODAY. Verdict at the end — with evidence, or you're fired.
  3. Leaderboard in view, same crews.
📺 On screen: mission slide (or “NL = №1 for teens??” on the board) · leaderboard (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: step into the role of agents. Expected: skepticism “come on, №1?” — the perfect mood for fact-checking.

8–13′
Gate: the lie detector
🎯 GATE · lie detector
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 6 loud claims — T/F in writing BEFORE reading, slips face down; we'll verify with facts after the hunt

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I display 6 claims: Thomas is 12 · Dutch people cycle 1,000 km/year · 75% of NL is under the sea · Dutch food is bad for you · most Dutch teens are happy at school · Thomas is from Amsterdam.
  2. I say: Commit: T or F, in writing, NOW. You can't check yet — that's the point. A detective's guess is worthless without the file. 2 min.
  3. I command everyone to turn the slips face down until the hunt. (Key for myself: F/F/F/F/T/F.)
📺 On screen: slide with the 6 claims (or in a column on the board) · answer slips (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: commit their guesses in writing and turn the slips over. Expected: 3–4 “caught” by plausible fakes. Typical error: peeking into the Student's Book — “Closed books! Guessing is the point!”

Sprint: text type by look
⚡ Base sprint · type the texts
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: 30 seconds WITHOUT reading: the type of each of 4 texts by look only + a photo match in 60 sec

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I say: Thirty seconds, DON'T read page 15 — match the four texts to a–d by LOOK only: numbers? photos? sentences?
  2. I check with a justification: +2 for a match with a reason (%-signs = fun facts panel!).
  3. I finish off ex.1: Words 1–5 to photos A–E — sixty seconds! Points in the bank.
📺 On screen: the Student's Book pp.14–15 · board: 4 type icons (📊 · 🧑 · 📰 · 🎉)
👦 Students do: identify text types by look in seconds and explain the giveaway. Expected: instant — their speed. Typical error: starting to read — “Look, don't read! Detectives scan first”.
⭐ Fast finishers (TB p.20): write down what you see in each photo (bicycles, a pancake…).		SB pp.14–15
20–33′
Challenge: a raid for facts across 4 stations
🔥 Challenge · timed fact raid (jigsaw)
4 stations

WHAT WE DO: everyone raids THEIR OWN station-text (3 facts, 4-min limit), reports under the 5-second rule; we open the lie detector

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 4 text-stations hung up before the lesson; I hand out hunt cards (3 facts per crew member).
  2. ICQ: Copy sentences? — No! Short answers: Amsterdam, euro, 17 million.
  3. I start: Four minutes cap — RAID!
  4. Return: report to the crew under the 5-second rule (What's the currency? — …five seconds!), a team sheet for 12 facts.
  5. We open the detector slips: Verify your guesses — WITH the source: False! Text 2 says Thomas is 13. +1 for a correction with a source.
📺 On screen: 4 stations · hunt cards · team sheets · timer · audio 10 as optional background (Backup: no track — I read the script aloud at listening speed.)
👦 Students do: raid stations solo, report against the timer, check their morning guesses against the facts and cite the source. Expected: “My guess was wrong — text 4 says 25%!”. Typical error: a fact without a source — no point given, the evidence rule.
⭐ For the fast ones: the “Fact+” envelope — a number, a name, and a % nobody has used yet.		stations · hunt cards · timer
33–42′
The word-work duel: six words from the text
Word work duel
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: Numbered Heads Together: partners split 6 definitions in half and drill each other under the 5-second rule; a sentence about Russia

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I split the roles: Partner A owns definitions 1–3, B owns 4–6. Find your bold words in the text — two minutes.
  2. I run the check as Kagan Numbered Heads Together: numbers 1–4 in the crew, Heads together — make sure EVERYONE in your crew can answer! → I call a number — exactly that person answers, no prompting from the floor.
  3. Duel: Test each other. Five-second rule! The money people use? — Currency!
  4. Finale: every pair — a sentence about Russia with any word; the best from the crew read out loud (+1 richness for an adjective).
📺 On screen: the Student's Book p.14 ex.6 · timer
👦 Students do: own their trio of words and drill their partner at speed; compose a sentence about Russia. Expected: “Our education is free”. Typical error: an is/are slip — I note it silently: fuel for gate P3.
⭐ TB Challenge (p.21): duel answers — FULL sentences ONLY: The currency of the Netherlands is the euro.✔ Assess: are is/are slips still alive after P1 — 2 examples for gate P3.		SB p.14

Bonus level: interrogating Thomas (Have you ever…?)
🚀 Bonus level · interview Thomas
pairs

WHAT WE DO: role play: one is Thomas from the text, the other a detective with 3 “Have you ever…?” questions (GMF4 structures)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I raise the status: Beginners ask Thomas: ‘Are you Dutch?’ You can time-travel his whole LIFE. Not in this book.
  2. I write the frames: Have you ever (lived in England / cycled to school in snow)? — Yes, I have. / No, never.
  3. Role play: A = Thomas (facts from the text + imagination), B = detective with 3 questions. Swap.
  4. The class's best question → +2 to the leaderboard.
📺 On screen: board: Have you ever…? frames · facts about Thomas on station №2 nearby
👦 Students do: interrogate “Thomas” with the perfect, answer in character. Expected: “Have you ever eaten Dutch cheese? — Yes, I have!”. Typical error: “Did you ever…” — I accept it (also correct!), praise both versions.

51–62′
The happiness ranking — ammunition for the debate
Evaluate · happiness ranking (debate ammo)
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: crews rank 8 cards on “what matters most for happiness”; their top-3 becomes the line of argument in the debate

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Remember step: One similar + one different thing vs Russia — sixty seconds, huddle.
  2. I hand out sets of 8 cards, launch Evaluate: Order them 1–8 for happiness. Everyone touches at least two cards. Four minutes.
  3. I charge them up for the debate: Your top-3 = your ammunition: if NL wins on YOUR top-3 — the claim stands. If not — it falls.
📺 On screen: sets of 8 cards (education·family·free time·food·health·money·people·weather)
👦 Students do: rank the cards, knowing the ranking will become their argument. Expected: arguments over money/free time — already in frames. Typical error: arguing in Russian — the argument doesn't count (the rule's been announced).
⭐ TB Challenge (p.21): explain your order with a partner, using the models: (Health) is number 1 on my list — I'm not happy when I'm ill. For me, (money) isn't important, but people are.		8-card sets
62–80′
Product: trio debates — a verdict backed by facts
🏁 PRODUCT · verdict debates in trios
trio circles

WHAT WE DO: trios: prosecutor against defender (90 sec each), the judge expects 3 facts; we rotate roles 3 times

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out roles and cards: Prosecutor (NL isn't №1 because…) · Defender (It is — text 3 says…) · Judge (card: ☐ 3 facts? ☐ answered the opponent? verdict).
  2. Rules of order: 90 sec per side + 30 sec for the verdict. Facts — ONLY from the hunt sheets; a made-up fact = a point to the opponent.
  3. I rotate roles ×3 — everyone gets to try all of them.
  4. Judging points go to the leaderboard, gold-run debaters announced.
📺 On screen: role cards · judging cards · team fact sheets
👦 Students do: debate by the rules, judge by the card, swap roles. Expected: quotes like “text 3 says 95%!”. Typical error: emotion instead of facts — the judge doesn't tick the box, the side loses quietly.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — clean forms (1–3) · Richness — adjectives, connectors, bonus structures (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		role cards · judge cards

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can verify claims against four informational texts and deliver a 60-sec verdict supported by 3 facts.
  • Subsidiary. Reading subskill (identifying types of text) applied at speed; Word work as a duel; Critical thinking Evaluate = ranked debate in trios; GMF4 bonus: interview Thomas with Have you ever…?
  • Personal. Reading is where continuers actually stretch — texts are new even if grammar isn't. Keep hunts timed and competitive; verdicts get the full judging bar.
By the end, students can…
  • commit predictions and verify them in texts
  • identify a text type in 10 seconds by look
  • define/use 6 Word-work items
  • argue a verdict with facts, win a trio debate

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Detectives read every word?” → No — they scan for THE fact.
“A claim without a fact — does it win a debate?” → Never.
“Have you ever…? — which tense, GMF4 grads?” → Present perfect. Not in this book. In you — yes.

Assumptions: be is fluent; the NL content and Word-work items are genuinely new. Debate format needs training wheels: frames + judge cards. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“Reading is boring” resistanceNever ‘read the text’ — always ‘verify the claim in 90 seconds’. The stopwatch reframes everything.
Scanning = random shoutingAnswers must include WHERE: “text 4 says…” — evidence rule, +1 only with the source.
Debate collapses into RussianFrame strips + rule: only English sentences count as arguments.
Word work done by the strongest onlyDuel format: definitions split between partners, each owns three.
Fast crews idle after huntsEnvelope «Fact+»: find a number, a name, a % not yet used by anyone.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
