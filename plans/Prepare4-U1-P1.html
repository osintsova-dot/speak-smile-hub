Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to describe people’s appearance and personality and choose between Present Simple and Present Continuous to talk about habits/facts vs. what is happening now — in the context of describing themselves and people they know.
  • Subsidiary. Reading for gist then detail (SB p. 11 text); recycling Present tenses from Prepare 3; fluency in a personalised speaking task; learner training in recording lexis as chunks.
  • Personal. Keep TTT low; set an ICQ before every task; use guided discovery (elicit rules, don’t lecture); stage a stirrer/settler rhythm across 90 min with one mid-lesson break.
By the end, learners can…
  • use ~14 appearance + personality adjectives accurately in sentences;
  • read a short text for gist and locate specific detail;
  • form & contrast Present Simple / Present Continuous and self-correct the two typical mistakes.

Structure
Present Simple (habits/facts) ↔ Present Continuous (now/temporary). She wears glasses. / She’s wearing sunglasses today.
Chunks
have + adj + hair (She has curly blonde hair) · be/look/feel + adj (He looks miserable; I feel confident) · in her early twenties.
Form
PS: V / V-s · do/does + not + V · Do/Does…? — signals: usually, often, every day. PC: am/is/are + V-ing · Am/Is/Are…? — signals: now, at the moment, today.
Pron. watch
Word stress: at·tractive, con·fi·dent; /ɜː/ in curly; weak are /ə/ in “What are they wearing?”.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
elderly — “Is this person young or old?” → old  ·  middle-aged — “About 15 or about 45?” → 45.
“She’s wearing a jacket.” — “Every day or right now?” → now → Present Continuous.
“She wears glasses.” — “Just today or generally?” → generally → Present Simple.

Assumptions: from Prepare 3, learners already form the Present Simple and Present Continuous — today is choosing between them, not first presentation. They can read a short factual text and know a few appearance words (tall, short, hair). Groups: pairs for talk, teams of 4 for Quiz-Quiz-Trade; odd numbers → one trio.

0–8′
Lead-in
seats → board

Hook. Project 3–4 photos of well-known people (a footballer, a singer, an actor, a grandparent-type). Look at these people. Which one is most like someone in your family? Tell your partner why.

Elicit + set the topic. Take 2–3 ideas open-class; board any useful words learners produce (hair, tall, kind…). Today we describe people — how they look and what they’re like — and we look at when we use two present tenses. Open the book at page 10, Unit 1, “All about me”.

ICQ: Are we describing looks only? — No. Looks AND personality.

8–24′
Vocabulary 1 · age, looks, hair
seats → mingle

Meaning first. Use the SB p. 10 vocabulary set. Sort the words into looks (attractive, bald, blonde, curly, dark, elderly, fair, good-looking, handsome, middle-aged, pretty, straight, teenage) and personality (cheerful, confident, friendly, funny, miserable, polite, shy + opposites). Present the tricky pairs together with a quick board sketch/gesture: curly ↔ straight, fair/blonde ↔ dark, friendly ↔ unfriendly.

CCQ + drill. Check with the CCQs above (elderly / middle-aged). Model the chunks and drill chorally then individually: She has curly blonde hair. He’s in his early twenties. My cousin is really cheerful. Watch word stress (at·trac·tive, con·fi·dent).

24–42′
Reading · gist → detail
seats, pairs

Predict. Books open at the SB p. 11 text. Look at the title and the picture. What do you think this text is about? 15 seconds — then tell your partner.

Gist task (fast). Set ONE global question before reading. Read quickly — one minute. What is the main idea? ICQ: Reading every word? — No, fast, for the main idea. Pair-check → open class (1 answer).

Detail task. Learners do the SB comprehension questions (true/false / matching / detail — as printed on the page). Now read carefully and answer. Underline where you found each answer. Set a time limit; monitor.

Peer check → feedback. Compare with your partner. Give the line that proves it. Open-class feedback by nomination; accept only answers with evidence.

Vocab from text + reaction. Match the 3–4 highlighted words to meanings (SB). Then a short ▶ Think–Pair–Share reacting to the text’s idea: Do you agree? Why / why not? (recycles the new adjectives in real use).

47–66′
Grammar · discovery
board → seats

Guided discovery (I do → we do). Board the pair from the vocab set: She wears glasses. / She’s wearing sunglasses today. Ask: Which sentence is about every day? Which is about right now? Which words tell you — “glasses/sunglasses”, or “today”? Elicit the two meanings (usual/fact ↔ now/temporary) and the time signals (usually, every day ↔ now, at the moment, today).

Form check (concept + form). Elicit the forms onto the board (V/V-s vs am/is/are + V-ing; questions/negatives). Highlight the TWO errors from the problems row: Does she wears…? ✘ · She wearing… ✘ — elicit the fix.

A5 grammar card — the question “Is it usual/a fact, or happening now?” splitting into USUAL/FACT vs NOW/TEMPORARY, the paired example (wears / is wearing), and the two common mistakes fixed on the card.
  • Glue the card in and colour the two sets of time clues in two colours.
  • Highlight the two fixed mistakes — they are the ones the class makes.
  • Write a pair of sentences about yourself: one usual, one right now.
  • Check the helper: after do/does the verb goes back to the base form.
When: straight after the rule is on the board, as the anchor for practice. Prep: 1 × A5 per pupil; scissors, glue, coloured pencils.

Controlled practice (you do). Ss do the SB p. 11 grammar exercises (choose the correct form / complete). ▶ RallyCoach (Kagan) in pairs: A solves item 1 aloud, B coaches & ticks; swap for item 2. Monitor for the two target errors. Feedback by nomination; re-elicit the concept, not just the answer (Why continuous here?).

66–82′
Freer practice · About you
pairs, standing option

Personalised production. Task: describe yourself and one person you know, using both tenses and today’s adjectives. Give sentence frames on the board (scaffold — remove for stronger Ss): My [best friend] is [confident] and quite [funny]. He/She has [dark curly] hair and is in his/her [early twenties]. Right now, he/she is probably [studying / working]…

Rehearse → perform. ▶ Timed Pair Share: A speaks 60 sec while B listens for “one thing in common / one difference”; swap. Then ▶ RallyRobin: pairs take turns naming a classmate/celebrity while the partner guesses from the description. Monitor and note errors + good language for feedback (do NOT interrupt fluency).

ICQ: Do you use only one tense? — No, both: what they’re like AND what they’re doing now.
