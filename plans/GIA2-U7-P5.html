🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): Past questions and ago are not announced as the topic: what students get is a press conference where a broken question costs you your turn. The book supplies the whole mechanism — the tables in ex.1, building questions in ex.2–3, the “circle machine” in ex.6 with ago, the Grammar round-up ex.7 about Yash Gupta. The clinic of burnt forms from P3–P4 opens here: “What did you ate” is worked through with no names and becomes the law of the classroom.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Press conference. Doors open. One of you sits in the hot seat as a hero — Thomas from the boat, Easton with his robot hand, Susie in the Formula 1 car, Amineh with her poem. The rest of you are the press. You get ninety seconds to get the story out of them. But here's the rule: a broken question ends your turn. 'What did you ate?' — sit down. 'When did you born?' — sit down. Clean questions only. Who's first in the chair?”
The gist, if you need to restate it: a press conference: one student in the hot seat plays a hero from the unit, the rest are the press. 90 seconds to get the story out. A broken question (“What did you ate?”) ends that journalist's turn.

WHAT WE DO: a 90-second press conference: the hero in the chair, the press asking clean questions, a broken question ends the turn

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes of preparation: Heroes — prepare your story in the past. Press — prepare five questions with FIVE different question words.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can build clean past simple questions (Did … ? / What–When–How–How much–Who did … ?) with short answers, use ago correctly, and run a 90-second press conference.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 two tables; ex.2 building five questions on the podcast; ex.3 rewriting five sentences; ex.4 interviews in pairs; ex.5 time words in ascending order; ex.6 the circles with ago; ex.7 Grammar round-up (Yash Gupta) + Research.
  • Personal. The word “grammar” is not said: say “the rules of the press conference”. Open the P3–P4 clinic here. Keep the error log running — you will need it in P8.
By the end, students can…
  • build a Yes/No question with Did and answer it short
  • build a Wh- question with a question word + did
  • keep the main verb in the infinitive after did
  • use ago after a period of time (two days ago, a year ago)

Lexis

Structure
Did it cost a lot? — Yes, it did. · Did she speak English? — No, she didn't. · When did they move? — A year ago. · How much did Momo's hand cost? · 🚀 bonus: What time did you get up?
Form
question = (question word) + did + subject + the infinitive of the verb. The past lives in did, so the verb stays clean. ago always goes AFTER the period of time and never takes a plural.
Pron. watch
did in a question is unstressed and almost swallowed: “What-d-you say?” — but in the short answer it is stressed: “Yes, I DID.” That contrast is what a live question sounds like.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“What did you ate?” — where is the mistake? → did is already past: What did you EAT?
“When did you born?” → this one takes be: When WERE you born?
“ago ten minutes” — is that right? → no: ten minutes AGO.

Assumptions: SB p.90, syllabus pair 58. The irregular forms were fixed in P3 and the P4 podcast is fresh in mind — the questions in ex.2 are built on it. Did…? is partly familiar from A1+ but falls apart at speed.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“Did you saw?” — a double pastThe switch gesture from P3 works here too: one hand on did — “the past is here”, the other opens — “the verb is clean”. The turn burns without a word.
One-word answers and the conversation diesThe press conference rule: a short answer + one detail. “Yes, I did” on its own does not count as a turn.
ago gets stuck anywhereA prompt on the board: [a period of time] + ago. Check it with your finger: ago is always on the right. And never “agos”.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
