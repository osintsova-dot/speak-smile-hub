🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Listen to four speakers arguing about whether we rely on technology too much and identify both their position and their reasoning, then use gerunds and infinitives (2), where the form after stop, remember, forget and like decides what the sentence actually means.
What is genuinely hard
The listening is a double judgement — agree or disagree, AND is it a real problem or not — so a student who hears only the topic gets half the marks. The grammar is worse in a quieter way: Russian marks the difference between «I stopped in order to smoke» and «I gave up smoking» with extra words, so the student never has to choose. English makes the choice invisible and puts it in one suffix.
Typical error to expect
«I stopped to smoke» for «I gave up smoking»; «remember to lock» for «remember locking» — with stop, remember, forget and like the FORM chooses the meaning. Russian expresses the difference with extra words, so the student never has to decide — until the exam makes them.
«interested in see», «responsible for produce», «think about spend» — after a preposition English takes the -ing form, always, and Russian takes an infinitive. It is one rule and it is worth four marks across a paper.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson opens on an opinion the class has to take a position on before hearing anybody else's — that is what makes the recording worth listening to. The second listening is done with the ten statements read and key-worded first, which is the exam habit, and the answers are taken with the proof line, not with the letter. The grammar half then grows out of the same topic: the pairs of sentences on p.116 are about multitasking, chores and satnavs. The rule is built from meaning towards form, never the other way, and the productive stage is personal — things you have stopped doing, and things you always forget to do.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“A man drove into a lake and blamed his GPS. Another person keeps a list of everything they cannot remember any more. Four people, one question: do we rely on technology too much? And then one letter — «to» or «-ing» — that changes what your sentence means.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: two minutes of private thinking, then a real disagreement in the room

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two minutes, silent, individually: do you agree with the statement? Write two reasons. Nobody speaks yet — this is the part that makes the discussion honest.
  2. Kagan Think-Pair-Share, three minutes: compare with a partner, then one pair reports the place where they disagreed, not the place where they agreed.
  3. Take a show of hands and write the score on the board. It gets compared with the four speakers at minute forty.
  4. Pre-teach only three items, thirty seconds: rely on, infallible (never wrong), common sense.
📺 On screen: the class vote written up as two numbers on the board — it is compared with the four speakers later
👦 Students do: write two reasons alone, argue them with a partner, then vote publicly

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 with track 51: four speakers, agree or disagree, and is it a real problem

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Move break · the opinion line
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: four minutes on their feet, sorting themselves along a line of opinion

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. One wall is STRONGLY AGREE, the opposite wall STRONGLY DISAGREE. Everybody stands somewhere on the imaginary line between them.
  2. You call four short statements from the recording — Technology is making our memories worse. · A satnav is no excuse for bad driving. · We could not survive without technology now. — and everyone moves.
  3. After each move, two people who are standing far apart say one sentence each. No debate, just position and reason.
  4. Last instruction before they sit: read the ten statements in ex.3 standing up, and underline the key idea in each. That is the preparation for the second play, done on their feet.
📺 On screen: no media — the statements are called out by you
👦 Students do: physically place themselves four times, justify once, then key-word the ten statements before sitting

Listening · second play, with the proof line
individual → pairs → class
Why: Listening · second play, with the proof line

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: ten statements matched to four speakers, each answer defended with what was said

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two minutes in pairs before the audio: for the underlined key idea in each statement, say a synonym. Technology → the Internet, computers; minds → brains. This is the step that stops word-matching.
  2. Play track 51 a second time, straight through. Students write the speaker number next to each statement.
  3. One minute in pairs to compare, then check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: A 4 · B 3 · C 4 · D 1 · E 2 · F 2 · G 3 · H 1 · I 1 · J 3.
  4. For each answer the nominated student says the PROOF, not the letter. Two proofs worth having said aloud: D — «It's like a muscle. If you don't use it, you lose it!» and G — «technology became the solution to all our problems, but now it is the problem because we need it to survive».
  5. Critical thinkers, ex.4, four minutes in pairs: which two or three of the arguments are the strongest, and why? Take two answers to the floor and compare them with the class vote still written on the board.
📺 On screen: track 51, second play. Backup: the same Teacher's Book audioscript — hand out or read the four turns and let the class find the ten statements in the text, which is a different skill but keeps the content
👦 Students do: synonym-map ten statements, listen again, then defend each answer with a line from the recording

Why: Grammar · discovery, from meaning to form

WHAT WE DO: ex.1a and 1b: five pairs of sentences, one letter apart, and what actually changes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books at p.116, right-hand column. Warmer, thirty seconds, spoken: enjoy, learn, agree, avoid, suggest, want, decide, can't stand, manage, don't mind — gerund or infinitive? The class answers in chorus. This is the known part.
  2. ex.1a in pairs, three minutes: in which of the five pairs do the two sentences mean the SAME thing? Answer: only pair 2 (started to multitask = started multitasking).
  3. Take the four pairs that differ one by one, and draw them. Two stick figures for stop, a door and a key for remember. Ninety seconds of chalk replaces five minutes of explanation.
  4. ex.1b, three minutes: match the eight verb patterns to their meanings. Answers 1 a, 2 b, 3 b, 4 a, 5 a, 6 b, 7 b, 8 a.
  5. The class writes the four decisive lines into their notebooks in their own words: stop to do / stop doing · remember to do / remember doing · forget to do / forget doing · like to do / like doing.
  6. Grammar reference for anyone who wants it in writing is SB p.120.
📺 On screen: the four stick-figure pairs drawn on the board and left there — chalk only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: decide which pairs mean the same, match eight patterns to meanings, then write the four contrasts themselves

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 (eight choices with reasons) and ex.3 (ten transformations)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2, four minutes, individually: eight sentences, choose the form. The rule of the stage: every answer comes with a reason in five words.
  2. Check with the reasons attached — 1 to charge (it's a good idea) · 2 watching (the activity ended) · 3 to text (intended, didn't) · 4 playing (a memory) · 5 to bring (intended, didn't) · 6 to switch (remember you need to) · 7 sitting (stop the activity) · 8 reading (enjoys it).
  3. ex.3, five minutes: ten transformations. Kagan RallyCoach — one pen, partners take turns to say the answer while the other writes and challenges.
  4. Check the four that carry the marks: 3 'll always remember breaking · 4 remember to help me · 8 remembered to back up · 10 never forget dropping my phone.
  5. Fast finishers get the Teacher's Book task: rewrite items 3, 4, 5, 8 and 10 using the OTHER verb of the pair and see how the meaning moves.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 and ex.5: the five environments where the form is automatic, then ten errors in a real text

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 with books shut. Write five environments on the board and let the class decide the form for each: after a preposition · as the subject of a sentence · to explain why you do something · after certain adjectives · with go for physical activities. (gerund, gerund, infinitive, infinitive, gerund.)
  2. Pre-teach one word for the text — skull (the bones of the head) — and set the context in one line: our skulls may be changing shape because of the way we hold our phones.
  3. ex.5, six minutes, individually: ten sentences in the text, find and correct. Answers — 1 stopped spending · 2 looking at · 3 responsible for producing · 4 normal to find · 5 has decided to investigate · 6 interested in seeing · 7 big enough to be · 8 think about spending · 9 like playing · 10 remember to do.
  4. Every correction is said aloud in the same formula: «in» is a preposition, so «seeing». Four repetitions of that formula is the point of the stage.
  5. Fast finishers answer the four comprehension questions from the Teacher's Book on the same text.
📺 On screen: the five environments listed on the board before the text is opened — they are the checklist for the corrections
👦 Students do: build the five-environment list as a class, then repair ten errors alone and justify each one aloud

WHAT WE DO: ex.6: a personal task that cannot be done without today's grammar

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three stems on the board: Something I stopped doing last year … · Something I always forget to do … · Something I'll never forget doing …
  2. Two minutes, silent, in writing: finish all three. Every sentence contains one of today's four verbs, and the form is underlined.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can identify opinion and reasoning in a four-speaker recording and choose between gerund and infinitive when the form changes the meaning.
  • Subsidiary. Reading a set of statements for key ideas before listening; justifying an answer with a heard line; gerunds after prepositions and as subjects.

Structure
I stopped to think about it. / I stopped thinking about it. · I'll never forget breaking my first phone. · Remember to back up your files. · I like driving without a satnav.
Form
verb + gerund or infinitive with NO change of meaning (start, begin, continue, love, hate, prefer) · verb + gerund or infinitive WITH a change (stop, remember, forget, like) · gerund after every preposition, as the subject of a clause, and after go for activities · infinitive to explain why, and after adjectives.
Pron. watch
the /tə/ of a weak «to» disappears in speech (I forgot to call = /fəˈɡɒtə/) which is why students hear the gerund everywhere · rely on /rɪˈlaɪ ɒn/ links into one word · chores /tʃɔːz/, not «chorez»
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«He stopped to charge his phone.» Did he keep walking? → no, he stopped in order to charge. «He stopped charging his phone.» → he was charging, now he isn't.
«I remember locking the door.» Is the door locked? → yes, and I have the memory. «I remembered to lock the door.» → I had to do it and I did.
«Interested in …» — what form comes next? → -ing. Why? → because «in» is a preposition, and that is the whole rule.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Half the class hears only the topic in the first listeningThat is why the first play answers only two small questions per speaker (A or D, P or NP) and nothing else. The ten statements are not even looked at until the recording has been heard once.
Statements matched by a repeated wordBefore the second play, pairs underline the KEY IDEA in each statement and think of a synonym for it. Then the recording's own words cannot be used as a shortcut.
«I stopped to smoke» produced for «I gave up»Both versions go on the board with a stick figure each — one walking and stopping, one with a cigarette in the bin. Ninety seconds of drawing beats five minutes of explanation.
The gerund-after-preposition rule is known but not appliedEvery correction in ex.5 is said out loud in the same formula: «in» is a preposition, so «seeing». Four repetitions and it starts to transfer.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 1–9 (the listening section: task 1 is the multiple-matching of speakers the class does today) and tasks 19–25.

Four speakers, one statement each, distractors that repeat the words of the wrong option — this is the machine of EGE task 1, and Gateway runs it in Cambridge clothes. The transferable habit is the same in both papers: decide from the IDEA of the whole turn, not from a matching word. The grammar half is the gerund–infinitive contrast, which the Russian paper tests in tasks 19–25 through remember / forget / stop, three verbs where the form changes the meaning and Russian gives no warning at all.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
