🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): For new students the project is not built “from nothing” but from a model: first we go through the finished Glasgow plan and see what went into it and what did not, then we build our own on the same skeleton. The Super skill of the unit (speaking so people understand) is given through concrete phrases on cards, not through an appeal to “everybody join in”. The Quick review is sewn into the middle as stations — revision for the test without the word “test”. And the year is closed here too: the question of the unit and the ribbon of eight units.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“The last big thing we build this year. Marta, Emily and Nathan went to Glasgow for three days. They stayed in a B & B because it's cheaper than a hotel. Day one: burgers, because everyone likes burgers. Day two: the Science Centre and a film. Day three: the transport museum and home. That's the whole plan — and it fits on one page. Your group builds one just like it. You have a model, you have the phrases, and everybody in the group speaks. Three days. Go.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the last big thing of the year. Marta, Emily and Nathan went to Glasgow for three days — the plan fits on one page. Your group builds one like it from the model. There is a model, there are the phrases, and everybody speaks.

WHAT WE DO: circle + collecting the message exchanges + a warm-up on the Graphic organiser p.121

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Circle: One place you'd like to visit for three days.
  2. The homework exchanges onto the desks: You already know how to agree on a day and a place. That's half the project.
  3. Warm-up on the Graphic organiser p.121 (TB routine): Five minutes with a partner. Look at the unit map: types of holiday, places to stay, facilities, activities. What do you remember? Model it on yourself first: point at the map and say two examples out loud — a camping holiday … a swimming pool …
  4. Then Kagan RallyRobin: in pairs, taking turns, one word from the map per turn. Can't remember? Say pass and the turn moves on, that is not a loss. The map stays open: the point is not to test memory but to get the language of the unit moving before the project.
  5. While the pairs are talking, walk round the class and write 5–6 of the words that came up most often on the board — they will be useful in the plans.

👦 Students do: go through the unit map in pairs, taking turns, with the book open. Expect: the map does half the revision, and taking turns makes even the ones who usually just nod speak

WHAT WE DO: STEP 2: Super skills + Key phrases on cards + choosing the city and filling in the skeleton

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The Super skills tips, simply and briefly: Look at each other. Give a reason. Ask the quiet one. Agree together.
  2. The Key phrases onto cards, two each: I think this is a good idea because … · Do we all agree? · You're very quiet. What do you think? · What do you think is best? Say them chorally.
  3. Show one group what it looks like live: sit down with them and act out thirty seconds of a discussion out loud with a phrase from the card — I think the museum is a good idea because …
  4. And only now start Kagan Talking Chips: three chips each, every contribution costs one — you speak and you put a chip into the middle of the table. Out of chips — you listen until the others have put theirs down. That is the Super skill of the unit turned into a rule: the structure gives the quiet student a turn and the loud one a stop, and “everybody speaks” happens by itself, without your reminders.
  5. Choosing the city — 60 seconds, then fill in the skeleton line by line. TB advice: they may look online and write down places and food they liked.
  6. The five conditions of ex.5 on the screen: accommodation · at least one cultural and one outdoor activity · interesting to all three · varied food · not too expensive.
📺 On screen: SB pp.106–107 · Key phrases cards · three chips per person · the skeletons · tablets/phones
👦 Students do: reach agreement using the cards. Expect: the phrase “You're very quiet. What do you think?” works literally — the quiet ones start speaking

Check before you set off · Quick review stations
stations

WHAT WE DO: 4 stations of 4 min: vocabulary · will · going to · present continuous (SB p.105)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before you go, four checks. Four minutes each. Your sheets and cards are allowed — this is not a test.
  2. Station 1 — the four lexical sets of Quick review p.105 (types of holiday · places to stay · facilities · holiday activities) through Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: the card has the word on one side and the translation and a picture on the other. Model the move with one student in front of everyone: you ask — they answer — you praise — you swap cards. Then the same but everyone at once; looking at the back of the card is allowed.
  3. Station 2 — will: two predictions, one question, one short answer.
  4. Station 3 — going to: an affirmative, a negative, a question, a short answer.
  5. Station 4 — present continuous: a calendar → three sentences with a time and a person.
  6. Stations 2–4 finish with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: you give one check item, the group puts the answer together and makes sure any of them could say it, then you call a number and that student answers for the whole group. The sheets and cards are open: what matters here is that the answer passes through every student, not that it is recalled from memory.
  7. A personal list: Write down what YOU missed. That's your test list — and it's short.
📺 On screen: SB p.105 Quick review · station cards · timer (Backup: four A3 sheets in the corners of the room, with the groups moving round)
👦 Students do: go through the stations with support. Expect: permission to use their sheets takes the anxiety away and the revision really works
📓 Interactive notebook — Get_Involved_A2_Unit8_Translation_Match_It_2up_A4 + Get_Involved_A2_Unit8_Translation_Translate_It_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to English A–L (holiday types and places to stay, will for predictions, going to and arrangements, holiday activities, invitation and refusal), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 from memory. The last translation sheet of the year.
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the unit's exit line.

WHAT WE DO: STEP 3: finishing the skeleton + How to… tips p.121 + rehearsal in roles

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Fill the sheet first. Colour it later. Twelve minutes.
  2. How to… tips p.121, kept simple: Start with 'I'm going to talk about …' · Use first, after that, finally · Say thank you at the end. Write those three words straight into the margins of the skeleton.
  3. Rehearsal in roles — Kagan Timed RoundRobin: round the circle, 40 seconds per person, everyone says their day out loud from the completed skeleton while the group listens without interrupting. Go round twice: the first time just to say it at all, the second already with first / after that / finally. Before the first round, say your own “day” yourself as a model, so they can hear how notes sound in speech.
  4. A form check before the stage: Where's your 'we're staying'? Where's your 'we're going to'? Both must be there.
  5. TB advice: think about what you will be asked and write the answer down.
📺 On screen: the skeletons · markers · SB p.121 How to… tips · timer
👦 Students do: fill it in and rehearse. Expect: rehearsing out loud twice is what makes the presentation possible for the quiet ones

WHAT WE DO: STEP 4: presenting to another group (in small groups) + peer review + Final reflection

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The TB presentation tips, short and to the point: Look at your audience — or just above their heads if you're nervous. Speak slowly. Don't fidget. If you make a mistake, stay calm and start the sentence again.
  2. Present group to group, not to the whole class (TB advice for unconfident students).
  3. Peer review ex.8, kept simple: Would you go on this holiday? Is it clear? One question for them.
  4. Final reflection p.107 in three questions: Is the plan clear? Did everyone in the group speak? Did you use language from the unit — give one example.
  5. Praise something specific: Clear day! Good 'after that'! Everyone spoke!

👦 Students do: present in small groups. Expect: the group-to-group format lets everyone speak, including the quietest

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, groups can build and present a three-day holiday plan from a model, using project Key phrases, and revise the whole unit through Quick review stations.
  • Subsidiary. The project video as a model; STEP 1 going through the model plan; STEP 2 Super skills + Key phrases; STEP 3 building it on the skeleton; STEP 4 presenting to small groups; Quick review p.105 as stations; Final reflection; closing the question of the unit and of the year.
  • Personal. Hand out the plan skeleton printed — they fill it in line by line instead of inventing the shape. Give every student in the group their block and their phrase in advance.
By the end, students can…
  • fill in a three-day plan with accommodation, activities and meals
  • use two project Key phrases in a group discussion
  • present my part of the plan from notes
  • pass all Quick-review stations of Unit 8

Structure
I think this is a good idea because … · Do we all agree? · You're very quiet. What do you think? · What do you think is best? · I think this is the best option because … · I don't think that's a good idea because …
Form
the language of a plan is short notes, each item on a new line (TB Model project notes). In the presentation: We're staying at a B & B (an arrangement) · We're going to visit the museum (a plan) · It'll be great (a prediction).
Pron. watch
the three signpost words the presentation rests on: first · after that · finally — with a pause after each
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Do you need the price of every ticket in the plan? → no (TB key ex.2): too much detail and the plan stops being readable.
Can you write it all out in sentences and read it off the sheet? → no: the notes are there so that you SPEAK (TB Model project notes).
Is the Quick review a test? → no, it is a check before you set off.

Assumptions: SB pp.105–107, syllabus pair 68. All the language of the unit has been covered; what is new is working as a three and presenting in public.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They don't know where to startThe plan skeleton is printed: three days, each with the lines “where we sleep · morning · lunch · afternoon · evening”. Fill it in line by line.
One talks, two are silentEveryone has been given their block (their day) and their Key phrase on a card in advance. TB advice: settle who does what BEFORE building it, not just before the stage.
The project eats the revisionThe revision stations take 16 minutes IN THE MIDDLE of the lesson, before building — they are “the check before you set off”.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
