🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that two units work without the book: 24 words, the article and the determiner set, the relative pronoun, the conjunction, and correct spelling.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the two invisible things. The article cannot be heard in Russian at all, so it is the first thing to disappear under time pressure; and which versus that survives orally and collapses on paper.
Typical error to expect
I went to cinema · She is best student in our class — the article simply dropped, because Russian has none [high priority, A2/B1]
the film what I saw yesterday — what used as a relative pronoun, a direct carry-over from Russian «который» [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is retrieval practice, not re-teaching: the two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it and what shows you, twenty minutes before the papers, which forms still break. Unlike blocks 3 and 5, this block HAS a Review page in the coursebook — Review 3 on SB p.74–75 covers Units 9–12, so the stations run on real book material and cover the whole half-year rather than just these two units. The Life Skills spread sits between them, six minutes, because its project is a film-poster competition and it lands straight on Unit 12. The game comes before the briefing on purpose: it is the settler that turns revision into a reason to argue, and the briefing is only useful once you have seen which five words belong in the «watch out» column. And the paper itself is different this time — for the first time this year it is the official Cambridge Unit test, two of them, with audio and an answer key.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. First forty-five minutes we play the block back — and the revision page in your book covers four units, not two, so some of it is from before the winter holidays. Then forty-five minutes you show it. This time the paper is the real Cambridge one. Your score is not the point: your own list of five mistakes is.”

WHAT WE DO: «Four in a row!» — the Cambridge review game for Units 9–12

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two teams of two to six. One word grid and one Topics ticklist per group; each team needs a different coloured pen. A team picks a word from the grid AND a topic from the ticklist, then makes a sentence on that topic containing that word. If the other team accepts the sentence, the team colours in the circle and the topic is ticked off and cannot be used again until all the topics have gone. A wrong sentence loses both the word and the topic — they stay available. The winner is the first team to colour four connected circles in a line — vertical, horizontal or diagonal.
  2. The grid covers Units 9–12, so most of it is revision of the whole half-year, which is exactly what we want two days before the test. The cells that belong to OUR two units: old town · shopping area · skyscraper · stadium · palace · fountain · mosque · cathedral · money · electricity · jewellery · staff · homework (Unit 11) and a comedy · a thriller · a musical · drama · while (Unit 12). The topics Cities and Films on the ticklist are ours; the rest (Sports, Websites, Homes, School, The future …) is blocks 4–5 and is fair game as revision.
  3. The teacher's page is honest about the hard question — when is a sentence «acceptable»? The rule the book gives: the sentence must show the MEANING of the word. At home we like board games does not; At home we like playing board games like Monopoly does. Decide out loud how strict you are being before the first turn, not after the first argument. Decide this BEFORE the first turn.
  4. The printed tip is worth reading out: a team can block the other team by taking the circles they need. That single sentence turns the game from vocabulary practice into a game.
  5. Use one variation only, and use this one: Four variations are printed, and two of them are worth using: You give me the topic — team B chooses the topic for team A, which stops teams cherry-picking the easy pairs; and It must be true — the sentence has to be true for at least one member of the team, which turns a vocabulary drill into real speaking. Bottom to top (you may only win circles that sit on top of circles you already own) makes the game longer and more tactical.
  6. Seven minutes on a visible timer. When it rings, the team with the most circles wins — a line of four is unlikely in seven minutes and that is fine.
📺 On screen: the timer on the board (Backup: the sheets are photocopied in advance from PREPARE_3_Review Games_Units 09-12.pdf and the game is entirely paper — nothing here can fail)
👦 Students do: play in two teams. Expect the argument about whether a sentence «shows the meaning» to be the most useful English of the stage. Typical problem: one team stalls choosing — thirty seconds per turn, timed

WHAT WE DO: Unit test 11 and Unit test 12 (Standard) — 40 marks each, part 5 of each from audio

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
