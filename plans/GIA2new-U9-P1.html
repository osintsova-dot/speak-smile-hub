🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The second-to-last lesson of the course, and for new students it is built differently from the one our own students get. A student who joined from outside carries two worries at the end of the year at once: «there is still a lot I don't know» and «now they are going to test me». A unit with no new language makes both worries louder, so the frame has to collect, not check: today we are not looking at what is missing — we are building a «What I know» page. Grammar is named directly and laid out in the colours of the book: for a new student the spread on pp.110–111 is a gift, because everything there is sorted by topic and the edges of it are visible. We work through one colour section at a time, with a model before the task and self-checking against the key after it. Every section closes with a sticker — by the end of the lesson the margins show the student where to go next, and it is their own conclusion, not my mark.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two lessons left, and we build one thing: your book of the year. Today, page one — WHAT I KNOW. Words first, then reading, then grammar in four colours: red, green, blue, yellow. One colour at a time, with a model on the board before every task, and you check your own answers with the key. No marks today. The test is later, and this IS how we get ready for it. At the end you'll hold one page with everything you know on it. Open page 108.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: over two lessons we build «my book of the year»: today page 1 — what I know (words, reading, grammar in four colours). Every block: model on the board → task → check against the key → sticker. There are no marks today.

WHAT WE DO: p.108 ex.1–4: nine pictures with support → the letter puzzle → the odd word out with a verb, from a model

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 with support, as the Teacher's Book advises: One word per picture. B and E are places. F is a job. Let them flick back through the book — this is revision, not a test.
  2. Check ex.1 chorally and only after that move to ex.2 — that is what the TB says (otherwise the puzzle does not come out). Key to ex.1: penguin · skate park · yoga · sunglasses · car park · waiter · pasta · feather · yoghurt. The puzzle spells the word geography.
  3. A MODEL before ex.3 on the board, three columns: go swimming / do gymnastics / play football — with the rule on your fingers: «ball or racket → play; sport with no ball → do; word ending in -ing → go».
  4. ex.3 in pairs with the model still visible: in every line one word does not go with the verb. ex.4 straight after it — which verb that word needs.
  5. A 6-line mini-test (yours, modelled on ex.3) → self-check against the key on the slide → sticker.
  6. 🟢🟡🔴 Self-check stickers: after every mini-test the student sticks a sticker in the margin themselves: 🟢 got it · 🟡 so-so · 🔴 didn't get it. This is NOT a mark — it is my map of who to go to on the next task. Say this out loud every single time.
📺 On screen: SB p.108 ex.1–4 · Get Involved A2 flashcards from the units you need · the word bank on screen · a slide with the mini-test key (Backup: write the three-column model in marker on the board and leave it up; read the mini-test key out loud and students check themselves)
👦 Students do: work with the pictures, put the puzzle together, sort out the collocations. Expect: almost nobody remembers feather and yoghurt — give the first letter, as the TB advises. Mistake: «do football» — the three-column model catches it without you stepping in

WHAT WE DO: p.108 ex.6–7: 12 compound nouns with support + the holiday text

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: p.109 ex.1–6: the three tips of the year → where the texts come from → predicting → track 56 → True/False with a line as evidence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Do ex.1 together, because it is the instruction manual for all the reading of the year: 1 b (look at the headline and the pictures → predict) · 2 c (first reading — the general idea) · 3 a (looking for a detail → underline the key words of the question).
  2. ex.2 in 40 seconds, without reading: Where are these texts from? Look at the layout, not the words. Answer: c, a newspaper.
  3. ex.3 predicting in pairs, in writing: three words for each headline. Write them down. We check them against the text — that's the point.
  4. Track 56 (ex.4): they listen AND follow the text with their eyes, as the TB requires, marking unknown words. Matching the headlines: 1 b · 2 a · 3 d · 4 c.
  5. ex.5 True/False with compulsory evidence: Write the line number, or the words from the text that prove it. ‘True’ alone is not an answer.
  6. ex.6 — correct the four false ones (1, 4, 5, 8) in full sentences, together on the board, because this is the hardest spot on the page.
  7. Self-check against the key → sticker.
📺 On screen: SB p.109 · track 56 · a slide with the True/False key (Backup with no sound: read the four news items aloud yourself while students follow the text — that is exactly how the Teacher's Book intends this task)
👦 Students do: predict, listen while following the text, prove True/False with a line. Expect: the predictions partly match the text — and that is a strong moment, students see the skill working. Mistake: they guess True/False at random — the «prove it with a line» rule removes that

WHAT WE DO: pp.110–111: four colour sections of 5 minutes each — model → the edge questions of your colour → exercises → key → sticker

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Announce it directly, as always in this line: Today's grammar: everything, in four colours. One colour at a time. Cover the rest of the page. A sheet of paper covers everything except the current colour — physically.
  2. 🔴 Red (5 min): model on the board — I sit with HIM. She studies. She never watches TV. → edge questions 1·5·9·13·17 → ex.1 pronouns · ex.2 choose the form · ex.3 present simple questions.
  3. 🟢 Green (5 min): model — I'm walking now. It's the easiest subject. You must listen. → questions 2·6·10·14·18 → ex.1 simple vs continuous · ex.2 comparatives and superlatives · ex.3 school rules.
  4. 🔵 Blue (5 min): model — There are some shops. There isn't any milk. How many books? → questions 3·7·11·15·19 → ex.1 find the mistake · ex.2 How much/How many · ex.3 a dialogue in the past.
  5. 🟡 Yellow (5 min): model — He didn't go. Did they take it? I'm going to buy it. → questions 4·8·12·16·20 → ex.1 past simple forms · ex.2 rebuild the questions · ex.3 choose the future form.
  6. After EVERY colour: key on the slide → self-check → a 🟢🟡🔴 sticker in the margin. Between colours — 20 seconds to stand up, sit down and change the colour of the pen.
  7. The TB tip on where to go back to if a colour collapsed: 🔴 SB p.16, 18, 28, 30 · 🟢 p.42, 52, 54 · 🔵 p.64, 66, 76 · 🟡 p.88, 90, 100, 102.
  8. 🟢🟡🔴 Self-check stickers: after every mini-test the student sticks a sticker in the margin themselves: 🟢 got it · 🟡 so-so · 🔴 didn't get it. This is NOT a mark — it is my map of who to go to on the next task. Say this out loud every single time.
📺 On screen: SB pp.110–111 · a covering sheet for everyone · four slides with the keys by colour (Backup: print the keys, one sheet per desk — that is even more convenient, nobody has to wait for the pace of the class)
👦 Students do: work through the four colours in turn, check themselves, stick the stickers. Expect: red and yellow go more confidently (present simple and past simple have had the most practice), blue produces the most 🔴 — much/many and any. Mistake: they peek at the next colour and get confused — the covering sheet solves that physically

WHAT WE DO: the product of the lesson: fill in page 1 of the book of the year — words, grammar by colour, three «to work on» lines

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Your page. Three boxes. Point at your own sample:
  2. 1. Words I know: six words or compounds they remembered or relearned today (from p.108 and Word work).
  3. 2. Grammar I know: one EXAMPLE for each colour — their own sentence, not the rule. Not ‘present simple’. Write: ‘My brother studies computer science.’
  4. 3. To work on: exactly three lines — from the 🔴 and 🟡 stickers. Three. Not ten. Ten is impossible.
  5. Kagan Timed RoundRobin, 30 seconds each: everyone reads the crew their example for one colour of their choice. The crew listens and does not comment.
  6. Praise something concrete: Good example! That's a real sentence, not a rule.

👦 Students do: fill in the page, read one example each in the crew. Expect: EVERYBODY has a page — that is the main result of the lesson. Mistake: in the second box they write the names of rules instead of examples — send it back to be rewritten, because a rule will not come back to them but their own sentence will

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use vocabulary and grammar from the whole course in short guided tasks and complete a one-page ‘What I know’ sheet, naming at least three things they can do and three to work on.
  • Subsidiary. Vocabulary SB p.108 (ex.1–7) with flashcards and a word bank: nine pictures, the go/do/play/have/stay/start collocations, 12 compound nouns. Reading SB p.109 step by step: the three subskill tips of the year, predicting from the headlines, track 56 with students following the text, True/False with a line of the text as evidence, Word work. Grammar SB pp.110–111 — the four colour sections in turn, with a model and self-checking.
  • Personal. The word «revision» does not frighten them, the word «test» does — so say «no marks today» at least twice and then keep your word: not a single sheet is collected today. Help only in short bursts and only after the student has looked in the word bank: the «look up» gesture instead of feeding them the word.
By the end, students can…
  • name the vocabulary sets of the whole course with support
  • read four short news texts using the three subskills of the year
  • complete the red, green, blue and yellow grammar sections with a model

Pron. watch
-ed endings by ear (SB p.116): /d/ played · /t/ watched · /ɪd/ visited — say them through at every past simple. From the Wordlist: average /ˈæv(ə)rɪdʒ/ · desert /ˈdezət/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«He go» or «He goes»? → goes: he/she/it always takes -s (that is the red section).
«There is three museums» — why not? → there are three, so it is plural: there ARE.
«How much books» — why not? → books can be counted → How MANY books.
«He didn't went» — what has to go? → the -ed / second form: after didn't the verb is in the first form, didn't go.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«I don't remember half of it anyway» — for new students this is the big end-of-year fearThe shape of the lesson answers it directly: the page is called «what I KNOW», not «what I have forgotten». Everything they find goes down as a plus. Gaps are marked with a 🔴 sticker, but no more than three per page — a long list demotivates.
The four colour sections feel like four tests in a rowEvery section opens with a MODEL on the board (2–3 sentences) and closes with self-checking against the key and a sticker. Between sections there is a compulsory physical break of 20 seconds: stand up, sit down, change the colour of your pen.
The spread on pp.110–111 looks frightening: 20 questions round the edge plus four blocksThe order comes from the Teacher's Book: first ONLY the edge questions of your own colour (there are five), then the exercises of the same colour. The rest of the page is covered with a sheet of paper — physically, not «just don't look at it».
The word bank is up, but nobody uses itThe «look up» gesture (two fingers towards the screen/board) instead of feeding them the word, the same as all year. Plus the rule: if you ask for help, first show me where you looked.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
