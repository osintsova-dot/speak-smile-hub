🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Both productive skills of the unit in one lesson: the speaking task rehearsed in pairs until it holds, and the written genre taken apart down to a plan of paragraphs — the clean copy written at home.
What is genuinely hard
This is behaviour, not knowledge, and that is why one lesson of it is worth three of vocabulary. Two failure modes appear in every group. The first is the pair who agree immediately, run out of things to say in forty seconds and sit in silence — the Teacher's Book names this explicitly and tells you to send them back. The second is the confident student who takes eighty per cent of the airtime and thinks they have done well. Both are fixed by structure, not by advice.
Typical error to expect
«I don't think sports stars deserve it» inside a for-and-against essay — contractions are the fastest way to lose the register mark, and the hardest habit to break because speaking practice trains them in.
Why the lesson is built this way
Speaking first, while the room is awake and the model is fresh; writing second, because a plan survives the walk home and a first draft does not. Nothing is cut from either half except the part that works better alone: the twenty minutes of silent writing, which happen at home where there is time to think.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two things today, and both of them are what the exam actually asks for: speak with a reason, and write a text that answers every point. We build both here — you write the clean copy where nobody is rushing you.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of second-conditional advice, and a student-made list of dos and don'ts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: two people doing the same task, judged against the same six questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before the first play, hand out the job: You are the examiner. Six questions on the board. Tick or cross for each one. Every student marks the model.
  2. Play track 32 once, straight through, no pausing.
  3. Ninety seconds in pairs to compare ticks and crosses, then collect: the model pair did it well on all six — they covered all five options fully, gave reasons, took equal turns, listened, disagreed politely and reached a decision.
  4. Play track 32 a second time with a different job: hands up the moment you hear an expression from any of the four Speaking bank sections. Stop the audio each time and write the expression on the board.
  5. Then the honest comparison, and it is the point of the stage: How do you think you did in comparison? Two minutes in pairs. Not «were they better» — «what exactly did they do that you did not».
  6. Name two students by name for something they genuinely did well in the baseline. Specific praise, thirty seconds, no general encouragement.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 Practice makes perfect: why everyday activities matter, with talking chips

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Re-pair everybody. The Teacher's Book is explicit: a different partner from ex.2, and it matters — half the skill is adapting to who is in front of you.
  2. Hand out talking chips, three per student, and explain in one line: One chip per turn. When your chips are gone you may only ask questions until your partner has spent theirs.
  3. 🎯 Read the new task from the Practice makes perfect box: a teacher is planning a class discussion about important everyday activities — play sport, eat healthy food, spend time with friends, help with housework, study. Talk about why they matter, then decide which is most important.
  4. Two to three minutes on part one. Sit down at the side and do not intervene at all this time. Then one minute for the decision.
  5. Immediately afterwards, ninety seconds of the pair's own debrief, as the Teacher's Book instructs: how did we do, and what still needs work — taking turns, responding, deciding?
  6. Collect one improvement out loud from four pairs. Nothing general is accepted: Name the thing you did that you did not do in round one.
📺 On screen: the timer and the five activity prompts stay on the board. Backup: the prompts are printed in the book, so if there is nothing to project, students simply keep p.78 open
👦 Students do: run the full task again with a new partner under a chip system that forces equal airtime, then debrief in pairs
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains Task 4 of the Russian oral paper (the joint discussion) and the interaction marks throughout. The Russian oral rewards exactly what the talking chips force: equal airtime, a reaction to what was actually said, and an explicit joint decision at the end. The expressions transfer word for word — nothing has to be relearnt for the other exam.

Move break · stand up if you did it
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that turns the six criteria into a physical audit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands. You read the six criteria one at a time; they sit down when they reach one they did NOT do. By the sixth criterion most of the room is sitting, and the point is made without a word from you.
  2. Everyone up again. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: mill, clap, pair with the nearest person, and tell them the ONE thing you will do differently in round two. Fifteen seconds each.
  3. Two more mixes, two more partners — saying an intention out loud three times to three different people is what makes it stick.
  4. Last round standing: you call an option from the task — a talk by a doctor — and the pair produces one argument FOR it and one AGAINST in thirty seconds.
  5. Freeze and sit: Now listen to two people who do all six of those things.
📺 On screen: nothing to project — the criteria are read aloud and the room answers with its feet
👦 Students do: audit their own performance physically, then state one concrete improvement to three different people

WHAT WE DO: ex.3–4: read for agreement first, then discover the architecture

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3, three minutes, silent: read the model essay quickly and tick any idea that is already in your own table. That is the only job on the first read.
  2. Take two of the essay's arguments to the floor and ask for agreement or disagreement — Top sports stars help society by acting as inspirational role models. Ninety seconds, no more; the point is that they engage with the content before analysing it.
  3. ex.4 as a whole class, four minutes. Question 1 — what is each paragraph for? Answers: 1 introduces the topic · 2 arguments AGAINST the statement · 3 arguments FOR it · 4 summary plus the writer's opinion. Note out loud that the order of 2 and 3 is the writer's choice, not a rule.
  4. Question 2 — how is this different from an opinion essay? Answer: both sides are given, and the writer's own view appears only in the conclusion. Write that sentence on the board next to the skeleton.
  5. Point at one paragraph and ask the class to find the topic sentence. Then ask what would happen if it were deleted. Thirty seconds, and it teaches paragraph structure faster than a diagram.
📺 On screen: the model essay is in the book — nothing needs projecting. The board carries only the paragraph functions as the class names them
👦 Students do: read a model for content, then name the function of each of its four paragraphs

WHAT WE DO: ex.5a–5b: the underlined linkers sorted into the bank, then extended from memory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5a, four minutes individually: put the underlined words from the model into the right section of the Writing bank. Answers: 1 Firstly, Lastly · 2 What is more, Furthermore, In addition · 3 However, On the other hand, Nevertheless · 4 Therefore · 5 to my mind, I believe · 6 All in all.
  2. ex.5b, three minutes in pairs: add anything else you know to each section. Collect on the board and add the book's own extras — not only … but also, In contrast, Despite / In spite of, and so, as a result, In my opinion, Personally, As far as I'm concerned, In conclusion, To sum up.
  3. Now spend three minutes on the one pattern that will be wrong tomorrow: Despite / In spite of. Put the three forms on the board and drill two transformations from the Teacher's Book — Despite their wealth, rich people aren't always happy → Despite being wealthy / Despite the fact that they are wealthy.
  4. Kagan Showdown to check, two minutes: you give a sentence with «although», pairs rewrite it with Despite and hold it up on the signal. Four rounds, everyone answers every round.

Planning the four paragraphs

Why: Planning the four paragraphs

WHAT WE DO: ex.6: the new topic, the same table, and a plan that is checked before anyone writes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the new task aloud: We should always try to avoid any type of stress in our lives. Then draw the same two-column table on the board — REASONS FOR, REASONS AGAINST.
  2. Four minutes, silent and individual: notes in both columns. Feed in content only if a table is stalling — some stress is useful before an exam; long-term stress makes you ill; a life with no pressure teaches you nothing; deadlines get work finished; the health cost is real.
  3. Then, and this is the step students skip: decide WHICH paragraph each note goes in and write 2 or 3 next to it. Two minutes.
  4. Kagan Think-Pair-Share, two minutes: show your plan to a partner and ask one question — Which of my arguments is weakest? Cross it out and replace it.
  5. Read the Exam tip on p.79 with them, and put the four non-negotiables on the board where they stay for the writing: four paragraphs · both sides · opinion only in the conclusion · formal, no contractions · 140–190 words.
  6. Last thirty seconds: everyone writes their first sentence — the introduction — and reads it to their partner. Starting is the hardest part and it is now done.
📺 On screen: the empty two-column table and the four non-negotiables written on the board — chalk only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: make notes on both sides, assign every note to a paragraph, have the plan challenged by a partner and write the first sentence

WHAT WE DO: the writing task set properly, so the clean copy is worth marking

WHAT WE DO: self-check against today's aim, then the homework said once and written on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two minutes — the expression test. Books shut. You say a function — «ask their opinion», «disagree politely», «make a choice» — and the class produces two expressions for it in chorus, with the right stress.

Structure
What do you think about …? · Don't you think so? · I see what you mean, but … · I agree up to a point, but … · My choice would be … · I think we should choose … · Shall we choose …? · I'd suggest going for that one.
Form
the four sections of the Speaking bank — 1 asking your partner's opinion, 2 agreeing, 3 disagreeing, 4 making choices · after What about and How about comes a noun or an -ing form, never an infinitive · Shall we …? and Let's … take the bare infinitive · I'd suggest takes -ing.
Pron. watch
the expressions are memorised as whole tunes, and the tune is carried by two stressed words only: My CHOICE would be a TALK. · I think we should CHOOSE a WEEKEND in the country. · rising intonation on the invitation questions (What do you think? Don't you think so?) or they sound like a challenge · and the disagreement openers are said slowly — I see what you mean, BUT — because the pause is what makes them polite.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«You agreed with your partner after twenty seconds. Is that a good discussion?» → no — the conversation ends and you lose two minutes of speaking. What could you have done instead? → agreed AND offered a different idea.
«Which is stronger: «I don't agree» or «I see what you mean, but …»?» → the second one, because it keeps the other person talking. Which one scores better? → the second one, for the same reason.
«This essay says top sports stars deserve their money AND that doctors deserve more. Is the writer confused?» → no — those are paragraphs two and three, and both sides are required.
«Where is the writer's own opinion?» → the last paragraph, and nowhere else. Which two words tell you it is an opinion? → «I believe», «to my mind».

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
A pair finishes the three-minute task in forty secondsDo not rescue them — name it. The Teacher's Book instruction is explicit: if they finish early, they agreed too fast or did not explore the options. Send them back with one instruction: Take the option you both rejected and argue FOR it for one minute.
One student takes eighty per cent of the airtimeTalking chips, three each. You spend one chip every time you speak; when your chips are gone you may only ask questions until your partner has spent theirs. It is mechanical, it is silent, and it works on the students who cannot hear themselves.
Both body paragraphs argue the same sideThe notes table is built BEFORE the model and checked at the desk: if a column is empty, the student does not start writing. The Teacher's Book insists on this and it is the whole difference between a for-and-against essay and an opinion essay.
Contractions everywhereMake them audible rather than explaining them. In the peer check the reader reads the essay aloud in a whisper and circles every contraction they HEAR. Ten seconds per essay, and the class starts self-editing next time.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.

The same skill is tested by: Task 38 (the extended written project, criteria К1–К5) and task 37 when the prompt asks for two sides.

The for-and-against essay is the skeleton the Russian task 38 is marked on: a stated topic, a paragraph of arguments, a paragraph of counter-arguments, a conclusion where — and only where — the writer's own opinion appears. Two things transfer literally: the ban on contractions (register is a criterion) and the linker families, which are what К3 «logical organisation» actually counts.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
