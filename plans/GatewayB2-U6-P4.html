🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Run a two-part collaborative speaking task — discuss five ideas for making students healthier, then choose the best two together — using the four families of expressions from the Speaking bank: asking your partner's opinion, agreeing, disagreeing politely, making choices.
What is genuinely hard
This is behaviour, not knowledge, and that is why one lesson of it is worth three of vocabulary. Two failure modes appear in every group. The first is the pair who agree immediately, run out of things to say in forty seconds and sit in silence — the Teacher's Book names this explicitly and tells you to send them back. The second is the confident student who takes eighty per cent of the airtime and thinks they have done well. Both are fixed by structure, not by advice.
Typical error to expect
«Yes, I agree. … So, the second one? Yes. OK.» — the fastest way to fail a collaborative task is to be agreeable. The task is not to reach agreement quickly; it is to explore every option, with reasons, and only then decide. The second commonest failure is answering the examiner instead of the partner: eyes on the desk, no questions asked, no reaction to what was said.
Why the lesson is built this way
Baseline first, model second. The class does the whole task cold (ex.2) before they hear anything, so the model on track 32 answers a question they have already asked themselves. The Teacher's Book gives six diagnostic questions to listen with — did they cover all five options, give reasons, take equal turns, listen, disagree politely, decide — and those six become the checklist the class then applies to its own second attempt. Talking chips make equal airtime physical rather than moral. The second round uses a different partner and a different task, so nothing is a rerun.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Your school wants to make you all healthier. It has five ideas — cookery classes, a sports day, a talk by a doctor, a weekend in the country, free gym membership. You have three minutes to discuss them and one minute to choose two. That is an exam task, word for word. We will do it twice today: once now, badly, and once at the end, properly.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of second-conditional advice, and a student-made list of dos and don'ts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — how did you sleep last night, and how do you feel about it? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.
  2. 2 minutes — negotiating with your parents. The Teacher's Book warmer, in pairs, Kagan RallyRobin: you want a new phone and you have not got enough money. Take turns saying what you should and should not do, every sentence with If I were you, I would / wouldn't …
  3. 1 minute — the Dos and Don'ts list. Collect six of their sentences on the board in two columns. Leave the list up: it is the first version of today's criteria, produced by them.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): point at the two columns: Everything on this board is about negotiating with one person. Today you negotiate with a partner, in front of an examiner, for three minutes. Page seventy-eight.

WHAT WE DO: name the aim, show the shape of the exam task, set the bar

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the advice generated by the class, then completed from the Exam tip on p.29

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs, four minutes: what advice would you give someone doing this task for the first time? Push for concrete behaviours, not «be confident».
  2. Collect on the board and shape their words into the book's points: do not agree or disagree completely at once, or the conversation ends · do not dominate — take turns and help your partner in · listen and react to what was actually said · give full reasons · if you are stuck, ask What do you think? · if you did not understand, ask them to repeat.

  4. One more thing, said plainly because it changes scores: Do not be afraid to say something obvious. An obvious sentence in English beats a brilliant sentence in your head.
  5. Teach the fillers here, not later, because they are what stops the silence: Let me think … · The thing is … · Well …

WHAT WE DO: ex.2: both parts of the task, three minutes plus one, before any model is heard

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pair them yourself and make the pairs mixed. Say the rules once: three minutes on part one, then one minute to decide, and you will not help.
  2. Start the timer and sit down at the side of the room. Do not circulate for the first ninety seconds — let them find the silence themselves.
  3. Listen and write. You are collecting three things: two good sentences to praise by name, two errors for the delayed correction at the end, and the names of the pairs who finished early.
  4. At three minutes, stop them and give the one-minute decision phase separately, so they feel the difference between exploring and choosing.
  5. Any pair that finished early gets the Teacher's Book instruction, not a rescue: You came to an agreement too quickly. Go back and argue for the option you both rejected.
  6. Thirty seconds of honest self-report against the six criteria on the board: hands up for each one you did. Nobody comments.
📺 On screen: the timer is the only thing on screen, and the six criteria stay on the board. Backup: if there is no timer to project, use your phone and call the minutes aloud — the stage needs nothing else
👦 Students do: run the full exam task in pairs with no preparation, then self-report against six criteria

Move break · stand up if you did it
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that turns the six criteria into a physical audit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands. You read the six criteria one at a time; they sit down when they reach one they did NOT do. By the sixth criterion most of the room is sitting, and the point is made without a word from you.
  2. Everyone up again. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: mill, clap, pair with the nearest person, and tell them the ONE thing you will do differently in round two. Fifteen seconds each.
  3. Two more mixes, two more partners — saying an intention out loud three times to three different people is what makes it stick.
  4. Last round standing: you call an option from the task — a talk by a doctor — and the pair produces one argument FOR it and one AGAINST in thirty seconds.
  5. Freeze and sit: Now listen to two people who do all six of those things.
📺 On screen: nothing to project — the criteria are read aloud and the room answers with its feet
👦 Students do: audit their own performance physically, then state one concrete improvement to three different people

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: two people doing the same task, judged against the same six questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before the first play, hand out the job: You are the examiner. Six questions on the board. Tick or cross for each one. Every student marks the model.
  2. Play track 32 once, straight through, no pausing.
  3. Ninety seconds in pairs to compare ticks and crosses, then collect: the model pair did it well on all six — they covered all five options fully, gave reasons, took equal turns, listened, disagreed politely and reached a decision.
  4. Play track 32 a second time with a different job: hands up the moment you hear an expression from any of the four Speaking bank sections. Stop the audio each time and write the expression on the board.
  5. Then the honest comparison, and it is the point of the stage: How do you think you did in comparison? Two minutes in pairs. Not «were they better» — «what exactly did they do that you did not».
  6. Name two students by name for something they genuinely did well in the baseline. Specific praise, thirty seconds, no general encouragement.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4a–4b: the making-choices section completed, the other three reconstructed from memory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4a, three minutes individually: section 4 of the Speaking bank completed with the seven words given. Answers: a choice · b choose · c best · d better · e Let's · f Shall · g going.
  2. ex.4b with the Mixed ability instruction from the Teacher's Book: BEFORE opening p.26, less confident students write two expressions for each of sections 1–3, more capable students write five. Four minutes, silent.
  3. Then open p.26 and check. Add anything missing to their own lists in a different colour — what they could produce from memory matters more than what they can copy.
  4. The stress drill from the Teacher's Book, two minutes and worth every second: write My choice would be a talk. and I think we should choose a weekend in the country. Pairs decide which words are stressed; you then say the sentences with natural stress and underline them. Drill chorally, then in pairs.
  5. Extend the same treatment to two invitation questions, because the intonation is what makes them polite: What do YOU think? · Don't you think SO?

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 Practice makes perfect: why everyday activities matter, with talking chips

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Re-pair everybody. The Teacher's Book is explicit: a different partner from ex.2, and it matters — half the skill is adapting to who is in front of you.
  2. Hand out talking chips, three per student, and explain in one line: One chip per turn. When your chips are gone you may only ask questions until your partner has spent theirs.
  3. 🎯 Read the new task from the Practice makes perfect box: a teacher is planning a class discussion about important everyday activities — play sport, eat healthy food, spend time with friends, help with housework, study. Talk about why they matter, then decide which is most important.
  4. Two to three minutes on part one. Sit down at the side and do not intervene at all this time. Then one minute for the decision.
  5. Immediately afterwards, ninety seconds of the pair's own debrief, as the Teacher's Book instructs: how did we do, and what still needs work — taking turns, responding, deciding?
  6. Collect one improvement out loud from four pairs. Nothing general is accepted: Name the thing you did that you did not do in round one.
📺 On screen: the timer and the five activity prompts stay on the board. Backup: the prompts are printed in the book, so if there is nothing to project, students simply keep p.78 open
👦 Students do: run the full task again with a new partner under a chip system that forces equal airtime, then debrief in pairs

Rotation round · three partners, three minutes each
standing pairs, rotating
Why: Rotation round · three partners, three minutes each

WHAT WE DO: the same skill against a moving target, so nobody rehearses the same conversation twice

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two lines facing each other. Each pair gets ninety seconds on one option from either task — you call which one — and must reach an explicit decision before the time is up.
  2. One line moves one place along. New partner, new option, ninety seconds. Three rotations.
  3. The rule for this round, and it is the one worth keeping: Every turn must begin by reacting to what your partner just said. No fresh starts, no monologues.
  4. Round four, if the group is strong: one student in each pair is the OBSERVER with the six criteria and says nothing for ninety seconds, then gives thirty seconds of feedback. Swap.
  5. This is where the quiet students speak most, because ninety seconds with a stranger is easier than three minutes with a friend.
📺 On screen: nothing is projected and nothing is printed — the options are called out and the two lines do the work
👦 Students do: hold three short decision-making conversations with three different partners, each one opening with a reaction

Delayed correction
seated, whole class
Why: Delayed correction

WHAT WE DO: the errors and the good sentences collected all lesson, handed back without names

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six sentences on the board, all of them heard today, none of them attributed. Three are wrong, three are good.
  2. Pairs, ninety seconds: find the three that need repairing and fix them. Kagan Numbered Heads Together for the check.
  3. Expect these three: an expression with the wrong form after What about, a bare «I don't agree» with nothing after it, and a decision announced with no reason attached.
  4. Then read the three GOOD sentences aloud with the names of the students who said them. Public, specific, thirty seconds.
  5. Close the loop out loud: Everything you did today transfers. The examiner is not marking your ideas about gyms. They are marking whether you can keep another human being talking.

👦 Students do: repair three errors in pairs and hear three of their own sentences praised by name

WHAT WE DO: self-check against today's aim, then the homework said once and written on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Workbook p.53
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can sustain a three-minute collaborative discussion covering all the options with reasons, and reach an explicit joint decision using the language of making choices.
  • Subsidiary. Asking for and reacting to a partner's opinion; disagreeing politely; sentence stress in fixed expressions; self- and peer-assessment against six named criteria.
  • Personal. Deliberate silence: say nothing for the whole of the second round. Note who never asks a question and who never gets asked one, and pair them differently next week.
By the end, students can…
  • keep a discussion going for three minutes without agreeing too early
  • invite a partner in with a question instead of taking another turn
  • disagree politely and still move the discussion forward
  • state a joint decision out loud with a reason for it

Structure
What do you think about …? · Don't you think so? · I see what you mean, but … · I agree up to a point, but … · My choice would be … · I think we should choose … · Shall we choose …? · I'd suggest going for that one.
Form
the four sections of the Speaking bank — 1 asking your partner's opinion, 2 agreeing, 3 disagreeing, 4 making choices · after What about and How about comes a noun or an -ing form, never an infinitive · Shall we …? and Let's … take the bare infinitive · I'd suggest takes -ing.
Pron. watch
the expressions are memorised as whole tunes, and the tune is carried by two stressed words only: My CHOICE would be a TALK. · I think we should CHOOSE a WEEKEND in the country. · rising intonation on the invitation questions (What do you think? Don't you think so?) or they sound like a challenge · and the disagreement openers are said slowly — I see what you mean, BUT — because the pause is what makes them polite.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«You agreed with your partner after twenty seconds. Is that a good discussion?» → no — the conversation ends and you lose two minutes of speaking. What could you have done instead? → agreed AND offered a different idea.
«Which is stronger: «I don't agree» or «I see what you mean, but …»?» → the second one, because it keeps the other person talking. Which one scores better? → the second one, for the same reason.
«You cannot think of anything to say. What is the one thing you can always do?» → ask the partner a question. It buys thinking time and it counts as interaction.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
A pair finishes the three-minute task in forty secondsDo not rescue them — name it. The Teacher's Book instruction is explicit: if they finish early, they agreed too fast or did not explore the options. Send them back with one instruction: Take the option you both rejected and argue FOR it for one minute.
One student takes eighty per cent of the airtimeTalking chips, three each. You spend one chip every time you speak; when your chips are gone you may only ask questions until your partner has spent theirs. It is mechanical, it is silent, and it works on the students who cannot hear themselves.
The pairs speak to you instead of to each otherSit down. Physically — take a chair at the side, out of the sight line, and stay there for the whole of the second round. Say beforehand that you will not answer anything during the task.
Weaker students freeze at the expression listMixed ability from the Teacher's Book: less confident students write TWO expressions per section before they look at p.26; more capable students write five. Everyone arrives at the model with something already on paper.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Task 4 of the oral paper (the joint discussion with a partner) and the interaction marks across the whole speaking exam.

Negotiating and collaborating is the one exam skill that cannot be revised the night before, because it is behaviour, not knowledge. The Russian oral rewards a candidate who invites the partner, reacts to what was actually said and drives the pair to a decision — the three things this lesson drills. The language is portable word for word: «What do you think about …?», «I see what you mean, but …», «Shall we choose …?» work in Cambridge and in the Russian paper alike.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
