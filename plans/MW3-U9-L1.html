Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, children will be better able to name six bugs — ant · bee · butterfly · ladybird · snail · worm — and match each to its colour on the Big Wheel: “Where's the ladybird? — It's on red!”
  • Subsidiary. Mime every bug (TPR); play Binoculars! & Genki Karuta (31); sing Genki «Creepy Crawlies» (73); Phonics Hero finale — read a whole Part-4 story from our workbook (a year-of-reading celebration, p.66–90); stick the bug stickers on the wheel & play Memory (PB p.89).
  • Personal. Tiny teacher-talk, big TPR — Speaky brings a garden alive and each bug crawls/flies/slides in. Genki recipe: disco first, phonics early, input through curiosity, energy up in the games then down for the table. Every bug gets its own big movement and its own colour. This is our last unit — enjoy how much they can already do.
By the end, pupils can…
  • mime and name six bugs;
  • say a bug for a colour — “It's on red — ladybird!”;
  • join in the bug hunt & song.

Structure
Where's the (ladybird)? — It's on (red).  ·  It's a (bee).  ·  (Speaky says) Fly like a butterfly!
Form
Where's the + bug → It's on + colour. Bugs as single naming words + one big movement each. No new structure — just naming the bugs.
Pron. watch
clear final sounds: ant /-t/, wasp? no — worm /wɜːm/, ladybird three beats; soft butter-fly; keep colour words short.
Phonics
Phonics Hero 2.0 — FINALE of the course. Not new sounds: today a free-reading celebration — children read a whole little story from Part 4 of our workbook (p.66–90), fingers under the words, proud and fast. A year of learning to read from zero, GMF1-style — celebrate it.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“A snail — does it fly or slide slowly?” → slide slowly!
“Where's the bee? — is it on red or yellow?” → yellow!

Assumptions: Last unit of the year — carpet, story-time & Big Wheel routines are second nature (Units 1–8). Colours are fully secure and re-used to pin the bugs to the wheel. The six bug words are new but very picturable/mimeable. Children read confidently now; today they read a whole story, not just rows.

17–25′
Curiosity input · six bugs + the wheel
carpet → Big Wheel mat

Guess & move! Slowly slide a bug flashcard out of an envelope — What's coming out of the garden? Guess! …a LADYBIRD! Reveal + do the BIG movement together, whole class echoes the word. Do all six (ant · bee · butterfly · ladybird · snail · worm).

Bugs on the wheel. Lay the six flashcards on the wheel — one per colour. Point to each pod: Where's the ladybird? It's on red! Where's the bee? It's on yellow!

📱 Coursebook app (Macmillan digital): put the interactive Big Wheel on screen with the six bug pods (red = ladybird · blue = butterfly · pink = worm · orange = snail · green = ant · yellow = bee) — children point & answer It's on red — ladybird!. The wheel comes from the coursebook app; our songs & games add to it, we don't reproduce it.

💪 Support: move & copy the puppet. ⭐ Extension: say the bug for a colour — It's on green — ant!

25–32′
Game · Binoculars! + Genki Karuta (31) ⚡
carpet → whole room

▶ Binoculars! Stick the bug flashcards on the walls. Children make binoculars with their hands: Look through your binoculars… Where's the butterfly? …There! say a bug — everyone turns, spots it, mimes looking through binoculars and names it. All hunt at once.

▶ Genki Karuta Genki Karuta: each child has the six bug mini-cards spread in front. Speaky calls a bug — children grab it FAST and shout it. Calm, seated, everyone plays every round.

32–38′
Reading corner 📖
carpet

Read-aloud. Read our own Speak & Smile story-book «Speaky's Garden» aloud — Speaky's sunny garden — flowers, vegetables and all the little bugs that live there. The perfect calm read to open our Bugs unit, straight after the hunt: look for a bug on every page.

Hold up each spread — children look at the pictures, do the gestures and echo the key line Look! A butterfly! A bee!. Pre-readers: they listen, watch & repeat, they don't read the words.

Turn each spread — children spot the bug, do its movement (flutter, buzz, wiggle) and echo Look! A butterfly!; on the last page count how many bugs they found.
