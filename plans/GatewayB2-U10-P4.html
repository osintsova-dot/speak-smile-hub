🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Both productive skills of the unit in one lesson: the speaking task rehearsed in pairs until it holds, and the written genre taken apart down to a plan of paragraphs — the clean copy written at home.
What is genuinely hard
Not the language — the move. A seventeen-year-old asked to argue a case gives three reasons for their own side and stops; raising the opposing view feels like losing. Both the Cambridge speaking exam and the Russian oral reward exactly the opposite, and so does any real discussion. The second difficulty is delivery: the expressions are long chunks and students read them off the page in a monotone, which kills them.
Typical error to expect
An article written like an essay — no title, no reader, no voice, and «In this essay I want to tell about…» at the top. An article speaks to a reader, asks questions, and may use I; an essay does not. The task on p.131 says «tell US» — that word decides the register.
Why the lesson is built this way
Speaking first, while the room is awake and the model is fresh; writing second, because a plan survives the walk home and a first draft does not. Nothing is cut from either half except the part that works better alone: the twenty minutes of silent writing, which happen at home where there is time to think.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two things today, and both of them are what the exam actually asks for: speak with a reason, and write a text that answers every point. We build both here — you write the clean copy where nobody is rushing you.”

Why: Everyone speaks English inside the first two minutes, and the rewind pulls the previous lesson's language back into working memory so that today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of arguing against two absurd claims, and the discovery of the day's move

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — what is the last thing you read or watched that was NEWS? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.
  2. 2 minutes — three things you disagree with. The Teacher's Book warmer, and the best possible opening for this page. Say, with a straight face: It is true that the world is flat. · I accept that humanity did not land on the Moon. Students answer back in pairs with logical arguments — Kagan RallyRobin, thirty seconds each.
  3. 1 minute — two answers to the floor. Listen for anybody who begins by agreeing with part of it. If somebody does, write their sentence on the board and leave it there: that is the whole lesson, produced by a student.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Notice what the best answers did. They did not start with «no». They started by admitting something. Page one hundred and thirty.
📺 On screen: nothing to project — voices only, and the board carries one student sentence if you get one
👦 Students do: argue back at two absurd statements in pairs, then hear two answers

WHAT WE DO: ex.3–4 with track 59: agree or disagree first, then harvest the twelve expressions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the first listening properly: You do not need every word. One question: do you agree with this person, yes or no, and why? Play track 59 once.
  2. ex.3, ninety seconds in pairs: agree or disagree, and one reason. Take three answers.
  3. Before the second play, point at the Speaking bank half of the page: five ways to present an opposing view, seven ways to respond to one. Read the two headings aloud, not the twelve phrases.
  4. ex.4, second play: complete the gaps in the Speaking bank. The four actually used in the recording are There's no denying that … · You can't argue with the fact that … · Let's not forget that … · Having said that, …

  6. Drill the twelve chunks, sixty seconds, whole class, hand marking the stress: there's no deNYing that · HAving said THAT.

WHAT WE DO: the Teacher's Book extra: four gapped main ideas rebuilt by the class from the recording

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the four gapped ideas on the board exactly as the Teacher's Book gives them: 1 Freedom of speech in the press is very important, but ____ · 2 In addition, ____ · 3 Some people use the press to become famous. However, ____ · 4 Furthermore, ____
  2. Play track 59 a third time. Pairs complete the four ideas in note form, four minutes.
  3. Brainstorm the missing halves with the whole class and write them up: 1 that doesn't mean journalists can write things that cause hate or violence · 2 sometimes, for national or personal security, some control is better · 3 that doesn't mean they have no right to keep some details private · 4 some papers print stories they know are false, just to sell copies.
  4. Now make the structure visible: draw an arrow from each concession to its answer. Four concessions, four answers, one presentation — the model on the board, in the students' own words.
  5. Sixty seconds in pairs: which of the four arguments is the strongest, and why?

Why: 🎯 Presentations · everybody speaks

WHAT WE DO: ex.8: four simultaneous presentations per round, with a listening job and peer feedback

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Groups of four, seated in a circle, timer visible. Round one: student A presents for two minutes while B, C and D listen with jobs — B counts the concessions, C writes down one strong expression, D watches the delivery (eyes up? not too fast?).
  2. After each presentation, ninety seconds of feedback in that order: one thing that worked, one thing to change. Then rotate the roles.
  3. Four rounds, three and a half minutes each: every student presents once and listens three times. Circulate and write down errors; do not interrupt.
  4. 🎯 Then two presentations to the whole class, chosen by the groups, ninety seconds each. Ask the room for one concession they heard and one answer to it — the audience has a job here too.
  5. Applaud both. It costs five seconds and it changes who volunteers next time.
📺 On screen: only the timer is shown — nothing else, so that all eyes are on the speaker. Backup: if there is no timer app, the wall clock and your voice do the job; call the two-minute mark yourself
👦 Students do: present once for two minutes, listen three times with a specific job, and give and receive structured feedback
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains Task 4 of the oral paper (the argued monologue). The Russian oral rewards a candidate who names the other side and answers it — that is the difference between a description and an argument, and the concession language of this lesson is exactly the tool for it.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: give one pair the Russian-format task instead of the book's: two minutes on «Some people say social media is the best source of news» with a four-point plan on the board. It replaces one round and costs no extra time.

Move break · walk to your side
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that forces everyone to defend a position aloud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two ends of the room: AGREE and DISAGREE. The middle is closed today.
  2. You read a statement, everybody walks, and then Kagan Stand Up–Hand Up–Pair Up ACROSS the line — each student finds a partner from the opposite end and has thirty seconds to state their reason.
  3. Three statements: Celebrities have no right to privacy. · Free newspapers are better than paid ones. · Social media is now the main source of news for people our age.
  4. After the third, ask two students to say their PARTNER's argument back, not their own. That is the listening skill the presentations need.
  5. Freeze and sit: Now listen to somebody who does this professionally.
📺 On screen: the two end-of-room cards, written by hand — nothing to prepare and nothing to fail
👦 Students do: commit physically three times, argue across the line with three different partners, and report a partner's argument

WHAT WE DO: ex.1a–1b: the content bank built before any genre work, and left on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six headings on the board: SERIES · FILMS · MUSIC · GAMES · FASHION · SPORT. The Teacher's Book asks whether students would add a category — take one or two (creators, food, apps).

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: the article «The Name of the Wind» read for content against the task

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say the rule for the first read: Ignore the underlined words completely. One question: does this article include everything the notice asked for?
  2. ex.3, four minutes silent. Then pairs compare for one minute.
  3. Check as a class: yes. It talks about the latest crazes in series and films, names what the writer thinks is best right now (hopepunk — optimistic, hopeful stories in a genre full of dark dystopias) and what is old news (endless remakes of superhero films with the same plots and different actors).
  4. Now pull the genre features out of the text with three questions: Where is the writer's voice? Who is being spoken to? How does the first sentence work? «Let me begin by saying that I'm really into fantasy» — that is an article, not an essay, in nine words.
  5. Fast finishers, from the Teacher's Book: write a short paragraph saying whether you would be interested in the writer's suggestions.
  6. Sixty seconds in pairs: hopepunk or dystopia — which do you actually want to watch, and why?

WHAT WE DO: ex.5–6: the Writing bank labelled, the stages ordered, then a personal plan

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. ex.6 in pairs, ninety seconds: order the six stages of writing. Answer: brainstorm ideas (e) · make notes (b) · organise the notes into paragraphs (a) · write the first version (d) · check and edit (c) · write the final version (f).

Structure
It is true that … · You can't deny that … · There's no denying that … · You can't argue with the fact that … · I accept that … || But that doesn't mean that … · However, … · Let's not forget that … · I would still say that … · Even so, I still believe that … · Having said that, … · That doesn't alter my opinion that …
Form
the concession comes FIRST and is followed by a full clause (It is true that newspapers need to sell copies) · the response opens with a contrast marker and returns to the speaker's own position (But that doesn't mean that they can print lies) · Having said that is followed by a comma and a clause, never by an infinitive.
Pron. watch
these are long chunks and they carry only two or three stresses: there's no deNYing that … · you can't ARgue with the FACT that … · let's not forGET that … · HAving said THAT. Everything between the stresses is compressed. Drill them as single words, with the hand marking the beat, before anybody uses them in a sentence
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«It is true that the press needs to sell newspapers» — am I agreeing with the other side or with myself? → with the other side, on purpose, before I answer it.
«Having said that, …» — does what comes next support what I just said, or contradict it? → contradict. It is a signal that I am about to turn.
«Despite the rain, we went out» — is despite followed by a noun or by a clause? → a noun. And if I want a clause? → despite the fact that.
The task says «tell US what YOU think» — who is us? → the magazine and its readers. So can I write I? → yes. Could I in an essay? → much less freely.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The presentation is one-sidedThe requirement is structural, not stylistic: no presentation is accepted without at least one concession and one response. Write the skeleton on the board — opinion, argument, THE OTHER SIDE, the answer to it, conclusion — and point at it during preparation.
Students write the presentation out and read itTake the paper away: preparation happens on the four-line planner only, and full sentences are not allowed on it. The Teacher's Book asks the class to name the benefit of notes over sentences — ask it, and they will produce it themselves.
«I have nothing to write about»That is why ex.1 comes first and takes ten minutes: six categories on the board, everybody contributes, and the pooled list stays up during the writing. Nobody has to invent content at minute sixty.
The article turns into an essayThree requirements stated before writing and checked in the peer check: a TITLE · a question to the reader somewhere in the first paragraph · at least one I. Any piece without all three goes back.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Task 38 (the written project piece, criteria К2 and К3) and task 37 for the register work.

An article is not a Russian exam genre, but everything that decides task 38 is trained by it: a paragraph plan agreed before writing (К2), linkers of contrast, addition, consequence and conclusion used once each and correctly (К2), an opinion signalled with a fixed phrase rather than «I think» seven times (К3). The Writing bank on p.131 is a seven-family linker table — it is the most exam-useful half page in the unit, and it is worth photographing.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
