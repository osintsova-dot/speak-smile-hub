WHAT WE DO: Inside-Outside Circle for the review: I meet them at the door; a U6 rewind (Present continuous) woven into the fame theme — we describe a celebrity photo; a bridge

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: Hi! One word — how are you? Mood fingers 1–5.
  2. I build Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: two circles facing each other, a question → both answer → Outside circle, rotate two steps! — a new partner, a new question; 3 rotations, everybody speaks.
  3. Rewind U6 → U7: a celebrity photo on the screen. Tell your partner: What's she doing? What's she wearing? — 60 sec by the frame on the board (+1 to the team for two full answers).
  4. Bridge: You can say what she IS doing. But WHO is she? What's her JOB? That's our new unit — Famous lives.

👦 Students do: they greet with one word, describe the photo using a familiar frame. Expected: a confident start on familiar grammar. Typical error: “she wear” — I fix it with a gesture, it's not the target today.

Presenting: ten jobs
Discover · 10 jobs

WHAT WE DO: presenting jobs from the ex.1 photos with pictures and chorus → ex.2 “who works where” → the rule What do you do?

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. We open pp.84–85: Look at the photos. Who are they? What do you know about them?
  2. Ex.1: five photos A–E ↔ words (key TB p.96: A actor · B astronaut · C singer · D artist · E inventor). Every word — chorus ×2 + a gesture (actor — “camera”, astronaut — “helmet”, singer — “microphone”, artist — “brush”, inventor — “light bulb”).
  3. We present the other five words from the box the same way: cook · doctor · farmer · teacher · waiter.
  4. Pronunciation (TB p.96): act-OR, farm-ER, sing-ER — the ending is always /ə/. Never ‘actOR’ loud.
  5. Ex.2 by teams: Who works in a hospital? in a kitchen? in the country? (key TB p.96; here country does NOT mean “a nation”, it means “the countryside” — say this plainly).
  6. ABCD (TB p.96): She's AN inventor. He's A singer. And we ask: What do you DO? — not ‘What are you?’ Chorus ×2.
📺 On screen: SB p.84 · picture cards of 10 jobs · board: A/AN + “What do you do?”
👦 Students do: they repeat in chorus with gestures, answer in teams. Expected: the gestures make the words stick for good. Typical error: “he is doctor” with no a — the yellow A/AN card on the board.

Play · Charades & Hot seat

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: an active consolidation of jobs: gesture charades + a “hot seat” with Yes/No questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Charades (Activities bank TB p.6): one team member draws a job card and mimes it with no words; the team guesses in a full sentence — He's a farmer! 4 rounds, rotate.
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face-down, on the call No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up at once — I see every answer, nobody can hide.
  3. ▶ Hot seat (Activities bank TB p.6): a student sits with their back to the board, I write a job; the class asks Yes/No questions: Do you work in a hospital? Do you work with animals? — guess in 5 questions.
  4. Engagement rule: while one is on the chair, EVERYONE ELSE writes their own question on a mini-board — whoever's question I hold up gets to ask it.
  5. Points to the team for a guess in a full sentence.
📺 On screen: job cards · a mini-board each · a chair with its back to the board
👦 Students do: they mime, guess, everyone writes questions on mini-boards — nobody just watches. Expected: laughter, the words stick. Typical error: guessing with one word — points only for a full sentence.

Target language
Lexis
actorartistastronautcookdoctorfarmerinventorsingerteacherwaiterdatebirthdaycenturyexpeditionadventurestadiumstamp
Structure
She's an astronaut. · What do you do? — I'm a teacher. · Who works in a hospital? — A doctor. · When's your birthday? — It's on the third of May. · Laika was a street dog. (just noticing it)
Form
job = be + a/an · ordinals: +th, except first/second/third and fifth/ninth/twelfth/twentieth · date = the + ordinal + of + month · months take a capital letter.
Pron. watch
-or/-er = /ə/: actor, farmer, singer · -th /θ/ — tongue against the upper teeth · silent r and a long /ɜː/ in first, third · month stress: March O · April Oo · July oO · January Ooo · September oOo (TB pp.96–97).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“What are you? — is that how you ask about a job?” → No: What do you do?
“Do we write ‘the 3 of May’?” → No: the THIRD of May.
“march or March?” → March — months always take a capital letter.

Assumptions: After U1–U6: be, have got, present simple, can, comparatives, present continuous are all active; numbers and some months are known. New: ordinals as a system, saying years aloud, 10 jobs, reading a long article. We do NOT explain past simple today.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Ordinals: ninth/twelfth/twentiethThree groups on the board in colour (+th · the word changes for 1–2–3 · spelt differently) — stay up till the end of the lesson; check every answer against the colour.
They read years digit by digitA years ruler on the board (TB p.97): 1969 = nineteen sixty-nine · 2008 = two thousand and eight · 2015 = twenty fifteen. Every year — chorus.
The article on p.87 scares them with its sizeWe don't read it whole: three passes, one paragraph, one task each time. Reinforcement TB p.98 — hint which category is in which paragraph.
They drown in the pace of a compressed pairNo seated block longer than 9 minutes; a short physical change between blocks (miming, arrow-hands, stand-up-sit-down).
“Too many new words”Only 10 jobs are active; the Word work from the article is for recognition, not for production (say this to the class plainly).
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
