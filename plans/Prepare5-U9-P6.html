🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Do Speaking Part 3 — a two-minute paired discussion of a set of options that has to end in an agreement — and Speaking Part 4 — personal questions on the same topic, answered fully, with the partner brought in.
What is genuinely hard
Part 3 is the one part of the exam where a strong student can lose marks by being good. The marks are for INTERACTION: inviting the other person, responding to what they said, reaching an agreement together. A confident candidate who delivers a brilliant monologue and decides in thirty seconds has failed the task as set. The second difficulty is silence: two minutes is much longer than it sounds, and a pair that agrees immediately has ninety seconds of nothing left.
Typical error to expect
Deciding in the first thirty seconds. The task says talk about ALL the options first, then decide. A pair that jumps to the decision has skipped the assessed part.
«Yes.» «I agree.» «OK.» — an agreement with no reason attached is not a contribution. Every agreement in this lesson has to carry a because.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is a full oral rehearsal and it is deliberately the lesson after the test, because a speaking hour is the right reward for a writing hour. It opens with twelve quiet minutes of Reading Part 6 — the Review 5 exam corner, the open cloze about the paparazzi — so that the block's own exam page is not wasted and so the room starts seated before it starts standing. Then the video: nine minutes of two real candidates doing the real thing is worth an hour of description. Everything after that is rotation in threes, examiner included, because being the examiner is what teaches a candidate what the examiner is listening for.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today nobody writes anything after the first quarter of an hour. You are going to run the speaking exam yourselves — and each of you will sit in the examiner's chair, which is where you find out what they are actually listening for.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, marked papers returned face down, and one error repaired in pairs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what was the last message you sent today? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — the two test papers come back, marked. By name, face down, with the weakest section circled on each. One sentence each, quietly. No scores read aloud, ever.
  3. 1 minute — Kagan RallyCoach: in pairs, find ONE item you both got wrong and fix it together, out loud. One item, sixty seconds. Then papers into bags.
  4. Say the honest headline in one line — the section the class as a whole lost most on, and when it comes back. Then move on; today is not about the test.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): into the lesson: Fifteen quiet minutes, and then nobody writes anything for the rest of the hour.
📺 On screen: the marked papers handed back face down — paper only, and the papers are theirs
👦 Students do: take back their own paper, then repair one shared error aloud with a partner

WHAT WE DO: name the aim and put up the discussion frame

WHAT WE DO: the block's last exam page: an open cloze, six gaps, one word each

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn to the Prepare for the exam corner of Review 5. Say what it is and why it is today: it is the last exam task of the block, it is about celebrities, and it takes twelve minutes.
  2. Remind them of the method from lesson 59, in one line: read the whole text first, then look at both sides of the gap and decide what KIND of word is missing.
  3. The Teacher's Book gives the function of each gap and it is worth putting on the board as a hint list for weaker students: 1 purpose, 2 example, 3 a passive action, 4 contrast, 5 time, 6 introducing what somebody else says.
  4. Six minutes, silent, individually. Then pairs compare for two minutes and must agree.
  5. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: 1 In, 2 like, 3 having / getting, 4 Although / While / Whilst, 5 when, 6 According. Gap 3 is the one worth a minute — it is have something done, from three lessons ago, inside an exam cloze.
  6. Close the writing part of the day out loud: Pens away. Everything from here is spoken.
📺 On screen: the six functions listed on the board as a hint column — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: work silently against a clock, then agree in pairs and justify each gap by its function

WHAT WE DO: nine minutes of Bertille and Miray doing the real test, watched with one job

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out a slip with four boxes before you press play: asked a question · used their partner's idea · disagreed politely · reached an agreement. One tick per box, plus the exact words used.
  2. Play Parts 3 and 4 of the video. Do not pause; the point is the shape of the whole thing.
  3. Three minutes, pairs, then floor: what did they DO that the frame on the board describes? Take the exact phrases and write them up beside the frame.
  4. Then the honest question, and it changes attitudes: Was their English perfect? No — and they still did the task. Interaction beats accuracy in this part and they have just watched the proof.
  5. If the examiner-feedback PDF is available, read two lines of it aloud. Two lines only, and choose the ones about listening to each other.
📺 On screen: PREPARE_B1_Speaking test video on the projector, Parts 3 and 4. Backup: if the video fails, you and the strongest student perform Part 3 live in front of the class while everybody ticks the same four boxes — second best and genuinely good
👦 Students do: watch with a four-box observation slip, then name the exact phrases that made the discussion work

Move break · agree, disagree, invite
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that installs the three moves physically

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands. Three gestures agreed in ten seconds: thumb up for agree, thumb sideways for partly, open hand for what do you think?
  2. You read an opinion — Famous people should never complain about photographers. Writing letters is better than texting. Everyone would enjoy being famous for a week. Everybody makes a gesture, then Kagan Mix-Pair-Share: find the nearest person with a DIFFERENT gesture and give them one sentence with a because in it.
  3. Four opinions, four mixes. Nobody sits down.
  4. Freeze into groups of three: Sit down in your threes. One of you is now the examiner.
📺 On screen: no technology in this stage — gestures and voices only, and nothing can fail
👦 Students do: everybody signals an opinion four times and speaks to four different partners who disagree

Part 3 · the paired discussion, in threes
groups of three
Why: Part 3 · the paired discussion, in threes

WHAT WE DO: SB p.133: the penfriend task, run three times so everyone plays every role

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the situation from the page: the situation on the page: a teenager is writing to a new penfriend and is deciding what to write about. The pictures are the possible topics — sports equipment, a computer game, a band at a concert, a girl with a laptop, food and drink, clothes and trainers, postcards of different places. Talk about all of them, then decide which would be most interesting for the penfriend to read about.
  2. First, the pictures. Two minutes, whole class: what can you see in each one? Put the vocabulary on the board — sports equipment, a computer game, a band at a concert, a girl with a laptop, food and drink, clothes and trainers, postcards and a statue. Nobody should lose two minutes of their discussion to a missing noun.
  3. Groups of three, roles named: examiner · Candidate A · Candidate B. The examiner reads the script from the page word for word — that is deliberate, because hearing the real rubric three times is half the preparation.
  4. 🎯 Kagan Three-Step Interview structure: two minutes of discussion, then rotate the roles, three times, so everyone examines once and candidates twice.
  5. The examiner has a job beyond reading: they tick the four boxes from the video slip for their pair, and give ONE sentence of feedback at the end of each round. That is what makes the role worth having.
  6. Rule enforced by the clock: no decision in the first ninety seconds. Call it out at ninety seconds so nobody has to watch their own watch.
  7. Monitor and note. Do not interrupt for language; interrupt only for a pair that has gone silent, and interrupt with a question, not a correction.
📺 On screen: the discussion frame and the picture vocabulary on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everybody discusses twice and examines once, with a scored observation slip in the examiner's hand

Why: Part 4 · the personal questions

WHAT WE DO: SB p.134: five questions on the same topic, rotated so everyone answers all five

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 first, as a class, ninety seconds: what is the general theme of the five questions? Answer: communication — and the class has to name the word in each question that proves it (penfriend and write, emails and letters, communicate, phone calls and texting, information and share).
  2. Point out the connection out loud: Part 4 is always on the topic of Part 3. If you have just discussed what to write to a penfriend, the questions will be about writing and keeping in touch. Knowing that is worth thirty seconds of thinking time in the real exam.
  3. Same threes, roles rotated again. The examiner reads the five questions and uses the back-up prompts when a candidate stalls: How about you? Do you agree? What do you think?
  4. Ten minutes, roles rotating every three or four minutes so everyone answers and everyone prompts.
  5. One rule on the answers, on the board: every answer has two parts — the answer, then a reason or an example. A bare answer gets a back-up prompt from the examiner, immediately.
  6. Take thirty seconds at the end to say the thing candidates never believe: asking the examiner to repeat a question is printed in the exam tips. It is allowed, it costs nothing, and using it is better than answering the wrong question.
📺 On screen: the five questions and the three back-up prompts on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: answer five personal questions with a reason each, and prompt a partner when they stall
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains the EGE oral section (tasks 1–4 of the speaking paper). Answer, then reason, then example — the two-part answer trained here is the difference between a two-mark and a four-mark response on the Russian speaking card.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: after the Part 4 rotation, give each three one Russian-format monologue plan for five minutes of silent preparation and one delivery. Take the time from the full run, which works with two rotations instead of three.

🎯 The full run · Parts 3 and 4 back to back
groups of three → class
Why: 🎯 The full run · Parts 3 and 4 back to back

WHAT WE DO: the two parts joined up and timed, then feedback from the examiners, not from you

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Now the whole thing, joined, because in the exam they are one continuous stretch: Part 3 for two minutes, straight into Part 4 for three. Five minutes per pair, no break between them.
  2. 🎯 New threes, so nobody performs for the same audience twice. Three full runs, fifteen minutes, roles rotating.
  3. The examiner fills in the observation slip properly this time — four boxes plus the two best phrases the pair used, written down word for word.
  4. Six minutes of feedback and it comes from the students, not from you: each examiner reports ONE thing their pair did well, naming the phrase. Collect the phrases on the board as they come.
  5. Then two minutes of delayed correction from your own notes: four sentences, on the board, names removed, repaired by the class. Choose errors that block meaning, not slips.
  6. Close with the count: Every person here has now done the paired speaking test three times and sat in the examiner's chair three times. When the real one happens, it will be the fourth.
📺 On screen: the collected phrases and the four repaired sentences on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: run the full five-minute test three times, observe three times, and give evidence-based feedback to a partner

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can hold a two-minute paired discussion that covers all the options and reaches an agreement, and answer personal follow-up questions with a reason and an example.
  • Subsidiary. An open cloze under exam conditions (Reading Part 6); recognising interactive behaviour in a model performance; using back-up prompts without treating them as failure.

By the end, students can…
  • talk about every option before deciding anything
  • invite a quiet partner in without taking over
  • agree and disagree with a reason attached
  • ask the examiner to repeat a question without losing composure

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The pair decides in twenty seconds and then sits in silenceRule announced in advance and enforced with a timer: nobody may say the word «decide» in the first ninety seconds. Give them the count of options — four to discuss before any decision.
One candidate dominates completelyGive the quieter one the Prepare to speak card and the explicit job of asking two questions. Then swap the roles and run it again. Say why: the dominant speaker is losing marks, not winning them.
«I don't know what to say about a photograph of postcards»The examiner-role student uses the printed back-up prompts — How about you? Do you agree? What do you think? They exist for exactly this and using them is not a failure.
The video will not playRun the model live: you and the strongest student do Part 3 in front of the class while everybody else ticks the interaction behaviours on a slip. It is second best, and it is genuinely good.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: The oral section (tasks 1–4 of the speaking paper) and task 37 (the personal email).

The Russian oral has no paired discussion, and that is exactly why today matters: the skill Cambridge Part 3 trains — keep a conversation alive, invite the other person, disagree without stopping — is the skill that turns a Russian monologue from a list into an answer. Part 4 is even closer: personal questions answered with a reason and an example, which is the difference between a two-mark answer and a four-mark one on the Russian speaking card.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
