🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Consolidate the whole block — city and country vocabulary, phrasal verbs, film words, reporting verbs, articles and reported speech — and then produce it under test conditions in the two official Cambridge unit tests.
What is genuinely hard
Both grammar systems of this block are invisible to the person making the mistake. An article is missing and the sentence still means what it meant; a tense does not shift and the report still gets understood. That is exactly why they have to be produced under a clock today, with somebody checking — recognition tasks will flatter the class and the test will not.
Typical error to expect
«I went to cinema», «she is doctor», «in city where I live» — Russian has no articles at all, so they are not forgotten, they were never there. Every English noun needs a decision: a, the or nothing — and the decision is made about a hundred times in a hundred-word email.
«He said he is tired», «she said she will help» — Russian does not shift the tense in reported speech, so the present survives into an English past sentence. Will → would and can → could go the same way, and both are marked.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is the review page and the photocopiable game, in that order: the page finds the holes item by item, and «LIST IT!» — this time on the twelve Units 15–16 cards left in the box — makes the vocabulary retrievable rather than recognisable. The exam corner of the review page is an open cloze, which is the Reading Part 6 they met in lesson 2 and the closest thing on the page to the Russian task 11. The test is briefed honestly: what is in it, how long it runs, and the two sections where marks actually go — articles that have to be written rather than chosen, and a section that runs backwards from reported speech into direct speech.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty minutes of play, then forty minutes of paper. The longest test of the year so far — sixty-nine items — and every single one of them, we have done in this room.”

WHAT WE DO: the photocopiable review game, played on the twelve Units 15–16 cards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in forty seconds: «LIST IT!» — PREPARE_5_Review_game_Units_13-16.pdf, the same printed cards as block 7. Teams of two or three, one pile face down. A player picks a card and reads it out — List five places where you can see wildlife. Every team races to write FIVE items and shouts Listed!; the others stop. Scoring: one point for the first valid five, one bonus point for every item nobody else wrote, and one more if the whole list is spelled correctly. Students will argue about what counts; that is the best English in the game, so referee it.
  2. The pile: this time play the twelve cards block 7 left in the box — sports centre facilities · films that have come out this year · things that cause pollution · types of performance · reasons people move out · places where you can see ruins · places where you can see wildlife · places that have air conditioning · animated films · famous film directors · musicals · reasons people stay in. The nine Units 13–14 cards were played five weeks ago; take them out of the pile before the lesson rather than skipping them in front of the class
  3. Eight minutes, hard stop, and announce the last card when you get there.
  4. Referee actively: List five types of performance will produce an argument about whether stand-up counts. Give them thirty seconds to argue it in English, then decide.
  5. Keep the score on the board where everyone can see it, and name the spelling point aloud each round — this block has conditioning, facilities and soundtrack in it.
  6. Follow-up, one minute: everyone adds two words from another team's list to their notebook.
📺 On screen: the running score on the board and the card pile face down — paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone writes in every round, and each team argues its own list in English

Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«He asked me if I want to go» — right or wrong? → wrong: WANTED. Reported questions shift too.
«I like the films» — do I mean films in general? → then no article. The exam test has this twice.
In the test, if you write the correct verb but forget the pronoun — is that a mark? → no. The whole sentence has to be right.

«We never did this» during the testEvery section maps to a lesson and you can name it: place words — lesson 1 · articles — lesson 2 · phrasal verbs — lesson 3 · film words — lesson 4 · reported speech — lesson 5 · reporting verbs — lesson 6. Say this in the briefing, not during the paper.
Forty minutes is not enough for sixty-nine itemsIt is, if nobody stalls. The briefing says it: two minutes per section is the pace, and anyone stuck moves on and comes back. Announce the halfway point at minute twenty.
The game overruns and eats the testHard stop at minute forty, cards counted rather than finished, timer visible to the room, and the last card announced.
Fast finishers in the test disturb the restThey turn the paper over and re-check the two sections named in the briefing, then read silently. Nobody leaves and nobody compares until every paper is in.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper), plus task 11, since the review page ends with an open cloze.

The block test asks for production and spelling, and the two places marks go are the two the briefing names: an article where Russian has none, and a backshifted verb in reported speech. The open cloze in the review corner is the Russian task 11's cousin — one word per gap, and the word is almost always grammatical rather than lexical.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
