🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The article about the saiga and the mountain gorilla is not read 'because it was set', but as two closed cases: a species almost disappeared — and came back. The crew has to find WHAT exactly worked and back it up with a line. The subskill of the unit (understanding the content of paragraphs) is built into the rule: read the headings first, then the paragraph — otherwise you cannot match them. The TB names this an exam skill outright. Critical thinking becomes the finale of the mission, not an add-on.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two case files on your desk. The saiga: over a million in the 1990s, under fifty thousand by the year 2000. The mountain gorilla: five hundred in 1960, two hundred by the 1970s. Both should be gone. Both are still here and the numbers are going UP. Somebody did something right. Your crew has twenty-five minutes to find out exactly what — and every answer needs a LINE from the text. No line, no point. Open the files!”
The gist, if you need to restate it: two cases: the saiga and the mountain gorilla almost disappeared — and came back. Find what exactly worked and prove it with a line from the text. At the end — a 60-second report.

WHAT WE DO: Critical thinking 2–3 (Evaluate + Create) + upgrading the report with concession and contrast

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Basic report: 'People helped them. It's good.' Boring!
  2. Upgrade frames: Even though the population is growing, the saiga is still in danger. · Unlike the dodo, the gorilla got a second chance.
  3. Evaluate (TB key): protecting the habitat — plus: the animals have somewhere to live and that place will not be destroyed; minus: if you only protect it and never extend it, the population will not grow. Laws against hunting — plus: the numbers stop falling; minus: the survivors need more space and more food.
  4. Create: every crew invents its own campaign (which animal, what we do) — at the end of the lesson the class votes for the best one (TB technique).

PRODUCT · the case report
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second report: two numbers + the reason for the fall + what worked + a plus/minus + the verdict

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Preparation 7 min: Keywords only. Everyone owns a block: numbers · the problem · the solution · the verdict.
  2. Sixty seconds. Judges: fluency · accuracy · evidence. Proof from the text or it didn't happen!
  3. A challenge from the class after each one: You say the campaign worked. But the text says they are STILL in danger. So did it work or not?
  4. The vote for the best campaign from Create — the winner goes on the unit wall.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can match headings to paragraphs (subskill), find specific facts in an article with an evidence line, use 6 new words (campaign, population, hunting, laws, habitat, deserts) and deliver a 60-second case report.
  • Subsidiary. The extinct-animals quiz (ex.1–2, track 37) as a warm-up; ex.3 the general idea; ex.4 headings; ex.5 saiga/gorilla; ex.6 true/false with a correction; Word work ex.7–8; Critical thinking Remember → Evaluate → Create.
  • Personal. The reading speed of the strong ones drowns out the weaker ones — hold the rule 'an answer only counts with an evidence line, finger on the line'. Do not explain the forms was/were: collect them from the text as a store for P3.
By the end, students can…
  • read the headings first and match them to paragraphs
  • quote the line that proves an answer
  • use six new words (campaign, population, hunting, laws, habitat, deserts)
  • report a conservation case in 60 seconds with evidence

Structure
In the 1990s there were more than a million saigas. · There wasn't enough open space for the saiga. · There weren't any laws to stop this until 2008. · The text says… — that's why the answer is 'false'.
Form
quoting: The text says + the line; for 'where exactly' — the letter of the paragraph (A–E) or the first word of the line.
Pron. watch
from the Wordlist: campaign /kæmˈpeɪn/ · population /ˌpɒpjʊˈleɪʃ(ə)n/ · habitat /ˈhæbɪtæt/ · hunting /ˈhʌntɪŋ/ · deserts /ˈdezə(r)ts/ (deserts /ˈdezə(r)ts/ — not desserts!)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'I think there weren't any laws until 2010' — does that count? → no: only with a line from the text (it says 2008).
Does extinct mean 'in danger'? → no: the species is completely GONE. In danger — still here, but very few.
Why read the headings BEFORE the paragraph? → a heading = the content of the paragraph in short; with it you know what to look for (TB tip).

Assumptions: SB pp.74–75, syllabus pair 47. They read confidently, but 'headings first' is a new discipline. All six Word work items are in the official Wordlist. The TB asks you to pre-teach extinct, conservation and have in common.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They skim and then argue 'I'm right'The evidence-line rule: only a line from the text settles an argument, finger on the line.
They match headings by guessworkDo the first one out loud: heading 4 'What is a saiga?' → paragraph A, because it contains ONLY a description of the saiga. After that the method is 'take one key word from the heading and look for it in the paragraph'.
The report turns into 'poor animals'The report rubric: 2 numbers from the text + the reason + what worked + one plus and one minus from Critical thinking.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
