WHAT WE DO: gate: 8 adjectives → comparative in writing; the big/heavy/expensive/good traps; personal score

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Slide: cheap · big · heavy · expensive · good · easy · thin · slow.
  2. Write the comparative. Three minutes. Spelling counts — and yes, one of them is irregular.
  3. Key: cheaper · bigger · heavier · more expensive · better · easier · thinner · slower.
  4. Score in the margin. The reveal: Everybody got ‘cheaper’. Half of you lost ‘bigger’ and ‘heavier’. The rules are five, they take four minutes, and then you never lose those points again.

Sprint: the five comparison rules
⚡ Base sprint · five rules
desks → standing drill

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–4 p.66 as a race: the two/than rule → forms → match with rules a–e → 9 forms; the traffic-light rule on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 in chorus (key: 1 two · 2 than), then ex.2 and ex.3 in crews, 4 minutes. Keys: ex.2 — better–good, heavier–heavy, bigger–big, more expensive–expensive · ex.3 — a heavier · b smaller · c more expensive · d better · e bigger.
  2. I put up the traffic-light rule (stays till the end of the unit): 1) one syllable → + -er: cheap → cheaper · light → lighter · fast → faster · new → newer · old → older · slow → slower · 2) one vowel + one consonant (not -w/-y) → double the consonant + -er: big → bigger · hot → hotter · thin → thinner · 3) two syllables ending in -y → -y drops, + -ier: heavy → heavier · easy → easier · ugly → uglier · 4) two or more syllables → more + adjective: expensive → more expensive · difficult → more difficult · interesting → more interesting · 5) irregular: good → better · bad → worse.
  3. Ex.4 as a volley: 9 forms, key cheap–cheaper · difficult–more difficult · easy–easier · fast–faster · light–lighter · new–newer · old–older · slow–slower · thin–thinner (+1 to the crew for a clean volley). I draw attention to slow → slower and rule e (TB p.77).
  4. Pronunciation (ABCD TB p.77): smalle(r) — no R sound. THAN is weak: /ðən/. Say it fast: The laptop's HEAVier than the TABlet. Choral ×3.
  5. A standing drill (sorting with your body): I say an adjective — short word, -er → hands UP · long word, more → hands OUT · irregular → JUMP. ×10 (cheap · expensive · big · good · easy · difficult · thin · bad · new · interesting) — everyone reacts at once.
  6. Fast finishers (TB p.77): 5 comparative forms from the adjectives on p.17.
📺 On screen: SB p.66 ex.1–4 · the traffic-light rule on the board (stays up) · timer
👦 Students do: they fill in tables as crews and bat back forms in a volley. Expected: the system is closed in 9 minutes. Typical error: thin → thiner — I point at the doubling rule.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.
  3. Possessive pronouns · chain game
standing · circle

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: ex.6–7 fast → ▶ Chain sentences (TB p.77): the chain «my … but yours …», repeat the noun and you’re out

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A demo with a real object (TB p.77): I take a student's phone: This is my phone. That's your phone → This is MINE. That's YOURS.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly around the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. Ex.6 table as a volley (key: 2 you – yours · 3 he – his · 4 she – hers · 5 we – ours · 6 they – theirs (his = both an adjective and a pronoun; all except mine end in -s)), ex.7 on mini-boards (key: 1 mine, hers · 2 theirs · 3 his · 4 yours).
  4. We notice (TB p.77): Which one is the same as the adjective? (his) How do the others end? (in -s, except mine).
  5. ▶ Chain sentences (Activities bank TB p.6): we stand in a circle. My phone is old, but yours is new. — Your phone is new, but his is smaller. — His is small, but hers is better… Rule: name the noun a second time and you sit down. We change the object (laptop, camera) on the second round.
  6. The class streak on the board.
📺 On screen: board: a mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs table · streak counter · students' real belongings
👦 Students do: they stand in a circle and continue the chain — everyone speaks, nobody just watches. Expected: a streak of 10–15, the thrill of “don't get out”. Typical error: “it's hers phone” — the rule “nothing after a possessive pronoun” + the class in chorus.

🏁 PRODUCT · Versus pitch + instruction demo
stage

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share: a double finale: a 60-sec versus pitch (comparatives) + PERFORM the instruction, with the partner PHYSICALLY carrying out the steps

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Part 1 — Versus pitch (60 sec): Your gadget versus theirs. Four comparatives minimum, one possessive pronoun (‘ours is …’). GO. Everyone in threes at the same time, then 2 pairs “on air”.
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A talks for their timed turn, B is an active listener (counts pauses on fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. Part 2 — PERFORM the instruction: a pair performs for ANOTHER pair (TB p.79). The listeners physically “carry out” the steps with their hands/on paper.
  4. Peer review (ex.7) — only 2 checks: 1 Were the instructions clear? 2 How many Key phrases did they use?
  5. Judges: fluency / accuracy / richness 1–3; gold run +5. A separate +3 “followable” bonus — if the listeners managed to repeat the steps.
📺 On screen: stopwatch · judges' cards · a 2-item peer-review checklist
👦 Students do: everyone pitches in their three, then instructs another pair and carries out someone else's steps. Expected: 2–3 “followable” bonuses. Typical error: a missed step — discovered physically, the best feedback there is.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — can with no to and no -s, comparative by the rule, than not then (1–3) · Richness — 2+ adjectives, a comparison, precise tech vocabulary (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		stopwatch · rubric cards

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can win a 60-sec ‘This vs that’ pitch with 4+ correct comparatives and possessive pronouns, and give a 5-step instruction that a partner can actually follow.
  • Subsidiary. All five comparative rules are closed as a system in one sprint; possessive pronouns through the Chain sentences game; a Grammar round-up (cumulative U1–5) as a team quiz; Key phrases for instructions worked right through to PERFORM.
  • Personal. ⚠️ Grammar is exactly 45 minutes. Their “we know -er” breaks on bigger/heavier/more expensive and on than↔then. The target is written accuracy and than, not understanding the idea.
By the end, students can…
  • apply all five comparative rules, including doubling and -y → -ier
  • use mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs instead of repeating a noun
  • ask for and give instructions with First/Then/Next/Finally
  • deliver a versus-pitch and an instruction demo to the bar

Structure

Form
one syllable → + -er: cheap → cheaper · light → lighter · fast → faster · new → newer · old → older · slow → slower · one vowel + one consonant (not -w/-y) → double the consonant + -er: big → bigger · hot → hotter · thin → thinner · two syllables ending in -y → -y drops, + -ier: heavy → heavier · easy → easier · ugly → uglier · two or more syllables → more + adjective: expensive → more expensive · difficult → more difficult · interesting → more interesting · irregular: good → better · bad → worse · possessive pronouns: I → mine · you → yours · he → his · she → hers · we → ours · they → theirs (no noun after!).
Pron. watch
than is unstressed, sounds /ðən/: The laptop's HEAVier than the TABlet (TB p.77 ABCD) · -er = /ə/, the R is silent: smaller /ˈsmɔːlə/ (TB p.77).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“more cheaper — how many mistakes?” → two: more is extra, and cheap is short → cheaper.
“Is this your tablet? — Yes, it's your.” → yours: there's no NOUN after a possessive pronoun.
“than or then?” → than = comparison, then = afterwards. In an instruction — THEN.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: they know the idea of comparison, they put on -er. Gaps: doubling the consonant, -y → -ier, more with long words, than/then, and almost completely — possessive pronouns. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (question word order with can / comparative spelling / possessive pronouns), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.
