🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Report what people said with said/told + the tense shift: present → past, present continuous → past continuous, will → would, can → could — and never say «he said me».
What is genuinely hard
Russian reports speech WITHOUT shifting the tense («она сказала, что работает»), so every English shift feels unnecessary; and Russian «сказал мне» maps onto both said and told, so the said/told split has no home-language anchor at all.
Typical error to expect
Katy said she likes reading · Cosmo said the board will be full — the tense left unshifted after a past reporting verb; Russian keeps the original tense, so the interference is systematic [A2/B1]
He said me he made films — say glued to an object where English wants tell; the pair error is she told that… with the object dropped [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The grammar is mined, not presented: all four model sentences in ex.1 come from the reading the class did LAST lesson, two of them parked on the board verbatim — so the discovery stage starts from «we have already read this». The shift table is built by the students from the match, then hammered in three rounds of rising freedom: choose (ex.3), transform (ex.4), report a whole speaker (ex.5). The standing shift-o-meter break makes the tense move physical. The productive stage is a gossip chain — interview, re-pair, report your partner's words — because reporting real sentences about a real person is the only version of this grammar anybody uses outside a workbook.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Yesterday Mum said she was working till 6.30 — remember the board? Today you learn the machine inside that sentence. By the end you can report anything anyone tells you — accurately, which is rarer than it sounds. Gossip, but grammatical.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a three-slip repair clinic, and the bridge into the parked reported-speech sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — error clinic from the reading maps. Three anonymous slips from your lesson-1 log on the board (a one-word answer, «in my phone», an article/paper swap). Kagan RallyRobin in pairs: find and fix all three.
  3. Repair together, generously: These are OUR mistakes — and they are already shrinking.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): point at the two parked sentences: «Mum said she was working till 6.30.» Her exact words were NOT these. Today — what happened to them.
📺 On screen: three faulty sentences in chalk — the board is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hunt and fix in pairs. Expect the one-word answer to be «fixed» with two words — push for the verb

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: four bubbles from yesterday's texts → the shift table and the said/told rule

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs, 3 minutes: match the four speech bubbles («I can take you later» · «We won't get angry» · «I want people to send a CV» · «I'm working till 6.30») to the four reported versions. Key: a → 4 (could take us later) · b → 2 (wouldn't get angry) · c → 1 (wanted people to send a CV) · d → 3 (was working till 6.30) — all four come from yesterday's reading texts.
  2. ex.2: extract the shifts into the board ladder. Key: a present simple → past simple · b present continuous → past continuous · c can → could · d will → would; pronouns change; say + no object / tell + object. The class fills the ladder, you only hold the chalk.
  3. Land the said/told rule with the object test: He said me → broken. Who hears it? Me — so he TOLD me.
  4. Concept checks from the plan header — all three, cold-calling by name.
  5. Signpost: Grammar reference page 156 — the same ladder in a table, tonight, two minutes.
📺 On screen: the ladder fills up in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: match, extract, formulate. Expect the pronoun change (I → she) to be spotted last — ask «whose words were they first?». Typical error: reading the reported version as if the fact changed

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: the shift-o-meter — the tense moves with the feet

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up, face me. I say a sentence — if it's DIRECT, hands up like quote marks. If it's REPORTED, one step back. Fire six: «I'm hungry» · she said she was hungry · «We'll win» · he told me he could help · etc.
  2. Last one: I say direct, the whole class says the reported version and steps back together. «I can dance!» — Go!
📺 On screen: instructions by voice, nothing to display and no backup required
👦 Students do: step, quote, shift chorally. The step-back is the tense shift in the body — cheap and it sticks

pairs, then re-pair
Why: 🎯 Speaking out · the gossip chain

WHAT WE DO: interview → re-pair → report your partner's words accurately

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1 — interview, Kagan Timed Pair Share, 2 minutes each way, three questions on the board: What are you reading these days? What will you read this summer? What can you read in English? Interviewers note the ANSWERS word for word.
  2. Re-pair (odd rows shift two seats). Round 2 — the report: Dasha said she was reading a graphic novel. She told me she would try Harry Potter in English this summer. The listener writes down the three reported facts.
  3. Verification round — close the chain: listeners find the ORIGINAL speaker and check the facts survived two mouths. Did you really say that?
  4. You walk and log silently: unshifted tenses, said me, lost pronouns — three best finds open lesson 3.
  5. Debrief in 60 seconds: whose report survived the chain fully intact? Applause — accuracy is the game.
📺 On screen: the three interview questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: interview, report, verify. Expect the verification round to be loud and delighted — controlled chaos is fine for four minutes. Typical error: she said me she reads… — double trap, log it

Form

Pron. watch
said /sed/ — not /seɪd/ · would /wʊd/ — silent l · she said she'd — the 'd is there, make them hear it
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Katy said she liked reading» — does Katy still like reading? → probably yes; the past tense is the REPORTING rule, not a change of fact.
«He told me he would come» — what were his exact words? → «I will come»: will steps back to would.
«She said me the answer» — what is broken? → say never takes a person: she TOLD me / she SAID the answer.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The shift skipped entirely («Katy said she likes reading»)Physicalise it at the shift-o-meter break: one step back = one tense back. Then police it with the board arrow only — point, don't re-explain.
«He said me» — the object glued to the wrong verbThe object test as a chant: Who hears it? If the listener is IN the sentence — told. If not — said. Run it on every ex.3 item that carries the trap.
would/could felt as «conditional only»Name the second job out loud: would is also the past of will; could is also the past of can. Same words, new shift.
Fast finishers in the transformation stageTB-style extension: they write two direct quotes of their own on scrap paper and pass them left for the neighbour to report.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the OGE — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 20–26 (grammar inside a text) — tense control under pressure. Reported speech itself is rare in the OGE grammar block, but the tense-shift discipline it trains (present → past, will → would, can → could) is exactly the muscle those items test.

A student who can shift «I'm working till 6.30» into «she said she was working till 6.30» without blinking has stopped choosing verb forms by feel. That transfer — form chosen by rule, not by sound — is the whole grammar block of the written paper.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
