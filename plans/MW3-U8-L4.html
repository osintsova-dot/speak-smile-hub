Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, children will be better able to sing & act the story song «Welcome to our show» (CD4 T8) with full actions, and say what they're doing: “I'm playing the drum! I'm singing!”
  • Subsidiary. Re-tell the story; play Spot the deliberate mistake! & the thematic Ball and Music (16); act the song in groups with instrument roles; Phonics Hero — the /ɜː/ spellings er ir ur, blend & speed-read p.83–85; follow the paths & answer (PB p.82).
  • Personal. Sing in stages — join first with actions, then words. Repeat the song several times without it feeling repetitive by changing HOW (giant voice, whisper, super-fast). After the big acting, use the calm read before the table.
By the end, pupils can…
  • sing «Welcome to our show» with the instrument actions;
  • act out being in the band;
  • say “I'm playing the drum! I'm singing!”

Phonics

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“In the show — do the children play instruments and sing, or just sleep?” → play & sing!
“I say I'm playing the drum but I mime a guitar — is that right?” → No! spot the mistake!

Assumptions: Story met in L3 — today deepens it through the song. Children know the instruments, the four activities & the roles from L1–L3. Reading continues on /ɜː/. Pre-readers follow the song by pictures & actions, not text.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Bored by repetition of the songChange the mode each time: giant voice, whisper, super-fast, slow-motion.
Only a few singGive everyone an instrument action job; sing the actions even without the words.
Mixes up which instrumentOne fixed mime per instrument; hold the flashcard up as its verse comes.
Over-excited after actingUse the calm read (Speaky's Band!) to settle before the table.
Answers in RussianPuppet models I'm playing the drum! with the mime; child copies action + words.
Classroom management — signals
“Class? — Yes!” — you: “Class!” → pupils stop & echo “Yes!”
“WiFi” 🖐 — say “WiFi” + raise a spread hand → pupils silently mirror & tune in.
Volume control — raise/lower your arm = louder/quieter → settler.
“Give me five” — eyes · ears · mouth · hands · feet.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1… pencils down.”
“Freeze!” — statue, mid-movement reset.

9–17′
Phonics Hero 🔤 ⚡
carpet, moving

① Recognise / review. Phonics Hero lesson 30 · long /ɜː/ — sounds er · ir · ur  (all say /ɜː/). 🦝 Speaky shows each with its Jolly action (gesture technique); children do the action and NAME the sound — they already know all 26. We don't drill bare sounds: teacher voices, children gesture.

② Blend / build. Build the word: h-er → her, b-ir-d → bird, t-ur-n → turn — sweep the sounds together + a picture. ▶ Speed-read race — beat Speaky's clap-clock reading the /ɜː/ row.

③ Speed reading. open Phonics Hero 2.0 (our workbook) p.83–85 — finger under each word, read the row FAST as a group (whole words, playful, celebrate speed — real reading from zero, GMF1-style).

Tricky words: read people · school together as whole words (we don't sound tricky words out).

24–31′
Game · Spot the deliberate mistake! ⚡
carpet, standing

▶ Spot the mistake! Mime an instrument & say a sentence — sometimes matching, sometimes WRONG: I'm playing the drum! Children wave & shout Yes! if it matches, or No! You're playing the guitar! if it's wrong & fix it. Everyone judges every round.

31–38′
Game · Ball and Music (16)
carpet, seated circle

▶ Ball and Music Children pass a soft ball round the circle while «Welcome to our show» plays. Music stops — the child holding the ball answers What are you doing? — I'm singing! and mimes it. Music on again — pass. Calm, seated, quick turns for all.

38–43′
Reading corner 📖
carpet

Read-aloud. Read our own Speak & Smile story-book «Speaky's Band!» aloud — A calm reprise of Speaky's band getting ready for their show — the perfect settle-down bridge before we perform «Welcome to our show».

Hold up each spread — children look at the pictures, do the gestures and echo the key line Welcome to our show! I'm playing the drum!. Pre-readers: they listen, watch & repeat, they don't read the words.

Children point to each player and mime the instrument, echoing I'm playing the drum! before we sing the show song.
