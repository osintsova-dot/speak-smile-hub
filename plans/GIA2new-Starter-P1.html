🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The first pair of the year with new students does two jobs, and the language one is the second. First: pull the class together and explain a format they have never worked in — crews, roles, points, “the product of the pair”, stickers. If you skip this now, the whole year goes on explaining as you go. Second: give them a safe way into the language — they have seen countries, nationalities and be before, but it has to be served as review with support: the model on the board, the word bank on screen the whole lesson, written fixing before any speaking task. The product is modest and reachable: a five-line card about yourself, and they present it with the sheet in their hands, because this is the first lesson, not an exam.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Welcome. This year each of you builds ONE thing — your English card. Four lessons, four parts: who I am and where I'm from · my family and what I look like · what I can do · and then you present the whole card to the class. Today: part one. Two things to know. One — we work in crews, and points go to the crew, never to one person. Two — you can always hold your notes. This is not a test, ever. Let's start with MY card…” (and I show my own card on the board straight away)
In simpler words, if the class needs support: All through Starter we build one thing — your English card: who I am and where I'm from · my family and what I look like · what I can do. Today, part one. We work in crews, points go to the crew, you may present with your sheet in hand — this is not a test.

WHAT WE DO: names round the circle + the four rules of the format: crews, roles, points, the product of the pair

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Name circle with repetition: I'm Dasha and I like pizza. — the next one repeats the previous person and adds themselves.
  2. The format on a slide, in plain words, four points, one at a time with an example:
  3. 1. Crews. We work in groups of 4, I put the crews together, new ones every unit.
  4. 2. Roles. I hand out cards: captain · writer · timekeeper · speaker. Everyone has a job. Nobody just sits.
  5. 3. Points. Points go to the CREW, not to a person. For the product, for the roles doing their job, for English without prompting. I show the empty leaderboard sheet on the wall.
  6. 4. The product of the pair. Every lesson your crew makes ONE thing. Today: your card — who I am and where I'm from.
  7. And the key promise: You can always hold your notes. This is not a test.
📺 On screen: the format slide (backup — the 4 points in marker on the board + an empty leaderboard sheet on poster paper, it stays up the whole unit) · role cards
👦 Students do: they get to know each other, sort out the roles, understand the rules of the game. Expect: wariness → curiosity. Problem: they clump with their friends — I form the crews, and it was announced as a rule

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can introduce themselves in 5 written lines (name · age · country · nationality · languages) and ask three questions to a new person, using be in affirmative, negative and question forms with correct short answers.
  • Subsidiary. Onboarding into the Get Involved format: crews, roles, points, “the product of the pair”, stickers 🟢🟡🔴. 14 country↔nationality pairs (SB p.6 ex.1–2) with cards and a support table; 10 classroom words and six teacher instructions (SB p.7 ex.2, ex.4) — they will be heard all year.
  • Personal. I put nobody on the spot without a draft: every speaking task comes AFTER the written fixing. The word bank stays on screen the whole lesson, and I point at it with a gesture instead of feeding the word. I name the grammar openly — new students need certainty, not intrigue.
By the end, students can…
  • say and write 14 countries and their nationalities
  • make affirmative and negative sentences with be
  • ask Yes/No and Wh- questions and give correct short answers
  • work in a crew: role, score, product

Structure
I'm British. / I'm not French. · You/We/They're Spanish. / You/We/They aren't American. · He/She/It's from Japan. / He/She/It isn't Irish. · Are you Spanish? — No, I'm not. · Are you Mexican? — Yes, I am. · Wh-: What's your name? · Where are you from? · How old are you?
Form
in a Yes/No question the verb comes FIRST (Are you…?), the subject after it · the short affirmative answer takes NO contraction: Yes, I am (not «Yes, I'm») · the short negative answer DOES contract: No, I'm not / No, they aren't · in a Wh- question the verb comes AFTER the question word: What's this? Where are you from?

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Yes, I'm» — can we say that? → no: no contractions in a short affirmative answer, Yes, I am.
«You are from Spain?» — is it a question? → by intonation yes, but the rule puts the verb first: Are you from Spain?
«I'm Spain» — what's wrong? → I'm from Spain (country) OR I'm Spanish (nationality).
“I'm from Russian” — what's wrong? → the country goes after from: I'm from Russia. Or drop from: I'm Russian.

Assumptions: SB pp.6–7, KTP pair 1 (lessons 1–2). Students from outside: language roughly A1+, but the format (crews, score, product, stickers) is completely new to them. Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Fear of speaking in front of people they don't know (first lesson!)The iron rule of the pair: first we write, then we read it to a partner, and to the board only with the sheet in hand. I say this out loud in the first five minutes and then I keep to it.
They don't understand the format: what the points are, why the roles, what a product isThe first 8 minutes are “how our lesson works” in plain words and with examples. I award the first points in this very pair, saying out loud what they are for. I hand out the roles myself, on cards.
The word bank is up there but they don't use it — they just go quietThe “look up” gesture (two fingers up, towards the screen) instead of feeding the word. Plus the mini-glossary in the margin of the notebook, which we start today and keep all through Starter.

Classroom management — signals
