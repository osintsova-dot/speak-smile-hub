🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Work three First for Schools task types with the strategy said out loud before each one: an open cloze (eight gaps, one word each, nothing given), a gapped text (six sentences put back into an article, one extra), and a discussion of six questions with full answers. Then produce the fourth: a review of a film, 140–190 words, for an international magazine website.
What is genuinely hard
The open cloze is hard for one reason and it is not vocabulary: the missing words are grammatical — articles, auxiliaries, prepositions, relative pronouns, comparatives — and students look for a content word. The gapped text is hard because students read the gap instead of reading backwards and forwards from it. And a review is a genre most of the class has never written in any language: they produce a plot summary and forget the recommendation, which is half the mark.
Typical error to expect
Two or more words in an open-cloze gap — a lot of where one word is asked for scores zero even if the meaning is perfect. Say it before they start: ONE word. If your answer has a space in it, it is wrong.
Retelling the whole plot in a review — the task asks for the plot, the acting, special effects, other features and a recommendation. A review that is only plot loses under content and under communicative achievement at the same time.
«In my opinion this film is very interesting and I liked it very much» — three empty adjectives in a row. A review needs specific evaluation: the pacing drags in the middle, the lead is convincing, the effects look dated.
Why the lesson is built this way
Three receptive tasks and one productive one in ninety minutes is only possible if the strategy work is short, said before the task and checked after it — never a lecture. So each of the two reading tasks gets the same three-step treatment: strategy in ninety seconds, the task, then evidence-based checking in which no answer is accepted without the words that prove it. The discussion is placed in the middle, deliberately, because it is oral and it feeds the review with ideas and evaluative language. The review is then planned in class, started in class and — honestly — finished at home, because there is not enough clock for both a full discussion and a full essay.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
Two of the three reading tasks today have no options at all. In the first one you write the missing word yourself — eight gaps, one word each — and almost every answer is a small grammar word you use a hundred times a day and never notice. In the second you put six sentences back into an article about a teenager who started a business at eight. Then we argue about the planet for a quarter of an hour, and then you write a film review, which is the only writing task in this course where you are allowed to say something is bad.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.108 exercise 1 — gist and purpose before any gap is touched

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books open at page 108. Exercise 1: read the text HOW TO BE A BOOK REVIEWER quickly — two minutes, timed — and answer two questions in pairs: Who do you think it is written for? What is the purpose of the text?
  2. Answers: it is written for somebody who wants a job reviewing books, and its purpose is to explain how to get employers to notice you.
  3. Say why that stage exists rather than skipping to the gaps: You cannot fill a gap in a text you have not understood. Two minutes of reading first saves five minutes of guessing.
  4. One quick personalisation before the grind, thirty seconds: Hands up if you have ever written a review of anything — a book, a game, a café. Where?
📺 On screen: the two questions on the board. Backup: the text is in their own book.
👦 Students do: read a short text against the clock and agree its audience and purpose in pairs.

12–26′
Open cloze · eight gaps, one word each
desks → pairs
Why: The open cloze is a grammar test disguised as reading, and naming that in one sentence changes what students look for. Rally Coach forces the reason to be spoken, which is where the strategy actually transfers.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.108 exercise 2 with the Reading exam tip taught explicitly

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the Reading exam tip on the page, then convert it into four instructions on the board: (1) read the whole text first for context; (2) before you write, read along the whole sentence, not just the gap; (3) one word only — if your answer has a space in it, it is wrong; (4) when you finish, read the text through again to check it makes sense.
  2. Add the one thing the tip does not say and which is the actual key: The missing words are almost never nouns. They are the small words — articles, auxiliaries, prepositions, relatives, comparatives. If you are hunting for a clever word, you are hunting in the wrong place.
  3. Do gap a together on the board as a worked example — Lots of us would like to be able to review books and get paid for it. But it's not always easy to see (a) ______ you can start to do this job. Elicit how and make them say why (it is a question word after see).
  4. Seven minutes, individually. Then Rally Coach in pairs: partner A explains their answer for gaps b–d, partner B for e–h, and the listener may only ask why?
  5. Check with the class, and demand the grammar reason for each: Exam success Units 7–8, Reading. 1: the text is written for somebody who wants to become a book reviewer; its purpose is to explain how to attract employers' attention. 2 (open cloze, «How to be a book reviewer»): a how · b thing · c other · d by · e to · f because / since / as · g you / some · h be. 4: the topic focus of «Young business owner» is b — the change in how businesses can be run. 5 (gapped text): 1 E · 2 G · 3 A · 4 C · 5 B · 6 F (the extra sentence is D)
  6. Do the count that makes the point land: Eight answers. How many are nouns? Not one. That is the whole task.
📺 On screen: the four-step strategy and the worked first gap written on the board. Backup: the text is printed in their own book — nothing to project.
👦 Students do: fill eight gaps alone, then explain half of them to a partner who may only ask why.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.108 exercise 3 — a genuine six-minute conversation between two heavy reading tasks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Exercise 3: What kind of books would you like to review? Why?
  2. Timed Pair Share, ninety seconds each way, and one rule that turns it into language practice: «I don't read» is not an answer. Then review something else — a game, a series, a restaurant.
  3. Take three answers to the whole class and push each one for a reason.
  4. Then plant the seed for the writing stage without announcing it: What makes a review worth reading — the summary, or the opinion? Take two answers and leave the question hanging.

SB p.108
32–36′
Stir · the sentence-insertion warm-up
standing, two teams
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies. And it derives the gapped-text strategy from the class's own reasoning ninety seconds before they need it, which is worth more than any explanation.

WHAT WE DO: four minutes on their feet that rehearses the exact skill of the next task

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Cut a short paragraph (five sentences) into strips before the lesson, one set per team of four. Any paragraph from page 97 or 103 works.
  2. Teams stand, receive the strips shuffled, and have ninety seconds to put the paragraph back in order on the desk — but they must stand to do it and each person may only move one strip at a time.
  3. Then the question that makes it a strategy stage rather than a puzzle: How did you know? What was the word that told you? Collect three answers: usually a pronoun (this, they, it), a linker (however, as a result), or a repeated noun.
  4. Write those three clue types on the board and leave them up for the next stage. That is the entire strategy for a gapped text and the class has just discovered it.
📺 On screen: the three clue types written on the board and left up. Backup: cut the strips by hand from a photocopy or have students write the five sentences on scraps.
👦 Students do: reorder a cut-up paragraph standing up, then name the words that made it possible.

36–52′
Gapped text · six sentences back into an article
desks → fours
Why: A gapped text is a cohesion task, not a comprehension task. Requiring the proving word for every answer is what converts guessing into reading, and the extra sentence is the most instructive item on the page.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.108 exercises 4 and 5 — the second Reading exam tip, then evidence-based checking

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Exercise 4 first, and keep it to ninety seconds: read the article YOUNG BUSINESS OWNER quickly and decide the topic focus — (a) how to start your own business, (b) the change in how businesses can be run, or (c) what you need to be successful. Answer: b.
  2. Read the second Reading exam tip on the page — check every sentence in each gap; don't ignore one because you have already selected it — and add the three clue types from the board: pronouns, linkers, repeated nouns.
  3. Give the working method explicitly, because it is what separates a five from a two: Read the sentence BEFORE the gap and the sentence AFTER it. The answer nearly always links backwards. Then try your chosen sentence in the gap and read all three aloud in your head.
  4. Eight minutes, individually. Then Numbered Heads Together in fours: agree the six answers and, for each one, agree the word that proves it. Call a number.
  5. Take the answers with evidence, never without: 1 E · 2 G · 3 A · 4 C · 5 B · 6 F (the extra sentence is D)
  6. Two are worth showing explicitly. Gap 1 is E because the next sentence begins He had support because … — support for what? For the goal named in E. Gap 3 is A because it begins What's more, which can only add to the sentence before it. That is a linker doing your work for you.
  7. Name the extra sentence and why it is a trap: D is about paying people and changing a business model — plausible, on topic, and linked to nothing.
📺 On screen: the three clue types still on the board from the stirrer. Backup: the article is in the students' own book.
👦 Students do: decide a topic focus, place six sentences alone, then agree answers and evidence in fours and be called by number.

🎯 Discussion · six questions about protecting the planet
pairs → fours → whole class
Why: The individual written preparation is what makes the discussion worth having — without it, four minutes of talk becomes two students improvising. The peer assessment gives the listeners a job and teaches the criteria far faster than being told them.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.147 exercises 6, 7 and 8 — the oral half of Exam success, with peer assessment

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn to page 147. Read the Speaking exam tip aloud: make sure you give a full answer with your opinion and reasons for your point of view.
  2. Exercise 6, four minutes, individually and in writing — this is preparation, not discussion, and the Teacher's Book is explicit that it should be done alone. Six questions: Should we all do something to protect the planet or just governments? What is the easiest thing individuals can do? Why do some people not help? How far are natural disasters caused by climate change? Should countries help each other? What is one thing you would like to do? Notes only, with an explanation or an example for each.
  3. Before they talk, elicit the rules of a discussion rather than stating them: What should you do in this kind of task? What should you NOT do? Collect and board the answers — understand exactly what is asked, give reasons, listen and respond, do not dominate.
  4. Exercise 7, eight minutes, in fours. Two speak while two listen, then swap after four minutes. The listeners have one job: mark the four assessment points of exercise 8 for each speaker.
  5. Exercise 8, three minutes, the peer feedback, and keep it structured: each listener gives each speaker one thing that was good and one thing to change, using the four points — range of grammar and vocabulary, whether the question was fully answered, whether the opinion was clear, whether there were enough reasons or examples.
  6. Take two errors you overheard to the board, anonymised, and fix them with the class.
📺 On screen: the discussion rules and the four assessment points written on the board. Backup: nothing to fail — this is a spoken stage with a printed page.
👦 Students do: prepare six answers alone in writing, discuss in fours while the other pair assesses, then give and receive structured feedback.

Writing · a review, planned and started here
whole class → desks
Why: A review is a genre nobody has written, so it needs its features named and a plan on the board. Starting it in class sets the shape and guarantees the part written at home is a continuation rather than a fresh, unguided attempt.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.147 exercises 9, 10 and 11 — the genre analysed, planned and begun in class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Exercise 9, two minutes, whole class: What makes a good film? Why? Take four answers fast and board the evaluative words that come out — plot, cast, pacing, effects, soundtrack, ending, predictable, gripping, convincing, dated. Feed the ones nobody supplies; a review lives on these.
  2. Exercise 10: read the advert — REVIEWS WANTED! on an international film magazine website — and do the Teacher's Book's four questions in pairs before anybody writes: (a) What kind of text? (b) Who for? (c) What information must you include? (d) What else must you do? Answers: a review · an international film magazine website · the plot, the acting, special effects and any other important features · say whether you would recommend it to other teenagers.
  3. Elicit the paragraph plan and write it on the board: P1 introduction — what the film is and one sentence of set-up · P2–P4 plot, acting, effects and anything else, one paragraph each with an opinion, not just a description · P5 conclusion and recommendation.
  4. Name the genre trap explicitly, because it is the one that costs the marks: This is not a summary. If I finish reading and I do not know what YOU thought, you have written the wrong text. And do not tell me the ending.
  5. Brief notes for the three main paragraphs, three minutes, silent — the Teacher's Book instruction, and it is what makes the writing possible.
  6. Then write, eight minutes of silence — as far as they get. Aim: the introduction and one full middle paragraph finished in class, so that the shape is set before it goes home. Circulate and unstick.
  7. Exercise 11, two minutes: swap what you have with a partner and use the Writing checklist on page 141 to give one suggestion. The rest is finished at home — say so honestly rather than pretending: You finish it tonight, in the same colour pen, and I want the word count at the bottom. A hundred and forty to a hundred and ninety.
📺 On screen: the evaluative vocabulary and the five-paragraph plan written on the board and left up. Backup: the advert and the checklist are in the students' own book — no display dependency.
👦 Students do: supply evaluative vocabulary, analyse the task in pairs, make notes, write the opening of a review in silence and swap it for one suggestion.

Structure
It's not always easy to see how you can start to do this job. · What's more, they are using their creativity in intellectually challenging ways. · As far as I'm concerned, individuals can do far more than governments. · The plot is gripping, although the ending is a little predictable. · I would definitely recommend it to other teenagers.
Form
an open-cloze gap takes exactly one word, and it is almost always grammatical: an article, an auxiliary, a preposition, a relative pronoun, a quantifier or half a fixed phrase · a gapped text is solved by cohesion — reference words (this, they, he, it), linkers (what's more, although, meanwhile) and lexical repetition · a review is present tense for the plot (the film tells the story of …) and past for your experience of watching it, with evaluation in every paragraph.
Pron. watch
reviews live on stress for evaluation, not on new sounds. Drill the contrast that carries an opinion: the plot is GRIPPING versus the plot is a bit PREDICTable — the heavy stress falls on the evaluative adjective, and Russian speakers tend to flatten it. Two words that break in discussion: ˈgovernment /ˈɡʌvənmənt/ (the first n disappears) and enˈvironment /ɪnˈvaɪrənmənt/ (stress on the second syllable, and the -ron- is a schwa).
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
open cloze: How many words go in the gap? — One. If I write «a lot of», how many marks? — Zero.
gapped text: Do I read only the sentence before the gap? — No, before AND after. Does the missing sentence usually link back or forward? — Back.
a review: Do I tell the whole story? — No. Do I say the ending? — Never. Do I have to say whether I recommend it? — Yes, that is half the task.
ICQ before the discussion: Do you write full sentences in exercise 6 or notes? Do you prepare alone or in your group? What do the two listeners do while the other two talk?

Assumptions: Students have met multiple-choice cloze and gapped text once before, in Exam success 5–6, and will remember the shape but not the strategy. Open cloze with nothing given is the least familiar of the three. Nobody in the group has written a review in English, and most have not written one in Russian either.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Students write two words in an open-cloze gap.Say the rule before they start and again when you check, and make the count visible: eight gaps, eight single words, none of them nouns. It is a rule about the task, not about English, so it has to be stated.
The gapped text is answered by elimination and luck.Numbered Heads requires the proving word for every answer, and the two worked examples (gap 1 and gap 3) show exactly what proof looks like. If a four cannot produce the word, the answer does not count even if it is right.
The discussion is dominated by two students.The four-in-a-group structure with two assessing is what prevents it, and the assessment sheet has «did not dominate the conversation» on it. Swap the pairs after four minutes on the clock, not when it feels right.
There is no time to write the review.There isn't, and the plan says so honestly: notes and the opening in class, the rest at home. Do not cut the discussion to buy writing time — the discussion is half of what this Exam success page is.
«This film is very interesting» appears in every review.Board the evaluative vocabulary in exercise 9 before anybody writes and ban the three empty adjectives out loud: «interesting», «nice» and «very good» are not opinions. Tell me what was gripping, what dragged, who was convincing.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 and 26–31 (the open cloze and word-formation halves) and tasks 39–40 (the oral discussion).

The open cloze on this Exam success page — eight gaps, one word each, no options given — is the closest thing in the Cambridge format to the Russian grammar-in-a-text block, and it fails for the same reason: candidates read the gap instead of reading the sentence. The discussion half is the shape of the Russian oral: six questions, a full answer with a reason, and no one-word replies.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
