Opening · check-in + rewind
desks

WHAT WE DO: entry; rewind P1 is woven in: I describe a room — they draw (ex.1) and compare with a neighbour

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Entry: Fist bump — one room word each!
  2. Rewind P1 = ex.1 p.74: I read the room description TWICE, slowly, they draw it as a diagram.
  3. Compare with your partner. Where's the ball? Where's the guitar? I check against the text: bed between the door and a desk · laptop on the desk · chair in front of it · window above the desk · ball under the bed · school bag near the door, guitar behind it.
  4. I praise: You drew a room from WORDS. That's real English.
📺 On screen: SB p.74 ex.1 (I read) · sheets for drawing · board: preposition gestures from P1
👦 Students do: draw by ear and check with a partner. Expected: 4–5 out of 6 objects in place. Typical error: they mix up behind/near — I reread the line slowly.

Hook: what an escape room is
Hook · what an escape room is
screen · pairs

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 (what kind of rooms are in the photos) + ex.3 (we read ONLY the box) + Fun facts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.2 in pairs, 2 min: Photos A–C. What rooms and furniture can you see? I help with a frame: I can see a sofa, some books and a table.
  2. Ex.3: Read ONLY the small box above the title — five lines. Why is it called an ESCAPE room? Key: escape room = you’re in a room and have to find clues and solve puzzles to get out.
  3. Fun facts: the idea of escape rooms came from Japan; there's a three-hour room in Athens. Three hours! Would you go? — a vote by hands.
  4. Words on the board with pictures: escape · clue · puzzle.
📺 On screen: SB pp.74–75 · board: escape / clue / puzzle with drawings
👦 Students do: look at the photos, read only the box. Expected: the topic hooks them — many know escape rooms. Typical error: they start reading the whole text — the stop gesture “just the box”.

Read: one team — one room
Discover · three rooms, three teams

WHAT WE DO: jigsaw: each team reads its own paragraph and fills in an expert card; then ex.4 (photos↔texts) with the whole class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out roles: team 1 — Sherlock, 2 — Wizarding School, 3 — Room 33. Read ONLY your room. Four minutes. You are the experts!
  2. Expert card (I hand out): Place: ___ · Time: ___ minutes · Players: ___ · Problem: ___ · One strange thing: ___.
  3. I help each team personally for 40 seconds: I hint at WHICH line to look in.
  4. Ex.4 with the whole class, 60 sec: Match photos A–C with texts 1–3. Key: A – 2 (a witch’s hat on the sofa, books) · B – 3 (a vase, museum, lasers) · C – 1 (Sherlock’s hat, papers on the walls). I ask: Which thing in the photo helped you? (the witch's hat · the vase and the museum · Sherlock's hat and papers).
📺 On screen: SB p.75 · expert card for each team · timer
👦 Students do: read a short chunk and fill in a card — a manageable amount, an honourable role. Expected: every team's card is filled in. Typical error: they write in Russian — the card's English sentence starters won't let them.

24–33′
Try it: exchanging facts in trios
Try it · exchange facts + ex.5
mixed trios

WHAT WE DO: Numbered Heads Together: mixed trios (one expert from each room) — answer 6 questions in ex.5, each for their own room

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I reseat them into mixed trios: One expert from each room. Now you have ALL the information.
  2. I run the check as Kagan Numbered Heads Together: numbers 1–4 in the team, Heads together — make sure EVERYONE in your crew can answer! → I call a number — that exact person answers, no prompting from the floor.
  3. Ex.5, 5 min: 6 questions, whoever's room it is answers. Answer frame on the board: That's Room 33 — the players travel in time. Key: 1 Room 33 · 2 The Wizarding School · 3 Sherlock: The Game Is Now (Dr Watson needs your help) · 4 The Wizarding School · 5 Sherlock: The Game Is Now · 6 Room 33.
  4. Kagan mode (Numbered Heads): I call a number — that person answers for the whole trio. Everyone prepares, one answers.
  5. Check in chorus + points to the trios.
📺 On screen: SB p.74 ex.5 · board: answer frame
👦 Students do: exchange facts in trios — everyone speaks, because only they read their own room. Expected: 5–6 out of 6. Typical error: the expert stays silent — Numbered Heads doesn't let them hide.

33–45′
Build the skill: true/false with proof
Build · true/false with proof
teams

WHAT WE DO: subskill in 4 steps + ex.6 (6 statements) with showing the line; we correct the false ones together

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Subskill on the board, 4 steps (SB p.74): 1 read the sentence · 2 find the place in the text · 3 read carefully · 4 same = TRUE, different = FALSE.
  2. We do step 1 TOGETHER on the board, pointing at the line — a model of the procedure.
  3. Ex.6 in teams, 6 min, rule: Point at the line. No line — no answer. Key: 1 True (para 1) · 2 True (para 3) · 3 False — the museum is empty (para 4) · 4 False — the game starts in a shop (para 2) · 5 False — Sherlock is 100 minutes (para 2) · 6 True — monster + lasers (para 3 and 4).
  4. We correct the false ones in CHORUS with a full sentence: False — the museum is EMPTY.
  5. Reinforcement (TB p.85): I tell weaker teams which paragraph to look in.
📺 On screen: SB p.74 ex.6 · board: 4 subskill steps (stay up the whole unit) · timer
👦 Students do: look for lines and point at them, correct the false ones in chorus. Expected: 4–6 out of 6 when working step by step. Typical error: “I think so” — the rule “show the line” cures it.

teams · standing

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: ex.7 definitions ↔ words (from context) + ex.8 gap-fill; ▶ Charades/drawings to reinforce

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rule (TB p.86): Read the WHOLE sentence with the bold word. What can it be? We work through the first word together.
  2. Ex.7 in pairs, 4 min. Key: 1 lost · 2 vase · 3 clues · 4 emperor · 5 monster · 6 crime.
  3. Ex.8 in chorus round the chain: 1 crime · 2 monster · 3 emperor · 4 lost · 5 vase · 6 clues.
  4. ▶ Draw or mime: a team gets a word (clue · vase · monster · crime · emperor · lost) and has 30 sec to draw it on the board or mime it — the rest guess in English. 6 rounds, one from each team.
  5. Pronunciation drill: vase /vɑːz/ — not ‘vaze’! EM-peror.
  6. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
📺 On screen: SB p.74 ex.7–8 · word cards · board for drawing
👦 Students do: work out meanings from the sentence, then draw/mime — they move and laugh. Expected: 6/6 after the game. Typical error: translation instead of explanation — the game's rules ban translation.

WHAT WE DO: critical thinking through CHOICE: Remember (what people do to escape) + ranking 6 reasons with a support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Remember, 2 min with a support: What do people do to escape? Key TB p.86: Sherlock — solve a crime · Wizarding School — find a lost object · Room 33 — travel in time to get a vase.
  2. Analyse: 6 ideas from the book on the board as icon pictures (real-life games · fantasy · friends · a different world · the time limit · puzzles).
  3. The trios choose their TOP-3 and put stickers on the board (a vote, not a discussion!). Frame: Number one is FRIENDS because it's fun with your friends.
  4. We count the votes as a class, 3 comments out loud.
📺 On screen: board: 6 reason icons · voting stickers · frame “Number one is … because …”
👦 Students do: choose and vote with stickers, explain from the frame — everyone takes part, no long discussion. Expected: “friends” wins. Typical error: a vote with no because — the gesture “because…?”

Create & Share · your own room

WHAT WE DO: from a 7-sentence frame the trio invents its own escape room, draws the plan and presents it to the class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame on the board in full: The name of our escape room is … · It's for (three/four) players. · The players are in (place) in (time). · The idea of the game is to … · The problem is that … · To escape, players … · Be careful! There's a surprise!.
  2. Roles in the trio: Storyteller (the plot) · Designer (the room plan with 3 prepositions!) · Puzzle master (the riddle).
  3. 8 min: they write from the frame (copying the structure is allowed!) and draw a plan on A3.
  4. Show: each trio, 40 sec — 3 sentences from the frame + show the plan. ALL trios present.
  5. Vote with tickets: everyone has 1 sticker — where they'd go. Points to the teams.
📺 On screen: board: 7-sentence frame · A3 + markers · “ticket” stickers
👦 Students do: write from the frame (that's allowed and takes away the fear), draw and present — everyone has their own role. Expected: every trio has a finished mini-text. Typical error: a plan with no prepositions — that's exactly what the Designer role is for.

WB Unit 6 (pp. 44–51) · notebook · a phone photo of the board — allowed
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can answer true/false questions about an entertainment guide and find the line that proves the answer (subskill), and use 6 new words from the text.
  • Subsidiary. The long text is cut into 3 chunks (one per team); rooms and prepositions from P1 work in a new context; critical thinking — through choice and ranking, not a long discussion; your own escape room from a frame.
  • Personal. Don't read the whole text frontally: they'll drown. Each team reads ONE room and becomes the “expert” — that's both motivation and a manageable amount. The lesson's main habit: point at the proof line.
By the end, students can…
  • find information in a short text and show the line
  • answer true/false with a reason
  • explain 6 new words in simple English
  • describe an imaginary escape room from a frame

Pron. watch

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Escape room — is that a room in a hotel?” → no, a game: you look for clues and get out.
“Clues — what's that?” → things that help you solve a puzzle.
“True or false — can you guess?” → no: first we find the line in the text.

Assumptions: Rooms and prepositions from P1 are fresh in memory — we build on them while reading. A long authentic text — the first such experience in the unit, so only in chunks and with tasks. Present continuous hasn't been introduced yet.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The text looks hugeEach team reads ONLY its own paragraph (jigsaw) — 6–8 lines instead of 25. A manageable amount.
Unfamiliar words block themRule: underline only the THREE most disruptive words; the rest — from the picture and context.
They guess true/falseI require them to point at the line. We work through the 4 steps of the subskill from the board.
Word work — they reach for a translatorFirst we read the WHOLE sentence with the bold word (TB p.86), then match it to the definition.
The Create stage stallsA full 7-sentence frame on the board + roles in the trio; you're allowed to write straight from the frame.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
