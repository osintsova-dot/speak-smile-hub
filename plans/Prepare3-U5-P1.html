🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Name fifteen sports, games and activities, and say what is compulsory, what is forbidden and what is simply not necessary with must, mustn't, have to and don't have to.
What is genuinely hard
One contrast, and only one: mustn't and don't have to look like a pair and mean opposite things. Russian expresses both of them with a negative plus one short word, and the two Russian phrases sit close enough together to hide the difference.
Typical error to expect
We mustn't bring food where the meaning is «it isn't necessary». mustn't = forbidden, don't have to = not necessary. This is the single biggest confusion of the unit [A2/B1]
You must to wear a hat / You mustn't to talk — a to-infinitive after a modal. After must and mustn't the verb is bare (SB p.55 ex.2) [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The grammar arrives inside a real document: ten climbing-club rules that decide whether you get to climb. That is far better input than ten example sentences, and it makes the mustn't / don't have to contrast obvious — one rule gets you thrown out, the other is just information. Vocabulary comes first because the reading and the final task are made of it. The past forms (had to / didn't have to) get their own short slot rather than a footnote: they take two minutes to teach and are lost in every exam paper. The final stage has students write the rules of their own invented club, which is the only way to make all four forms necessary at once.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two sentences that look like twins. You mustn't bring your own shoes. You don't have to bring your own shoes. One of them gets you thrown out of the club. By the end of today you will know which — and you will write the rules of your own club.”

Grammar · discovery
pairs → class
Why: Guided discovery from the rules they have just read. The four forms are simple; the whole difficulty is one contrast, so the discovery is aimed at that contrast and nothing else.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2 p.55: sort the four forms into three meanings, and find the form of the verb after them

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pre-teach one word (TB p.106): necessary — something you need to do. Test it fast: drink — necessary? eat chocolate? sleep? travel by taxi?
  2. ex.1 in pairs, 4 minutes: underline the four forms in the club rules and match them to three meanings. Key: 1 c · 2 c · 3 a · 4 b. Note that one meaning is used TWICE — must and have to share it.
  3. Now make the contrast explicit with the CCQs: You mustn't take photos — can I take one photo? You don't have to bring shoes — can I bring my own shoes?
  4. If they still translate the two the same way, use the prison picture: TB p.106, for the have to / don't have to confusion: draw a barred window on the board with a face behind it. Fred is in prison. Can he get up when he wants? (no) → Fred has to get up at six o'clock. Is it all right to stay in bed? (no) → Fred mustn't stay in bed. There are other people in prison. Does Fred need to see them? (yes) → Fred has to see other people but he doesn't have to be friends with them.
  5. ex.2: underline the main verb after must and have to. Key: the infinitive without to.
  6. Add the past in one line and put it on the board to stay: must and have to → had to. don't have to → didn't have to.
📺 On screen: the four forms and the three meanings on the screen, matched live as the class decides (Backup: draw the three meanings as three boxes in chalk and let students come up and write the forms into them)
👦 Students do: sort the forms, then answer the concept questions. Expect the mustn't / don't have to pair to be wrong at first and right after the prison picture. Typical error: they put must and have to in different boxes

Grammar · practice
solo → pairs
Why: Controlled practice in two directions: rewriting rules, then shifting them into the past. The past forms take two minutes to teach and are forgotten in every exam paper.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 p.55 (rewrite the dance class rules) and ex.5 (the same forms in the past)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4: eight dance-class rules to rewrite with must, mustn't or don't have to. Do the first one together — Is it OK to eat in class? So which form do we need? (mustn't). Then solo, 5 minutes. Key: You mustn't chew gum or bring food into the class. · You mustn't talk while the teacher is talking. · You don't have to wear special clothes. · You must put your phone on silent during the lesson. · You mustn't leave a class before the end. · You mustn't wear street shoes inside the dance studio. · You don't have to call if you need to miss a class. · You must take off all jewellery before class..
  2. Kagan RallyCoach to check: the partner asks Forbidden or just not necessary? before accepting an answer.
  3. ex.5 — the past. Pre-teach goggles (glasses you wear to swim or dive) and set the frame (TB p.106): When was the competition? (last week) So can we use must? (no).
  4. Solo, 4 minutes. Key: They had to arrive at the pool at 8.30 am. · They had to wear a swimming hat. · They didn't have to wear goggles. · They had to bring sandwiches for lunch. · They didn't have to stay until 6 pm..
  5. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Sentence three — number twos, boards up!
📺 On screen: the eight dance-class rules on the screen, one at a time (Backup: they are printed on SB p.55 — uncover them on the page with a ruler and keep the must / mustn't / don't have to summary in chalk on the board)
👦 Students do: rewrite, check with the meaning question, then shift into the past. Expect ex.5 items 3 and 5 (didn't have to) to be the ones that go wrong. Typical error: They must to arrive at 8.30

Grammar · error hunt
teams
Why: Correcting other people's sentences is faster and safer than producing them, and the Showdown means every student answers every item instead of one student answering one.

WHAT WE DO: ex.7 p.55: five sentences that are wrong, corrected as a team against the clock

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Five sentences, 4 minutes. Kagan Showdown: the team agrees a correction, writes it on the mini-whiteboard, and on Showdown! every board goes up at once.
  2. Key: The skatepark is free — we don't have to pay anything. · You have to bring your pencil case to the next lesson. / You must bring your pencil case to the next lesson. · We don't have to bring food to the party — Jake's mum is making everything. · When I was younger, I had to live far away from my grandparents. · Dad says I don't have to help him on Saturday, so I can come to your house!
  3. For every item, one team says WHY — not what. Forbidden, necessary, or not necessary?
  4. Note that item 2 has two correct answers (have to or must) and say so: Two teams can both be right here.
  5. Item 4 is the past one — if nobody catches it, that is your line for the review lesson.
📺 On screen: the five sentences one at a time on the screen so the whole class repairs the same one (Backup: write the five sentences on the board before the lesson and uncover them one by one with a sheet of paper)
👦 Students do: correct as a team and reveal together. Expect 3–4 out of 5. Typical error: item 5 (I mustn't help him → I don't have to help him) — the exact contrast of the lesson, and the one that decides whether it worked
SB p.55 ex.7 · mini-whiteboards
74–80′
Pronunciation · must and mustn't
class
Why: Six minutes on a sound that changes the meaning: if the t is not heard, mustn't and must become the same word — and they mean opposite things.

WHAT WE DO: ex.8–9 p.55 (tracks 70–71): the weak form, and a listening discrimination round

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Play track 70 and repeat: You must listen carefully. You mustn't speak now.
  2. TB p.106: the weak, normal pronunciation of must is /məs/ before a vowel and /məst/ before a consonant — the final t is not heard. The strong form /mʌst/ exists but is only used for emphasis. mustn't is /ˈmʌsnt/ — the first t is silent there too.
  3. The reason it matters, in one sentence: If you cannot hear the t, you cannot hear the difference between 'you must' and 'you mustn't'. Those are opposite instructions.
  4. ex.9 — play track 71: seven sentences, a tick for must and a cross for mustn't. Key: 1 ✓ · 2 ✓ · 3 ✗ · 4 ✓ · 5 ✗ · 6 ✓ · 7 ✗.
  5. Play it again and repeat all seven chorally.
📺 On screen: tracks 70 and 71 in the board app (Backup: say the seven sentences yourself, twice each — once weak, once strong — and have the class show one finger for must and two for mustn't)
👦 Students do: listen and mark, then repeat. Expect 5 out of 7. Typical error: sentences 3 and 5 (both mustn't) heard as must, because the t is genuinely almost inaudible

🎯 Speaking out · your own club
pairs → fours
Why: The productive outcome: they invent a club and write its rules, which forces every one of the four forms for a reason that is theirs. Errors are logged silently.

WHAT WE DO: ex.10 p.55 and SB p.137: invent a club, write its rules, then explain them to another pair

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. SB p.137 (Unit 9 ex.10): in pairs, students choose a sport and a name for their club and write its rules with the modal verbs; then pairs join into fours and tell one another about their club.
  2. Set the frame: Choose a sport. Choose a name. Write six rules — at least one MUST, one MUSTN'T and one DON'T HAVE TO. 4 minutes.
  3. Then pairs join into fours and read their rules to each other. The listeners' job: decide whether they would join, and say why in one sentence.
  4. The standard from the lesson frame: Every rule must be a rule somebody else could follow. A rule nobody understands does not count.
  5. Log errors silently on your correction sheet; do not correct aloud — the feedback slot is the start of the next lesson.
📺 On screen: the three required forms written on the board as a checklist (Backup: the same checklist dictated and copied into notebooks — the task needs no technology at all)
👦 Students do: invent a club and write six rules, then present. Expect the mustn't rules to be the funniest and the don't-have-to rules to be the rarest. Typical error: all six rules use must — the checklist is what prevents it

WB Unit 9 (p.36–39)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can express obligation, prohibition and lack of necessity with must / mustn't / have to / don't have to, in the present and in the past.
  • Subsidiary. Fifteen sports and activities; reading a set of rules for detail; the weak pronunciation of must.
  • Personal. Teach one contrast, not four forms. If the class leaves knowing that mustn't and don't have to are opposites, the lesson has worked even if the past forms need another visit.
By the end, students can…
  • name fifteen sports, games and activities
  • say what is forbidden and what is simply not necessary, and hear the difference
  • put a rule into the past with had to / didn't have to
  • write the rules of a club so that another group can follow them

Form
must / mustn't / have to / don't have to + infinitive WITHOUT to. Past: had to / didn't have to for all of them (SB p.55).
Pron. watch
the weak form of must — /məs/ before a vowel, /məst/ before a consonant, the final t not heard; mustn't is /ˈmʌsnt/, also with a silent t (TB p.106). Word stress: ˈbadminton · ˈskateboarding · ˈfitness class.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«You mustn't take photos.» Can I take one photo? → no. It is forbidden.
«You don't have to bring your own shoes.» Can I bring my own shoes? → yes, if I want. It is simply not necessary.
So which one is stronger, mustn't or don't have to? → mustn't. They look alike and mean opposite things.
«I must to go» — what is wrong? → no to after must.
«Last week I must fill in a form» — what is wrong? → the past of must is had to.

Assumptions: SB p.54–55, syllabus lesson 28. must and have to have both been met passively; neither has been contrasted. Of the fifteen activities they usually know badminton, cricket, golf, karate and video game; fitness class, dance class, board game and puzzle are the ones that need teaching.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The whole class translates mustn't and don't have to identicallyDo not explain. Use the TB p.106 prison picture: Fred is in prison. Can he get up when he wants? Is it all right to stay in bed? Does he need to be friends with the others? Three questions, three sentences on the board, and the contrast is visible.
“Is puzzle really a sport?”Let the argument run for exactly one minute — it is the argument the whole of the next lesson is built on, and it costs nothing here. Then: Keep that. We come back to it on Thursday.
The past forms disappear the moment the sentence is longerGive them the ONE thing to remember, written on the board and left there: must and have to → had to. don't have to → didn't have to. There is no past of mustn't.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
