🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the present perfect with just, already and yet — the right word in the right slot: just and already inside the sentence, yet at the end of negatives and questions — to talk about the very recent past and unmet expectations.
What is genuinely hard
Russian says «уже», «только что» and «ещё не» with any tense at all, so the hard part is not the words but the package: the present perfect must come WITH them, and each word owns a slot. Already keeps sneaking into negatives where English wants yet.
Typical error to expect
We just see your advertisement · I haven't already bought it · Did you finish yet? — present simple or past simple where the perfect is needed, already trapped in a negative, yet with the wrong tense; the frames are I've just seen · I haven't bought it yet · Have you finished yet? [high priority, A2/B1]
A one-word reply («Comedy.», «Yes.») does not count as an answer at all: an answer needs a verb in it [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
⚠️ The КТП's page note says «p.77 + Grammar reference», but the grammar section physically sits on p.78 (top) — the plan follows the book. This is a pure grammar lesson (the extended block allows it), built on the block's proven discovery shape: every example in ex.1 comes from the worst-day article the class argued about on Tuesday, so the rules are extracted, not announced. The controlled work climbs from choose (ex.2) through build (ex.4) to correct real learner errors (ex.5 Corpus — the same errors the Russian exam reports list). Kate's party picture becomes a memory game per the TB, the walked reset drills the three slots, and the productive interview asks about real lives — with a truth-or-lie round to finish, because nobody listens harder than a lie-detector. Homework closes on WB p.54, whose grammar page was built for exactly this lesson.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Three little words — just, already, yet — and your English suddenly has a NOW button. I've JUST eaten. I've ALREADY done it. I haven't done it YET. Same tense you already own — plus three words that tell everyone exactly where you are in your day. By the end you'll interview each other and catch liars with them.”

Grammar · controlled choice
pairs
Why: Choose-and-justify practice: the deciding question is said out loud every time (when? sooner than expected? still waiting?). The justification is what transfers to new sentences.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 choose + ex.4 build full sentences — the reason said every time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2, four dialogues — solo, 3 minutes, then pair check with the reason compulsory: Say WHY: negative → yet, at the end. Key: choose: 1 yet · 2 already · 3 just · 4 yet.
  2. ex.4, six prompts into full sentences — Kagan RallyCoach, one pen: partner A builds 1–3 aloud, B coaches; swap for 4–6. Key: make sentences: 1 We've just heard some amazing news · 2 I've already put off my dentist's appointment once · 3 It's eleven o'clock and she hasn't got up yet · 4 I've just sat down and I'm absolutely exhausted · 5 Have you tried Jack's cake yet? · 6 My phone was fully charged this morning and it's already run out of battery.
  3. Kagan Showdown, three calls: you give a situation (Your friend came home a minute ago. · The homework was finished at lunch. · The bus is late — still no bus.), teams WRITE the sentence and show on three.
  4. Every slip goes into your silent log — the interview is coming.
📺 On screen: situations called by voice, answers on mini-whiteboards — voice and boards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: choose, justify, build, commit in writing. Expect ex.4 item 3 (hasn't got up yet) to test the negative slot — it is the item to slow down on. Typical error: I've yet sat down — point at the card, silently

Corpus clinic
pairs → class
Why: Real learner errors — the exact ones Russian students make — get fixed by the class, not by the teacher. Finding someone else's mistake is the fastest way to stop making it yourself.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: six real learner sentences, one mistake each — find it, fix it, name it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame it honestly: Six sentences by real students. Each has ONE mistake — tense or word order. You are the examiners today.
  2. Pairs, 5 minutes — find, fix, and NAME the mistake type. Key: Corpus, correct the mistake (tense or word order): 1 We'VE JUST SEEN your advertisement · 2 I haven't bought a new book YET / I've ALREADY bought · 3 HAVE I TOLD you about my holiday yet? · 4 I'VE already askED a friend of mine · 5 He'S JUST moved · 6 I haven't been THERE YET.
  3. Collect with reasons: Number two — what was wrong? … already in a negative. Which word wanted that seat? … yet.
  4. The scoreboard moment: These six mistakes are THE six mistakes for Russian speakers too. You've just fixed all of them. Remember this feeling in the test.
📺 On screen: the six sentences are on SB p.78 in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hunt the error, fix it, classify it. Expect item 3 (Did I tell you yet?) to be defended by a film-English fan — the course rule from the frame settles it kindly. Typical problem: fixing without naming — the naming is the transfer

🎯 Speaking out · have you … yet?
mingling → pairs
Why: The productive outcome: the three little words in real questions about real lives, under time pressure, with follow-ups. Errors are logged silently — correcting now would kill fluency.

WHAT WE DO: the interview: real questions about real lives, both slots, follow-ups compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Question bank on the board: Have you had lunch yet? · Have you finished the WB page yet? · Have you already planned the weekend? · What have you just been thinking about? — plus everyone writes ONE question of their own.
  2. Kagan Stand Up–Hand Up–Pair Up: find a partner, ask two questions plus one follow-up each, answer in FULL sentences; three rounds, three partners.
  3. Round three upgrade: answers must contain a feeling from lesson 1 — No, I haven't started it yet, and I'm slightly stressed about it.
  4. Two reporters share the best discovery: Maxim has already bought a birthday present — in FEBRUARY. For September.
  5. You walk with the log: slips of order and tense go down SILENTLY — three of them open lesson 3.
📺 On screen: the question bank in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: interview three partners, follow up, answer in full sentences with feelings attached. Expect the own-question round to produce the funniest items — harvest two for the truth-or-lie round. Typical error: Have you finished already yet? — both seats claimed; logged, not corrected

76–86′
Truth or lie round
pairs → class
Why: Three sentences about today, one of them false — nobody can play without producing the target forms, and nobody listens harder than a lie-detector. Retrieval with an audience.

WHAT WE DO: three sentences about today — one is false; the class detects

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone writes three sentences about their real day: one with just, one with already, one with yet — and exactly ONE of the three is a lie.
  2. Pairs first: read your three, partner guesses the lie and must say WHY: You can't have already done the homework — it was set today!
  3. Three volunteers perform for the class; the class votes on the lie, then the truth is revealed.
  4. Close the grammar loop: To catch a liar you listened to three perfect little sentences each. That's the fluency the test wants — you already have it.
📺 On screen: sentences in notebooks and voices in the room — paper and voice are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write two truths and a lie, detect, justify the detection. Expect the lies to cluster on already (ambition inflates) — a nice pattern to name aloud. Typical problem: a lie so obvious it kills the game — model one subtle lie first

Lexis

Structure
I've just heard some amazing news. · I've already put it off once. · She hasn't got up yet. · Have you tried Jack's cake yet? · It's already run out of battery.
Form
have/has + past participle + just/already (between have and the participle) · yet at the END of negatives and questions · just = a moment ago · already = sooner than expected · yet = expected, not happened.
Pron. watch
the weak forms carry it: I've /aɪv/ · She's /ʃiːz/ · yet /jət/ at the end, unstressed · the stress lands on the participle: I've just SAT down · contrastive stress on already when surprised: I've ALready done it
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I've just eaten» — when did I eat? → a minute ago, very recently. Yesterday? → no, that's too long for just.
«I've already done my homework» — am I surprised it's done? → it happened SOONER than expected. Where does already sit? → inside: after have, before done.
«I haven't done it yet» — will I do it? → yes, I still plan to; yet says «expected, not happened». Where does yet go? → at the very end.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«I already did it» — past simple pulled in from filmsAcknowledge it exists in American English, then set the course rule: with just/already/yet we use the present perfect — that is the form the book, the test and the exam all expect.
already appears in negatives (I haven't already…)The slot card answers it: already lives in POSITIVES; negatives and questions belong to yet, at the end. One choral fix per casualty, then point at the card silently.
The participle goes missing (I've just eat)Drill the three-beat rhythm: I've – just – EATEN. The Showdown round makes teams write full sentences, so the missing participle becomes visible ink.
Fast pairs finish the book work in half the timeFast-finisher line is in the plan: write one extra sentence pair about THIS classroom (We've just… · We haven't … yet) — the truth-or-lie round will consume them.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
