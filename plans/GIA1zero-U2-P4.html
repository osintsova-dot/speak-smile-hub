Opening · check-in + rewind
desks → standing

WHAT WE DO: Showdown for revision: mood emoji; P3 blitz revision: body parts by gesture + 2 have got flips + 1 apostrophe fix; bridge to the secret person

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Entry: Your mood as ONE emoji on the board — up!
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everybody writes at the same time, boards face down, on the call No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up together — I see every answer, nobody can hide.
  3. P3 revision: I point at body parts — the class calls the word in chorus; a flip on boards: She's got glasses → negative!; a fix: my dads car → dad's.
  4. The red row chant: he-she-it — HAS got it!
  5. Bridge: You can SAY things about people. But can you FIND OUT? I'm thinking of a person in this class… and today you build the question machine.
📺 On screen: mini-boards
👦 Students do: they draw an emoji, flip, fix the apostrophe. Expected: clean after P3. Typical error: hasn't→haven't with she — the red row fixes it.

WHAT WE DO: teams fill in both tables ex.1; the politeness rule ✓/✗; a rhythm chant

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Race: Page 30, exercise 1 — BOTH tables. Four minutes! Key (TB), +1 per cell.

A5 grammar card — HAVE GOT: ASK & ANSWER: the question machine (HAVE + I/you/we/they + GOT…? · HAS + he/she/it + GOT…?), the short-answer routes with the CRITICAL WARNING short answer = HAVE/HAS, not GOT (never Yes, I've.) and the noun clue a/an + singular (a brother) / any + plural (any cousins)
  • Glue the card in; pupils cross out Yes, I've. and Yes, she's. in red.
  • Build six questions about family, three with a/an and three with any.
  • Drill short answers in pairs — nothing but HAVE/HAS allowed.
  • The card stays open through the 20-questions game.

18–26′
“Live questions”: Have jumps to the front
Move it · human questions
open space

WHAT WE DO: word-children: “Have” physically runs to the front — a question is born; chorus with rising ↗ intonation

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand word cards to a group of five: you / have / got / a / pet ? — we build the statement order.
  2. Clap: The QUESTION word runs to the front! — “Have” runs across: Have you got a pet?
  3. The class reads it in chorus with ↗. I run 4 sets (has your uncle got a beard? · have they got blue eyes?…), new students hold the cards each time.
  4. The a/any gesture: one finger = a/an (one thing), spread fingers = any (many): Have you got ANY cousins? Has she got A dog?
📺 On screen: 4–5 sets of word cards · space by the board
👦 Students do: they physically build questions, “Have” runs, the class reads with ↗. Expected: laughter + physical inversion. Typical error: the wrong word runs (“got”) — the class shouts “Have! Have!”.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask have got questions with short answers (Act 1, 45′) and act a ‘talking about photos’ dialogue with Key phrases (Act 2, 45′).
  • Subsidiary. Inductive question tables; etiquette rule (Yes, I have ✓ / Yes, I've ✗); a/an vs any; Ask Alex round-up as a team quiz; RWS video model (Elena & Owen); Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; peer review.
  • Personal. Inversion is THE hard move — drill it physically (human questions, U1 ritual). KTP boundary 45′: lesson A = questions, lesson B = Real-world speaking. Opinion phrases with real feeling (TB: like you MEAN it).

  • give polite short answers
  • use a/an vs any correctly in questions

Target language
Lexis
Who are they? / Who's this/that? / Which is (Charlie)?This is (my auntie). / That's (their sister). / These are (my cousins).Where are they? · How old is she?That's a great photo. / That's really nice. / That's very funny.
Structure
Have you got any cousins? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. · Has she got curly hair? — No, she hasn't. · Who's this? That's my auntie. That's a great photo!
Form
Have/Has jumps before the subject, got stays; short answers with have/has (never «Yes, I've»); a/an + singular, any + plural.
Pron. watch
questions rise ↗; rhythm: Yes-I-HAVE! No-she-HASn't!; opinion phrases with enthusiasm.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Have you got a sister? — can I answer just ‘Yes’?” → polite English wants Yes, I have.
“Yes, I've. — OK?” → ✗! Short affirmative answers never contract.
“Have you got ANY brother?” → ✗ — any goes with PLURAL: any brothers?

Assumptions: have got statements + possessive 's systematic after P3; questions heard receptively in Seven Families (P1!). New: inversion mechanics, etiquette, a/an vs any, opinion phrases.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
No inversion (“You have got a pet?”)Human-questions game: the child holding «Have» physically RUNS to the front — body remembers.
«Yes, I've» slipRule card ✓/✗ on board; buzzer game — class softly buzzes, speaker self-corrects.
any/a confusionGesture: one finger = a/an, spread fingers = any; drill 4 items on boards before writing.
Shyness at PERFORMPhotos may be drawn/invented; all pairs perform simultaneously first, volunteers go public; applause protocol.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
