🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A grammar pair, but the topic is NOT “Present continuous” — the topic is “the live broadcast desk”: a post with a form error does not go on air. That way be + -ing, the -ing spelling and the questions work under time pressure, not inside an exercise. The students build the rule themselves out of the feed they read last time (guided discovery) — I only confirm it. The Brain teaser and the Mia/Luke chat are the final broadcast checks.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Welcome to the Broadcast Desk. We report what is happening RIGHT NOW. One rule: a line with a grammar error does not go on air — the desk kills it. Yesterday you read a whole feed written in one single shape. Today you get your broadcast licence: fast and clean. Licence test in three minutes.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We are a live broadcast desk: we report what is happening right now; a line with a form error does not go on air. The licence = fast, clean writing about the moment.

🔥 Challenge · -ing spelling sorter
crews

WHAT WE DO: the -ing sorter: 12 verbs into three baskets against the clock + ex.3 “true about yourself”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Twelve verbs, three baskets: just +ing · drop the e · double the consonant. Two minutes — crew record?
  2. The basket key: buy/wear/play/watch → +ing · take/come/write/dance → drop the e · swim/run/sit/get → double it.
  3. The baskets are checked through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: the crew first agrees on the tricky verb and makes sure everyone could explain it; then I call a number — Number threes: 'sit' — which basket, and WHY? No explanation — the basket does not count for the whole crew.
  4. ex.3 in writing, 4 lines: Make them TRUE right now: I'm sitting in class. I'm not wearing a hat. My teacher is… My classmates and I…
  5. Constraint: at least one NEGATIVE in every four lines, otherwise the line does not count.
📺 On screen: verb cards · baskets (backup — three columns on the board) · SB p.40 ex.3 · timer
👦 Students do: they sort with their hands before writing, then write about themselves. Expected: swiming gets caught by the basket first time. Error: takeing — I send them back to the rule, I don't give the answer

PRODUCT · 60-second live commentary
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a silent scene from one crew — live commentary from another; 60 seconds, zero form errors

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 6 minutes of preparation: every crew invents a 60-second silent scene (a shop · Comic Con · a school corridor · the gym) — NO words, only actions.
  2. Crew A performs in silence. Crew B commentates — live, sixty seconds. Present continuous only. Then swap.
  3. The broadcast rule: A line with an error is dead air — the judge raises a red card and you lose that line.
  4. The judges count: clean lines (accuracy) · pauses (fluency) · clothing detail (richness).
📺 On screen: red judge cards · 60-second timer (backup — we commentate on a photo from the slide instead of a scene)
👦 Students do: they act and commentate live. Expected: by the third pair of crews the commentary cleans itself up — the price of an error works better than an explanation

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can produce clean present-continuous lines at speed — affirmative, negative and questions — applying -ing spelling automatically.
  • Subsidiary. Both tables on SB p.40 closed in a sprint (aff/neg + questions and short answers); the -ing spelling sorter; the Mia/Luke chat (ex.5–6); the Brain teaser ex.7; the pronunciation of /n/ and /ŋ/ (SB p.116); the clinic on the P1–P2 errors.
  • Personal. I do NOT re-explain the rule — the students build the table from the examples in the P2 feed. Error feedback is anonymous: only the forms go on the board, never the authors.
By the end, students can…
  • complete both present continuous tables from examples
  • spell -ing forms correctly (take→taking, swim→swimming)
  • ask and answer present continuous questions with short answers
  • commentate on a live scene for 60 seconds without form errors

Form
most verbs: + -ing (buy → buying, wear → wearing) · verbs ending in -e: drop the e + -ing (take → taking, come → coming) · some verbs with “consonant + vowel + consonant”: double the consonant (swim → swimming, run → running, sit → sitting)
Pron. watch
SB p.116 /n/ vs /ŋ/: thin–thing · win–wing · go in–going; the tongue twister “Lynne is wearing thin pink leggings”
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He wearing a hat” — what is missing? → the form of be: He's wearing a hat.
“Yes, they're.” — can you answer like that? → no: a short affirmative answer keeps the full form — Yes, they are. / Yes, I am.
takeing or taking? → taking: verbs ending in -e drop the e.
swiming or swimming? → swimming: consonant + vowel + consonant → double it.

Assumptions: SB p.40 + Spelling rules p.45 + Pronunciation p.116, KTP pair 23 (both halves are grammar). The form is familiar from A1+ — the load is in the speed, the spelling and the questions.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“Present continuous again, we know it”I don't argue: a gate on writing speed shows the real ceiling in 60 seconds. After that the thrill of the broadcast does the work, not the rule.
takeing / swiming when they rushThe -ing sorter as a separate station + a “spelling” flag for the judges; the students formulate the Spelling rules p.45 themselves.
“Yes, they're” in short answersA separate choral drill: Yes, they ARE. The full form is the law of the broadcast; a short answer with an apostrophe loses a point with the judges.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
