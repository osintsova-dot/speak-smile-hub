Lexis

Structure
He's an astronaut. · What do you do? (NOT What are you?) · It's on the twentieth of July, nineteen sixty-nine. · Laika was a street dog. · There were paintings of the best gladiators.
Form
job = be + a/an (She's an inventor) · ordinals: +th, except first/second/third and fifth/ninth/twelfth/twentieth · date: the + ordinal + of + month.
Pron. watch
-or/-er in jobs = /ə/ (actor, farmer, singer) · -th /θ/: tongue to the top teeth · silent r and long /ɜː/ in first and third · months: March O · April Oo · July oO · January Ooo · September oOo (TB pp.96–97).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“What are you? — the right question about a job?” → No: What do you do?
“20.07.1969 — do we read it as ‘twenty oh seven’?” → No: the twentieth of July, nineteen sixty-nine.
“Laika IS a street dog?” → No — WAS: that's the past, and that's our next lesson.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: they know the months and numbers, some of the jobs too; they've heard was/were. Real gaps: ordinals in writing (fifth/ninth/twelfth), saying years aloud, a long connected turn with facts. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (irregular forms / -ed spelling / was–were agreement), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The “We already know jobs” whineDon't argue — the gate is on dates, not jobs: 5 dates in words, in writing. The score speaks for itself.
They read years digit by digitA year ladder on the board (TB p.97 ABCD): 1969 = nineteen sixty-nine · 2008 = two thousand and eight · 2015 = twenty fifteen. Every date — with the year said aloud.
The text on p.87 is long, they read it straight throughSubskill in action: key words from the task first, then only the piece you need. A 90-sec timer for the scan — there's physically no time to read it all.
Critical thinking slides into chit-chatThe “walk to the wall” format: Advantage / Disadvantage / It depends — the body instead of a monologue, 30 sec per argument.
Time watch: 12 pages in 5 pairsA visible timer on every block; if a block runs hot, I cut the chat, never the reading, never the product.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
