WHAT WE DO: Showdown as the review: “one word about the unit holiday”; U1 blitz-review: 4 nationalities on mini-boards + a Where are you from? volley; bridge — “you're now a casting agency”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: One word — how's life? Go! Mood on fingers, 1–5.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everyone writes AT THE SAME TIME, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.
  3. U1 review on mini-boards: Country flash! The Netherlands → nationality? China? Turkey? New Zealand? Boards UP! +1 for clean spelling — an echo of the U1 rematches.
  4. A volley at a volunteer: Where are you from? Is your city big? — we hold to full short answers.
  5. Bridge: Unit 1 — you owned the WORLD. Unit 2 — something harder: your own FAMILY, in English, fast and clean.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · my list of 4 countries
👦 Students do: they write nationalities, answer with etiquette. Expected: spelling noticeably cleaner after the U1 rematches. Typical error: Dutch forgotten again — a choral “the Netherlands → Dutch!”.

🎯 GATE · family spelling blitz
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 3 minutes: they write 8 family words from definitions — fast AND without mistakes; self-check, a personal tally in the margin

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I put up 8 definitions: my father's sister · my brother's son · my sister's daughter · my uncle's children · my parents' parents (2 words) · my son's daughter · father and mother (1 word) · my aunt's son.
  2. I say: WRITE the word for each. You've known ‘mother–father’ for years — now prove NIECE and NEPHEW in writing. Three minutes! Timer.
  3. Key: aunt · nephew · niece · cousins · grandparents · granddaughter · parents · cousin. Self-check, tally in the margin.
  4. The turn: 8 of 8? …NIECE with -ie-, NEPHEW out of nowhere — THIS is our game: not ‘knowing family’, but fast-accurate-written. Rematch in Pair 6!
📺 On screen: slide: 8 definitions → key (or in a column on the board, I add the key as we go) (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: solo, they write 8 words against the clock, honestly count the clean ones (privately). Expected: 4–6 of 8 — the ceiling is exposed. Typical error: “neice/nefew” — I don't shame it: “that's the growth zone, rematch in P6.”

WHAT WE DO: riddles ex.3/ex.5 on the 5-second rule → crews write their OWN riddles from the tree and duel; adjectives ex.6–7 with nice as taboo

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1 (ex.3 + ex.5): Five-second rule! My sister's daughter is my…? My aunt's son is my…? — item by item, with rotation.
  2. Round 2: Write TWO riddles about the Canes (I'm Paul's mother. Who am I?). Duel the next crew! 2 min; the author's bluff = −2.

40–48′
Bonus level: “this is not in the book”
🚀 Bonus level · not in the book
circle

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: a chain around the circle: “My (grandad) has never (been to Japan), but he can (fix anything)” — no relative repeats, a class streak

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I raise the status: This structure is NOT in this book. Beginners can't say it. GMF4 grads can.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. I write the frame: My ___ has never ___, but he/she can ___ better than anyone. I model it myself: My mum has never played Fortnite, but she can drive in Norilsk snow.
  4. A chain around the circle: no relative repeats; if the streak breaks — count restarts (the class against itself).
  5. +1 to the leaderboard for every clean bonus sentence.
📺 On screen: board: the bonus frame in large letters · a streak counter in the corner
👦 Students do: they build the sentence with perfect + can, watch that no relative repeats. Expected: a streak of 8–12, laughs over granny's talents. Typical error: “has never played” → “never played” — I point to the frame silently.

🏁 PRODUCT · casting pitches
stage

WHAT WE DO: each crew pitches its family for 60 sec against the stopwatch; judges with the rubric; the channel votes “in the show / rejected” with a reason

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I start: Crew one — the channel is listening. Sixty seconds — GO! Stopwatch visible.
  2. Judging cards: fluency/accuracy/richness on 1–3; after the pitch, one judge justifies the score in English.
  3. The channel's vote: Signed for the show or rejected? Thumbs — and ONE reason: They've got a granny who's never lost at cards!
  4. I average it, log it to the leaderboard, +1 for bonus structures on air.
📺 On screen: stopwatch on screen · judging cards · leaderboard (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they pitch from keywords, judge and vote with a reason. Expected: 1–2 crews at a gold run. Typical error: a pause >3 sec — judges count on their fingers, the bar teaches itself.

  • Main aim. By the end, crews can pitch a family for a TV casting in 60 seconds without pauses, using 17 family words + 3 adjective pairs accurately (spelling included).
  • Subsidiary. Gate proves the family-spelling ceiling (niece/nephew/daughter); the Canes tree + male↔female system fixed at speed; Video skills (Jonas) watched ‘like producers’ with pause-quizzes; GMF4 bonus: present perfect + can as status structures.
  • Personal. Anti-“family? we covered that in GMF1” frame from minute 1: the mission is spelling + speed + fluent speech about REAL people. Launch the Unit 2 leaderboard ritual; U1 champions announced, race reset.

Pron. watch
/ə/ endings — mother, father, brother, sister, daughter (no ‘r’!); nephew /ˈnefjuː/; niece /niːs/; aunt /ɑːnt/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“You KNOW family words. Can you WRITE niece and daughter right in 10 seconds?” → that's the gate.
“Cousin — he or she?” → both, one word.
“In a casting pitch, is ‘my brother is nice’ enough?” → No — producers give facts: age, looks, one juicy detail.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: basic family words met in GMF1–2, describing appearance in GMF4 U1. Real gaps: SPELLING (niece/nephew/granddaughter), the full 17-word system, sustained fluent speech. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
