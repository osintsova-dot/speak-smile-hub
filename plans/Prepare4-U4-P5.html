🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that the vocabulary and grammar of two units work without the book: the be/do/have/make collocations, both phrasal-verb sets, the airport words, must/have to/should and the two future forms — and correct spelling.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the gap between oral and written: out loud the class knows almost everything, on paper queue loses letters, boarding pass gains them, and mustn't quietly means «not necessary» again under time pressure.
Typical error to expect
becouse · tomorow · alot · wich — the most frequent misspellings; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam In our block that means que · bordering pass · custums · agrument.
Plus mustn't vs don't have to swapped under pressure.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built as retrieval practice, not re-teaching: two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it — and shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which form still breaks. The official Cambridge game sits between the stations and the briefing on purpose: it hides revision inside a listening-and-guessing race, and a class that has just competed walks into a test calmer. Only the Unit 7–8 squares are called — the Unit 5–6 squares were burnt in block 3 — and the book's Review 2 spread is not touched at all: it is the NEXT lesson, whole and honest. Nothing new is introduced anywhere today — with one exception named in the briefing: the U8 paper needs GET IN, met in lesson 4.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. Everything on these two papers you have already done in this room — I can name the lesson for every line. First forty-five minutes we play it back. Then forty-five minutes you show it. Your score is not the point: the point is your own list of five mistakes to fix.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast card round of the block's words, and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — hot round. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the block's word cards (collocations + both phrasal-verb sets + airport words), two trades each: Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest: Everything you just said is in today's test.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, call definitions and pairs answer chorally
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety dropping. Typical stumbles: departure gate and have (lots) in common

5–8′
Lesson frame
board
Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: put the test sections up in advance — transparency takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the six sections of the two papers on the board, one line each: be/do/have/make · relationship phrasal verbs · must / have to ×2 (+ should) · airport words · travel phrasal verbs · going to + present continuous ×2.
  2. Look at the board. Have we done all of this in class? Yes or no? The class answers chorally — the answer is always yes; if anyone doubts, name the lesson for that line.
  3. The rule of the day: Your score goes in the register. Your five mistakes go in your notebook. The second one matters more.
📺 On screen: the six section lines in chalk — the board is the medium and it stays up for the whole first half, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the sections and answer chorally. Expect visible relief. Typical problem — they don't believe «we have done all of it»: point line by line at the lesson it came from

8–20′
Review station 1 · words
teams
Why: Retrieval practice rather than re-teaching: they pull the block's vocabulary out of memory in short bursts, which is what makes it stay.

WHAT WE DO: the block's vocabulary: 30+ items, against the clock and in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, oral: definitions and chunks at speed — ask a friend for help? (do me a favour) · become friends again? (make up) · the place you collect your suitcase? (baggage hall) · one sound, four silent letters? (queue). Twelve items.
  2. Round 2, journey order: the class walks the airport chorally, stop by stop — then backwards, for the laugh.
  3. Round 3, written: Kagan Showdown — you say the meaning, teams WRITE the word. Load the traps deliberately: queue, boarding pass, customs, argument, departure gate.
  4. Every misspelling goes into a «watch out» column on the board — it stays there right up to the test.
📺 On screen: definitions read aloud, answers on mini-whiteboards — voice and boards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: say, walk, then write the words. Expect almost everything orally and a visible drop in the written round — which is exactly why the column exists. Typical error: que
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Block4_Units7-8_Match_2up_A4 + Prepare4_Block4_Units7-8_Translate_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to be joined to their English pairs A–L (relationship chunks, must / have to / should, relationship phrasal verbs, the airport journey, going to against the present continuous, travel phrasal verbs), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 written out in English from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the block's exit line.

20–32′
Review station 2 · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar — and it shows you, twenty minutes before the test, exactly which form still breaks.

WHAT WE DO: the block's grammar: modals and futures — ten mixed sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ten mixed sentences, one at a time: four on modals (You ___ run in the corridors (mustn't) · Yesterday I ___ stay home (had to)…), two on should-advice, four on futures (A taxi ___ us up at 8 tomorrow · Look at the clouds — it ___ rain…).
  2. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the REASON, not the answer — Say WHY, not just the answer.
  3. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Sentence six — number fours, boards up!
  4. Boil every explanation down to the step: forbidden → mustn't; not necessary → don't have to; past → had to; arranged → continuous; intended/predicted → going to.
📺 On screen: the ten sentences one at a time on the screen (Backup: write them on the board before the lesson and uncover them one by one with a sheet of paper)
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with an explanation, then report by number. Expect the mustn't/don't-have-to step to be skipped by the fastest pairs — slow exactly them down. Typical error: I will meet still standing — a line of its own in the watch-out column

Review game
teams of 2 vs 2
Why: The official Cambridge game hides revision inside a reason to listen to each other; a class that has just competed walks into a test calmer.

WHAT WE DO: «What's the word?» — the official Cambridge review game, our block's squares only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Two teams (A and B) per group, one cut sheet each, sheets hidden from the other team. Team A reads out one of its gapped sentences, saying «blank» for the gap; team B picks the word from ITS OWN word box: right first time = 3 points, second try = 2, third = 1. Then the teams swap roles. One word in each box is never used (A: post box · B: transport).
  2. ⚠️ The sheet covers Units 5–8 and block 3 already used the Unit 5–6 squares — today call ONLY the Unit 7–8 squares: sheet A: 5 check-in desk · 11 baggage · 17 come round · 19 departure gate · 21 get together · 25 look after · 27 queue · 31 pick up; sheet B: 2 customs · 4 set off · 6 argument · 14 fall out · 20 get back · 24 boarding pass · 28 security check · 30 go away. Exactly 8 + 8; the crime and city squares are burnt — skip them.
  3. Follow-up from the teacher's notes if time allows: teams swap sheets and try to remember the sentence that was read for each word.
  4. Set the bar: Read your sentence clearly — your opponents' points depend on your reading. Say “blank” with drama.
📺 On screen: one cut A/B sheet pair per group, run off in advance — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed; with no printer, read the A-sheet sentences yourself and teams race to shout the word
👦 Students do: read, listen, guess from their own box, score 3-2-1. Expect the reading aloud to be as useful as the guessing. Typical stumble: boarding pass vs baggage — the sentence decides; make them read it again

Test briefing
class
Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to the «watch out» column: the five words that were misspelt. Look at these five words for thirty seconds. Then I close them.
  2. One honest heads-up: In the Unit 8 word box you will meet GET IN — arriving. We met it in the phrasal-verb lesson. Now you have met it twice.
  3. Instructions: two official papers, Unit 7 then Unit 8, about 40 minutes total; phones in the bag; finished early — five sentences about next weekend on the back.
  4. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  5. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect silence to settle in 20 seconds if the flat hand goes up before you start talking

47–85′
Block test
solo, silent mode
Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats, official papers. Everything in them has been done in class, so the test measures learning, not surprise.

WHAT WE DO: the official Unit 7 and Unit 8 tests, back to back — 64 items in about 38 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. Unit 7 first. You have thirty-eight minutes for both papers. Start.
  2. Walk the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  3. Write the time left in chalk: 20 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  4. Anyone who finishes early writes five sentences about next weekend on the back and sits quietly.
  5. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling — queue, boarding pass, argument.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; nothing at all is put up in the room while the test is running
👦 Students do: work in silence for 38 minutes. Expect the collocation and airport sections to be closed fast, the modal choices and the diary-based continuous items to slow them down. Typical error: ex.3 of Unit 7 (must/have to) rushed — mention it in the briefing

85–90′
Test wrap-up
circle
Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the feedback lesson.
  3. Homework: once the papers come back — rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly; for now, the Unit 7–8 vocabulary or grammar worksheet if anything is still open.
  4. Teaser: Next lesson — the book's own big Review: FOUR units on one spread, played in teams. Crime, cities, friends and airports, all at once.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is full, read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to name the modal split and the future forms most often — that is the plan for the feedback clinic

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Nerves before the testSay it out loud and early: nothing will come out of thin air, every format has been done. Put the section list on the board before you start.
Someone finishes both papers in 25 minutesThe rule: hand them in, turn the sheet over and write five sentences about next weekend — two arrangements, three intentions. It is read, not marked.
After the test they want to compare answers out loudCollect the papers in silence; the analysis is next lesson. Today, once a paper is in, the only thing left is personal reflection.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
