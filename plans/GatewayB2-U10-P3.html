🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Answer five three-option questions on a radio programme about fake news, then use question tags with the right auxiliary, the right pronoun and — crucially — the right intonation, and turn direct questions into indirect questions with the subject before the verb.
What is genuinely hard
Indirect questions are the single most reliable error a Russian speaker makes in English: «I wonder where IS he». Russian keeps identical word order in direct and indirect questions, so nothing inside the student warns them. Question tags add three separate rules at once (auxiliary, polarity, pronoun) plus one irregular form (aren't I?) plus a meaning carried entirely by melody — and Russian does the whole job with «не так ли?».
Typical error to expect
«I wonder where is he», «Can you tell me what time does the train leave?» — in an indirect question the subject comes BEFORE the verb and the auxiliary disappears. Russian keeps the same word order in both, so nothing warns the student. This is the most frequent single error in the whole unit and it is worth five points on the Test yourself page.
Why the lesson is built this way
The listening comes first and it is not a warm-up: track 57 is a professor of journalism explaining how to check a story, and it is full of the grammar of the second half — the presenter's questions are literally «Today anybody can report the news, can't they?» and «Can I ask you if you have any advice…?». So the class hears the target language doing a real job before it is named. Question tags are then taught as MELODY, not as a table: the same six words, twice, with two different meanings. Indirect questions are practised on a genuinely absurd true story — a woman in China whose pet dog turned out to be a black bear — and end in a role-play with a man in a banana costume who caught a shoplifter.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“A woman in China kept a puppy for two years. It grew. And grew. Eventually it was walking around the house on two legs — because it was not a dog, it was an Asiatic black bear. Today: how to check whether a news story is true, and how to ask a stranger a question without sounding rude.”

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the class builds its own checklist, which is what they will then listen against

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 in pairs, three minutes: how can you tell whether a photo or a news story is real? Make a list, minimum four ideas.

WHAT WE DO: Grammar in context 2 ex.1–2: six rules stated by the class, then seven tags produced

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed, thirty seconds, the Teacher's Book warmer: write You aren't listening to me, are you? Ask how else you could ask this (Are you listening to me?) and what the structure is called.

Structure
You can't trust them, can you? · Nobody is sure about the news now, are they? · I'm right in saying that, aren't I? · I'd like to know if/whether … · I wonder what you think about … · Can you tell me how they create these videos?
Form
tags use the auxiliary or modal of the main clause (do if there isn't one), the opposite polarity, and a subject PRONOUN — never a name · I am → aren't I? · nobody, somebody, everybody → they. Indirect questions: subject before verb, no auxiliary do/does/did, if or whether for yes/no questions, the same wh-word otherwise; the question mark belongs to the first half of the sentence.
Pron. watch
the headline words are short and stressed, and the group will swallow them: axe /æks/ with a clean final cluster · plea /pliː/ not «plee-a» · probe /prəʊb/ with a diphthong · blaze /bleɪz/ against blast /blɑːst/ · wed /wed/ · PM said as two letters, pee-EM, stress on the second · the collocations take a compound stress on the FIRST element (FRONT-page news · NEWS bulletin · PRESS conference · CHIEF editor) · and the question tag carries the whole meaning in its intonation: falling = I am sure, rising = I am asking. Drill the two contours physically, with a hand
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Everybody likes chocolate, don't they?» — why they and not he? → with everybody, somebody and nobody English uses they. And why don't, when the sentence is positive? → the tag is the opposite.
«You hate watching the news, don't you?» said with a falling voice — am I asking or am I sure? → sure, and only inviting agreement. Now say it with a rising voice: → now I am really asking.
«Can you tell me what time IS it?» — what is wrong? → an indirect question is not a question in form: what time IT IS. Say the correct version twice.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
