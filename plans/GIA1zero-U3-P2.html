👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Believe it or not: 1,600 litres for one burger?!
Hook · believe it or not
board

WHAT WE DO: believe it or not: 3 shock facts before reading; teams place their chips — we'll check with the text

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I put up 3 statements: ① You need 1,600 litres of water for ONE burger. ② Farm animals make more gas than all cars and planes. ③ Vegetarians eat fish.
  2. Teams place a chip: believe / don't believe. Commit! We check with the text today.
  3. Intrigue: One of these is FALSE. Which one? The text knows. (③ — it's pescatarians, fun facts box)
📺 On screen: the 3-facts slide (or on the board) · team chips (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they argue and commit to a guess as a team. Expected: ① feels fake (“that's too much!”) — perfect intrigue. Typical error: they demand the answer right away — “the text knows, we don't — yet”.

11–19′
Warm-up poll: how often do you eat…?
Warm-up · how often do you eat…?
standing pairs

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: frequency frames + a carousel survey: 3 questions to 3 partners; never — before the verb

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frames on the board from ex.1: I eat ___ every day / every week / one or two days a week. · I never eat ___ . Choral drill ×2.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. TB rule-gesture: never jumps BEFORE the verb: I NEVER eat… — the “jump” gesture.
  4. Carousel: two circles facing each other; question How often do you eat meat/fish/chocolate? — answer from a frame; the outer circle steps along. 3 rounds.
  5. Blitz wrap-up: Hands up if your partner never eats fish!
📺 On screen: board: frequency frames · the never gesture
👦 Students do: they drill the frames and survey three partners, moving round the circle. Expected: everybody speaks at the same time ×3. Typical error: “I eat never” — the “jump” gesture fixes it on the spot.

Reading in chunks, with comprehension checks
Read · chunk by chunk

WHAT WE DO: the text in chunks: 3 paragraphs = 3 timed tasks; match paragraphs↔questions; we reveal “believe it or not”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Chunk 1 (par. 1) + task: Find TWO numbers. Two minutes. (375 million · “one day a week”). Check.
  2. Chunk 2 (par. 2): Find: water for one burger? gas — who makes more? (1,600 litres; farm animals). We reveal chips ①–②: Believers — points!
  3. Chunk 3 (par. 3): Find: how many countries? who is Meghan? (40; a vegan student from Vancouver).
  4. Putting together ex.4 with support: paragraphs↔questions a–c by teams (key: a2·b3·c1). Fun facts box: vegetarian/pescatarian/vegan — we reveal chip ③ (False!).
  5. Audio 25 in the background if you like, while they read the chunks.
📺 On screen: the SB pp.38–39 · timer · “believe it or not” chips on the board
👦 Students do: they read in chunks on a concrete hunt, reveal this morning's guesses, match paragraphs. Expected: “1,600 — TRUE?!” — the wow-effect drives the reading. Typical error: translating word for word — the timer + “I need a FACT, not a translation”.
💪 Support (TB Reinforcement): for a weaker team — chunks with the key words underlined. ⭐ Stretch: a “Fact+” envelope: 1 more number + 1 more name from the infographic.		SB pp.38–39 · timer

54–64′
We vote and speak from a model: meat — yes or no?
Critical thinking · for and against
standing → desks

WHAT WE DO: a physical vote “Meatless Monday at our school?” + arguments from frames with TB hints

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Physically: Meat-free Monday at OUR school? Stand LEFT = yes, RIGHT = no, MIDDLE = not sure.
  2. Frames on the board: I think it's a good idea because… / I don't agree because… + TB hints: health · animals · jobs · family food.
  3. 2 volunteers from each side — a sentence from the frame (+2 for a fact from the text!).
  4. TB apply question: Good reasons FOR eating meat? — I collect 2–3 ideas, recast in English.
  5. Final regroup: Change sides if somebody convinced you! — who moved, and why (1 phrase).
📺 On screen: board: frames + hints · space to regroup in
👦 Students do: they vote with their bodies, argue from frames, switch sides if convinced. Expected: 2–3 switches — “words move people” is seen physically. Typical error: an argument in Russian — I accept it, recast, they repeat it for a point.

WHAT WE DO: they write a verdict: 3 sentences from the frames (opinion + fact + frequency about themselves); a peer check by 2 boxes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model: I think Meatless Monday is a good idea because you need 1,600 litres of water for one burger. I eat meat one or two days a week.
  2. Frames: I think Meatless Monday is a ___ idea because ___ . I eat meat ___ . I never eat ___ .
  3. Three sentences. Six minutes. Timer, I walk round with a pencil.
  4. Peer review: Swap. TWO boxes: ☐ a FACT from the text? ☐ frequency at the END of the sentence? Tick or underline.
📺 On screen: board: model → frames · the 2-boxes slide · timer (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they write 3 sentences with a fact, check in pairs against 2 points. Expected: the facts from the showdown go to work. Typical error: “because is good” — the whole frame: because IT'S good.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can get the general idea of a news story without understanding every word and report 3 facts: “375 million people are vegetarian. You need 1,600 litres of water for one burger.”
  • Subsidiary. Reading subskill: understanding the general idea — with support and in chunks; Word work — 6 words from the text; Critical thinking through a for/against vote with answer frames; frequency expressions (every day · never · one or two days a week).
  • Personal. The unit's first LONG text for beginners — take the panic away out loud: “you do NOT need to understand every word”. We read in chunks, with a task for every chunk; no whole-class reading aloud. Praise the speed of finding things, not the translation.
By the end, students can…
  • say how often they eat things (frequency frames)
  • get the general idea of a paragraph
  • find a number-fact in a text chunk
  • vote and defend a choice with a model sentence

Pron. watch
vegetarian /ˌvedʒəˈteəriən/ by syllables with claps · promise /ˈprɒmɪs/ · environment — don't read every letter.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Do you need to understand every word of the text?” → No — general idea: key words.
“Does a vegan eat cheese?” → No — no food from animals (fun facts box).
“I eat never meat?” → No — I NEVER eat meat.

Assumptions: The food words from P1 are fresh (the rewind reaches them). A long authentic text — for the first time; we redefine success as “finding a fact”. Numbers in the millions were practised in Starter (numbers 1–100 → we extend to million orally).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Panic at the wall of textRule slide: “You do NOT need every word. You need the idea.” I model one chunk myself, thinking out loud.
Reading one at a time out loud → boredomNever: every chunk = a task for the teams against the timer; reading is silent only.
Word work turns into a dictionary huntMatch only the 6 BOLD words, cards on the desks, a 3-min timer.
The vote slides into RussianFrames on the board: “I think it's a good idea because…”; only a frame counts as an argument.
Fast teams sit idleA “Fact+” envelope: find 1 more number and 1 more name in the text (TB Fast finishers style).
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
