🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A grammar pair, but the topic is NOT “countable and uncountable nouns” — the topic is “the lunchbox inspection”. An inspector has to be EXACT: three tomatoes, but a little rice; there is yoghurt, but there aren't any grapes. A form error (“there is three tomatoes”) fails the report on the spot — the cost of the error works better than the rule. The interview with Jonah (track 32) gives them a live model, and the Brain teaser closes the pair on a competitive high.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Food inspectors, gloves on. Every crew gets a lunchbox — a real one, from the photo. Your job: the inventory. Not 'there is food' — that's a failed report. Three tomatoes. Some water. A cheese sandwich. No grapes, no rice. Here's the trap: some things you can count and some you can't, and if you get it wrong the report goes in the bin. Then you inspect a real person's whole day — his name is Jonah and he's a vegetarian. Inventory starts now.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: a lunchbox inspection: each crew gets a box and writes the inventory. Not “there is food”, but exactly: three tomatoes, some water, a cheese sandwich, no grapes. The trap: some things count and some don't — get it wrong and the report goes in the bin.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 sorting “counts / doesn't count” as a race → ex.2 they build the a/an, some/any table themselves → Showdown over 6 flips

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Seven words. Which ones can you COUNT? One, two, three … Three minutes. (TB key for ex.1: countable — cake, chips, bananas, apples · uncountable — rice, beef, milk).
  2. The TB's tip: If you need 'a bottle of' or 'a bowl of' in front of it — it doesn't count on its own.
  3. ex.2: Now put the five examples into the table yourselves. (TB key: 1 It's a small cake · 2 Add some beef · 3 We haven't got any milk · 4 I'd like some chips · 5 I can't see any apples). They formulate the rule, I confirm it.
  4. Kagan Showdown: I give a word + a sign (+/−), everyone writes a / an / some / any — Boards up! 6 rounds.
📺 On screen: SB p.64 ex.1–2 · mini-whiteboards · timer
👦 Students do: they build the rule out of the examples. Expect: “any only in negatives and questions” is said in chorus by the 4th flip. Error: “some” in a negative — I catch it with a gesture

PRODUCT · the lunchbox report
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: each crew builds its OWN lunchbox (cards/a drawing) and writes a 6-line inventory: 3 with there is/are and 3 with there isn't/aren't any; the neighbouring crew checks it against the checklist

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 5 minutes to build: Build your lunchbox — six items. Then the report: three lines with 'there is/are' and three with 'there isn't/aren't any'.
  2. A peer air-check through Kagan RallyCoach: they sit in pairs from different crews, one reads their line out loud, the other runs a finger down the checklist and asks (a number → are? a negative → any? uncountable → no a?), after three lines they swap — and all of this happens BEFORE the stage.
  3. The inspection: Read your report. The other crew has the box — they say ACCEPTED or BINNED, and why.
  4. Points: +2 for a report accepted first time · −1 for a failed form (the judges log it).
📺 On screen: food cards · the checklist on the slide · timer (Backup: the checklist written on the board before the lesson)
👦 Students do: they write, check each other and inspect. Expect: the peer check catches most of the errors before the stage — and you can see it in the score

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can tell countable from uncountable nouns, use a/an, some, any and there is/isn't/are/aren't at speed to make an accurate inventory of what is and isn't there.
  • Subsidiary. ex.1–2 they work the rule out themselves (guided discovery); ex.3 the interview with Jonah (track 32, 10 choices); ex.6 the there is/are table; ex.7 the lunchbox; ex.8 the Bento box text; ex.9 Brain teaser; the error clinic on P1–P2.
  • Personal. The first grammar pair of the unit: I do NOT explain the rule — they finish the table and formulate it themselves. Error feedback is anonymous. The word “grammar” is not said once in 90 minutes.
By the end, students can…
  • decide if a noun is countable or uncountable
  • use a/an with singular countable, some/any with uncountable and plural
  • build there is/isn't and there are/aren't correctly
  • make a 6-line inventory of a lunchbox with no form errors

Structure
Singular countable: a small cake / — · Uncountable: some beef — not any milk · (no article: You eat it with rice.) · Plural: some chips — not any apples · two bananas · There is a wonderful smell. — There isn't a better snack. · There is some fantastic food. — There isn't any milk. · There are some great food stalls. — There aren't any chips.
Form
singular countable — a/an: Can I have a banana, please? · uncountable — some (+), any (−/?) or no article at all: I usually have some milk. · We haven't got any butter. · We often have pasta for dinner. · plural — some/any + noun, or a number: some chips · any apples · two bananas · there is/isn't — before singular and uncountable; there are/aren't — before plural
Pron. watch
there's is said as one blob /ðeəz/, not “there is” syllable by syllable · any never appears in an affirmative — you hear it straight away
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“There is three bottles of water” — what's wrong? → it's plural: There ARE three bottles.
“There aren't some strawberries” — correct it. → any in the negative: There aren't any strawberries.
“There isn't milk” — what's missing? → any: There isn't any milk.

Assumptions: SB p.64, KTP pair 40 (both halves are grammar). there is/are is known from A1+ in patches; what is new is the countable/uncountable pairing and the writing speed.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“there is again, we know this”I don't argue: a gate on writing speed shows the real ceiling in 60 seconds. After that it is the thrill of the inspection that works, not the rule.
“There is three tomatoes” — the classicA station of its own: the inspector counts on their fingers first and writes afterwards. If there is a number, it is ARE immediately.
Russian logic: “bread is one thing, so a bread”The TB list goes on the wall: advice, bread, furniture, information, luggage, news, traffic, weather, work — “these are never counted in English”.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
