Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, children will be better able to ask about a place — “Is there (grass)? Are there (trees)?” — and answer — “Yes. There's a (roundabout). There are (swings).” — a life skill: describing where you are.
  • Subsidiary. Watch the Auntie Vicky video (a new park); listen to the dialogue (CD1 T44); interview friends about the park; scaffold single words into full sentences (TB tip); sing the park song + Genki «Where Is It?» (82); read «Thank You, Speaky!»; Phonics Hero — Magic E (short/long vowel), speed-read p.53–54.
  • Personal. Never force a shy child; a point or single word counts, then recast it into the full sentence (TB tip). Keep exchanges ~20 seconds, then swap. Model the question with the puppet first.
By the end, pupils can…
  • ask “Is there (grass)?” and “Are there (trees)?”;
  • answer “Yes! There's a…” / “There are…”;
  • describe a place — say two or three things that are there.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I ask Are there trees? — one tree or many?” → many!
“The park has one bench — Is there a bench? Yes or no?” → Yes! There's a bench.

Assumptions: Park words & the There's a / There are frames are active from L1–L4; the new step is the QUESTION forms Is there…? / Are there…?. Reading reaches Magic E (linking to kite). Support with the video, poster & puppet.

9–17′
Phonics Hero 🔤 ⚡
carpet, moving

① Recognise / review. Phonics Hero lesson 19 · Magic E — sounds silent e: short vowel → long vowel. 🦝 Speaky shows each with its Jolly action (gesture technique); children do the action and NAME the sound — they already know all 26. We don't drill bare sounds: teacher voices, children gesture.

② Blend / build. Show the magic: kit → kite, pin → pine, cap → cape — the silent e makes the vowel say its name. Story link: k-i-t-e! ▶ Magic-E wave — Speaky adds a silent e card; children wave a wand & read the new long word.

③ Speed reading. open Phonics Hero 2.0 (our workbook) p.53–54 — finger under each word, read the row FAST as a group (whole words, playful, celebrate speed — real reading from zero, GMF1-style).

Tricky words: read other · were together as whole words (we don't sound tricky words out).

17–25′
Curiosity input · Auntie Vicky's park video
carpet

Watch & guess. Explain: Auntie Vicky is telling Alex & Lucy about a new park. What's in the park? Trees? A pond? Guess! Children guess. Play the video.

Ask & answer. Play again, pausing on There's… / There are… — children repeat. Then model the QUESTIONS with the puppet: Is there grass? — Yes! Are there swings? — Yes! There are swings!

📱 Coursebook app (Macmillan digital): put the Big Wheel with the park items on their colour pods on screen (blue–slide · red–bench · yellow–swing · green–roundabout · pink–seesaw · orange–pond) and play the park story video & songs from the coursebook (Navio) app. Our Genki songs & games add to it — we don't reproduce the coursebook media.

31–37′
Reading corner 📖
carpet

Read-aloud. Read our own Speak & Smile story-book «Thank You, Speaky!» aloud — Speaky & his friends on the grass under the trees — a warm, calm read after the mingling. Perfect to DESCRIBE: what's on the page?

Hold up each spread — children look at the pictures, do the gestures and echo the key line There are trees! There's a present! Thank you!. Pre-readers: they listen, watch & repeat, they don't read the words.

On each grassy page, children describe what they see — There are trees! There's a dog! — and answer your Is there a present? — Yes!; models today's questions on a friendly scene.
