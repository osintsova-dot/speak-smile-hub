WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share as a warm-up: I meet them at the door; rewind U7 (past simple affirmative from a frame) — remembering “yesterday/last summer”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: Hi! One word — how are you today? Finger mood check 1–5.
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A talks for their time on the timer, B is an active listener (counts pauses on their fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. Rewind U7 from the frame on the board: Last summer I went to ___ . It was ___ . — 60 sec in pairs, then 3 answers aloud (+1 to the teams).
  4. Bridge: You went somewhere. But HOW did you get there? Plane? Train? Car? That's our whole unit: from A to B.
📺 On screen: the board: the frame “Last summer I went to … . It was … .” · a clean U8 scoreboard
👦 Students do: they greet with one word, talk about summer from the frame. Expected: a warm, calm start. Typical error: “I go last summer” — I point at the word last, they fix it themselves.

WHAT WE DO: the unit question + a warmer TB p.109: three zones on the board (air/sea/land), we throw in everything we know

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I draw a cloud, waves and land on the board; I label them air · sea · land (Warmer TB p.109).
  2. Come to the board. Write ONE type of transport in any zone. English only! — they come up one at a time, everybody moves.
  3. We read the resulting lists aloud in chorus; I leave them on the board until the end of the lesson.
  4. WDYT slide: What's the best way to travel, and why? We vote by show of hands for 4 options (plane · train · car · bike) — I note the numbers down, we'll come back to them in Pair 5.
  5. Teams of 3, the scoreboard goes up on the wall.
📺 On screen: the board: the three zones air/sea/land (stay up) · the WDYT slide · scoreboard (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they come up to the board and write a word each — everybody moves; they vote by hand. Expected: they already know 10–14 words. Typical error: they write in Russian — the rule “English only”, I prompt the word.

22–30′
Try it: by, on foot, in / on
Try it · by, on foot, in / on
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.4 categories + Language note by/on foot + Extra activity TB p.109; a choral drill of prepositions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4 p.97 by teams (60 sec per item): Four ways to travel between cities · three with two wheels · three types of public transport in a city · two on water · two in the air. Key TB p.110.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYBODY writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift them all together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. Language note p.96 in big letters on the board: by bus · by car · by plane — BUT on foot. A red ON FOOT card.
  4. Extra activity (TB p.109) in chorus with mini-boards: We go home ___ (on foot) · My mum goes to work ___ train (by) · My aunt doesn't go to work ___ bike (by) · Our teacher goes to school ___ (on foot).
  5. Columns ON / IN (TB p.110): ON — bike, bus, train, plane, tram, underground · IN — car, taxi, ambulance, lorry, helicopter. I check with a gesture: “two-wheeled and big — ON; small and personal — IN”.
  6. International words (TB p.110): the underground = the tube in London, the subway in the USA, the metro in Paris.
📺 On screen: the board: columns ON / IN + a red ON FOOT card (stay up) · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they sort transport into categories, drill the prepositions on mini-boards. Expected: by/on foot sticks through the chorus. Typical error: “in the bus” — a finger on the column.

WHAT WE DO: a moving game, ex.2 p.98: we describe transport from a support frame and guess; a card for everybody

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model on the board: I've got two wheels. I'm faster than a bike. — Are you a motorbike? — Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
  2. A support bank alongside: I've got … wheels · I travel on water / in the air / on roads · I'm bigger/faster than … · I take people to …
  3. ▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade: everybody has a transport card. Describe — they guess — swap cards — new partner. 5 min, everybody moves at the same time.
  4. Separately, ex.1 p.98 (three riddles) as a finale: a coach · the underground · a boat — teams hold up an answer card.
  5. +1 to the team for every riddle guessed.

WHAT WE DO: the subskill step by step: a question bank on the board → everybody chooses or writes their own question about scooters (books closed)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I explain it simply: Before you read, ask a question. Then reading is a hunt — you look for YOUR answer. (Subskill p.98).
  2. A question bank on the board (a support for those who find it hard to think of one): How old are scooters? · Who made the first scooter? · Are scooters dangerous? · How fast are they? · Why are they popular?
  3. Books closed (important, TB p.111): everybody picks a question from the bank OR writes their own in their notebook.
  4. 3–4 questions aloud; Now open the books. Hunt for YOUR answer — in the first text only.
📺 On screen: the board: a bank of 5 questions · SB p.98
👦 Students do: they choose/write a question with their books closed. Expected: the bank removes the freeze for everybody. Typical error: they pick the simplest one — that's fine, the goal is the mindset of searching.

WHAT WE DO: we read ONLY the first text, paragraph by paragraph (audio 55): after each paragraph — one question; then 3 T/F items with support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Paragraph 1 (we listen/I read aloud, the children follow): How old are scooters? → over a hundred years.
  2. Paragraph 2: Who made the first scooters? And who made a modern version? → kids, as toys · Wim Ouboter, 1997, to ride to his favourite shop.
  3. Paragraph 3: Which company made cheaper scooters? Where did businessmen use them? → Razor · Tokyo.
  4. Paragraph 4: Why are scooters good? → faster in traffic, good exercise, good for the environment.
  5. Whoever found the answer to THEIR OWN question raises a hand, +1 to the team.
  6. Everybody UP! The second text (Q&A) — headings only: How did it start? Why did it become popular? What about the future? Ex.6, we take 3 items out of 6 (1, 5, 6) standing: stand if TRUE, sit if FALSE — everybody reacts; key TB p.111.

👦 Students do: they follow the text paragraph by paragraph and answer one question at a time. Expected: everybody gets 4 facts. Typical error: they try to translate every word — the rule “we're hunting for the answer, not translating”.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 15+ types of transport and say how they travel (I go to school by bus. · I come on foot.), and can find 4 facts in the article about scooters using their own question asked before reading.
  • Subsidiary. WDYT? is opened; the transport set is introduced through photos and definitions with supports; the prepositions by / on foot / in / on are noticed and fixed with a game; Video skills (Nadiya) — 2 passes with visual tasks; the subskill “asking questions before you read” — step by step with a question bank; we hear the first didn't-phrases in the text from a model, with no theory.
  • Personal. The topic is concrete and “grown-up” — pride is guaranteed by minute 20. Keep the supports on the board until the end of the pair; read the text ONLY in chunks, never as a whole-class front read.
By the end, students can…
  • name 15+ types of transport, including coach, lorry, tram, underground
  • say how they travel using by + transport and on foot
  • ask one question before reading and look for the answer
  • say 4 sentences about scooters from the article

Structure
I go to school by bus. · I come to school on foot. · It's got two wheels. · It's faster than a bike. · Are you a motorbike? — Yes, I am.
Form
by + transport (by bus/car/plane), BUT on foot · ON — bike/bus/train/plane/tram/underground · IN — car/taxi/ambulance/lorry/helicopter (TB p.110).
Pron. watch
coach and boat — the same sound /əʊ/ · bus /ʌ/ · pilot /aɪ/ · passenger and journey /dʒ/ · ticket /ɪt/ (TB pp.109–110).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I go to school by foot?” → No — ON foot. One exception — we remember it as a picture.
“A coach — is that a bus?” → Yes, but a BIG one, between cities. Bus — around town.
“The underground — is that above the ground?” → No, UNDER a city: trains under the city.

Assumptions: After U1–U7: be, have got, present simple, can, present continuous, past simple affirmative (U7). They partly know transport words (bus, car, plane, taxi); coach, lorry, tram, underground, travelcard are new. We do NOT explain past negatives today — we only hear them in the text.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
‘by foot’A red ON FOOT card on the board the whole lesson; the class fixes every slip in chorus — no shame, like a ritual.
Mix up coach / bus and tram / trainA pair of pictures side by side on the board + a gesture (coach — hands show “long”; tram — “on rails, in the street”).
The text on pp.98–99 is long and scaryNever as a whole: first one student question, then ONLY the first text, paragraph by paragraph — the second text is only the Q&A headings. Success = finding 4 facts, not understanding everything.
The video is too fastA visual task (WHAT YOU SEE), two passes, hands up on recognition — success is guaranteed for everybody.
They get tired by the middleA change of pace every 8–10 min: sitting → standing → cards on the move → back to the desk.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
