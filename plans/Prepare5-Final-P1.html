🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Sit a genuine Cambridge B1 Preliminary for Schools paper — the Reading in its full 45 minutes and the Listening in its full 40 — on the official answer sheets, to the official clock, with no help of any kind.
What is genuinely hard
The English on this paper is not new. Three other things are. The sheet: forty-five minutes of good reading are worth nothing if the answers never reach the boxes, and the Reading paper gives no extra time to copy them — unlike the Listening, which builds six minutes into the recording. The length: forty-five unbroken minutes is longer than anything this class has sat. Writing what you hear: listening Part 3 asks for six written answers, spelling counts, and one of them is a web address spelled out letter by letter.
Typical error to expect
Spending fifteen minutes on reading Part 2. The matching section feels solvable so people stay in it, and then Part 6 — six one-word gaps, the easiest marks on the paper for this class — gets four minutes at the end.
Answering on the question paper and transferring at the end. In the Reading there is no «at the end». Answers go straight onto the sheet, in pencil, as they are decided.
Why the lesson is built this way
The Reading goes first, while heads are fresh and while the teacher can still catch somebody using the wrong paper. The Listening goes second because the recording owns the clock: the four tracks run 40 minutes 11 seconds back to back, the second play of every piece and the six minutes of transfer time are inside the audio, and the lesson therefore ends itself — the last words in the room are «That is the end of the test». Between the two papers there are two standing minutes, which is the smallest possible break and the largest this lesson can afford. The Writing paper does not fit and is not cut: it goes home tonight as a timed forty-five minutes on the official sheet, and the Speaking test is sat in lesson 71.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today is not a test I wrote. It is the Cambridge paper, on the Cambridge answer sheets, to the Cambridge clock. Forty-five minutes of reading, two minutes on your feet, forty minutes of listening — and the recording, not me, decides when we stop. It does not go on a report. Next lesson you mark it yourselves.”

WHAT WE DO: three minutes, and they are the only three the teacher owns today

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before they arrive. Reading question paper face down on every desk, official answer sheet beside it with the name already written, a pencil and an eraser on top. This stage only works because the room was set in the break.
  2. At the door: phones into bags, as a routine and not as an accusation. Two spare pencils in your hand.
  3. 40 seconds, standing behind the chairs, Kagan Think-Pair-Share: what do you do with a question you cannot answer? The sentence you want said out loud is Guess and move on — an empty box scores the same as a wrong one.
  4. The three rules of the sheet, pointed at on the enlarged copy pinned up beside the board: pencil only, one box per question, and Part 6 is six WORDS written into the boxes at the bottom, in capitals if your handwriting is not clean.
  5. Read the front page of the Reading paper aloud, once, exactly as printed — you hold the only copy of page 1, because they get pages 2 to 11. Do not paraphrase it: Answer all the questions. Write your answers on the answer sheet. Use a pencil. You must complete the answer sheet within the time limit. That last sentence is the one they need and it is not yours to soften.
  6. The two end times, already written up: reading ends at ___ , listening ends at ___ . They stay there for ninety minutes.
  7. The honest sentence, said once: This is the real Cambridge paper and it is a rehearsal. It does not go on a report. Next lesson you mark it yourselves.

WHAT WE DO: the full official forty-five minutes, thirty-two questions, no extra time to copy anything

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start them together and then stop talking: Turn the paper over. Forty-five minutes. It ends at ___ .
  2. The suggested pace is already on the board (written before the lesson, never dictated during it): Part 1 five minutes · Part 2 ten · Part 3 eight · Part 4 eight · Part 5 five · Part 6 five · four minutes to check.
  3. Minutes 3 to 8 — the only patrol that matters. Look for one thing: answers being written on the question paper instead of the sheet. Whisper, point at the sheet, walk on. Say nothing about any answer, ever.
  4. Halfway call, minute 22, said once and quietly: Half the time. You should be starting Part 4. That sentence rescues everyone still polishing Part 2.
  5. Ten-minute call: Ten minutes. If Part 6 is empty, do it now — six gaps, one word each, and it takes five minutes.
  6. Two-minute call: Two minutes. Every box has something in it, and the numbers on the sheet match the numbers on the paper.
  7. Pencils down. Answer sheet on top of the question paper. Collect both, face down, by row — it takes forty seconds and it belongs to this stage, not to the break.

Break · two minutes standing
standing
Why: A stirrer that also recycles language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies — and in an examination week that is truer, not less true.

WHAT WE DO: the smallest possible break, and the largest this lesson can afford

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody up. Nothing about the reading — not one answer, not one reaction. That rule is worth defending: the first person to say What did you put for six? takes forty minutes of concentration out of the room.
  2. Kagan Stand-Up Hand-Up Pair-Up: find somebody who was not sitting near you. Windows open, shoulders, water.
  3. Sixty seconds of Kagan RallyRobin on anything except the paper — the fastest way to clear a room's head is to make it think about something else in English.
  4. Sit down on your signal, listening paper and the one-page listening sheet in front of you: Forty minutes. The recording does everything. I will not stop it.

WHAT WE DO: four tracks back to back, forty minutes and eleven seconds, including the second play of every piece and the official transfer time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say the three differences BEFORE you press play, and say them off page 1 of their own paper rather than out of your head — the listening paper is printed whole and its instructions page is in their hands: you write on the QUESTION PAPER while you listen · every piece is played twice by the recording itself · at the end you get six minutes to copy your answers onto the sheet, in pencil.
  2. Press play on Part 01 and touch nothing. The pauses, the repeats and the instructions are all inside the audio: 11:05 · 10:07 · 5:49 · 13:10. Start the next file the moment the previous one ends.
  3. Part 3 is the written one — six answers to be spelled, including a web address said letter by letter. Say nothing during it, but note who writes nothing: that is the group who needs the spelling work next lesson.
  4. Watch Part 1 as well, and write down who decides early. Items 2 and 3 are both settled by the last line of the conversation, so a pencil that goes down at the halfway point is a mark lost. You say nothing about it today — it is the first thing on the board next lesson.
  5. The last six minutes of track 04 are the transfer time. Use them for the quiet jobs and do not speak: put the Writing papers and the four-page writing sheets in a pile at the door, and write tonight's homework on the board.
  6. The recording says That is the end of the test. Let those be the last words about the paper — do not add a summary to them.
  7. Papers and sheets in, face down, by row. Homework at the door, one set each, in one sentence: forty-five minutes, timed, in pen, on the official sheet, back next lesson.
  8. Write down, before you leave the room, the three things you SAW: who stalled, who never checked, who stopped writing in Part 3. It is data you cannot get any other way.

Writing paper, 45 minutes, on the official answer sheet
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students have completed the Reading and Listening papers of an official Cambridge B1 Preliminary for Schools sample test under examination conditions, recording every answer on the official answer sheet.
  • Subsidiary. Budgeting time across six reading parts; using an optically-read answer sheet correctly; writing and spelling answers in a listening test; using six minutes of transfer time for what it is for.
  • Personal. Do not teach today. Not one word of it. The only intervention allowed is a finger pointed at an answer sheet in the first five minutes of the Reading. Walk, watch, and write down what you SEE — who is still on Part 2 at minute twenty-five, who never turns back to check, who writes nothing during listening Part 3.
By the end, students can…
  • work through thirty-two reading questions in forty-five minutes without stopping
  • fill in an official Cambridge answer sheet in pencil, in the right boxes
  • write and spell six listening answers rather than choose them
  • use the six minutes of transfer time for what it is for

Reading · Part 1Part 1 · questions 1–5 — five short real-world texts (a teacher's notice about a careers talk, a competition advert, an email between two students, a bicycle for sale, a note from Mum) with three interpretations each. The question is always WHY the text exists, not what is in it. About five minutes.
Reading · Part 2Part 2 · questions 6–10 — five people who want a family cycle ride, matched to eight route descriptions. Three routes are never used and that is the whole difficulty: all three requirements have to match, not one. About ten minutes.
Reading · Part 3Part 3 · questions 11–15 — «Flying kites», fourteen-year-old Olivia Clarkson on the dragon kite her parents bought her: five four-option questions on detail, feeling and attitude, the last one about the text as a whole. About eight minutes.
Reading · Part 4Part 4 · questions 16–20 — «Travelling frog!», a family who brought a frog home in their luggage: five sentences removed from the article, eight offered. Reference words decide it — this, they, but, so. About eight minutes.
Reading · Part 5Part 5 · questions 21–26 — «Chocolate train», a 34-metre train made of chocolate: a six-gap lexical cloze with four options a gap. About five minutes.
Reading · Part 6Part 6 · questions 27–32 — «Basketball tournament final», a student report: a six-gap open cloze, ONE word a gap and nothing offered. It is the hardest five minutes on the paper and it must not be left until minute forty-four.

Writing · Part 2Part 2 · question 2 OR question 3, one of them — an article for the school magazine about hobbies (one hobby or many? which do you enjoy?), or a story that must begin «As Victoria entered the room, she couldn't believe her eyes!». About 100 words.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

The same skill is tested by: The whole paper. The Reading paper sits on EGE tasks 10, 11, 12–18 and, in Parts 5 and 6, on tasks 19–25 and 32–38; the Listening paper sits on tasks 1–9. The Writing paper, set as tonight's timed homework, is task 37.

This is the first time in the year that the class sits a real examination paper on real answer sheets, and that is the point of it — not the score. Two things are being measured that nothing else measures: stamina across forty-five unbroken minutes, and the transfer of answers onto a sheet that is read by a machine. Both are worth marks in every exam these students will ever sit, and neither has anything to do with English. The full mapping is written out for the class in lesson 70, not today; today they simply sit the paper.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
