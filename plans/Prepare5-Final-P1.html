🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Sit a complete official paper — vocabulary, grammar, listening, reading, writing and paired speaking — in one ninety-minute sitting, under real timing, with no support of any kind.
What is genuinely hard
Nothing on this paper is new. What is new is the LENGTH. A student who is accurate for twenty minutes and careless for the next forty loses marks that have nothing to do with English, and there is no way to discover that except by sitting the whole thing once. The second difficulty is the writing section, which gives one line of instruction and no prompt questions — the class has never written without a prompt this year.
Typical error to expect
Spending twenty minutes on the first eight items. The two vocabulary sections are worth the same per item as the grammar ones and take a third of the time. Nobody budgets, so somebody always arrives at the writing with four minutes left.
Writing a review with no plan because there is no prompt. The absence of questions is not permission to improvise; it is an invitation to use a shape you already know.
Why the lesson is built this way
The listening goes first because its clock belongs to the recording — everyone starts and stops together, which settles a nervous room, and a lapse of attention there cannot be recovered later. Then the long silent block of vocabulary and grammar, then reading, then writing, and the paired speaking last, because it releases the room instead of tightening it and because it is the one section everybody can do. The briefing is five minutes and it is mostly arithmetic: how many marks each block is worth and how many minutes it therefore deserves. That is the single most useful thing anyone can be told before a first full paper.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today you sit the whole thing. Everything on this paper has been in this room since January, and I will show you where. The only new thing is the length — and that is exactly what today is for. Next lesson we take it apart together.”

Opening · check-in + papers back
door → desks
Why: The room has to change state in five minutes. Returning the unit tests first is deliberate: it reminds them that a paper produces information rather than a verdict, which is exactly the frame today needs.

WHAT WE DO: the unit tests returned, phones away, and the room set for an examination

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — the Unit 19 and Unit 20 papers come back. By name, face down, with the weakest section circled. One sentence each, quietly. No scores read aloud.
  2. 1 minute — Kagan Think-Pair-Share: in pairs, thirty seconds each way — say the section you lost most on. It is the last time anybody says it out loud before today's paper, and it focuses them.
  3. Phones into bags at the door, as a routine and not as an accusation. Pens checked — you have two spares.
  4. Bridge (30 seconds): Papers away. Today you sit a whole one, start to finish, and every single thing on it has been in this room since January.
📺 On screen: the marked unit tests handed back face down — paper only, and the papers are theirs
👦 Students do: take back their own paper, name one weak section to a partner, then clear the desk completely

5–10′
Exam briefing
board
Why: A time budget is the single most useful thing anybody can be given before a first full paper, and writing END TIMES rather than durations removes the arithmetic they cannot do under pressure.

WHAT WE DO: five minutes of arithmetic that decide the marks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the six blocks on the board with their marks and their minutes: twelve numbered sections in six blocks: Vocabulary 1–4 (32 items) · Grammar 5–8 (32 items) · Listening 9 (5 items, one audio file) · Reading 10 (5 items) · Writing 11 (a review, about 100 words) · Speaking 12 (five questions completed, then asked and answered in pairs). Seventy-four objective marks plus a written piece and a spoken one.
  2. Then the time budget, and this is the useful part — write the clock time each block should END at, not its length. Students cannot subtract under pressure but they can read a clock.
  3. Say the order out loud and why: listening first because the recording controls the clock, speaking last because it is the one everybody can do.
  4. The writing warning, one line, before anything is opened: Section 11 gives you one sentence and no questions. A review still answers three — what it was, what was good or bad, and who should see it.
  5. The rules: silence, pens only, read the rubric of every section before you start it, and if you finish a section early you go ON. Nobody leaves the room.
  6. The honest sentence about what today is for, and it changes how hard they work: This is a rehearsal. It does not go on a report. Next lesson you mark it yourselves and we find three things each.
📺 On screen: the six blocks with their end-times written on the board and left there for ninety minutes — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: copy the six end-times onto the top corner of their own paper before it is turned over

10–22′
Listening · section 9
desks, individual
Why: Listening · section 9

WHAT WE DO: the one section whose clock belongs to the recording

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Papers open at section 9 only. Everything else stays face down and say so.
  2. Ninety seconds to read the five statements and underline the key word in each. That is not generosity, it is the technique — and it is what the real paper expects.
  3. Play the audio ONCE, whole, no pausing. Then ninety seconds of silence to write.
  4. Play it a SECOND time. The paper does not say to, and you are going to anyway — but say out loud that you are doing it, so nobody assumes a second play in a real exam.
  5. Two minutes of silent checking, then Pens down. Section nine is closed.
  6. Note who wrote nothing on the first play. That is the group who needs the paraphrase work in lesson 70.
📺 On screen: PRE-TT-3S_ex.10.mp3, played twice, on the board app. Backup: the complete audioscript is printed in the answer key — read it twice at natural speed, standing still, and say so aloud; the five items survive intact
👦 Students do: underline five key words, listen twice, and write in silence

22–26′
Break · papers face down
standing
Why: A stirrer that also recycles language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies — and in an examination week that is truer, not less true.

WHAT WE DO: four minutes of standing between the first section and the long silent block

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Papers face down. Everybody stand up. Nothing is said about the listening — not a single answer, not a single reaction. That is the rule and it is worth defending.
  2. Two minutes of moving: to the window, shoulders, water. Talking is allowed as long as it is not about the paper.
  3. Kagan RallyRobin, sixty seconds standing, on ANY subject except the test — the fastest way to clear the room's head is to make it think about something else in English.
  4. Sit down on your signal: Sections one to eight. Thirty minutes. Sixty-four marks. Go.
📺 On screen: no technology in this stage — voice and feet only, and nothing can fail
👦 Students do: stand, move and speak about something else entirely for three minutes
the end-time list on the board
26–56′
Vocabulary and grammar · sections 1–8
desks, individual
Why: Vocabulary and grammar · sections 1–8

WHAT WE DO: thirty silent minutes, sixty-four marks, no support of any kind

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start the whole block together and point at the end time already on the board.
  2. Halfway call at fifteen minutes, said once and quietly: Half the time. You should be somewhere in section five. That single sentence rescues the students who are still polishing section two.
  3. Circulate slowly. Say nothing about any answer. The ONLY thing you intervene on is a misread rubric — somebody writing sentences where the paper wants one word — and the intervention is a whisper and a finger on the instruction line.
  4. Write down what you see, because it is data you will never get again: who never goes back to check, who erases and rewrites the same item three times, who has stopped.
  5. Five-minute warning, then a two-minute checking call: Two minutes. Nothing new. Read the articles section again and read the reported speech section again.
  6. Pens down on sections one to eight.
📺 On screen: the end-time list on the board, and nothing else — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: work in silence for thirty minutes across eight sections and check their own work in the final two

Reading · section 10
desks, individual
Why: Reading · section 10

WHAT WE DO: ten minutes on a five-question text about moving to the countryside

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Section 10 only. Ten minutes on the board as an end time.
  2. Say the one thing that is technique rather than help, and say it before they start: Read the whole blog first. Then read a question, find the paragraph, and read THAT part carefully.
  3. Silence. Circulate but do not read over shoulders here — a reading section is where a teacher's presence is most disruptive.
  4. Two-minute warning and a checking call: Two minutes. Question one asks WHY. Read your answer again and make sure it answers why.
  5. Pens down on section ten.
📺 On screen: the end time for the section written on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: read a 450-word blog and answer five questions in ten minutes, alone

Writing · section 11
desks, individual
Why: Writing · section 11

WHAT WE DO: fourteen minutes and a hundred words on a task with no prompt

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Section 11. Read the instruction aloud once, exactly as printed, and add nothing to it.
  2. Two minutes of planning, and say what a plan is here: three notes, one for each of the three questions from the briefing — what the film was, what was good or bad about it, who should see it. Nobody writes a sentence until there are three notes.
  3. Ten minutes of writing, silent, end time on the board.
  4. Two-minute call: Two minutes. Count your words and write the number at the bottom. The count takes thirty seconds and it is part of the discipline.
  5. Do not circulate correcting. Circulate checking that everybody has three notes and then that everybody is writing.
  6. Pens down. That is the written paper finished. Say it — the room needs to hear that the hard part is over.
📺 On screen: the three planning questions and the end time on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: plan in three notes, write a hundred words alone against a clock, and count them

🎯 Speaking · section 12, in pairs
pairs
Why: 🎯 Speaking · section 12, in pairs

WHAT WE DO: the section that releases the room instead of tightening it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Part A first, two minutes, silent and individual: complete the five questions with the right word. It is marked, and it is worth saying that it is the only part of section 12 that is.
  2. 🎯 Then part B, four minutes, Kagan Timed Pair Share: ask and answer all five with a partner. Everybody talks at once; you sample two or three pairs and note what you hear.
  3. One rule on the board and it is the only instruction: every answer has two parts — the answer, then a reason or an example.
  4. Do not stop them early. The noise after seventy-five minutes of silence is the point, and it is what they will remember about the mock.
  5. Take one answer to the floor at the very end, volunteered: Why do people like spending time in quiet places?
📺 On screen: the two-part answer rule on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: complete five questions alone, then ask and answer all five simultaneously with a partner

Wrap-up + homework
desks → door
Why: Self-assessment against the aim they heard at the start, so the lesson closes the loop it opened — and in these four lessons the loop is the year, not the hour.

WHAT WE DO: self-check against today's aim, then the homework said once and written on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Papers in, face down, in one pile, by row. Nothing is discussed in the room — and say why: You mark this yourselves next lesson. If we discuss it now, you will remember my answer instead of finding your own.
  2. Thirty-second exit slip, one line: which section felt longest? Not hardest — longest. That is the stamina question and the answers will surprise you.
  3. Homework said once and written up: no writing tonight. One thing, ten minutes — the Speaking bank Part 1 questions, prepared with one extra piece of information attached to each answer. We use them in two lessons' time.
  4. Say what is next: Next lesson you get this paper back with no marks on it and the answer key beside it. You mark it. Then we find three things each.
📺 On screen: the homework on the board while they pack — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: hand in the paper face down and name the longest section on a slip

Speaking bank, Part 1 ex.7
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students have completed a full six-section paper under examination conditions and have produced a sample of every skill the course has taught.
  • Subsidiary. Time budgeting across sections; reading a rubric before answering; writing to a word count without a prompt; sustaining accuracy across ninety minutes.
  • Personal. Do not teach today. Not one word of it. The temptation during the silent block is enormous and every hint destroys the only data the lesson exists to produce. Walk, watch, and write down what you SEE — who stalls, who rushes, who never turns back to check.
By the end, students can…
  • work through six different task types in one sitting
  • budget the minutes across a paper rather than section by section
  • write about 100 words on a task with no prompt questions
  • ask and answer exam questions with a partner at the end of a long paper

Honest note · the syllabus says «OGE format»The syllabus calls lesson 69 «a mock in the OGE format». That label does not fit this group: these are grades 10–11 continuing towards the EGE and towards B1 Preliminary for Schools, and the OGE is a ninth-year paper. The mock in this plan is therefore the publisher's own full-skills paper — vocabulary, grammar, listening, reading, writing and paired speaking — with the Russian-exam addresses of each section given in lesson 70. Nothing home-made is used anywhere in this block.
Honest note · why this paperWhy the Term 3 paper is the mock and the End-of-year paper is the final. Both are official, both have the same six-section shape, both come with an answer key and an audio file. The Term 3 paper covers the second half of the year and is the lower-stakes one, which is what a dress rehearsal should be; the End-of-year paper covers all twenty units and is the one that produces the number for September. Running the same shape twice in four lessons is deliberate: the second sitting measures what the review lesson actually changed.
Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Somebody finishes forty minutes earlyThey check. Then they turn the paper over and, on the back, write three sentences on any topic from the year. Nobody leaves the room and nobody starts a conversation — that is the rule and it is said in the briefing, not invented at minute fifty.
The audio failsThe full audioscript is printed in the answer key. Read it twice, at natural speed, standing still, and say so out loud so nobody thinks they are getting a different test. The five items survive intact.
Panic in the writing section because there is no promptOne line in the briefing, before the paper is opened: a review answers three questions even when nobody asks them — what it was, what was good or bad about it, and who should see it. That is enough and it is not a hint about content.
A student is absentThey sit the same paper in the next available slot, before lesson 70 if possible, because the review lesson is unusable without their own script in front of them.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: The whole paper. Section by section: vocabulary and grammar 1–8 sit on EGE tasks 19–31, the listening section on tasks 1–9, the reading section on tasks 10–18, the writing section on task 37, and the paired speaking on the oral section.

This is the first time in the year that the class sits everything at once, and that is the point of it — not the score. Stamina is a measurable skill and it is the one nobody practises: a candidate who is accurate for twenty minutes and careless for the next forty loses marks that have nothing to do with English. The mapping above is written out in full for the class in lesson 70, not today; today they simply sit the paper.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
