WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.3 as a card game: flip the statement into a negative (and back) with a slap; a blitz on mini-boards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out Reverse decks (ex.3 sentences): Flip a card, SLAP it, reverse it: My mum's got two sisters → My mum HASN'T got two sisters!

WHAT WE DO: “whose?” — the owner's apostrophe: the ex.4 rules through 2 houses on the board; an ex.5 duel on mini-boards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I draw 2 houses: House 1: 's = HAS got (verb) · House 2: 's = owner (whose?). We sort the examples together as a class: He's got glasses → house 1! Lena's brother → house 2!
  2. Ex.4 rules at a sprint: one owner → 's · owners ending in -s → s' (apostrophe AFTER). Picture: 1 brother = brother's room · 2 brothers = brothers' room.
  3. Duel ex.5 on mini-boards, 10 sec each: mums → mum's · grandparents → grandparents' · dads → dad's · cousins → cousins' · parents → parents' · Lucys → Lucy's.
  4. The giant apostrophe card (U1 ritual): a volunteer physically places it in “my mum s name”.
📺 On screen: board: 2 houses + the 1/2 brothers picture (stay up) · mini-boards · giant apostrophe
👦 Students do: they sort examples into the houses, fix words at speed, place the apostrophe physically. Expected: “cousins' — TWO cousins!”. Typical error: apostrophe placed at random — the question “how many owners?” out loud fixes it.
✔ Assess: whoever mixes up the houses — a support strip for them in Build.		mini-boards · giant apostrophe

WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin: Extra activity TB: draw an avatar from your partner's description — a tough check of the new words

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody secretly draws a simple avatar (face + hair, 90 sec).
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we alternate one answer at a time — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!
  3. A describes by the frame: She's got long, straight, dark hair. She's got big eyes and a small nose. B draws FROM THE DESCRIPTION. We compare — laughter. Swap.
  4. I keep the frames on the board: He's/She's got ___ hair. He's/She's got ___ eyes.
  5. +1 to a pair for a recognisable portrait.
📺 On screen: slips of paper · frames on the board

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can build affirmative and negative have got sentences and use possessive 's (Act 1, 45′) and understand key facts of a radio programme about unusual twins using 13 body/face words (Act 2, 45′).
  • Subsidiary. Inductive tables SB p.28; apostrophe accuracy ('ve/'s got + owner 's/s'); Brain teaser; avatar vocabulary + hair rule length–type–colour; listening subskill: using what you know; a 4-sentence description portion.
  • Personal. System lesson — but sell it as engineering, never “grammar” with a sigh. KTP boundary 45′: lesson A = grammar, lesson B = lexis+listening. Max 10 min per game shell, change modes.
By the end, students can…
  • complete the have got tables (aff/neg)
  • flip aff ↔ neg with contractions
  • place possessive 's after the owner
  • catch who-is-who and looks facts from audio

Form
've got → haven't got; 's got → hasn't got; possessive 's = owner (singular), s' = owners (plural in -s); hair order length–type–colour.
Pron. watch
unstressed have /həv/; moustache /məˈstɑːʃ/, beard /bɪəd/; foot–feet, tooth–teeth.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He's got — he IS or he HAS?” → he HAS got (today 's = has!).
“my brothers' room — how many brothers?” → two or more.
“Brown long curly hair — OK?” → No: length–type–colour: long, curly, brown.

Assumptions: Two pairs of contextual exposure (P1 frames + Seven Families, P2 corrections) — forms sound familiar; the SYSTEM (tables, has for he/she, possessive 's) is new. Apostrophes are the hardest bit for this age (two jobs of one sign!).

Apostrophes lost (mums, dads)Physical apostrophe card placed on giant words (U1 ritual); peer checks target apostrophes.
Drill fatigueMax 10 min per shell: table race → Reverse → 's intro → quiz → teaser; modes change.
Listening panic at natural speedPlan announced (3 passes, small targets); notes = ONE word; backup script TB pp.131–132.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
