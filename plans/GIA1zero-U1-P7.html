Week 05.10–11.10 · Pair 11 (Unit 1 · P7)📝 TEST · Unit 1~12 pupils · individual seatsTest = 100 marks · Standard variant
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. To fairly and calmly measure what each pupil can already do in Unit 1 across five skills — vocabulary, grammar (be), reading, listening and writing — plus a one-to-one speaking check, using the Macmillan Unit 1 Progress test (Standard).
  • Subsidiary. Lower test anxiety in first-year beginners: walk through every section format first (model-first), play the listening track twice, run speaking privately 1:1, keep the room quiet and unhurried.
  • Personal. Give a clear ICQ before each section; keep marks private; protect a “you already did this on P6” mindset; monitor without hovering.
By the end, pupils will have…
  • completed all five written sections of the Standard test independently;
  • heard the listening track twice and answered all listening items;
  • shown speaking 1:1 (act-out dialogue + English↔Russian word pairs aloud);
  • left knowing marks come back individually, not read aloud.

Marking & what counts · Assumptions
Objective sections (Vocab/Grammar/Reading/Listening) → answer key from Test Generator; one mark each, no half-marks unless the key allows.
Writing /10 & Speaking /10 → mark for communication first (message understood / dialogue completed), then accuracy — don’t let spelling sink a clear beginner message.
Contractions accepted where natural; capital letters expected in Writing (it’s a named subskill).

Assumptions: pupils met every task format already (P1–P6: WDYT, reading, grammar drills, keypal writing, Quick review on P6) — today shows what they can do, it is not new content. These are true beginners → the format itself must not become the barrier; walk it through first. Classroom management — signals
Attention — raise a hand + “Pens down, look up.” → pupils stop & listen (used only between sections).
Ask for help — hand up, wait; you come over. A word, not an answer.
Listening ×2 — “You’ll hear it TWICE.” announced before playing; settles the panic.
Finished early — turn paper face-down, check silently, wait. No leaving, no talking.
Quiet corner — one chair, back to the room; speaking stays private.
Time cues — gentle: “About 10 minutes left” — never a loud countdown on a test.

TimeStage / whereProcedureMaterials
0–6′
Arrival & calm-down
door → seats

Settle. Greet each pupil at the door, quiet and warm: Hi — come in, find your seat. Seats spaced out, bags away, one pen. Low lights of energy on purpose.

Self-efficacy frame (say it slowly). Today is your Unit 1 test. Here’s the good news: everything on it, you have already done — countries, nationalities, the verb “be”, a keypal message. On P6 you did the Quick review. Today just shows what you can do. Nobody sees your marks but you and me.

Normalise: If a bit feels hard — that’s normal. A test is meant to make you think. Do what you can, in any order.

 quiet room · spaced seats · one pen each
6–18′
Model-first walkthrough
board / projector

Show every section with YOUR OWN example (not a test item). Keep each to ~1 minute:

Vocabulary. Country → nationality (France → French; Japan → …?). “Choose the option” & the “two extra words in the box” trap — two words you will NOT use — that’s normal, don’t panic.

Grammar — be. Positive / negative / question on the board: I’m … / She isn’t … / Are you …? — Yes, I am. Remind: Use short forms — I’m, you’re, isn’t.

Reading. First look at the little texts, then read the questions. Match, true/false, choose. It’s all IN the texts — you don’t need to know it from your head.

Listening. You will hear it TWICE. First time — just get the idea. Second time — the details. Read the tick/true-false questions with them before you play anything.

Writing. A short message, 60–80 words. Point at the checklist: start it, say why, ask them to write back, capital letters.

Speaking. I’ll call you one at a time to that quiet corner — a short talk, just you and me. Keep working while you wait.

ICQ: How many times will you hear the listening? (→ twice) · If you don’t understand a WORD, what do you do? (→ hand up)

 board / projector · your own model examples (NOT the test) · checklist for Writing

seats

20–34′
① Listening (whole class)
seats

Guided, then twice. Turn to the Listening page together. Read the three tasks aloud so everyone knows what to listen for: the tick-3-topics, the choose-the-option, the true/false — a radio programme about a city. Listen for the idea first.

First listen — just the general idea. Play the track once. Pause. Now the second listen — this time for the details. Fill in your answers. Play the SAME track again.

Optional third play only if the room clearly needs it: One last time. Then: Pens down for a second — listening done. Well done.

34–36′
Reset (settler)
at seats

Stand up by your chair. Roll your shoulders, take one slow breath… and sit. A silent 60-second reset — not a game, just a breath before the self-paced part. Now — Vocabulary, Grammar, Reading and Writing, in any order you like. Take your time.

36–74′
② Written test — self-paced
seats · silent

Vocabulary · Grammar · Reading · Writing. Pupils work silently at their own pace. You monitor quietly — no hovering. Clarify only unclear words, never answers.

Writing support (allowed, it’s a beginner test). Point a stuck pupil back to the on-page checklist and profile; don’t supply sentences. Quiet reminder near the end: Check your capital letters.

Gentle single time cue around 68′: About 10 minutes left — finish the section you’re on.

 test papers · pupils’ pens · answer key (for you, out of sight)
36–74′
③ Speaking 1:1 (parallel)
quiet corner

Runs at the same time as the written test — call pupils one by one in register order so everyone is assessed with no downtime. ~2–3 min each. Back to the room, private.

Part A — act-out (Ex 17 / Speaking task PDF, Standard). Warm start, model one line: Hi, I’m [name]. What’s your name? Pupil uses the printed “Meeting new people” prompts to complete the short dialogue (name · age · where from · big/small city).

Part B — EN↔RU word pairs aloud (our format). Show a small set of Unit 1 words; pupil reads each English word and gives the Russian, or reads Russian and gives the English — country → страна, Spanish → испанский, crowded → …? Mark for recognition + pronunciation, not perfection.

Close each pupil warmly: Nice job — back to your paper. Record Speaking /10 privately.

Attribution + reassurance: You’ve finished your first English test. You could do it because you practised all through Unit 1 — that’s effort, not luck. I’ll mark these and give them back to each of you privately next lesson, with a short calm look at any tricky bits. No marks read out loud.
