WHAT WE DO: Inside-Outside Circle as a rewind: quiet music, “one word about how you feel”, a look at the wall of countries — pride instead of fear

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I play quiet music before entry. At the door: One word — how do you feel? Any word is fine.
  2. I build Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: two circles facing each other, a question → both answer → Outside circle, rotate two steps! — a new partner, a new question; 3 rotations, everyone speaks.
  3. I point at the poster wall: Look at your countries. People who INVENT nations can handle a test.
  4. I ask: Show test-readiness 1–5 on fingers. Whatever you showed — first we warm up. Together.
📺 On screen: quiet music · the poster wall
👦 Students do: settle into a calm mode, ground themselves in pride over the project. Expected: anxious students breathe out at the words “warm-up first”.

Lightning revision (first half of the pair)
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 5 lightning items from Quick review, solo on a draft + a self-check — “that was a mini-test, you just passed it”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I display 5 lightning items: nationality spelling · an opposite · a be gap · a short answer · capitals — they solve them on a draft, solo, quietly.
  2. I open the key, a self-check. I say: That was a mini-test. You just passed it.
  3. Fingers: Which round was hardest — one to five? — a 60-sec choral rewind of exactly that item (by the frame).
  4. Bridge: Same formats, bigger paper. Strategy first — then calmly.
📺 On screen: a slide with the 5 items → the key · drafts (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: solve the mini-review in silence, self-check, name their own hard round — the fear visibly drops. Expected: 4–5 out of 5 for most.

72–80′
A gentle review of typical mistakes
Gentle error clinic
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: the unit's top-5 mistake types, anonymously; “doctor teams” treat a patient sentence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I display type No.1 (“apostrophe drift”) + the “patient” sentence.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everyone writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they all lift them together — I see every answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. I command: Doctor teams — what's the cure? Fix the patient on your mini-board!
  4. I go through 5 types: apostrophe drift · Yes, I'm · adjective+s · lowercase countries · no inversion (the last two straight from TB Common errors and our own observations).
  5. The pace is calm, with NO points — a conversation between experts, not a race.
📺 On screen: slides: the mistake type + the patient sentence (anonymous, from my notes over the unit) (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: diagnose in teams and write the cure on mini-boards. Expected: “It's Yes, I AM — affirmative never contracts!”. Typical error: looking for “whose is this” — the rule said aloud: mistakes on the board belong to the whole class.

Workbook U1 · notebook
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. Assess Unit 1: countries/nationalities, place adjectives, be (all forms), listening notes, a short writing task — in a calm, model-first format, with a first-half revision block (there's no separate revision pair in the grid).
  • Subsidiary. The Quick-review lightning round as revision and taking the fear away; test-taking basics (example first, manage time, final apostrophe/capital sweep); self-marking of objective parts; gentle error clinic; error log + a line of pride.
  • Personal. Low-stress protocol: quiet music before start, clear timings per part, no competitive games during the test. Feedback = kind, specific, forward-looking. Revision BEFORE the test — so everyone walks in feeling “I already did this today”.
By the end, students can…
  • work through a 4-part test with strategy
  • self-mark objective parts against a key
  • name their own top-2 error types
  • say one thing they're proud of from Unit 1

“Test finished early?” → The two checks: apostrophes + capital letters.
“Is a mistake a catastrophe?” → No — it's a target for Unit 2.

Assumptions: Quick review done in P5, revision games in P6, WB p.11 self-evaluation at home — every test format has come up at least three times. Listening: if no track — the teacher reads the script twice (script in the test pack).

Unit 1 test — specification (DRAFT until the TRC pack: Progress tests standard/higher in the Teacher's Resource Centre, TB pp. 5, 30)
PartContents & marking
Part 1 · Vocabulary (14 pts)A) 8 items: country → nationality adjective, spelling counts (China → Chinese; the Netherlands → Dutch). B) 6 items: opposite adjectives (crowded → empty; safe → dangerous).
Part 2 · Grammar be (18 pts)A) 8 gaps aff/neg with contractions (My parents ___ from Moscow. It ___ (not) cold today.). B) 6 items: order the words to make questions + write a true short answer (from / you / Are / Russia ?). C) 4 items: correct the mistake (I is 11. → I'm 11.).
Part 3 · Listening (8 pts)A short description of a country (script in the plan; teacher-read twice if no track): 4 true/false + 4 notes to complete (population / capital / weather / sport) — mirrors SB p.17 ex.6–7.
Part 4 · Writing (10 pts)6 sentences about you (name, age, country, nationality, your city + one adjective). Marked for: be forms correct (4) · capital letters (3) · task complete (3) — mirrors keypal criteria SB p.21.

Total 50 pts · pass focus: not the number but the personal error log. Built strictly from Quick review SB p.21 — every format was met in class during the unit. As soon as the TRC pack is downloaded, this is replaced by the official Progress test (🐣 standard / 🚀 higher).

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
