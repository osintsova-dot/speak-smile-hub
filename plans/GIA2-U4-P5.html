🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The modal verbs are hidden inside a rulebook: the crew writes the law of its own camp, and the inspector hunts in someone else's law for holes and broken forms — 'must to', 'You not must'. The three columns (obligation · permission · prohibition) are put together not as 'a rule' but as three shelves in a legal code. The Yosemite rules from P4 (ex.4) are ready-made material: the students have already heard them in the guide's live speech and now they write them up. Weak/strong can (SB p.116) is served as 'the ranger's accent'.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“You've been in Yosemite. Now it's YOUR park. Each crew writes the rulebook: what visitors must do, what they can do, what they mustn't do. Then the inspector arrives — another crew — and hunts for two things: holes in your law and broken English. 'You must to wear boots' — that rule doesn't exist, it's badly written. And one more thing: rangers don't say /kæn/ in a question, they say /kən/. Eight rules per crew. Write!”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The park is ours now: the crew writes a rulebook (what you must do, what you can do, what you mustn't do), and another crew, the inspectors, hunts for holes in the law and broken forms. Plus the ranger's accent: in a question can sounds weak — /kən/.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can use must / mustn't / can / can't correctly (affirmative, negative, question, short answer) and write and defend an 8-rule rulebook for their own place.
  • Subsidiary. The three columns ex.1 and the two tables of forms ex.2 are built out of the examples from the P4 briefing; ex.3 rewriting by the symbols ?, −, +; ex.4 the park rules; ex.5 school rules for their own school; Grammar round-up (canyoning) as the entry ticket; the pronunciation of can /kən/ and /kæn/ (SB p.116).
  • Personal. I watch three things: no to after must, no -s in the 3rd person, Can comes first in the question. The rules that burn I write down for the clinic in this same pair. I give the can't / mustn't difference the TB way: can't — 'that's the rules', mustn't — 'I forbid it'.
By the end, students can…
  • put a rule in the right column (obligation / permission / prohibition)
  • build questions and short answers with can
  • rewrite a rule as +, − or ?
  • write and defend an 8-rule rulebook

Structure
Obligation: All food must go in these special boxes. · Permission: You can take photos. / Can we take food into the park? — Yes, you can. · Prohibition: You can't leave any food in your backpack. / You mustn't take any plants or flowers with you.
Form
subject + can / can't / must / mustn't + bare verb (no to and no -s: She can swim, NOT 'She cans swim') · question: Can + subject + verb? · short answer: Yes/No, + subject + can/can't
Pron. watch
SB p.116: in questions and statements can is unstressed — /kən/; in short answers it is stressed — /kæn/. can't is /kɑːnt/ in British, /kænt/ in American English. In mustn't the first t is not pronounced: /ˈmʌs(ə)nt/ (TB tip)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'You must to wear boots' — what is extra? → to: You must wear boots.
'She cans swim' — correct it. → She can swim: modals take no -s in the 3rd person.
'Can we to take photos?' — and here? → Can we take photos? — the verb is bare.

Assumptions: SB p.54 + Pronunciation p.116, KTP pair 34 (the KTP says p.55 — a +1 shift, the real page is 54). can is familiar from A1+ as 'be able to'; what is new is can as permission, must/mustn't and keeping the form clean under the pressure of the game.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
must to / can to — the classicsThe inspection rule: a rule with an extra to does not exist, the inspector strikes it out of the code. The ones that burn we go over in the clinic in this same pair.
They confuse can't and mustn'tI give it the TB way, as a shade of meaning rather than an error: can't = 'the rules don't allow it', mustn't = 'I, the owner of the park, forbid it'. The code has to have both.
The rules degenerate into 'everything is banned'The quota for the code: 8 rules, of which at least 2 with can (permission) and at least 1 question with a short answer.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
