A2 pre-teens (~10–11, confident readers) · 60 min · Phonics /aɪ/ (i_e · ie) + Culture (street food, NYC) + Literacy (write a recipe) · carpet + desks
Week 28.09–04.10 · КТП урок 8✨ Phonics i_e–ie~12 pupils · 3 teams of 4📖 Pupil's Book p.13–17
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & spell the /aɪ/ sound in its two spellings (i_e · ie) and to write a short procedural text — a recipe using imperative verbs and time sequencers.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: recognise → blend → segment /aɪ/. Reading for gist & detail (street-food text). Speaking: compare food cultures (critical thinking). Recycle Unit 1 ingredients / cooking verbs / How much–How many + need.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; run a stirrer→settler rhythm; make each block end with a visible product (notebook card / recipe / spoken comparison).
By the end, pupils can…
  • read & spell /aɪ/ words in i_e and ie;
  • name NYC street foods & compare them with their own culture;
  • deconstruct a recipe and write their own with First/Next/Then/After that/Finally.

Recycled
How much / How many … do we need? — We need a lot of / some / a few / a little (…).
Form
Recipe = title + ingredients (quantities) + equipment + numbered imperative steps in order. /aɪ/: silent e in i_e; -es in pies = /z/.
Pron. watch
long diphthong /aɪ/ (not /ɪ/): Mike ≠ mick; rice ≠ miss. Stress on the sequencer at the start of each step.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Phonics. “rice and pie — same sound or different?” → same /aɪ/. “How many ways to spell it here?” → two.
Quantity. “Sugar — can we count it? a few or a little?” → a little (uncountable).
Recipe. “Any order or in order?” → in order. “First or Finally to begin?” → First.

Assumptions: Unit 1 ingredients (L1) & cooking verbs (L4) known; some / a few / a little / a lot of + need met in L2; pupils are confident readers & can write short sentences. Teams: 4 max; other sizes 12=3×4 · 10=3+3+4 · 9=3×3 · 8=2×4 · 7=3+4.

Warm-up · retrieval
carpet, standing

Retrieve, don't re-teach. Unit 1 brain-dump — 30 seconds! In your team, name as many ingredients and cooking verbs as you can. Teams call out; you scribe a quick tally on the board (recycles L1/L4 into today's cooking theme). Class? — Yes!

▶ Rock–Paper–Scissors bridge: pupils pair up, RPS — winner asks How much / How many [flour] do we need?, loser answers We need a lot of / a little / a few [flour]. Best of three, swap partners once. Fast, everyone speaking at once.

 board + marker · ▶ RPS bridge
4–20′
Block 1 · Phonics /aɪ/ (i_e · ie)
carpet → desks

Present (recognise). Write rice and pie. Listen — rice… pie. Same sound? Yes — /aɪ/. But look — two spellings! Underline i_e and ie. Say the memory line together, cupping the long sound: Spike likes rice and Mike likes pies and wearing a tie. ×3 — slow, fast, whisper.

📓 Interactive notebook (~4′): glue the “i_e or ie? /aɪ/” A5 card in the phonics section. Highlight i_e in one colour, ie in another. Rule strip: HEAR /aɪ/ → LOOK AT THE SPELLING. [card: Spike · likes · rice · Mike / pie · pies · tie + memory line]

Blend & segment. Flash 8–10 word cards (rice, kite, five, pie, tie, lie, Mike, time, nice, cries). Class blends aloud; then you say a word, pupils segment on fingers (r-i-ce).

▶ Flyswatter (Мухобойки) — teams: ~14 /aɪ/ words + 3 distractors (miss, big, sit) taped on the board. One player per team with a swatter; you say a word, first to swat it wins — but must then say if it's i_e or ie to keep the point. Rotate the swatter every round so every child plays; non-swatters point at their answer in the air.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down (Kagan): you call words — i_e → stand up tall, ie → crouch down. Fast, whole class moves every time = a full-class spelling sort. Throw a distractor (fish) → nobody moves.

Segment to write (retrieval). Dictation of 4 words straight into the notebook beside the card (rice · pie · Mike · tie); pupils self-check against their own card. Then Pupil's Book p.13 “Speak and spell” — say & spell chorally.

 word cards (i_e/ie + distractors) · 3 flyswatters · 2 highlighters · glue · PB p.13 · notebook

Block 2 · Culture · Street food, NYC
carpet → seats

▶ Guess-by-a-piece (Угадай по кусочку): reveal 5 NYC street-food photos slowly, a sliver at a time (taco · pretzel · Belgian waffle · falafel · hot dog). Whole class calls guesses — Is it a…? It's a…! Stop when they start guessing, let them finish the word. Pre-teaches the culture lexis with zero list-copying.

Read for detail. Pupil's Book p.16–17 — read/listen (CD1 24). Answer Luke's four questions: Which food is in a hard shell? Which is big & salty? Which comes with salad? Which is from Belgium? Pairs check → whole-class feedback.

Culture video. Play the street-food video (Navio / Teacher's Resource Bank). Task: Which street food isn't in the video? Hands up on the answer.

🧠 Think critically — “Think about your culture”. ▶ Timed Pair Share (20 sec each) with a board frame: In my country we eat… It's like the [pretzel] because… It's different because… Push one step: Why do big cities have so much street food from other countries? Take 2–3 opinions — no single right answer.

34–52′
Block 3 · Literacy · a recipe
desks, pairs

Deconstruct the model (I do / We do). Project the banana-bread recipe (PB p.13) or the Knickerbocker glory (PB p.14). Think aloud, then hunt as a class: Where's the title? the ingredients with quantities? the equipment? the steps? Highlight the four sequencers First · Next · Then · After that · Finally. Name the genre rule: A recipe = title + what you need + steps in order, and every step starts with a doing-word.

📓 Interactive notebook (~5′): glue the “Write your own recipe” A5 template (PLAN → MODEL → WRITE → CHECK). Fill the PLAN box in notes only — recipe name · ingredients · equipment · prep time. Find the four sequencers in the MODEL. [genres this year: recipe → autobiography → encyclopaedia entry …]

▶ RallyCoach (pairs): partners write ONE recipe together (their invented snack). A writes a step aloud, B coaches & ticks the frame; swap each step. Use the WRITE frame: First, … Next, … Then, … After that, … Finally, … and quantities (a few nuts, a little cream, some flour).

CHECK. Swap notebooks; tick the partner's CHECK list — recipe name ✓ · ingredients & equipment ✓ · steps in order ✓ · spelling ✓. (Peer feedback = formative assessment.) Fold in PB p.15 “Investigate a text / Writing”.

 projector (model recipe) · 📓 recipe template A5 · glue · PB p.13–15 · notebooks
