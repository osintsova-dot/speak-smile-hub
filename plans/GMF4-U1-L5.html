Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /aɪ/ sound spelt i_e and ie (Spike likes rice · Mike likes pies · a tie), talk about street food in New York City, and read & plan a recipe — three mini-blocks, one “food” thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that i_e and ie both spell /aɪ/ (recognise → say → sort, next to igh & y); culture — street food in New York City (tacos · waffles · pretzels · falafel) + Channel 21 video, 21C: collaboration & teamwork; literacy — text type a recipe, time sequencers (First/Next/Then/After that/Finally), plan & write a recipe.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (chant/action bridges); the Spike-likes-rice tongue-twister is play; cap each block hard so the recipe actually gets planned.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the /aɪ/ twister and sort i_e / ie words;
  • name 2–3 NYC street foods and compare with ours;
  • find the parts of a recipe and plan one with a partner.

Structure
Spike likes rice and Mike likes pies and wearing a tie.  ·  You can eat (tacos) in a hard shell.  ·  recipe: First, chop the… Next, add the… Finally, cook for…
Form
/aɪ/ = i_e (Spike, rice, Mike, tie… wait — split digraph i_e) AND ie (pies, tie) — (also igh: light · y: fly). Recipe layout: ingredients · equipment · numbered instructions · time sequencers.
Pron. watch
/aɪ/ clean in Spike/rice/pies/tie; falafel /fəˈlæfəl/; pretzel /ˈpretsəl/; tacos /ˈtækəʊz/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“rice and pies — same /aɪ/ sound? Same letters?” → same sound, different spelling (i_e vs ie)!
“A recipe — do the instructions have an ORDER?” → yes — First, Next, Then… Finally.

Assumptions: Ingredients, cooking verbs, quantifiers & need to are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from GMF3 phonics is enough to enjoy the i_e / ie noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short recipe texts with support.

Block 1 · Phonics /aɪ/ (i_e · ie)
carpet → desks

Speak and spell (act. 1, CD1 20). PB p.13: Spike likes rice and Mike likes pies and wearing a tie. Remember! light · fly. Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort the /aɪ/ spellings. Hoops/columns: i_e (rice, Mike, tie, bike) · ie (pies, lie) · (spares: igh — light · y — fly). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card.

Notice the rule. Rice and pies — same sound /aɪ/, different letters! i_e has a magic e; ie is two letters together. (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more /aɪ/ word to a hoop (kite, fries).

✔ Assess: who still reads i_e as short /ɪ/? Note them — extra RallyRead next unit.

39–52′
Block 3 · Literacy · a recipe
carpet → desks, pairs

Read a recipe (PB p.14, act. 1–3, CD1 22). «Knickerbocker glory»: What ingredients do you need? Is it healthy? What's the last ingredient you add? Read/listen; find the ingredients, equipment & instructions.

▶ Recipe scavenger hunt. Race in pairs to find on PB p.15 «pizza» recipe: What equipment do you need? How long does it take? What do you do FIRST? First hands up read the answer aloud — everyone scans.

Writing tip (PB p.15). We use “First” to start. “Next, Then, After that” for the next steps. “Finally” for the last one! Chorus the sequencers with finger-counting.

▶ Plan a recipe (Cooperative learning, AB p.12 format). Pairs choose a simple dish & make notes: Recipe name · Ingredients · Equipment · Preparation time · Steps (First… Finally). 2 pairs share one step aloud.

💪 Support: fill the plan template with word/verb support. ⭐ Extension: write the full recipe with all six sequencers.
