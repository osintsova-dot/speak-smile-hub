WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin for the review: entry; a P2 rewind: a quick circle “where were you on Saturday” — was/were on autopilot; a bridge

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Entry: Hi! One feeling word each!
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. P2 rewind round the circle: Where were you on Saturday? Were you happy? — a short answer by the poster (+1 to the team for a clean one).
  4. Bridge: You can say WHERE you were. But WHAT did you do? ‘I was at home and I… watch TV?’ No — WATCHED. Today: the real verbs in the past.
📺 On screen: “two lanes” poster · verb wall · board: timeline
👦 Students do: they answer round the circle about Saturday with was/were. Expected: was/were is already confident. Typical error: “Yes, I wasn't” — I point at the poster.

Hook · my Saturday

WHAT WE DO: I tell them about MY weekend with pictures — they catch the new endings; we work out the rule +ed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I tell it with pictures on the slide, slowly, stressing with my voice: On Saturday I WATCHED a film. I COOKED dinner. I PLAYED with my dog. And I WALKED in the park.
  2. What do you HEAR at the end of every verb? — they catch /d/, /t/.
  3. I write the pairs on the board: watch → watched · cook → cooked · play → played · walk → walked.
  4. The rule in one line: The past of most verbs = verb + ED. Same for I, you, he, we, they. ONE form. Chorus ×2.
  5. Ex.1 p.90 we confirm from the book (key TB p.103: end/ask/stay/watch/play; add -ed).
📺 On screen: a slide with 4 pictures of my weekend · board: 4 verb pairs + the rule +ED (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they listen to the story, catch the endings, work out the rule themselves. Expected: they discover the rule in 2 minutes. Typical error: they think the form changes by person — I say plainly: one for everyone.

12–21′
Presenting: four rules for -ed
Discover · 4 spelling rules
board → desks

WHAT WE DO: four rules SB p.93 = four gestures; ex.2 a race on 7 verbs with a support; check with the gesture

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four rules on the board in colour (SB p.93) + a gesture for each:
  2. 1 usually +ed — the “little tail” gesture (walk → walked) · 2 ends in -e → +d — the “scissors” gesture (dance → danced) · 3 consonant + y → -ied — the “twist the y” gesture (study → studied) · 4 one vowel + one consonant → double it — the “double fist” gesture (stop → stopped).
  3. Drill: I say a verb — the class shows the rule NUMBER on their fingers, then says the form. ×10.
  4. Ex.2 p.90, pairs, 3 min, the rules in front of them: chat · decide · plan · play · study · walk · want. Key TB p.103: chatted · decided · planned · played · studied · walked · wanted.
  5. The trap aloud: Why PLAYED and not ‘plaied’? Look — a VOWEL before the y. Rule three needs a CONSONANT.
📺 On screen: SB p.90 + rules SB p.93 · board: 4 colour rules + 4 gestures (stay up all lesson)
👦 Students do: they show the rule number on their fingers, then write the forms in pairs. Expected: the gestures give support without hints. Typical error: “plaied” — the “vowel before y” gesture solves it.
🧠 Load: past simple — the first “big” verb system after be/have got/present simple. Pair rule: one form at a time, each one on a board support, drilling only through games, writing in chunks of 4–6 sentences. If the group starts to struggle, I cut the game, not the support.		SB pp.90, 93
21–28′
Practice: gap-fill + -ed pronunciation
Try it · gap-fill + clapping drill
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.3 gap-fill all at once → -ed pronunciation through clapping (an extra syllable only after t/d)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.3 on mini-boards all at once, 7 rounds (key TB p.103: decided · planned · studied · chatted · walked · wanted · played).
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face-down, on the call No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up at once — I see every answer, nobody can hide.
  3. Pronunciation (ABCD TB p.103) — ONE rule: After T or D — the -ed is an EXTRA syllable: want-ED, decid-ED, chatt-ED. Everything else — just /t/ or /d/, no extra syllable.
  4. ▶ Clapping drill: I say a verb — the class CLAPS if there's an extra syllable, and stays silent if not. wanted 👏 · walked ✋ · decided 👏 · played ✋ · chatted 👏 · asked ✋ · ended 👏 · helped ✋. I speed up.
  5. A team with no errors in a run of 4 — +1.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · my list of verbs · board: the T/D rule
👦 Students do: they answer all at once, then clap/stay silent — everyone reacts physically. Expected: the T/D rule settles in 3 minutes. Typical error: they clap on played — I repeat the pair played/decided side by side.

28–36′
Presenting: the first irregular verbs
Discover 2 · the first irregulars
board · teams

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 four forms → memory cards on 12 pairs → we introduce the list SB pp.126–127 as a tool

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4 p.90 (key TB p.103: had · met · did · went). Look at GO. Where's the -ed? Nowhere. GO becomes WENT — a completely different word.
  2. Honestly: Some verbs are irregular. There's no rule. But there are only about twelve you need NOW.
  3. I write 12 pairs on the wall in the IRREGULAR column: go→went · have→had · do→did · meet→met · see→saw · take→took · get→got · win→won · leave→left · tell→told · wear→wore · write→wrote. Every pair — chorus ×2.
  4. ▶ Memory game (Activities bank TB p.6): 24 cards face-down, pairs “infinitive + past”. Teams take turns turning two over; if you take a pair, say both forms aloud. 5 min.
  5. I show the list SB pp.126–127: Every irregular verb in English is HERE. Column one — now. Column two — past. Column three — later, not now. We practise searching: Find ‘eat’. Ten seconds!
📺 On screen: verb wall, the IRREGULAR column · 24 memory cards per team · SB pp.126–127
👦 Students do: they turn over cards, name both forms, learn to search in the list. Expected: the memory game repeats every form 5–6 times without rote learning. Typical error: they look at the third column — I point at the right one.

Drill · reading the dialogue like actors

WHAT WE DO: a choral and paired dialogue drill with intonation; we practise Really? and weak was

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A choral drill by lines: I'm Eddie, the class is Kelly; then the other way round.
  2. Intonation (TB p.104): Really? ↗ — surprise (Eddie), Really…? ↘ — disbelief (Kelly). Half the room is surprised, half doesn't believe it; we swap.
  3. Weak was: How was (/wəz/) your weekend? — It WAS (/wɒz/) great. Chorus ×3.
  4. Pairs read the dialogue standing up, then swap roles. 3 min.
  5. Whoever wants to — performs a couple of lines “like in the movies” for the class (+1).

Performing the dialogue: PERFORM carousel
Share · PRACTISE and PERFORM
pairs → stage

WHAT WE DO: a run-through in pairs → a carousel “perform for three pairs” → two pairs for the class; peer review ex.6 by two questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. PRACTISE 2 min: pairs read their dialogue standing up, then once more with no paper (peeking allowed).
  2. PERFORM carousel: a pair performs for another pair, then shift — a new audience pair. Three rounds of 90 sec. Everyone's engaged, no idle spectators.
  3. The audience has a PAST card: if they hear the present tense, they silently hold it up; the speaker repeats the phrase correctly.
  4. Two pairs for the class (by choice), applause.
  5. Peer review (ex.6), two questions: Are any activities the same as yours? Do they use the Key phrases? — one sentence each.
📺 On screen: PAST cards for the audience · timer · SB p.91 ex.6
👦 Students do: they perform the dialogue for three different pairs, the audience watches with the card. Expected: by the third round the dialogue sounds fine with no paper. Typical error: the present tense — the PAST card fixes it silently.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can form the past simple of regular verbs with the four spelling rules and use 12 common irregular verbs — and tell a partner about last weekend with the Key phrases.
  • Subsidiary. A step-by-step presentation: +ed → four spelling rules through four gestures → -ed pronunciation → the first irregulars through games (memory, chain) → the list SB pp.126–127 as a tool; RWS video as a model, a dialogue with a support, PERFORM in pairs.
  • Personal. ⚠️ The second big chunk of the past in two pairs. Do NOT rush: we present regular and irregular verbs separately, each chunk through a game. The verb wall grows the most today.
By the end, students can…
  • add -ed with the right spelling (studied · stopped · played)
  • say and write 12 irregular past forms
  • find any verb in the list on SB pp.126–127
  • ask and answer about last weekend with the Key phrases

Structure
I watched TV and played video games. · We went to the cinema. · I had a piano lesson. · How was your weekend? — It was great, thanks. · What about on Sunday?
Form
the past is the same for ALL persons (I/you/he/we/they watched) · 4 rules for -ed (SB p.93) · irregulars — by memorising only (SB pp.126–127).
Pron. watch
-ed = an extra syllable /ɪd/ ONLY after t/d: wanted · decided · chatted; otherwise just /t/ or /d/: walked · played (TB p.103) · Really? with surprise ↗.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He watched, she watched, they watched — does the form change?” → No, one form for everyone.
“Studyed or studied?” → consonant + y → -ied. And played? → a vowel before the y → just +ed.
“I goed — is that allowed?” → No: go → WENT. It's an irregular verb, you have to memorise it.

Assumptions: was/were from P2 is active (we'll check it on entry). Regular verbs are a new but logical rule; irregulars are pure memorisation, so only 12 of them and only through games. We do NOT touch negatives and questions in past simple today (that's Unit 8).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They try to add -ed to irregularsTwo columns on the wall are physically separated: REGULAR (+ed) and IRREGULAR (memory). Every new verb goes up in its own column.
studyed / stoped4 rules = 4 gestures; a gesture instead of a hint. Check only with the gesture — the hand remembers faster than the head.
The -ed pronunciation rule overloads themWe give ONE rule: an extra syllable only after t/d. Everything else — “just /t/ or /d/, don't think”. A clapping drill.
12 irregulars at once is a lotNot a list but three games: memory cards, a chain, “slap”. By the end of the pair they've repeated every form 6–8 times without drilling by rote.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
