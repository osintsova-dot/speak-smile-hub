🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Write down the word or the number you actually hear in a page of notes, and choose the right option in a dialogue where all three are mentioned.
What is genuinely hard
Both tasks lose marks for reasons that have nothing to do with English. The notes task punishes three words where one was asked for, and a spelling slip in a word they know. The dialogue task punishes answering from the first thing they hear, because the speaker corrects themselves two seconds later.
Typical error to expect
Two or three words written into a gap that asked for one, and the mark is lost even though the content was right [exam report]
They write down the first thing they hear and never notice the correction that follows two seconds later. The answer is what is said LAST [exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
This is the one lesson of the block where the exam format IS the topic, and it is built the way an exam lesson has to be built: strategy before task, task in real conditions, then analysis of the wrong options. Listening Part 2 comes first because it is the more mechanical of the two and the strategy is teachable in one move — say out loud what kind of thing each gap needs before the recording starts. That prediction stage is the whole lesson in miniature, and the book puts it in as its own exercise. Listening Part 3 comes after the move break because it needs more attention: five questions where every option is said aloud. Both tasks get the same treatment — a worked example done out loud by the teacher, then real conditions, then the question that actually teaches: not «what is the answer» but «which words proved it». The last twelve minutes open the Russian version of the same job in the school trainer, so the two wrappers sit side by side and the difference turns out to be trivial.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today one skill in two wrappers. You will hear a page of notes with five gaps in it, and you write down what you hear. Then a phone call where all three answers are said out loud every single time. At the end I will show you the Russian version of exactly the same job — and you will see how little changes.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: papers returned, five personal errors written down and one sentence from everybody

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — test feedback, and it is the reason this stage exists. Hand back the two papers. No marks read out loud. One instruction only: Find your five. Write them on the inside cover of your notebook.
  3. Then one question round the room, fast: One thing you will never get wrong again. One sentence each.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): state today plainly: Today is a listening lesson, and there is one rule that decides half the marks.
📺 On screen: no equipment at all — the marked papers are the material and they are already in their hands
👦 Students do: take the paper back, find five errors, say one sentence. Expect the articles to appear on at least half the lists. Typical problem: somebody compares marks out loud — stop it once, quietly, and move on

Why: Frames the whole lesson: the skill is one, only the wrapping differs. That single idea stops the exam feeling like a separate subject.

WHAT WE DO: the map of the lesson: one skill, two wrappers, and what actually loses marks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Draw two boxes on the board and one arrow between them. Left box: notes with gaps — you write what you hear. Right box: the same thing, in Russian.
  2. The skill is one. The wrapper is different. Today we do the English one properly, and in the last twelve minutes I show you the Russian one.
  3. Then the two rules that decide most of the marks, written in the corner of the board where they stay all lesson: 1–2 words and spelt right.

10–20′
Listening Part 2 · format
class → pairs
Why: The move that earns the marks is made before the recording starts — say out loud what kind of thing each gap needs. The book's own exercise asks exactly that.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.129: what the task is, the warm-up, the example gap and the prediction move

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB p.256, books closed, three minutes: «How do you keep fit?» and «Do you go to a gym?» If anybody in the group uses one, ask them what the membership costs, what equipment there is and how they use it. Then open the book — half the vocabulary is already in the air.
  2. Books open at p.129. You hear one speaker giving practical information and you complete a page of notes. Five gaps, one or two words or a number in each. The recording is played twice. Read the EXAM INFORMATION box aloud with them.
  3. Read the tips and put the two mechanical ones on the board. read the notes BEFORE you listen and decide what kind of information each gap needs — a price, a time, a name, a piece of clothing;; a name will usually be spelled out for you, so be ready to write letters;
  4. Play the example (track 152) and do it together. The example is worked through together (TB p.256): the answer is Saturday, not Friday, because Saturday is the day of the club and Friday is the last day you can book. Two dates in one sentence and only one of them answers the question — that is the whole task in miniature.
  5. Now the move that earns the marks. The move that earns the marks, and it is ex.2 on the page: before the recording starts, say out loud what each gap is going to be. (1 a price · 2 a morning time · 3 a first name or a surname, not both, because only one word fits · 4 an article of clothing · 5 something connected to sport.) Tell them 3 will probably be spelled out and that 4 hides a negative — listen for what is NOT necessary.
  6. Kagan Think-Pair-Share on the prediction: twenty seconds alone, then the partner, then the class agrees on all five out loud before anything else is played.
  7. One minute of alphabet drill, because gap 3 is spelled out: you say a name, they write it from the letters. Hughie. Beattie. Vaughan.
📺 On screen: track 152 and the notes page on screen (Backup with no sound: the audioscript is on TB p.305 — read the example aloud yourself. The notes page is printed on SB p.129 and the prediction stage needs no audio at all)
👦 Students do: talk about gyms, read the notes, predict the five gaps. Expect the prediction of gap 4 to be the one they get wrong — it hides a negative. Typical problem: they want to start listening immediately; hold the line, the prediction is the lesson

20–32′
Listening Part 2 · exam conditions
solo → pairs
Why: Timed, silent, two plays: the strategy only counts if it survives real conditions. And two of the rules here are mechanical — one or two words, spelt right.

WHAT WE DO: the five gaps done in silence, twice through, then checked with the evidence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silent mode. Palm down. Pens ready, eyes on the notes. No talking from the first note of the recording.
  2. Play track 153 once, straight through. Confirm nothing.
  3. Thirty seconds alone to complete anything they can still remember.
  4. Play track 153 a second time. Tell them what the second play is for: to CHECK, not to answer.
  5. Kagan RallyCoach for the pair check: A says the answer and the words that proved it, B coaches, then swap.
  6. Class check. Key: 1 12.50 · 2 10.20 · 3 Hughie · 4 trousers · 5 towel (TB p.256)
  7. TB p.256, four questions after the check, and they are what turn a right answer into understanding: «How much are other clubs?» (£15 or £20) · «Why should people arrive by 10 o'clock?» (so they can meet the teacher) · «What should people wear?» (shorts) · «Why don't they need to bring water?» (the gym has it).
📺 On screen: track 153 in the whiteboard app, played twice (Backup with no sound: the audioscript is on TB p.305–306 — read it aloud yourself twice at natural speed, and spell the name in gap 3 rather than saying it. The task is identical)
👦 Students do: listen twice in silence, then check in pairs. Expect gap 1 and gap 2 to be right for almost everybody and gap 4 to be wrong for half. Typical error: towel spelt towl — and that is zero marks in both exams

32–40′
Where the marks go
pairs → class
Why: Analysis of what went wrong turns into a checklist the students wrote themselves, which is the only kind of checklist anybody uses.

WHAT WE DO: turn the mistakes into a checklist the students wrote themselves

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Where marks go: three words written where one was asked for, a price written out in letters and misspelt, and a name guessed instead of taken from the spelling. All three are mechanical and none of them is about English.
  2. In pairs, 3 minutes: Write three rules for this task. Rules you would give to somebody who has never done it.
  3. Collect them on the board and merge them into four. Expect them to produce «read the notes first», «one or two words», «check the spelling» and «listen for the negative» — which is the official tip list rediscovered.
  4. Kagan Numbered Heads Together: call a number, that student reads one rule out and gives an example of breaking it.
  5. Write the final four rules in the corner of the board. They stay there for the rest of the lesson and are used again at 70′.
📺 On screen: the four rules written on the board as they are agreed (Backup: none needed — this stage is chalk, and the chalk version is the one that stays up)
👦 Students do: write three rules, argue, agree on four. Expect one group to produce a rule about guessing, which is worth keeping: an empty gap is always zero, a guess sometimes is not

42–50′
Listening Part 3 · format
class → pairs
Why: The trap is named before the task: all three options get mentioned. Once that is said out loud, the task stops being a memory test and becomes a listening one.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.130: what the task is, the trap named out loud, and the worked example

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed first (TB p.257): in groups, What is a great way to spend an afternoon? Rank four ideas by how much fun they are. Two minutes.
  2. Books open at p.130. A dialogue between two people and five three-option multiple-choice questions. You listen for specific information — dates, prices — and for feelings and opinions. The answers come from BOTH speakers, not just one. The recording is played twice. Read the EXAM INFORMATION box aloud.
  3. Read the tip and write it on the board in six words. all three options are usually mentioned, so a word you heard is not an answer;
  4. Callum and Stella are talking about a skatepark: where it is, how big it has to be, what is going to be built there, what it will cost and when they are going to meet. Check they know what they are listening for: Who knows about the skatepark — Callum or Stella? (Callum) · Are there going to be any changes to it? (yes) · Do they decide to go? (yes).
  5. Play track 154 for question 1 only and work it through out loud. The first question is worked through together (TB p.257): the answer is C. A is wrong because he never says how close it is to the cinema; B is wrong because that one is on the other side of town, so it is NOT close; C is correct — it is opposite it, so it must be close.
📺 On screen: track 154 and the five questions on screen (Backup with no sound: the audioscript is on TB p.306 — read the exchange for question 1 aloud in two voices. The worked example is teacher talk anyway)
👦 Students do: rank ideas, read the questions, watch the example being solved. Expect the explanation of why B is wrong to be the moment the task clicks

50–62′
Listening Part 3 · exam conditions
solo → pairs
Why: Five questions under time, then a partner check on one thing only — which words in the recording proved the answer.

WHAT WE DO: the remaining four questions in silence, twice through, then checked with evidence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. One minute to read questions 2–5 and underline the question word in each.
  2. Silent mode. Palm down. Play track 155 once, straight through. Confirm nothing.
  3. Pairs compare, quietly, one minute — and they must say which words they heard, not just which letter they chose.
  4. Play track 155 a second time. Ask afterwards who changed an answer, which one and why. That question is the strategy.
  5. Class check. Key: 1 C · 2 B (it has to have enough space) · 3 A (they're going to put a shop there too) · 4 C (when it's finished, it'll be £5, but it's only £3 until then) · 5 A («We could meet there at ten…» «Yes, sure») — TB p.257
  6. TB p.257: «Is there another skatepark in town?» (yes) · «Why is the weather not a problem?» (the skatepark has a roof) · «Where does Stella need to be in the afternoon?» (her auntie's house). Then the question that makes it their own: does our town have enough places for teenagers, and what else could it have?
📺 On screen: track 155 in the whiteboard app, played twice (Backup with no sound: the audioscript is on TB p.306 — read the dialogue aloud yourself in two voices, twice, at natural speed. The five questions are printed on SB p.130)
👦 Students do: listen twice, compare, check. Expect question 4 (the price) to change between the two plays for several of them, which is the best possible advertisement for the second play. Typical error: choosing the first price mentioned

62–70′
Why the wrong option is wrong
pairs → class
Why: Explaining why B is wrong teaches more than confirming that C is right, because the wrong options are built from exactly what a careless listener hears.

WHAT WE DO: explain the distractors, not the answers — that is where the learning is

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Where marks go: they hear the price £5 and tick it, without hearing «but it's only £3 until then». The number that is said LAST, or with a but in front of it, is almost always the answer.
  2. In pairs, 4 minutes and one question: Take question 4. Why is B wrong? Not «because C is right» — why is B there?
  3. Collect the answers and name the mechanism out loud: every wrong option is built from something that was genuinely said. That is what makes it plausible, and it is why hearing a word is not an answer.
  4. Kagan Showdown to finish: you read three short exchanges of your own with a self-correction in each (It's at six… no, sorry, half past six), and everybody writes the answer and reveals at Boards up!
  5. Add one rule to the list on the board: the answer is what is said LAST.
📺 On screen: the four rules from 32–40′ still on the board, now growing to five (Backup: none needed — chalk, three spoken exchanges and mini-whiteboards)
👦 Students do: explain distractors, then catch three self-corrections. Expect the third exchange to catch somebody, and expect them to laugh about it — which is the point

Why: The same job in the Russian wrapper: notes with gaps, one or two words, written down as you hear them. Experiencing that is what stops the exam looking like a different subject.

WHAT WE DO: open the Russian version of the same job in the school trainer and compare the two

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Open the trainer on the screen: https://speakandsmile.online/oge.html#/hw?listening:g16 — no password, no sign-up, it opens straight away.
  2. One complete OGE listening set, eleven items in the order of the real paper: item 1 — five speakers matched to six headings, one heading extra; items 2–5 — three-option multiple choice; items 6–11 — write in the missing word or number. The recording is played twice, as in the exam.
  3. Only items 6–11 are done in class — they are the Russian twin of Listening Part 2 on SB p.129. The rest of the set is left for whoever wants it at home. The announcement is about a week of documentary films and an exhibition of contemporary art — the block's own topic, so no new vocabulary gets in the way.
  4. Before anything is played, the same prediction move as at 10–20′: what kind of information does each gap need? Two minutes in pairs.
  5. Play it once, then once more. They fill items 6–11 on paper, not on the screen — it is faster and it keeps everybody involved.
  6. Then put the two side by side and ask for the differences. Two differences, and only two. The labels of the notes are in Russian, and the answers are typed into a box rather than written on a line. Nothing else changes — not the skill, not the strategy, not the traps.
  7. And the sameness, which is the message of the lesson: Exactly the same job as SB p.129: a page of notes with gaps, and you write the word or the number you actually hear. One or two words, spelt correctly.
  8. Close it with the four rules on the board: read them out and ask which of them changed. Not one of them did.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can complete gapped notes from a recording with one or two words spelt correctly, and choose between three options in a dialogue.
  • Subsidiary. Predicting the kind of information a gap needs; hearing a self-correction; writing numbers and times as figures; explaining why a wrong option is wrong.
  • Personal. Confirm nothing on the first play, either task. The second play is the strategy, and if you confirm early it never gets used.
By the end, students can…
  • say what kind of information a gap needs before the recording starts
  • write a price, a time and a spelled name into a gap correctly
  • wait for the end of an exchange before choosing an option
  • explain why the option they did not choose is wrong

Structure
It costs £12.50 a month. · Please arrive by 10.20. · Ask for Hughie. · You don't need to bring water. · It'll be £5 when it's finished, but it's only £3 until then.
Form
notes are not sentences: the answer is one or two words, or a figure. Prices and times go in as figures — 12.50, not twelve fifty.
Pron. watch
the numbers that get confused by ear: 13 and 30, 10.20 and 20.10, and prices where the pound and the pence run together. Drill five of them before the first recording.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
The gap says «Wear: ______». Will the answer be a price? → no — an article of clothing. Deciding that BEFORE you listen is what earns the mark.
«How many words go in one gap?» → one or two, and a number counts as one. Three words is zero even if the meaning is right.
The recording says «it'll be £5, but it's only £3 until then». Which is the answer? → £3. The number after but is almost always the one.
«All three options were mentioned. So how do I choose?» → by meaning, not by memory. Which option does the whole exchange add up to?

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
