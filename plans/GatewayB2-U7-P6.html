🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Systematise the whole unit — reported statements, reported questions, other reporting verbs and their structures, and the four vocabulary sets (genres · words from music and film · review adjectives · compound nouns) — and then produce it under test conditions: Test yourself, SB p.95, forty points.
What is genuinely hard
The unit's grammar is three separate systems that look like one. Under test pressure students merge them: they backshift correctly in a statement and then leave the auxiliary in a question, or they know that «apologise» needs something but write «apologise to causing». The vocabulary half is a spelling problem — compound nouns written as two words score nothing, and «clichéd» loses its accent.
Typical error to expect
«She asked me where do I live», «He asked what is my name» — a reported question is not a question: no auxiliary do/does/did, no inversion, statement word order. She asked me where I lived. The Student's Book plants the trap on page 86 and the unit test collects on it.
«He apologised to be late», «She admitted to steal it», «He suggested me to go» — every reporting verb carries its own pattern: apologise FOR + -ing · admit + -ing · suggest + -ing or that-clause · accuse somebody OF + -ing · warn somebody AGAINST + -ing. Learned as chunks or lost.
«block buster», «cliff hanger», «sound track» — compound nouns are a spelling mark with no rule behind them. The test spells them letter by letter, and the stress falls on the FIRST word (BLOCKbuster), which is what makes them sound like one word.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is not revision by re-explanation — it is retrieval. The Grammar reference on p.94 is worked through by the students in pairs with a task attached (find the rule, find the example, find the trap), then the three systems are drilled fast in a Showdown, which forces every student to produce every item instead of waiting for the quick one. The vocabulary is recycled through Quiz-Quiz-Trade on the move and a spelling race, because spelling is what the paper actually marks. Then a four-minute briefing that names the two ghosts aloud, and thirty-four quiet minutes of testing. The test is the calmest part of the lesson by design.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty points, six sections, and not one thing on this paper is new — you have done every single format in the last five lessons. First half: we put the whole unit in order and I answer everything. Second half: you show me what stuck.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, reviews returned with one public repair, and the shape of the double lesson named

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: One sentence: what was the last thing you watched or listened to? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — rewind, and it is the whole unit today. Kagan Think-Pair-Share: Tell your partner about the book you reviewed yesterday — in three sentences, with two adjectives. Thirty seconds each way.
  3. 1 minute — hand back the reviews. By name, with one sentence each and nothing longer. Say the single most common problem aloud to the room and write the repaired sentence on the board. That is the last piece of teaching before the test.
  4. Say the shape of the day honestly and without drama: forty -five minutes of revision in which you answer everything, then forty-five with the paper.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): point at the wall: Everything on that wall is on the test. In forty-five minutes it gets covered up.
📺 On screen: the repaired sentence from the reviews on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: recycle the review vocabulary, then see their own writing returned and one common error repaired publicly

Why: Grammar reference, worked not read

WHAT WE DO: SB p.94: the three systems retrieved with a task attached

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books open at p.94. Pairs, ten minutes, with a written task — reading a reference page without one produces nothing.
  2. The task, on the board, three columns to fill in their notebooks: THE RULE in your own words · ONE EXAMPLE from the page · THE TRAP — what is easy to get wrong. Three rows: reported statements, reported questions, other reporting structures.
  3. While they work, circulate and collect the traps they write. The good ones go on the board under their authors' names.
  4. Whole class check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together, four minutes: call a number, take the trap first and the rule second. Traps to make sure appear: say has no personal object and tell must have one · could, would, should, might and the past perfect do not change · reported questions have no inversion and no do · verbs of the same meaning take different structures.
  5. Anything still disputed is answered by you, now, at speed. This is the last opportunity and they know it.
📺 On screen: the three-column table on the board, filled with the students' own traps — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: work a reference page in pairs against a three-column task, then answer under the Numbered Heads rule

WHAT WE DO: a fast retrieval drill where nobody can hide behind the quick student

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Showdown, and explain the mechanics in twenty seconds because they make or break it: you read an item, EVERY student writes the answer on their own mini-whiteboard or paper, face down. On Showdown! all four in the team turn them over at once, compare, and coach whoever differs.
  2. Twelve items, mixed deliberately across the three systems so that switching is part of the practice: four reported statements («I can't do this exercise,» Ethan said), four reported questions («Where have you just been?» Charlie asked Holly), four verb structures (apologise / accuse / refuse / remind).
  3. After each item, one team says the rule out loud — not the answer, the rule. That is the retrieval that transfers.
  4. Keep the pace brutal: twelve items in seven minutes. The point is automaticity, not discussion.
  5. The teams keep score. The score is worth nothing and they will play for it anyway.
📺 On screen: the twelve items read aloud, one at a time — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: every student writes all twelve answers themselves; nobody watches
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 19–25 and 26–31 (grammar and word formation). Thirteen marks of the written paper are produced-and-spelled, not chosen — which is precisely what a Showdown drills, because every student writes every item instead of one student answering.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: swap three of the twelve Showdown items for Russian-format ones: a sentence with the verb in capitals to be transformed. Costs nothing — it is the same twelve items.

Move break · Quiz-Quiz-Trade on the vocabulary
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Sixteen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: the four vocabulary sets recycled on the move before the written race

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands with one word card — thirty cards covering the four sets: genres, words from music and film, review adjectives, compound nouns.
  2. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: find a partner, quiz them on your word (definition, or positive or negative, or one word or two), get quizzed, TRADE cards, find somebody new. Four minutes, five or six partners each.
  3. The one rule that makes it a language activity rather than a card swap: the quizzer must not read the word aloud — they define it and the partner produces it.
  4. Freeze and sit with the cards collected: Now the half of this that costs marks. Spelling.
📺 On screen: thirty word cards, prepared before the lesson — no technology, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: every student defines and produces five or six words standing, with a different partner each time

Vocabulary · the spelling race

Why: Vocabulary · the spelling race

WHAT WE DO: SB p.94 Vocabulary lists recycled, with the compound nouns spelled letter by letter

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books open at the four vocabulary lists on p.94, two minutes silent: read them and mark any word you could not spell from memory. Honesty is the whole point and it takes two minutes.
  2. Then the race, six minutes, teams of four, one sheet per team: you say a definition, they write the WORD, spelled correctly. Fifteen items, weighted towards the compounds and the adjectives — the moment in a film when you don't know what happens next → cliffhanger · a disadvantage → drawback · too familiar, not original → clichéd.
  3. Marking rule announced in advance, exactly as the test marks it: one letter wrong, or a space where there should not be one, and the item scores zero. It sounds harsh and it stops the two-word habit in one round.
  4. Swap sheets between teams to mark. Read the correct spellings aloud, letter by letter for the compounds.
  5. Two minutes, individually and silently: everyone writes their own three worst words three times each. That is the last revision act of the lesson.
  6. Then the ceremony: cover the wall. Paper, cloth, whatever — and do it slowly enough that everybody sees it happen.
📺 On screen: the fifteen definitions read aloud, then the correct spellings written up letter by letter — chalk and paper only
👦 Students do: write fifteen words as a team under spelling pressure, then each drill their own three weakest

Briefing · before the papers turn over
desks
Why: Almost every avoidable mark on this paper is lost to two known errors and to not checking. Four minutes spent naming them is the highest-value four minutes in the lesson.

WHAT WE DO: four calm minutes that decide half the marks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Desks apart, everything off them except a pen. Books closed and on the floor.
  2. Name the two ghosts one final time, on the board, and then erase them: no «do» in reported questions · compound nouns are ONE word.
  3. Timing, said aloud: Thirty-four minutes. Grammar first, about eighteen minutes. Vocabulary, about twelve. Four minutes at the end to check — and you will use them.
  4. The checking instruction, which is worth more than any revision: When you finish, go back to section two and count the auxiliaries. Every «do», «does» or «did» in a reported question is a mark you are giving away.
  5. Early finishers: re-read section 2, then SB p.139 Reach higher Unit 7 on a separate sheet. Nobody leaves the room.
  6. Then silence, and start the clock.
📺 On screen: the two ghosts written up and then deliberately erased — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: clear their desks, hear the timing plan, and receive one concrete checking instruction

WHAT WE DO: thirty-four minutes with the paper: 40 points, six sections

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books open at p.95 only. Answers in the notebook, numbered by section — the book is not written in.
  2. Grammar test: 1 six reported statements from direct speech with the word given · 2 seven reported questions · 3 a text completed with the correct form of the verbs, prepositions added where needed.
  3. Vocabulary test: 1 seven words defined in simple English · 2 six anagrams of review adjectives, each marked positive or negative · 3 seven compound nouns matched from two columns, plus the odd one out.
  4. You do not walk around reading over shoulders. Sit down, and look up only to answer a hand — and only for a word that cannot be understood, never for an answer.
  5. Call the time twice: at minute twenty (vocabulary now, if you are not there yet) and at minute thirty (four minutes. Section two. Count the auxiliaries.).
  6. Collect at thirty-four minutes, by row, calmly.

Pron. watch
compound NOUNS take the stress on the first word (BLOCKbuster, CLIFFhanger, SOUNDtrack, BOX office) while compound ADJECTIVES take it on the second (best-SELLing, eco-FRIENDly) — that is exactly what track 38 tests · clichéd /ˈkliːʃeɪd/ keeps the French vowel and the class will say «kli-CHED» · genre /ˈʒɒnrə/ · lyrics /ˈlɪrɪks/, not «LYE-rics» · and in the emphatic I really DO think that… the stress lands on do, which is the whole point of the structure
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
Fast retrieval check, said as a chant before the test: Reported question — do we use «do»? — No. Do we invert? — No. Do we write a question mark? — No.
«Apologise…?» → FOR + gerund. «Accuse…?» → somebody OF + gerund. «Refuse…?» → to + infinitive. Twenty seconds, whole class, chorally.
«Best-selling» — is it a noun? → no, an adjective. It is the trap printed in the answer key of exercise 3, and naming it now is worth a point.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«We're doing a test? Nobody told us»It was announced in lesson 5 and it is in the syllabus. Say it calmly and once, then show the shape of the paper on the board — six sections, forty points, thirty minutes — and start the revision. Arguing costs revision time, which is the thing that actually helps them.
Silence in the revision half — nobody asks anythingThat is why the Grammar reference is worked with a TASK rather than read. A pair that has to find the trap in a rule will ask about it; a pair told to «revise» will not.
Finishing the test in twelve minutesThe instruction is given before the papers turn over: anyone who finishes early re-reads section 2 and counts the auxiliaries, then does SB p.139 Reach higher Unit 7 on a separate sheet. Nobody leaves early and nobody sits idle.
A student who genuinely panicsQuietly, at their desk, no announcement: they may use the backshift ladder from the board for section 1. Note it and mark accordingly. A panicking student learns nothing; a supported one does.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
