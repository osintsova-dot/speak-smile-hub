Into the lesson: greeting + rewind
Opening · check-in + rewind

WHAT WE DO: Showdown as a rewind: a word about the weekend at the door, ping-pong “country→nationality” (a rewind of P1), a bridge — “boarding is called”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: One word about your weekend — go! Finger mood 1–5.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everyone writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they all lift them together — I see every answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. Rewind of P1: Suffix ping-pong! I throw a country — you return the nationality! — in chorus: Turkey! — Turkish! 6–8 throws, pace rises.
  4. The last 2 rounds in writing: On your boards: CHINA! — boards up together, spelling matters, +1 for clean.
  5. Bridge: You can NAME the world. Now let's visit it. Boarding call — flight leaves in one minute!
📺 On screen: mini-boards · my list of countries for throwing
👦 Students do: answer in chorus at speed, the last 2 rounds write on mini-boards and lift together. Expected: Chinese, Dutch. Typical error: “Chinish” — I silently point at the suffix table in the exercise book.

6–10′
Where are we flying? Guess the country from a photo
Hook · destination reveal
screen

WHAT WE DO: we guess the country from photos (the Netherlands), vote “believe/don't believe” on the claim “No.1 for teenagers”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I open photos A–E from SB p.14 (or ask them to open the book): Which country are we flying to? Why? Team guess — thirty seconds!
  2. I collect the guesses, reveal: The Netherlands!
  3. I make the claim: Scientists say it's the №1 place to be a teenager. Believe it? Thumbs up or down!
  4. I record the vote count in the corner of the board: We'll check the facts and come back to this.
📺 On screen: SB p.14 photos A–E (Backup: books instead of the projector) · corner of the board: “Believe: 7 / Doubt: 5”
👦 Students do: confer for 30 sec, name the country with a reason (“bikes! cheese!”), vote with thumbs. Expected: they recognise it from the bikes.

10–20′
Learning to identify a text type by its look
Discover · text types (subskill)
desks

WHAT WE DO: Quiz-Quiz-Trade: we look at p.15 WITHOUT reading: we work out the type of each of the 4 texts by look alone

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I say: Page 15. DON'T read. Thirty seconds. What do you SEE: titles? photos? numbers? full sentences?
  2. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the unit's printed cards (handout GIA1-QQT: the question on top, the answer on the back after folding; no printout → the students write their own cards in 2 min).
  3. Teams: Stand up, hand up, pair up! Quiz your partner — praise — TRADE cards — find a new partner! 3–4 swaps, everyone asks at the same time.
  4. I collect observations, introduce the move: Pro readers look FIRST, read SECOND. That's a subskill.
  5. Task (ex.2): Match the four texts to a–d using ONLY the look. Teams, two minutes.
  6. I check with reasoning: How did you know? — Numbers and % = fun facts! +2 for a match with a reason.
  7. I finish off ex.1: Match words 1–5 to photos A–E. Sixty seconds!
📺 On screen: the Student's Book pp.14–15 · 4 clue icons on the board: 📊 numbers · 🧑 “I'm…” · 📰 sentences · 🎉 %-facts
👦 Students do: look for 30 sec without reading, in teams match texts to types and explain “how they knew”. Expected: “text 4 — numbers → fun facts”. Typical error: they start reading line by line — “Look, don't read!”
⭐ Fast finishers (TB p.20): write down what you see in each photo (bicycles, a pancake…).		SB pp.14–15 · timer · the unit's QQT cards (handouts/GIA1-QQT-U1.pdf)
20–34′
A 4-text quest: a fact hunt
Play · Fact Hunt stations (jigsaw)
4 stations

WHAT WE DO: everyone goes to THEIR OWN station-text with a hunt card (3 facts), comes back and reports — the team needs all 12

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before the lesson I put up 4 enlarged texts around the corners of the room.
  2. I give everyone a hunt card for their own station (No.1 fact file: capital·currency·population; No.2 Thomas: age·city·nationality; No.3 happy teens: %·2 reasons; No.4 fun facts: 86%·25%·900 km).
  3. ICQ: Do you copy the text? — No! Short answers: Amsterdam, euro, 17 million.
  4. I start: Four minutes — GO! Everyone heads to THEIR OWN station at the same time.
  5. On the timer I call them back: Report to your team! Fill the team sheet — all twelve facts. +5 to the first complete team.
  6. In the background, optionally — audio 10, quietly.
📺 On screen: 4 stations with texts round the corners · hunt cards (3 facts each) · a team sheet for 12 facts · a timer
👦 Students do: spread out to the stations, look for their 3 facts (short answers), come back and dictate to the team. Expected: Amsterdam · euro · 17 million · 13 · Zwolle · 95%… Typical error: copy out the whole sentence — I show a model: “Amsterdam” in one word.
⭐ For the fast ones: a “Fact+” envelope — find a number, a name and a % that nobody used.		station sheets · hunt cards · timer
34–43′
True/false on mini-boards
Check · true/false showdown
desks · mini-boards

WHAT WE DO: a statement on the slide → everyone writes T/F on mini-boards at the same time; we fix the false ones aloud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I display the first statement from ex.5 (Thomas is 12 years old).
  2. I command: T or F on your boards. Five seconds… Boards UP!
  3. False → the team corrects it: Thomas isn't 12 — he's 13! +1 for the correction.
  4. I go through 6–7 statements of ex.5. Key: F(13) · F(900 km) · T · T · F · F(25%) · F.
  5. A bookmark: Look at our move: IS — ISN'T. Remember it. Pair 3 — we open this machine.
📺 On screen: slides with the ex.5 statements, one at a time (Backup: I read them aloud from the book) · I keep the key
👦 Students do: everyone writes T/F on their own board and lifts it on command — EVERYONE answers; they correct false ones with an isn't sentence. Expected: “He isn't 12 — he's 13!”. Typical error: “he not 12” — a gesture at the isn't frame.
💪 TB Reinforcement (p.20): first EVERYONE just decides T/F, and only on the second pass do we fix the false ones — two easy steps instead of one hard one.		mini-boards · slides
43–52′
6 new words from the text (Word work)
Word work round
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: pairs race to match 6 words from the text to their definitions, then a sentence about Russia

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand pairs envelopes: 6 word cards (great·education·diet·bike·subjects·currency) + 6 definitions.
  2. I start: Match! Three minutes!
  3. I check with a choral echo: The money people use? — CURRENCY! — for all 6.
  4. An assignment task: ONE sentence about Russia with any word. Say it to another pair. Example: Our currency is the rouble.
📺 On screen: Word work envelopes for pairs · the key on a slide/the board after the match (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: match word–definition pairs, answer in chorus, make up a sentence about Russia and say it to a neighbouring pair. Expected: “Bikes aren't popular in winter”. Typical error: diet = “diet” (Russian sense) — CCQ: “Only to be thin? No! The food people eat”.

A carousel of questions about our country
Try it · question carousel
mingling

WHAT WE DO: two circles: 6 questions from ex.7 about their own country by the frames, a shift every 40 sec

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I put up the frames: Our currency is… · My favourite subject is… · Bikes are/aren't popular. · A great place is…
  2. I build two circles (inner/outer) facing each other.
  3. I explain: Outer asks question one: What's the currency? Inner answers with a frame. Forty seconds — rotate!
  4. I change the question number on every shift (all 6 from ex.7), music for the rotation.
  5. I walk round the outside, listen for is/are, note down 3 anonymous mistakes for pair 3.
📺 On screen: the frames on the board, large · the current question number on the slide/board · music (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: stand in two circles, ask and answer by the frame, move on the signal — everyone runs through all 6 questions and answers. Expected: full phrases by the frames. Typical error: “Bikes is popular” — I don't interrupt, I note it for the P3 clinic.
✔ Assess: 3 anonymous examples with is/are — fuel for the Pair 3 hook.		frames · music
63–74′
A happiness ranking: sorting 8 cards
Critical thinking · happiness ranking
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: teams rank 8 cards on “what matters more for happiness”, compare top-3s, a class top goes on the wall

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Remember step: One thing similar to Russia? One thing different? Sixty seconds, huddle! — a quick gathering.
  2. I hand out sets of 8 cards (education·family·free time·food·health·money·people·weather).
  3. Evaluate task: To be HAPPY — order them 1 to 8. Four minutes. Everyone touches at least TWO cards!
  4. I give a frame: Our №1 is family because it's important. — teams compare top-3s with their neighbours.
  5. A class-wide vote → I write up the “class happiness top-3” and put it on the wall.
📺 On screen: sets of 8 cards · the frame “Our №1 is… because…” on the board · a “Class top-3” sheet for the wall
👦 Students do: physically sort the cards, everyone moves at least 2; defend their top-3 by the frame. Expected: family/friends at the top, an argument about money. Typical error: arguing in Russian — only frame phrases count (announced in advance).
💪 TB Reinforcement (p.21): support frames on the board: For me, (family) is number 1. · I agree. / I disagree. · (Education) is last on my list.		8-card sets · wall

Writing a verdict: 3 sentences
Build · verdict portion
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: everyone writes a 3-sentence verdict (is it a good place + 2 facts from the hunt), a peer check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I write a frame: The Netherlands is/isn't a good place for teenagers. Fact 1: … Fact 2: …
  2. I say: Three sentences. Facts — from your hunt cards. Five minutes. Timer.
  3. Peer check: Swap. Two boxes: real fact from the text? is/isn't used? Fix and return. +1 for a clean check.
📺 On screen: the verdict frame on the board · a slide with 2 boxes · the hunt cards as the source of facts (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: write 3 sentences leaning on the facts they found, swap and check. Expected: “Fact 1: 95% of teens are happy”. Typical error: a made-up fact — the checker asks “Which text?”, the author looks for the source.

  2. I go back to the morning count: Morning: 7 believers. Now? Did FACTS change your mind?

Workbook U1 · notebook
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can find facts in four short informational texts and report them with simple be sentences: “Amsterdam is the capital. 25% of the country is under the sea!”
  • Subsidiary. Reading subskill: identifying different types of text; Word work — 6 text words; first Critical thinking ritual (Evaluate: order 8 ‘happiness’ words) — with heavy scaffolds.
  • Personal. Never read the page aloud frontally — the quest does the reading. Timer visible in every hunt. Praise fact-finding speed, not translation.
By the end, students can…
  • say what type a text is by its look
  • find a named fact in a text within a time limit
  • match 6 new words to definitions
  • rank ideas and say “№1 is family” with a reason word

Pron. watch
Dutch /dʌtʃ/; euro /ˈjʊərəʊ/; diet /ˈdaɪət/ (2 syllables).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“A fact file — full sentences or words and numbers?” → words and numbers.
“Diet — only when you want to be thin?” → No, diet = the food people eat.
“Currency — money or a shop?” → the money a country uses.

Assumptions: 13 nationalities from P1 are fresh (opening rewind retrieves them). Reading long texts in English is NEW — success = find facts, not understand every word. Say this out loud to lower anxiety.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Panic at a wall of English textRule on slide: “You do NOT need every word. You need THE fact.” Model one hunt yourself, thinking aloud.
Everyone reads text №2 (Thomas) onlyJigsaw: each team member owns ONE station text; the team needs all four to win.
Word work turns into dictionary timeDefinitions matched to BOLD words only — 6 words, cards on desks, 3-minute timer.
Ranking discussion slides into RussianFrame strips: “№1 is… because it's important / it's cool.” Only frame-sentences score.
Fast crews finish hunts earlyEnvelope ‘Fact +’: 2 extra hunts per station (find a number, find a name).
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
