Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read an informational text for gist and detail (“The Netherlands: a good place to be a teenager”) using the subskill identifying different types of text, and to compare and evaluate what makes a country a good place to live.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle countries & nationalities from Pair 1; pre-teach and use word-work lexis (great, education, diet, subjects, currency, bike); use be in reading context; scaffolded speaking about own country with opinion frames; critical thinking — compare (similar/different) and rank (Diamond values).
  • Personal. Keep it safe and unhurried (Foundation): pre-teach lexis before reading, model every subskill with a think-aloud, give an ICQ before each task, and run one clear stirrer (brain-break ~45′) between two settle stages.
By the end, pupils can…
  • look at a text and say what type of information it gives (title / photos / numbers / speech);
  • read for gist (ex 2–4) and for detail (true/false, ex 5);
  • match six word-work words to their meanings;
  • say one thing similar and one different about their own country, and rank what makes people happy.

Reading subskill
Identifying different types of text — before you read, look at titles, photos, maps, numbers & speech to work out what kind of information a text gives you. (SB p.14 subskill box)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Do I read every word to find the type of text?” → No — I look at the title & pictures first.
“diet — is it only about losing weight?” → No → it's the food people usually eat.
“Is Thomas is 12 true?” → No → he's 13 (correct the false one).

Assumptions: countries & nationality adjectives were introduced in Pair 1 (recycled today); be affirmative is met in the reading here but taught fully in Pair 3 → today it is exposure in context, not the grammar focus. Pupils are readers but new to the course → give the subskill and all instructions with a model first. Teams: max 4 (12 = 3×4 · 10 = 3+3+4 · 9 = 3×3 · 8 = 2×4 · 7 = 3+4).

Classroom management — signals
“Class? — Yes!” — you: “Class!” → pupils stop & echo “Yes!”
“Pens down, eyes up” — settle signal before instructions.
Volume control — raise/lower your arm = louder/quieter → settler.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1.”
Think–Pair–Share — 20 sec think, then tell your partner, then we share.
“Freeze!” — mid-movement reset (brain-break).

Recycle Pair 1. Quick fire on the map: Where is this flag from? — It's from Spain. They're Spanish. 5–6 countries/nationalities, whole class, fast.

Hook (What Do You Think?). Write on the board: What makes a country a GOOD place to be a teenager? Hand each pupil two sticky notes. Write ONE idea on each — one word is fine. They stick them on the board.

Word work — SB p.14 ex 6. Match the definitions 1–6 to the words in bold in the text. Pairs scan the text and find the bold words (they were pre-taught, so this confirms & anchors them).

Keys: 1 very good → great · 2 everything connected with school & learning → education · 3 the food people eat → diet · 4 informal word for ‘bicycle' → bike · 5 things you study, e.g. English, maths → subjects · 6 the money used in a country, e.g. dollars → currency.

Critical thinking
teams → board

Remember (Super skills 1). Find ONE thing about life in the Netherlands that is the SAME as your country, and ONE that is DIFFERENT. Frames: It's the same because … / It's different because … Think–Pair–Share; take 3–4 examples.

Evaluate (Super skills 2) — 📓 continues on the insert. To be happy, what things are most important? Rank the eight words: educationfamilyfree timefoodhealthmoneypeoplethe weather. Teams build a Diamond ranking (most important on top). ▶ RallyRobin — each pupil must give one reason: I think health is more important than money because …

One team presents its top three to the class; others agree/disagree with a reason.
