14–22′
Subskill: so vs because
🔥 Subskill · so vs because
board · crews

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.3–4 (find 3 examples of so) + a rule picture + ex.5 rewrite 4 sentences + a mini-round so/because (Extra TB p.93)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.3 30 sec: Why is Danny texting his friends? Because … Key: he wants to see the graffiti artists.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.
  3. A rule picture on the board: REASON → so → RESULT (arrow to the right) and RESULT ← because ← REASON.
  4. Ex.4 hunt: Find THREE examples with ‘so’ in the chats. Ninety seconds! Key: We're studying for our French exam so I can't come. · Mum's at work so I'm looking after my little sister. · We're finishing now so I can be there at 12..
  5. Ex.5 we rewrite 4 sentences (so in place), check down the chain. Key: 1 Jan's dad is from Berlin so he can speak German. · 2 It's raining so we can't go running now. · 3 Today's a holiday so we haven't got school. · 4 I haven't got any money so I can't go to the cinema..
  6. ▶ Mini-round “so or because” (Extra activity TB p.93) on mini-boards: It's my birthday … I've got a lot of presents. (so) · My dad walks to work … we haven't got a car. (because) · We haven't got a pet … we live in a small flat. (because) · My sister is only 16 … she can't drive. (so)
📺 On screen: SB p.80 ex.3–5 · board: the so/because rule picture · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they find so in the text, rewrite sentences, tell so/because apart on mini-boards — EVERYONE answers. Expected: 3–4 out of 4 in the mini-round. Typical error: so at the start of a sentence — a finger on the arrow.

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: ▶ chain (Activities bank TB p.6): everyone completes a result with so; the class streak; then ex.6 in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Chain sentences: I give the start — It's sunny today so … — everyone round the circle adds THEIR OWN result, without repeating. The class streak in the corner of the board.
  2. Three rounds with new starts: I've got an exam tomorrow so… · I can't swim so… · I don't like pizza so…

  4. I note: After ‘so’ we need a subject and a verb — not just a word.
  5. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!

🏁 PRODUCT · live chat theatre
stage

WHAT WE DO: finale: pairs voice their chat like a radio play — line by line; judges count so and present continuous

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1 (everyone at once): pairs join up two by two, read the chat to each other “at chat speed”, the listening pair judges.
  2. Round 2 for the class: 3–4 pairs go “on air”. Read it like you text — fast, real, with emotion. The class counts every ‘so’ on their fingers.
  3. Judges by the rubric + a bonus +2 for a refusal with a reason AND an alternative.
  4. Audience favourite prize: we vote by raised hand for the most believable chat. Gold run = +5.
📺 On screen: judging cards · leaderboard

WB Unit 6 (pp.44–51) · notebook · points to the leaderboard next pair
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write a 6+ line text message dialogue with present continuous, an invitation, a refusal with a reason and at least two correct uses of so.
  • Subsidiary. Text language is decoded and reassembled; the subskill so (result) is separated from because (reason); Key phrases for inviting/refusing (ABCD TB p.93); Quick review p.81 is done as a team quiz, as radar before the test.
  • Personal. Writing for pre-teens goes in portions — three short passes instead of one long one. Quick review isn't “revision”, it's DIAGNOSIS: everyone leaves with a personal list of two targets for the test.
By the end, students can…
  • decode and write text language (R U · C U · missing words)
  • use so to show a result, in the right position
  • invite, refuse politely with a reason and agree a new time
  • self-diagnose gaps from the Quick review page

Target language
Lexis

Structure
Hi. Are you busy? Do you want to meet? · I'm looking after my little sister so I can't come. · We're finishing now so I can be there at 12. · What about tomorrow?
Form
Subskill: so sits BETWEEN two parts; whatever comes AFTER so is the RESULT. Danny likes art SO he wants to go to the graffiti event. (because works the other way round: result first, then reason)
Pron. watch
the natural patter of chat: What R U doing → /wɒtəjuːˈduːɪŋ/; the intonation of an invitation — rising, of a refusal — softly falling.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“It's raining so we can't go — what comes after so, the reason or the result?” → the result.
“Danny likes art because / so he wants to go?” → because — reason; so — result. The order is different!
“C U later — is that a typo?” → no, it's text language: See you later.

Assumptions: Texting is their native environment, so motivation is high, but transferring it into the English format (and the so/because difference) is new. Present continuous is active after pairs 3–4; Quick review will show the real leftovers. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (-ing spelling / missing ‘be’ / question word order), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
