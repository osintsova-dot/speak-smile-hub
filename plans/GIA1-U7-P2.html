📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write and ask past simple be in all forms (was/were · wasn't/weren't · Was…? Yes, she was. · Where were you…?) and match speakers with what they say in a news report (subskill).
  • Subsidiary. Grammar = exactly 45′: gate → rules race → Beatles text → questions accuracy → Wh- rotation → Brain teaser; pron was weak /wəz/ vs strong /wɒz/ (SB p.117). Lesson B: 12 feelings adjectives → straight into reactions → listening battle → product “Time Machine Interview”.
  • Personal. Anti-whine: “you SAY was/were — but can you write the word order in a Wh-question and a short answer clean in 10 seconds?” Written accuracy is today's mission.
By the end, students can…
  • fill was/were and wasn't/weren't with clean agreement
  • build Yes/No and Wh- questions with correct inversion
  • give true short answers without ‘Yes, she wasn't’
  • match three speakers with six statements while listening

Structure
I/He/She/It was · You/We/They were · wasn't / weren't (was not · were not) · (Wh-) + was/were + subject: Was Laika an astronaut? · Were The Beatles from London? · Where were you yesterday? · Yes, she was. / No, they weren't. (short answer WITHOUT the full n't form: NOT ‘Yes, she wasn't’)
Form
there was / there were — the past of there is/are (TB p.100) · in a question the verb comes BEFORE the subject · in a short answer there's no ‘n't’ after Yes.
Pron. watch
was: weak /wəz/ in a question and inside a phrase, strong /wɒz/ in a short answer and under negation — SB p.117 (keys TB p.101) · -ed in bored/tired/relaxed is NOT an extra syllable: bored — 1, relaxed — 2, worried — 2 (TB pp.101–102).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Some gladiators … very popular — was or were?” → WERE (some gladiators = they).
“Was Doug the Pug the first dog in space? — Yes, he wasn't?” → No! Yes, he was. / No, he wasn't.
“Where you were yesterday — is that right?” → No: Where WERE you yesterday?

Assumptions: Spoken was/were has sounded fine since GMF4, but agreement breaks down in writing (there was/were, some gladiators) along with word order in questions. Feelings are partly familiar (tired, hungry), but -ed adjectives as a system and pleased/worried/relaxed are new. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (irregular forms / -ed spelling / was–were agreement), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The “Was/were is easy” whineA 3-minute gate: 6 gaps + 2 questions, in writing. The questions are what wreck the score.
‘Yes, she wasn't’A rule poster: after YES, never n't. A “spot the mistake” round — the error on the board belongs to the class.
A Wh-question with no inversionWord strips on the desks: they physically rearrange WHO / WAS / your best friend — the hand remembers better than the eye.
Listening: three voices blur togetherSubskill head-on: read ALL six phrases first, then listen; on the first pass — only notes and question marks.
Feelings get confused with nounsA frame on the board: I was / I felt + adjective. No “I have afraid” — a yellow card.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
