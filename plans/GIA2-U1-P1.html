Get Involved! A2 · Unit 1 “School life” · Pair 1 — Vocabulary: Daily routines
A2 teenagers (~12–14 y.o., readers) · 90 min · lead-in → presentation → controlled → freer → production → feedback · desks + open space
Week 14.09–20.09 · Pair 5 (lessons 9–10)✨ NEW UNITSB pp.12–13Kagan: RallyCoach · Timed Pair Share · Quiz-Quiz-Trade · Four Corners
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to talk about their daily routine — using ~20 daily-routine collocations with time expressions (at 7:00 · in the morning · on Monday) to say when they do everyday activities.
  • Subsidiary. Reading/listening for gist & detail on school days in four countries (SB p.12 map, p.13 video); comparing your school day with others (critical thinking); present simple in context (not the announced focus — that is Pair 3).
  • Personal. Keep TTT low; ICQ before every task; run a stirrer→settler rhythm with a brain-break at the ~45′ mark; use delayed error correction, not on-the-spot interruptions.
By the end, learners can…
  • name & order ~20 daily-routine phrases across morning / afternoon / evening;
  • say when they do things with at / in / on;
  • compare their school day with a peer's using comparison frames;
  • pitch one feature of their “ideal school” day.

Structure
When / What time do you (get up)? — I (get up) at 7:00 / in the morning / on Monday(s). · I usually… / I always… (frequency, receptive here)
Time prep.
at + clock time (at 7:00) · in + part of day (in the morning) · on + day (on Monday). Present simple I/you — context only.
Pron. watch
chunk stress: get UP · have breakfast; weak have a /həvə/; linking get_up, clean_my; sentence stress on the activity, not on do you.
Academic (Tier 2)

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I get up at 7:00.” — a clock time or a part of the day? → clock time → at.
“In the morning” — one exact minute or a general part of the day? → general part → in.
Hinge: choose the right word — I ___ my teeth after breakfast. (a) make (b) clean (c) do → checks verb–noun collocation before free stage.

Assumptions: Starter is done — learners can use be, this/these/those, countries & nationalities, tell the time and the days of the week. Present simple full paradigm comes in Pair 3, so keep it to I/you here. These are teenagers: no mascot, adult tone, choice & personalisation. Groups: pairs for RallyCoach; teams of 3–4 for the map hunt; whole-class mingle for Quiz-Quiz-Trade.

Likely problemWhat you do
Wrong verb–noun pairing (make my teeth, do my bed)Teach as fixed chunks with one gesture each; drill the pair contrast have / do / make / get.
Confusing at / in / onColour-code on the board: at=clock, in=part of day, on=day. Hold up the CCQ before ex.
Over-using present simple 3rd person too earlyKeep production I/you; if it comes up, acknowledge & park it for Pair 3.

Presentation · daily-routine lexis
board / cards

Tier the words (do this while presenting). Everyday chunks (Tier 1: wake up, get up, have breakfast…) — teach fast with mime. Academic words (Tier 2: routine · ideal · timetable · canteen · packed lunch) — stop, define student-friendly, give a context sentence, flag on the board. These recycle in the speaking stage.

Present & drill. Show each U1 flashcard / mime: What's she doing? Elicit or model the chunk: She's brushing her hair — “brush my hair.” Choral ×2 → 2–3 individuals. Spotlight the tricky collocations: clean my teeth (not “wash”), make my bed, do homework, get changed.

Chunk-stress drill. Back-chain the hard ones: …break → have a break → I have a break at 11. Mark stress with a clap on the activity word. ICQ: Do we stress “do you” or the action? (→ the action).

Glue-in: “MY SCHOOL DAY” day-strip (A5). Hand out the strip — MORNING · AT SCHOOL · AFTER SCHOOL · NIGHT — with the prepositions at 7:00 · in the morning · on Monday. Glue it in — it holds the whole unit.

Steps: ① Pencil your own time next to every action. ② Read your day quietly to yourself, watching at / in / on. ③ Cross out two actions that never happen in your day. Circulate, correct the prepositions in pencil.

56–70′
Freer · compare our days
mingle

SB p.12 ex.3 — comparing. Post the comparison frames (from their notebook strip):
In my day I [get up] at [7:00], but you [get up] at […]. · We're the same — we both […]. · One thing that's different is […].

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (Kagan): each learner has 1 routine card. Mingle → pair → ask What time do you [have dinner]?, answer with at/in/on, react, trade cards, find a new partner. Music off = freeze & pair. 3–4 rounds.

▶ Timed Pair Share: settle into fixed pairs. 40 sec each: What is similar and different in our school days? Push for one similar + one different using the frames.

70–82′
Production · my ideal day
desks → groups

Personalised output. Back to the first question — your ideal school day. Write 4–5 sentences: your real morning, then ONE thing you'd change. Model on the board: I usually get up at 7:00 and start school at 8:30. In my ideal school, we start at 10:00!

▶ Group pitch: in teams of 4, each reads their “one change”; team picks the best idea. One speaker pitches it to the class: In our ideal school, we… Class votes with thumbs. (Everyone writes & speaks; only the pitch is fronted — fast rotation across the lesson.)
