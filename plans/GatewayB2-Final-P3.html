🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Finish the examination: repair the group's own weak spots at four stations built from the error logs, sit the complete Listening paper (four parts, thirty questions, forty minutes, both playings), and run the Speaking test in threes so that every student speaks for a full twelve minutes.
What is genuinely hard
The listening is where B2 candidates lose most, and for two reasons that have nothing to do with hearing. Part 2 asks for ten written answers taken from the recording unchanged, so spelling and word class decide the marks. Part 3 gives eight statements for five speakers, and the three extra ones are built to look right. The speaking difficulty is different and it is organisational: with twelve students you cannot run a fourteen-minute paired test one pair at a time — the room would watch for an hour. Everything runs simultaneously or it does not run.
Typical error to expect
Rushing to agree in Speaking Part 3. The instruction says candidates do NOT have to reach agreement and do NOT have to discuss all five prompts — what is marked is negotiating, turn taking and collaborating. A pair that agrees in forty seconds has thrown away three minutes of assessed speech.
Why the lesson is built this way
The stations come first because they are built from the error logs written last lesson, and doing them while those logs are still warm is the whole point of a reserve lesson. Then the listening, in one unbroken forty-minute block at official timing, because that is the only way it means anything. Marking is immediate and spoken. The speaking test then runs in threes, everywhere at once, with the third student holding the printed examiner's script and the clock — which is the single best way to learn what an examiner is listening for.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Two things today. First you fix the three lines you wrote down last lesson — the stations are built from your own logs, not from my ideas. Then you sit the rest of the exam: the listening in full, forty minutes, each recording twice, and then the speaking test — all of you at the same time, in threes, one of you holding the examiner's script. Nobody watches, everybody speaks.”

WHAT WE DO: the error logs back on the desks and the room deliberately noisy after a silent lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Logs out on the desks before anybody sits down — the three lines each student wrote last lesson. Nothing else is needed for the first twenty minutes.
  2. Kagan Mix-Pair-Share, standing, ninety seconds: Read one of your three lines to your partner and say what you have actually done about it since Tuesday. Swap partners once.

  4. Say the shape of the day in one sentence: Your stations, then the listening paper, then all of you speaking at once.

groups of three, rotating
Why: Stations · built from your own error logs

WHAT WE DO: two six-minute rotations at the four commonest weak lines in the group

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four tables, four lines, everything printed from material the class already owns: the Workbook exercise, the Student's Book reference page and five items written by you from the marked papers.
  2. Station 1 — prepositions after verbs: the Unit 8 set (agree with, believe in, depend on, apologise for, dream of, belong to) plus five items from Part 2 of the paper.
  3. Station 2 — word formation: negative prefixes and plurals, using Part 3 of the marked paper as the diagnosis and Workbook p.95 as the drill.
  4. Station 3 — the passive and reported speech: five transformations of the Part 4 type, written out, with the word given in capitals.
  5. Station 4 — Part 6 reference words: the gapped text from the paper, with the instruction to underline every it, they, this, there in the seven options and say what each one points back to.
  6. Six minutes per station, then one rotation. Each student writes one sentence of their own at each station on a mini-whiteboard; all boards up before the group moves.
  7. You do not teach here — you walk, you listen and you keep the clock. There are thirty-eight minutes of teacher-led work after this and none of it can start late.
📺 On screen: no display — every station is printed material the class already has, laid out before the lesson
👦 Students do: work two stations chosen by their own error log and produce one sentence each at both

Move break · desks into threes
standing, whole room
Why: A stirrer that also recycles language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, and in an examination week that is more true, not less.

WHAT WE DO: four minutes that reorganise the room for the paper and get the blood moving before forty minutes of sitting

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Desks moved into rows for the listening — apart, facing forward. It takes ninety seconds if you say where each row goes.
  2. Then, standing behind their chairs, thirty seconds of shaking out: hands, shoulders, neck. Say why: You are about to sit still for forty minutes and write things you hear. Do it now, not in the middle of Part 2.
  3. One last piece of speaking before the silence, thirty seconds in pairs: Say one thing you fixed at your stations.
  4. Sit. Papers face down. Pens and pencils out.

Listening briefing · four parts, thirty questions, both playings
whole class
Why: Listening briefing · four parts, thirty questions, both playings

WHAT WE DO: the shape of the listening paper, the two habits that save marks, and the rules of the forty minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four lines on the board: Part 1 — eight situations, three options · Part 2 — ten sentences to complete · Part 3 — five speakers, eight statements · Part 4 — one interview, seven questions.
  2. Habit one, for Part 2: Write exactly what you hear. Do not improve it, do not shorten it, do not translate it. If you hear «an audience», you write «an audience».
  3. Habit two, for Parts 1, 3 and 4: read the questions in the pause before each part and try to answer them in your head before you look at the options. The option that repeats what you heard is usually the trap.
  4. The rules of the forty minutes: every recording is played twice, the pauses are on the recording, nobody asks for a third playing, and you write on the question paper as you listen.
  5. Say the one thing that stops a collapse: If you miss one, leave it and listen forward. The recording does not wait and the next answer is already coming.
📺 On screen: the four parts written on the board and left up during the paper — the equipment is checked and running before the class comes in
👦 Students do: read the four-part shape, then read the questions of Part 1 in the pause before it starts

28–68′
🎧 The Listening paper · four parts, forty minutes
individual, silent
Why: 🎧 The Listening paper · four parts, forty minutes

WHAT WE DO: the complete official listening paper, four audio files, every recording twice

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Part 1 (questions 1–8), then Part 2 (9–18), then Part 3 (19–23), then Part 4 (24–30). Four separate files, played in order, with the pauses that are already on the recordings.
  2. You start each file and then say nothing at all. Do not pause between parts to comment; the paper does not.
  3. Between the parts there is a short printed pause for reading the next questions — let it run. Students who use it gain three or four marks over students who look at the ceiling.
  4. Circulate once during Part 2 and check that answers are being written in the gaps, not on the desk. Say nothing.
  5. After Part 4, five minutes of quiet checking. In the real paper this is transfer time; here it is spelling time, and tell them so: Read your ten Part 2 answers again and check every spelling.
  6. Pens down. Papers stay on the desk, face up, for the marking.
📺 On screen: the four audio files, played in order at a volume checked before the lesson. Backup: the printed audioscript is on your desk — if the machine fails, read the remaining parts aloud yourself, twice each, at natural speed, and announce that you are doing so
👦 Students do: answer thirty questions across four parts, using the printed pauses to read ahead and the last five minutes to check spelling

Why: Marking the listening · out loud, immediately

WHAT WE DO: the official key read aloud, marked in red, with two minutes on the part that hurt

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the key at dictation pace. Part 1: 1 A, 2 B, 3 B, 4 C, 5 C, 6 C, 7 A, 8 B. Part 2, and spell the two-word answers: an audience, changes, confidence, strength, practical, rent, powerful, other surfers, safety, money.
  2. Part 3: 19 H, 20 B, 21 D, 22 E, 23 F. Part 4: 24 B, 25 C, 26 C, 27 A, 28 C, 29 B, 30 A.
  3. Totals on the front page, out of 30, written by the student. Nobody announces one.
  4. Kagan Timed Pair Share, ninety seconds: Which part cost you the most, and was it hearing or writing? That distinction is the whole diagnosis of a listening paper.
  5. Add the answer to their error log if it is a fourth line. Most groups add spelling in Part 2 and it is the easiest line in the whole log to fix.
📺 On screen: the key is read aloud, never displayed — everybody marks at the same pace
👦 Students do: mark thirty items, write their own total, and decide whether the loss was hearing or writing

🎯 The Speaking test · in threes, everybody at once
threes, all round the room
Why: 🎯 The Speaking test · in threes, everybody at once

WHAT WE DO: all four parts of the official speaking test run simultaneously, with the third student as examiner

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Groups of three at the tables from the board. Roles: two candidates and one examiner. The examiner holds pages 17–20, the clock and the printed back-up prompts.
  2. Part 1 (2 minutes). The examiner asks four personal questions from the printed list — best friend, free time, favourite place, the Internet. Extended answers with a reason, never one line.
  3. Part 2 (4 minutes). Candidate A compares the two photographs of people doing an activity with friends for one minute, then B answers the thirty-second question. Then the second pair of photographs, the other way round. The examiner times it with a phone and stops them on the minute — being stopped is part of the training.
  4. Part 3 (4 minutes). Both candidates: the five prompts on how to get more students into the school sports club. Two minutes of discussion, then one minute to choose two. Say the rule that changes the mark: You do not have to agree, and you do not have to discuss all five.
  5. Part 4 (2 minutes). Three of the six printed questions, answered together, with the examiner throwing in How about you? Do you agree? whenever somebody dries up.
  6. Then swap the examiner in and run Parts 1 and 2 once more for the student who examined — three minutes, and it is the difference between a lesson where everybody speaks and a lesson where two thirds do.
  7. You listen properly to two groups only, and write five things down: three good, two fixable.
📺 On screen: no display — the speaking pages with the photographs and the prompts are printed, one set per group of three
👦 Students do: take both candidate roles and the examiner's role, and speak for at least eight of the twelve minutes
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains the EGE oral section, task 4 in particular. Answer, then reason, then example — the two-part answer the Cambridge back-up prompts force out of a candidate is exactly what separates a thin response from a full one on the Russian speaking card. The printed prompts are the questions a Russian examiner will not ask you, which is why the habit has to be automatic.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: in the last three minutes, replace the examiner's Part 4 questions with one Russian-format monologue plan: ninety seconds of silent preparation, then ninety seconds of delivery to the other two. It replaces Part 4 and costs no extra time.

Structure
the language of the speaking test, which is what is actually being assessed: Both photos show … but the main difference is … · That's a good point, although … · Shall we say this one, then? · the printed back-up prompts How about you? · Do you agree? · What do you think?
Form
no new form. In the stations, whatever the logs named — in most groups prepositions after verbs, the passive, and word formation with negative prefixes. In the speaking, comparatives and speculation (they might be … it looks as if …), which the group met in Units 4 and 5.
Pron. watch
in listening Part 2 the answers are heard, not read, and three of them are traps for a Russian ear: an audience (two words, and the article is part of the answer), strength (the final cluster), rent against rend. In the speaking, watch the falling intonation on Do you agree? — said flat it sounds like a challenge.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
Part 2 asks for a word or short phrase. The recording says «an audience». Do you write «audience»? → no: write what you hear, including the article. The key says an audience.
In Part 3 there are eight statements and five speakers. How many letters are not used? → three, and they are written to sound right. Read all eight before the recording starts.
In Speaking Part 3 you and your partner disagree after two minutes. Is that a problem? → no. The instruction says it is not necessary to agree — what is marked is how you discuss it.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The audio fails, and forty minutes of the lesson with itTest it in the morning, not at the start of the lesson, and have the audioscript printed. If it dies mid-paper, read the remaining parts aloud yourself at natural speed, twice, and say out loud that you are doing exactly that — the task does not change, only the voice does.
Students want the recording a third timeNo, and say why once: Twice is what the exam gives you. A third playing tells us nothing we do not already know. The script comes out afterwards, and that is when the third pass happens, with eyes instead of ears.
The threes turn into two students talking and one watchingThe third student is not a spectator: they hold the script, keep the clock, and give the printed back-up prompt when a partner dries up. Rotate the role after Part 2 so that everybody examines once and speaks twice.
Twelve minutes is not the official fourteenIt is not, and say so honestly rather than pretending. Parts 1, 2 and 3 run in full; Part 4 runs for two minutes instead of four, with three of the six questions. Everything else is the real test at real length, and no student is left watching.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 1–9 (the listening section) and task 4 of the oral paper.

The Cambridge listening paper is the closest thing in the course to the Russian listening section: Part 1 is eight three-option items exactly like tasks 3–9, Part 3 is matching five speakers to a list of eight, which is task 2 with letters. The speaking half trains the two habits the Russian oral rewards most — a two-part answer (answer, then reason or example) and keeping going when the first idea runs out. Everything else in the lesson is built from the group's own error log, so its exam content is whatever they actually got wrong.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
