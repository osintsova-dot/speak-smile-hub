Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /ɔː/ sound spelt aw and au (Paul can draw with his claws · Saul drinks sauce), talk about dinosaur fossils in Canada, and read & plan an encyclopaedia entry — three mini-blocks, one “prehistoric” thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that aw and au both spell /ɔː/ (recognise → say → sort, next to or & al); culture — dinosaur fossils in Canada (Dinosaur Provincial Park · Writing-on-Stone) + Channel 21 museums video, 21C digital skills: exploring virtual museums; literacy — text type an encyclopaedia entry, facts vs opinions, extreme adjectives (enormous · tiny), plan & write an entry.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (chant/action bridges); the Paul-the-parrot twister is play; cap each block hard so the encyclopaedia entry actually gets planned.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the /ɔː/ twister and sort aw / au words;
  • say 2–3 facts about Canada's dinosaur fossils;
  • find the parts of an encyclopaedia entry and plan one.

Structure
Paul can draw with his claws and Saul drinks sauce with his paws.  ·  Canada is famous for its dinosaur fossils.  ·  entry: The tyrannosaurus was an enormous carnivore. It had 60 sharp teeth.
Form
/ɔː/ = aw (draw, claws, paws) AND au (Paul, sauce, Saul) — (also or: horse · al: walk). Encyclopaedia entry: facts (not opinions), labelled diagram, extreme adjectives — enormous = very big, tiny = very small (no “very” with these).
Pron. watch
/ɔː/ clean in draw/claws/Paul/sauce; palaeontologist /ˌpæliɒnˈtɒlədʒɪst/ (chunk it); herbivore /ˈhɜːbɪvɔː/ · carnivore /ˈkɑːnɪvɔː/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“claws and sauce — same /ɔː/ sound? Same letters?” → same sound, different spelling (aw vs au)!
“An encyclopaedia entry — FACTS or someone's opinion?” → facts (true information).

Assumptions: Prehistoric objects, animals, past questions & could are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from GMF3 phonics is enough to enjoy the aw / au noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short encyclopaedia texts with support.

Block 1 · Phonics /ɔː/ (aw · au)
carpet → desks

Speak and spell (act. 1, CD2 06). PB p.33: Paul the parrot can draw with his claws. Saul the squirrel drinks sauce with his paws. Remember! horse · walk. Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort the /ɔː/ spellings. Hoops/columns: aw (draw, claws, paws) · au (Paul, sauce, Saul) · (spares: or — horse · al — walk). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card.

Notice the rule. Claws and sauce — same sound /ɔː/, different letters! Both say “or”. (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more /ɔː/ word to a hoop (yawn, autumn).

✔ Assess: who still can't hear /ɔː/? Note them — extra RallyRead next unit.

39–52′
Block 3 · Literacy · an encyclopaedia entry
carpet → desks, pairs

Read an encyclopaedia entry (PB p.34, act. 1–2, CD2 08). «Dinosaurs»: What do you want to know? What…? When…? How…? Read/listen; find facts about tyrannosaurus (12 m long, could run fast, 60 teeth like bananas), diplodocus (long neck, couldn't run fast), triceratops (three horns).

▶ Fact scavenger hunt. Race in pairs on PB pp.34–35: Which dinosaur had 60 teeth? How long was the tyrannosaurus? Facts or opinions? First hands up read the answer aloud — everyone scans.

Writing tip · extreme adjectives (PB p.35). We don't say “very enormous”! Enormous already means very big. Tiny means very small. Chorus: enormous ✋ (big arms) · tiny 🤏 (tiny fingers).

▶ Plan an entry (Cooperative learning, AB p.32 format). Pairs choose a dinosaur & make notes: Name · When did it live? · Herbivore or carnivore? · What did it have? · What could it do? 2 pairs share one fact aloud.

💪 Support: fill the plan template with word support. ⭐ Extension: write full sentences with an extreme adjective (enormous · tiny).
