8–13′
Gate: the lie detector
🎯 GATE · lie detector
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 6 loud claims — written T/F BEFORE reading, slips face down; we check them with facts after the raid

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I show 6 claims: You need 100 litres of water for one burger · 375 million people are vegetarian · Meatless Mondays exist in 10 countries · Farm animals produce more gas than all cars and planes · Vegans eat fish · Meat production uses 33% of the planet's land.
  2. I say: Commit: T or F, in writing, NOW. You can't check yet — that's the point. A judge's guess is worthless without the file. 2 min.
  3. Slips face down until the raid. (Key for me: F(1,600!)/T/F(40)/T/F(fun facts box)/T.)
📺 On screen: slide with the 6 claims (or in a column on the board) · slips (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they commit their guesses in writing and turn the slips over. Expected: 3–4 “get caught” by plausible fakes (100 litres sounds logical). Typical error: peeking — “Closed books! Guessing is the point!”

WHAT WE DO: the infographic in 60 sec (what is NOT mentioned) + general idea: paragraphs↔questions in 3 minutes by the subskill

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The infographic (ex.3): Sixty seconds, look at page 39 — which subject from the box is NOT there: food, health, meat, sport? (sport) +1.

21–34′
Challenge: the timed fact raid across 4 stations
🔥 Challenge · timed fact raid (jigsaw)
4 stations

WHAT WE DO: everyone raids THEIR OWN station (paragraphs 1–3 + the infographic), a 4-min cap; report by the 5-second rule; we open the lie detector

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 4 stations set up before the lesson: P1 · P2 · P3 · infographic. I hand out hunt cards (3 facts per crew member: numbers, names, %).
  2. ICQ: Copy sentences? — No! Short answers: 1,600 litres. 40 countries. Meghan.
  3. I start it: Four minutes cap — RAID!
  4. Return: report to the crew by the 5-second rule (Water for one burger? — …five seconds!), a crew sheet for 10 facts.
  5. We open the detector slips: Verify — WITH the source: False! Paragraph 2 says 1,600 litres! +1 for a correction with a source. I also check ex.5–6 orally against the key (1a·2b·3b · 1d·2a·3b·4c).
📺 On screen: 4 stations (pieces of the text ARE POSTED UP, books closed — copyright: the text comes only from the SB) · hunt cards · crew sheets · timer
👦 Students do: they raid the stations one at a time, report against the timer, check their guesses against the facts and cite the source. Expected: “My guess was wrong — 1,600 litres!”. Typical error: a fact without a source — no point given, the evidence rule.
⭐ For the fast ones: the “Fact+” envelope — a number, a name and a % nobody has used yet.		stations · hunt cards · timer

A duel on 6 words from the text
Word work duel
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: Numbered Heads Together: partners split 6 definitions in half and quiz each other by the 5-second rule; we fix sentences in ex.8

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I split the roles: Partner A owns definitions 1–3, B owns 4–6. Find your bold words in the text — two minutes. (planet · less · production · promise · environment · gas)
  2. I run the check as Kagan Numbered Heads Together: numbers 1–4 in the crew, Heads together — make sure EVERYONE in your crew can answer! → I call a number — exactly that person answers, no prompting from the floor.
  3. Duel: Test each other. Five-second rule! The opposite of ‘more’? — Less!
  4. Blitz ex.8 on mini-boards: the wrong word in a sentence → we write the right one. 4 rounds out of 6 (TB key: promise·less·gas·planet·production·environment).
  5. Final: in pairs — a sentence about our school/city with any of the words; the crew's best one out loud.
📺 On screen: the SB p.38 ex.7–8 · timer · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they own their trio of words, quiz their partner, flip ex.8 on the boards at the same time. Expected: “Our school has got a promise…”. Typical error: they confuse promise as a verb/noun — a gesture card “I promise = a VERB”.

51–60′
Building arguments FOR meat and AGAINST
Apply & Analyse · building arguments
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: teams gather ammunition for the debate: 3 arguments FOR meat (Apply) + reasons “why people don't eat X” (Analyse)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Remember step (60 sec, huddle): Why is a vegetarian diet a good idea — three reasons from the FILE.
  2. Apply: Now the other side: good reasons FOR eating meat. Three ideas, two minutes. TB prompts on the board: health · jobs · family and friends.
  3. Analyse: Why else do people not eat a type of food? (religion · allergy · price — I accept the ideas, recast into English.)
  4. I charge them up: Your lists = your ammunition. In the debate, a claim without a fact loses the round.
📺 On screen: board: 3 columns (FOR meat-free · FOR meat · other reasons) · prompts health/jobs/family
👦 Students do: they gather arguments from both sides, knowing it's ammunition for the debate. Expected: columns of 3–4 points. Typical error: a Russian argument — I accept the idea, the point counts only for the English version.

Product: verdict debates in trios
🏁 PRODUCT · verdict debates in trios
trio circles

WHAT WE DO: trios: prosecutor against defender (90 sec each), the judge waits for 3 facts; roles rotate 3 times; the final — a class vote

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out roles and cards: Prosecutor (Meat-free Mondays are wrong because…) · Defender (They're right — paragraph 2 says…) · Judge (card: ☐ 3 facts? ☐ answered the opponent? verdict).
  2. Regulations: 90 sec per side + 30 sec for the verdict. Facts — ONLY from the crew sheets; a made-up fact = a point to the opponent.
  3. I rotate the roles ×3 — everyone tries all of them.
  4. Final: the class's personal vote Meat-free Monday at OUR school — thumbs? — now their own opinion is allowed.
  5. Judging points go to the leaderboard, the gold-run debaters are announced.
📺 On screen: role cards · judging cards · crew fact sheets
👦 Students do: they debate by the regulations, judge by the card, swap roles, and vote personally at the end. Expected: quotes like “paragraph 3 says 40 countries!”. Typical error: emotion instead of facts — the judge doesn't tick the box, the side loses silently.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — clean forms (1–3) · Richness — quantifiers, connectors, bonus structures (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		role cards · judge cards

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can get the general idea of a news story fast, mine it for facts and deliver a 60-sec verdict in a trio debate, supported by 3 sourced facts.
  • Subsidiary. Reading subskill (understanding the general idea) applied under a 3-minute cap; Word work as a duel; Critical thinking Remember/Apply/Analyse = debate ammunition; GMF4 bonus: interview a vegan with Have you ever…?
  • Personal. Reading is where continuers actually stretch — the text is new even if grammar isn't. Keep hunts timed and competitive; debates get frames + judge cards, not “discuss it”.
By the end, students can…
  • commit predictions and verify them in the text
  • match paragraphs to questions in 3 minutes
  • define/use 6 Word-work items
  • argue a verdict with sourced facts and win a trio debate

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Judges read every word first?” → No — general idea first, facts second (the subskill).
“A claim without a fact — does it win the case?” → Never.
“Have you ever…? — which tense, GMF4 grads?” → Present perfect. Not in this book. In you — yes.

Assumptions: Food set fresh from P1 (rewind retrieves it). The Meatless-Mondays content and Word-work items are genuinely new. Debate format needs frames + judge cards. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“Reading is boring” resistanceNever ‘read the text’ — always ‘crack the case in 3 minutes’. The stopwatch reframes everything.
Scanning = random shoutingEvidence rule: answers must include WHERE — “paragraph 2 says…” — +1 only with the source.
Debate collapses into RussianFrame strips + rule: only English sentences count as arguments.
Vegans/meat-eaters get personalRoles are assigned by card, NOT by real opinion — “you're the advocate, not you”; real opinions only in the final vote.
Fast crews idle after huntsEnvelope «Fact+»: find a number, a name and a % nobody has used yet (TB Fast finishers style).
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
