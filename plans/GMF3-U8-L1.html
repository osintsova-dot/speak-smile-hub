Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name 12 types of TV programme and talk about what they watch: “I like watching (cartoons). / What's on Channel 2 at five o'clock? — A (talk show).”
  • Subsidiary. Sing «The TV song» (CD3 45) & the vocabulary chant (CD3 44); brainstorm programmes they watch (Learning to learn); preview the Past-Simple question of L2 — “Did you watch (a cartoon) yesterday? — Yes, I did.”; early literacy — read the programme words on a TV guide (AB).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; stirrer→settler rhythm — the mime game is high-energy, plan the freezes. Insist on the article: a cartoon, a quiz show (but the news, the weather!).
By the end, pupils can…
  • name 12 types of TV programme;
  • say “I like / love / don't like watching …”;
  • read the programme words and match word ↔ picture on a TV guide.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“A cartoon — is it for children or the football results?” → cartoons, for fun!
“the news — do I say a news or the news?” → the news (also the weather).

Assumptions: Pupils have just finished Unit 7 (Detectives — Past Simple was/were). They know watch DVDs / play computer games from Unit 2 free-time. The 12 programmes are largely NEW; the Did you watch…? question is previewed only — full drill is L2. Readers use word cards alongside pictures.

Presentation · 12 programmes
carpet → board

Present with flashcards + vocabulary chant (act. 2, CD3 44). Each card: What's this? It's… a cartoon! Choral ×3 + one gesture (cartoon = drawn, the news = read a paper, the weather = point sun/rain, sports programme = kick). Contrast pair: CARTOON — COMEDY!

Article sort. Board: watch a … (cartoon, quiz show, film) vs the news / the weather. Hold each card — class calls the little word (a or the) BEFORE you place it.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: Stand up if you like watching… cartoons! — flash cards fast, everyone moves every time.

💪 Support: answer with the gesture + word. ⭐ Extension: full sentence — I love watching sports programmes.

A5 glue-in note — 12 TV programme types as picture words + a LOVE / LIKE / DON'T LIKE + watching + programme panel + a preference frame (Do you prefer cartoons or films? — I prefer cartoons.)
  • Trim and glue the note in under today's date.
  • Play the song: pupils point along the programme types as they hear them.
  • Second play — they mark each programme ♥ love / ✓ like / ✗ don't like for themselves.
  • Drill the trap off the note: I like WATCHING cartoons — the -ing is printed, point at it.
  • Pairs use the preference frame straight off the note; the map is the word bank for the 44–52′ “My TV guide” stage.

28–36′
Word ↔ picture · Memory
carpet, pairs

▶ Memory · I like watching… — in pairs. Each pair: 6 picture cards + 6 WORD cards (readers!) face-down in two rows. Turn one of each; a match is claimed only with a sentence: I like watching cartoons! Wrong / no article → cards go back. Most pairs wins.

ICQ: Can you keep the cards with no sentence? (→ no — say “I like watching…”!)

✔ Assess: stand behind one pair at a time — a/the automatic yet?

 6+6 cards per pair
36–44′
Mime & guess the programme
carpet, 2 teams

▶ Slow-motion TV mime. One player mimes what you'd SEE on a programme (reading headlines = the news, laughing = comedy, kicking = sports programme); their team guesses together: It's the news! Mimer confirms. Point per correct guess; rotate the mimer every round — everyone mimes once.

Whole team guesses together — no solo shouting: One voice, one team!

Write (notebooks / AB p.80 format). Frames from the board: I like watching ___. I love watching ___. I don't like watching ___. Each child writes 3 true sentences, copying programme words. Fast finishers add a 4th & 5th.
