🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Retrieve and repair everything from Units 3 and 4 — sports collocations, person nouns, the two present tenses, weather words, past simple, used to and the phrasal verbs — using the book's own Review 1 pages, and then produce it under test conditions in the two official Cambridge unit tests.
What is genuinely hard
Two spelling minefields meet on the same paper: person nouns (-er against -or) and used to after didn't. Both are invisible to the ear and both are worth marks. The other risk is complacency — the present tenses feel easy until eight items in a row demand a decision.
Typical error to expect
«sailer», «competiter», «footballor» — -er and -or are the same sound, so only the eye chooses. Word formation is pure spelling marks in the exam.
«I didn't used to like pizza», «I was used to play football» — after didn't the form is USE to, and used to never takes be. The d disappears in speech, which is exactly why it survives in writing.
Why the lesson is built this way
Review 1 in the book covers Units 1–4, and this block closes Unit 4 — so for the first time the whole spread is legally ours. It is used as station material rather than as a worksheet, which keeps everybody writing. Then the official Cambridge review game, which is the only stage where the block's language is used purely for fun and where the used to squares finally come into their own. The second half is the real paper, briefed honestly, with the two spelling ghosts named aloud before the papers turn over.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty-five minutes to get it all back — and today the Review pages at the back of the unit are finally legal, because we've just finished Unit 4. Then forty-five minutes to show what stuck.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of retrieval with a spelling penalty, and the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what's the best thing about today so far? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the block in twelve words. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs: one pen, one paper, ninety seconds — write twelve words from Units 3 and 4, taking turns, spelling each one aloud as you write.
  3. Swap papers with the pair behind and mark. One point per word, minus one for a spelling error — announced before they start, because that is exactly how the paper works.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): name the day honestly: First half: get everything back, including the Review pages we've never opened. Second half: show it.
📺 On screen: the two word walls, still up from the whole block — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write in turns, then mark someone else's list against the wall

WHAT WE DO: what is on the two papers, how long they take, and the two spelling ghosts

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Put the day's four blocks and the clock on the board so the class sees the whole 45 minutes at once.
  2. Brief the tests specifically, from the specification table in this plan: Unit 3 — the nine collocations, the person nouns, the two present tenses, and writing questions for given answers. Unit 4 — weather words, phrasal verb particles, irregular past forms and used to in all three shapes.
  3. The two ghosts, named now and not after: After didn't and Did, it's USE to — no d. And sailor ends in -or while climber ends in -er. Those two things are worth about ten marks today.
  4. Explain why Review 1 appears today: It covers Units one to four. We've just finished Unit four, so today it's finally ours.
  5. Promise the keys: Papers back next lesson with the answers.

WHAT WE DO: sports pairs, person nouns and weather words retrieved on paper, at speed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Review 1 (SB p.30–31) covers Units 1–4 — and this block closes Unit 4, so today the whole spread is finally legal. It is used as one of the stations rather than as a worksheet: the vocabulary exercises for the word station, the grammar exercises for the grammar station.
  2. Three tables, four minutes each, groups rotate on your signal.
  3. Table A — sports pairs: nine nouns, write the verb that goes with each. Table B — person nouns: twelve bases, write the person noun with the right ending. Table C — weather: the vocabulary exercises from Review 1 (SB p.30) plus five definitions from SB p.24.
  4. One rule at every table: Whole word, correct spelling, or it doesn't count. Each group checks the previous group's sheet before writing its own.
  5. Fast finishers at every table write two extra items for the next group.
📺 On screen: three prepared A4 sheets plus the open Review pages — printed before the lesson, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write, rotate, mark. Every student writes about forty words in twelve minutes

WHAT WE DO: the official Cambridge review game, now with the whole grid in play

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it up in ninety seconds: «I think you're lying!» — PREPARE_5_Review_game_Units_01-04.pdf. Groups of three to five, one game sheet per group, one dice. A player rolls twice (column, then row), reads the sentence in that square and says it as TRUE or FALSE about themselves, adding one detail. The others guess. One point for guessing right, one point to the speaker for every person fooled. Versions: Explain (the extra detail is compulsory) · Ask me! (others may ask one question first) · Listen! (only the speaker sees the sheet).
  2. Announce the coverage: today the whole grid is playable — it was written for Units 1–4 and the block finishes Unit 4. The squares that carry this block's language are: I entered a tournament last year · I won a match last week · I play several sports · I'm keen on joining a new sports club · I don't think beating the other team is the most important thing · I like taking part in competitions · I want to see a tornado in real life · My dad used to work in a shop · I used to have a different bedroom · I used to live in a different street · I didn't use to like pizza. Start with those and let the Unit 1–2 squares come round naturally
  3. Play the Ask me! version today: before guessing, the others may ask one question — which forces the speaker to invent detail in the past tense, on the spot.
  4. Circulate with the scoreboard on the board. Two minutes before the end, stop and take the best lie from each group; the class votes on the most convincing.
📺 On screen: one game sheet per group plus a dice. Backup: if you have no dice, use the rule sheet's own «No dice?» version — a neighbour chooses the square
👦 Students do: play in small groups; everyone speaks every round because everyone guesses as well as speaks

WHAT WE DO: the two-minute ritual that starts a test calmly

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out both papers face down, Unit 3 on top.
  2. Read the rubric of section one aloud yourself — thirty seconds that prevent four marks lost to a misread instruction.
  3. State the two rules: Silence. If you finish, check your spelling — you don't hand in early.
  4. One breath together, then: Turn them over.

WHAT WE DO: Unit 3 Standard and Unit 4 Standard, about 35 minutes with checking time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silence, and you sit down. Do not walk the rows for the first ten minutes — it makes nervous students slower.
  2. Answer only procedural questions, never linguistic ones.
  3. Write the remaining time on the board at 15 minutes and at 5 minutes. Say nothing else.
  4. Stronger students who asked for the Plus versions sit those instead — the sections are identical, so the timing does not change.
  5. At minute 30, one sentence: Five minutes. Spend them on the spelling in the vocabulary sections.
📺 On screen: the finish time and the two time-checks on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Review 1 turns into forty minutes of book workIt is cut into station material: the vocabulary exercises at one table, the grammar exercises at another, four minutes each, groups rotating. Nobody works through the spread page by page.
The strong students finish every station earlyEvery station has a fast-finisher line: write two more items of the same type for the next group. Their items get used, which is why they write them properly.
Test nervesThe briefing is specific and calm: sections named, minutes named, keys promised for the next lesson. Then the ritual — pens out, papers face down, one breath, start together.
Someone finishes the paper in fifteen minutesSaid before the papers turn over: If you finish, check every spelling in the vocabulary sections, then sit quietly. You do not hand in early.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The block test is built the way the exam block is: produce the form, spell it right. A footballer with one l, or a used to with the d kept after didn't, scores nothing at all — which is why the briefing names both ghosts aloud before the papers go face down.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
