🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Report what somebody said using the backshift — present → past, past and present perfect → past perfect, will → would, can → could — and change the pronouns and possessives that go with it.
What is genuinely hard
Russian does not shift the tense at all: «он сказал, что он устал» keeps the present. So «He said he IS tired» is not carelessness, it is the Russian sentence with English words. The second half of the difficulty is invisible to students — the pronouns and possessives move too (I → he, my → his), and half the class will shift the verb perfectly and leave the pronoun behind.
Typical error to expect
«He said he is tired», «she said she will help» — Russian does not shift the tense in reported speech, so the present survives into an English past sentence. Will → would and can → could go the same way, and both are marked.
«He said me», «she told that» — tell takes a person (tell somebody that), say does not (say that / say TO somebody). One Russian verb, two English patterns, and the test has it as a whole item.
Why the lesson is built this way
The examples are the three quotations the class circled at the end of the last lesson, from the interview with Evan Hara — so the lesson opens by cashing in a plant. The rule is derived by matching direct to reported rather than by being told, then built into a table on the board that stays up: five arrows, present to past, past to past perfect, will to would, can to could, and the pronoun line underneath. Practice runs from recognition to production, and the error-correction exercise is diagnostic — students say whether the problem is the verb, the pronoun or the modal. The productive stage is the book's own and it is perfect: interview a partner, then report them to the class, who check the verbs and pronouns as they listen.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Last lesson you circled three sentences in quotation marks. Today you learn how to say them without the quotation marks — which is what you do every time you tell somebody what somebody else said. Five arrows, and one thing Russian never does.”

WHAT WE DO: the last thing somebody said to them, then the arrows table

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: the matching, then the rule completed and checked against the arrows

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 as a whole class: read the four reported sentences and the class calls out the speech bubble letter. the direct speech matched to the reported: a 3 · b 2 · c 4 · d 1
  2. For each pair, ask ONE question before moving on: what changed? The class names the arrow.
  3. ex.2 individually, three minutes: the four-part rule. Then compare in pairs. the rule: a we usually CHANGE the verb tense · b one tense BACK INTO THE PAST · c we sometimes change the PRONOUNS · d will becomes WOULD and can becomes could
  4. Now go back to the two student sentences from the warmer and let the CLASS repair them, naming the arrow each time. This is the minute the lesson turns on.
  5. Add the say / tell line to the board, because the test has it as an item: tell SOMEBODY something · say something (to somebody). And note that that is optional in both.
  6. Grammar reference is SB p.162; the answer key is TB p.267.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: six sentences where only the verb has to move

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.3 individually, four minutes: six items, each one a single arrow. 1 liked / likes · 2 was making · 3 had made · 4 had made · 5 would make · 6 could
  2. Kagan RallyCoach, three minutes: partner A says the answer AND names the arrow, partner B checks against the board, then they swap.

  4. Drill the contracted forms aloud, thirty seconds, because they are why students do not hear the shift: he'd made · they'd seen · she'd help.

Move break · say it back
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer where every student reports somebody in the room

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands in two lines facing each other, A and B.
  2. On your signal, every A says a short sentence about themselves to their partner: I can play the guitar. · I'll watch a film tonight. · I've seen that film twice.
  3. Line B steps one place to the right and reports the previous partner to their new one: He said he could play the guitar.
  4. Three rotations, then swap roles. Everybody speaks in every round — nobody watches.
  5. Freeze and sit: Exercise four. Six sentences, and the pronouns move as well as the verbs.
📺 On screen: no display at all — two lines of students and the arrows table on the board behind them
👦 Students do: every student produces three reported sentences and hears three, in rotation

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: the productive stage, where the possessives move too

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Do item 1 on the board with the class and make the pronoun shift visible: 'I really enjoyed the performance,' my dad said → my dad said HE had really enjoyed it.
  2. ex.4 individually, five minutes. Mixed ability: weaker students choose three or four of the six, as the Teacher's Book suggests — better three correct than six half-done.
  3. Compare in pairs, then check on the board in WRITING, one student per item: 1 My dad said (that) he had really enjoyed the performance · 2 Anna's mum said (that) she was making her favourite dinner · 3 Maria said (that) her parents hadn't visited England · 4 Paul said (that) it had been his first trip on a plane · 5 Tom said (that) he would do his homework after dinner · 6 Jason told his friends (that) he could ride his bike with no hands
  4. At each check ask for the PRONOUN first and the verb second. Item 2 is the one that catches people: her favourite dinner in the original, and it stays her — because it is still Anna's mum's.
  5. Item 6 gets its own ten seconds because it uses told: Jason TOLD HIS FRIENDS that… — tell needs the person.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: five errors, each one diagnosed by type

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. First item on the board together, and the diagnosis is the point: I told him that my brother isn't at home — is the problem the verb, the pronoun or the modal? (The verb.)
  2. Mixed-ability pairs, four minutes, for items 2–5. Every correction comes with its diagnosis.
  3. Check by reading the whole corrected sentence aloud: the five corrections, and each one is a different kind of error: 1 …that my brother WASN'T at home (verb) · 2 …if I WANTED to go (verb) · 3 …that I HAD TO buy (modal) · 4 …if we WERE going (verb) · 5 …she WOULD help pay (will → would)
  4. Item 5 is the one worth stopping on — will → would — because it is the arrow the exam tests most often and the one students skip when they are hurrying.
  5. Two minutes: everybody writes their own wrong sentence, swaps with a neighbour, and repairs it. This is the cheapest error-correction exercise there is and it works.
📺 On screen: the five corrections with their one-word diagnoses on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: correct and diagnose five errors in pairs, then write and repair each other's

WHAT WE DO: ex.6–7: a real interview, then reporting a person who is in the room

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.6, thirty seconds: everybody chooses ONE topic from the four — favourite film, favourite food, favourite app, weekend activities — and writes three key words.
  2. Same-ability pairs. Three minutes each way: one talks, the other takes NOTES, not sentences. Notes are the point — you cannot report from a transcript.
  3. Four minutes to turn the notes into four reported sentences, in writing, arrows table visible.
  4. ex.7, seven minutes: volunteers report their partner to the class. The class has a job while listening — check the verbs, the pronouns and the possessives, and put a hand up only AFTER the speaker has finished.
  5. The reported person gets the right of reply in one sentence: Is that what you said? That is the moment accuracy starts to matter to them.
  6. Kagan RallyRobin to close, 60 seconds: Say one thing somebody in your family said to you this week.
  7. Deferred correction: five errors from your monitoring, unattributed, repaired by the class with the arrow named.

Form

Pron. watch
the film words move their stress when they change class and the class will keep it still: DIRector but diRECT · PERformance but perFORM · REcording (noun) against reCORDing (verb) · ANimated has four syllables and the stress on the first · and in reported speech the backshifted forms are unstressed and swallowed — he'd, they'd, she'd — which is why students do not hear the shift in the recording
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«He said he is tired» — what is wrong? → the tense did not move. Say it properly: he said he WAS tired.
«She said me she was ready» — what is wrong? → say does not take a person. Either she TOLD me, or she said TO me.
«'I'll help you,' he said» → he said he ___ help me. → would. And can? → could. Those two are the ones the exam counts.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The tense does not move — the Russian sentence in English wordsThe arrows table is the fix, and it is physical: every reported sentence is said while pointing at the arrow that was used. Ten seconds each time, all lesson.
The verb shifts and the pronoun does notThe pronoun line goes under the arrows in a different colour, and at every check you ask for the pronoun BEFORE the verb. «Whose free time? His.»
say and tell used interchangeablyTwo patterns on the board, one line each, and the test has this as a whole item: tell SOMEBODY something · say something (to somebody).
Reporting sounds robotic and nobody believes it is usefulThe final stage is real: they interview each other about their own favourite film, food, app or weekend, then report a person who is sitting in the room and can object. That is what makes the accuracy matter.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text) and the oral tasks, where reporting somebody else's words is unavoidable.

Reported speech is not a named line of the Russian codifier, but its machinery is everywhere in it: backshift into the past, will → would, can → could, and the pronoun shift. Those are precisely the forms tested in the grammar-in-a-text section, and they are also what a candidate needs in task 38 to report what the data says. A student who cannot backshift writes «the survey says teenagers prefer» in a past-tense paragraph.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
