🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Systematise Unit 2 — past simple and continuous, used to and would, the two past perfects, forty words of work and ten phrasal verbs — and demonstrate it under test conditions: Test yourself, six exercises, 40 points.
What is genuinely hard
The three grammar points of this unit look alike on the page and are separated only by meaning, which is exactly what a test with no labels exposes. The vocabulary difficulty is different: the definitions exercise asks the students to WRITE English, not to recognise it, and that is where the time goes.
Typical error to expect
«I would be shy when I was small», «I would have a cat» — would works for repeated past ACTIONS only, never for past states. Used to covers both, which is why it is the safer choice under exam pressure.
«I used to live there for ten years» — used to cannot take a stated duration. With a period, the past simple: I lived there for ten years.
«set on a company», «turn off an offer», «fill up a form» — the verb is right and the particle is wrong, which in the lexical section scores exactly the same as writing nothing.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first forty minutes are revision that does not look like a test: the Check it page is answered with the book covered, and the vocabulary comes back through Quiz-Quiz-Trade and Showdown, where everybody writes at the same moment. Definitions are rehearsed out loud because exercise 1 of the vocabulary test asks for six of them and students who have never said one in English freeze. Five minutes before the paper the three ghosts are named — would for a state, used to with a duration, the wrong particle — and then it is thirty-five quiet minutes.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Three weeks of work, forty points, thirty-five minutes. First we put the whole unit in one place — then you show me. And you will know your grammar score before you leave the room.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, the stories collected, one sentence of each read aloud, then straight into revision

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. One question each at the door, one full sentence back: One sentence — what did you do at the weekend? Any content counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — the stories come in. Collect them by name at the door and look at each for five seconds — you are looking for underlined structures, nothing else.
  3. 1 minute — one line each. Think-Pair-Share: read your partner the best sentence from your story. One sentence, no context, no apology.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Page twenty-eight. Three weeks of work on two pages — and in forty minutes it is on page twenty-nine with your name on it.
📺 On screen: nothing on screen — paper only. Backup: if a student has no story, they hand it in next lesson and it costs them nothing today; do not spend lesson time on it.

Why: A reference page that is read is forgotten; the same page answered from memory is revision that moves something.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.28: the reference page answered from memory, then checked

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Cover the page. I ask, you answer, then uncover and check.
  2. Six questions: which tense for the scene? · which for the event? · what usually follows «while»? · where does the comma go? · what happens to the comma when the while-clause comes second? · what is the form of the past continuous?
  3. RallyCoach, three minutes: A gives a sentence half (While I was cooking …), B finishes it with a past simple event. Swap after ninety seconds.
  4. Board the three sentences the test will echo: She got up and looked out of the window. It was raining. As she was looking out, she heard her phone ring.
📺 On screen: SB p.28 on the board app, revealed one box at a time; otherwise the cover-and-answer routine. Backup: the page is in their hands.
👦 Students do: answer six questions with the page covered, then build sentences in pairs

Check it · past habits and the past perfect
desks
Why: Fast oral judgement of correct/incorrect is the closest practice to the actual test format, and it involves every student in every item.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.28: the two restrictions and the two perfects

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Same routine, page covered. Five questions: what can used to do that would cannot? · what can used to NOT do? · what is the form of the past perfect simple? and the continuous? · which of the two focuses on duration?
  2. Then eight fast oral items — you say a sentence, they call CORRECT or WRONG and fix it: «I used to live there for ten years» ✘ · «She would visit her grandmother every summer» ✔ · «He would be very shy» ✘ · «They had been waiting for an hour» ✔ · «I had went home» ✘ · «She didn't use to like it» ✔ · «We would have a dog» ✘ · «By six o'clock they had finished» ✔.
  3. Send anyone still unsure to p.28 to read the box for themselves — naming the page is part of teaching them to revise.
📺 On screen: the three rules on the board: would ≠ state · used to ≠ duration · had + third form. Backup: dictate them.
👦 Students do: answer five rule questions from memory, then judge eight sentences aloud

32–40′
Vocabulary game
desks in fours
Why: The one exercise students lose time on is the one they have never rehearsed out loud — so it is rehearsed out loud.

WHAT WE DO: the unit word list, everybody writing at the same moment

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. «QUIZ-QUIZ-TRADE» with the forty-word unit list (SB p.28) followed by «SHOWDOWN» in fours: the leader reads a definition, everyone writes the word ALONE, then all four answers go up at the same moment. One point for the word, two for a correct sentence with it.
  2. Then the definitions rehearsal, because the test asks for six of them: board the frame It's a person who … / It's a situation when … / It's money you … and take six definitions aloud from six different students (self-employed, manual, promotion, salary, training, qualifications).
  3. Finish with sixty seconds of phrasal verbs: you say the meaning, the class calls the verb and the particle.
📺 On screen: the definition frame on the board. Backup: if there are no cards, the leader reads a word from the list on p.28.
👦 Students do: write definitions alone and show them simultaneously, then say six aloud

WHAT WE DO: SB p.29: six exercises, 35 minutes, silence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Thirty-five minutes. Grammar first, vocabulary second. If you are stuck, move on and come back. Write the finishing time on the board.
  2. Walk the room twice in the first five minutes, then sit down.
  3. At the halfway mark write 17 minutes left on the board without speaking.
  4. Fast finishers: go back to Vocabulary exercise 1 and write a second, better definition for each of the six words.
  5. Collect at 83 minutes exactly, in silence.
📺 On screen: the finishing time and the halfway warning on the board. Backup: a wall clock and your own watch.
👦 Students do: sit the paper alone in silence for thirty-five minutes

«We didn't revise» on the dayThe revision was the homework and the first half of today. Say it once, calmly, and start the paper on time.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks).

The unit test is the exam block in miniature: produce the form, choose the particle, spell the word. «Would be» for a past state and «used to» with a stated duration are both wrong, and both are one mark each. So is a phrasal verb with the right verb and the wrong particle.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
