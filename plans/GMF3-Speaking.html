Room setup — solving «1 speaks, 9 wait»
🎤 Exam corner (with you)
  • Two chairs at a small table, side-on (not interrogation-style), your back to the room.
  • Picture prompt + question cards face-down; assessment sheet + pen.
  • One child at a time, ~4–5′. A visible «now / next» name order so children know when they're up.
  • Warm body language: smile, nod, lean in. This is a chat, not a trial.
🧠 Waiting = busy (the other children)
  • Quiet menu (desks): end-of-year colouring, app games, a «draw & label your favourite unit» sheet — independent, silent.
  • Rehearsal buddies (carpet corner): pairs quietly rehearse the exam questions on prompt cards — RallyRobin, so everyone speaks while waiting.
  • A classroom assistant / reliable child manages the «now/next» order & keeps volume low.
  • Rule up front: Whisper voices — I'm listening to your friends.
The exam — three parts (~4–5′ per pupil)
Part · ~timeWhat happens · examiner script
➊ Warm-up dialogue
(interview) · ~1.5′		Settle the child, then ask personal Qs from across the year: Hello! What's your name? How old are you? · Are you good at Maths? What do you do after school? Do you help at home? What did you watch on TV yesterday? Aim: relaxed, flowing answers.
➋ Describe a picture
· ~1.5′		Show one busy scene (people + actions + objects). Look at this picture. Tell me what you can see. Prompt if needed: How many…? What is he doing? What's on the table? Is it big or small? Aim: name things + use a structure (there is/are, present continuous, comparatives).
➌ Question & answer
· ~1.5′		Pull 3–4 question cards mixing units & tenses: What time do you get up? What are you going to do this summer? What did you wear to a party? Which do you prefer, spicy or sweet food? Aim: understand the question + answer in a full-ish sentence.
Assessment criteria (per pupil — 1 to 4)
CriterionWhat you listen for
Fluency & interactionAnswers without long freezing; keeps the chat going; responds to your prompts. (Not speed — willingness & flow.)
VocabularyRange of unit words used correctly (school, food, sea animals, festivals, time…).
GrammarTarget structures attempted: tenses (past/going to), comparatives, questions, 3rd-person -s.
PronunciationIntelligible; key sounds from the year (-ed endings, th, j, ch); word stress.
Simple 4-point scale
  • 4 — Confident: answers all 3 parts, full sentences, minor errors that don't block meaning.
  • 3 — Competent: answers most, some support needed, meaning clear.
  • 2 — Developing: short answers/single words, needs prompts, some meaning through.
  • 1 — Emerging: mostly words/gestures, heavy support — note for next year.
📝 Score each criterion 1–4 on the child's sheet + one warm note. Total is for YOUR records & parent feedback, not announced competitively.
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. Each pupil demonstrates spoken English from Units 1–9 across three YLE-style parts (dialogue · picture description · Q&A) in a warm one-to-one, assessed on fluency, vocabulary, grammar & pronunciation.
  • Subsidiary. Waiting pupils keep practising speaking in pairs (RallyRobin) — no idle time; every child speaks a lot today, examined or rehearsing.
  • Personal. Make it feel like a friendly chat, not a test. Support nerves actively. Assess fairly & kindly; end every child on a positive note.
By the end, pupils can…
  • answer personal questions across the year's topics;
  • describe a picture with unit vocabulary & a structure;
  • understand & answer mixed-tense questions in a short exchange.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Child freezes / very nervousStart with the easiest personal Q (What's your name?), smile, wait, give a sentence starter (I can see a…); never rush; a nervous child may go last after hearing others.
One-word answers onlyPrompt to extend: Tell me more. Why? What colour? Score what they DO produce; don't over-prompt into their score.
Waiting children get loud / boredQuiet menu + rehearsal-buddy corner set up before you start; whisper rule; helper manages the queue.
Doesn't understand the questionRephrase once, simpler; gesture; if still stuck, move on kindly — note it, don't dwell.
Fast finishers of the examReturn to the quiet menu; can become a rehearsal buddy for someone still waiting.
Running over timeKeep a soft timer; 3 parts × ~1.5′; if a child is flowing, that's fine — trim prompts elsewhere.
Classroom management — signals
“Class? — Yes!” — you: “Class!” → pupils stop & echo “Yes!”
“WiFi” 👋 — say “WiFi” + raise a spread hand → pupils silently mirror & tune in.
Volume control — raise/lower your arm = louder/quieter → settler.
“Give me five” — eyes · ears · mouth · hands · feet.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1… pencils down.”
“Freeze!” — statue, mid-movement reset.
Procedure — lesson timeline (60 min)
highlighted = exact words to say (teacher / class) ▶ = game 🎮 = interactive (projector) 💪 support · ⭐ extension
TimeStage / whereProcedureMaterials
0–4′
Opening & reassure
door → carpet

Greet at the door: Hello! Come in! Carpet. Hello, superstars! Today we CHAT in English — just you and me, about all the fun things we've learnt. It's easy — I love hearing you talk!

One deep breath together: Breathe in… and out. Big smile!

 carpet
4–10′
Whole-class warm-up + set the stations
carpet → desks

Prime the language (all together). Quick chant of a few year-favourite question-answers so everyone hears the models: What's your favourite subject? — My favourite subject is…

Explain the setup. I'll call you one by one for our chat. While you wait: quiet menu at your desk, OR practise the questions with a partner in the corner — whisper voices! ICQ: Loud or whisper while I listen? (→ whisper). Show the «now/next» name order.

 question prompt cards · quiet menu sheets · «now/next» board
10–52′
🎤 One-to-one Speaking Exam
exam corner + desks

Call pupils one by one (~4–5′ each, ~10 children in ~42′). For each child run the three parts:

➊ Dialogue: Hello! How are you? What's your name? Are you good at English? What do you do after school?

➋ Describe a picture: Look at this picture. What can you see? What is she doing? How many…?

➌ Q&A cards: What are you going to do this summer? What did you have for breakfast? What time do you go to bed?

Close each child warmly: Thank you — that was great! Well done! ✔ Score fluency / vocab / grammar / pronunciation 1–4 on the sheet + one note, right after each child (while fresh). Send them back to the quiet menu.

💪 support: sentence starters + picture prompts for nervous / weaker pupils · ⭐ extension: an extra “tell me more / why?” follow-up for confident speakers.

 picture prompts · question cards · assessment sheets (1 per child) · quiet menu · timer
52–58′
Whole-class celebrate
carpet circle

Everyone back to the carpet. ▶ Timed Pair Share: Tell your partner: what was your favourite question today? (20 sec each). Whole-class cheer: You all spoke SO well — give me five! Each child hears a specific praise: You told me all about your summer plans!

58–60′
Closing / Exit ticket
carpet → door

I'm so proud of your English this year! Let's sing Goodbye. Thank you for the lesson! Sing Goodbye. High-five line at the door — each child says one English sentence about their summer → out: Thank you! Have a great summer! Goodbye!

Homework

В день устного экзамена — без письменного ДЗ. По желанию: рассказать дома о летних планах по-английски («This summer I'm going to…»). Впереди — резервные уроки 71–72 (повторение + итоги года).

 no written HW
After the exam — records & feedback

Collect assessment sheets; transfer scores + notes to the class list. Combine with Final-Test data for a full end-of-year picture. In parent feedback: lead with a spoken strength («confidently described the picture») + one next step («working on past-tense endings»). Weak spots → feed the Reserve 2 revision (Lessons 71–72).
