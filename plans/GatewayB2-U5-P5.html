🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Consolidate the whole unit — modals of obligation and advice, past modals, speculation and deduction, money vocabulary, idioms and phrasal verbs — and then produce it under test conditions: the Student's Book Test yourself on p.69, six exercises, 40 items, every one written out.
What is genuinely hard
Everything on this paper is productive and most of it is multi-word: ought to have read, needn't have worried, may not have received. One missing have, one second form instead of a third, and the whole item scores nothing. The vocabulary half is a definition-writing task, which is harder than recognition and needs one rehearsal before the test.
Typical error to expect
«should have went», «must have be», «can't have saw» — past speculation and past advice are modal + have + THIRD form. Always have, never has, and never the second form.
«You mustn't pay in cash» for «you don't have to pay in cash» — the single most expensive modal error in the unit. Mustn't forbids; don't have to frees. Russian «не надо» covers both, which is why it has to be taught as a choice, not as a translation.
«I'll pay back you», «set aside it» — with a pronoun object, the particle goes after the pronoun: pay YOU back, set IT aside. With a noun, both orders work.
Why the lesson is built this way
Forty-five minutes of active retrieval, then forty of quiet paper. The retrieval is deliberately in three different shapes — a showdown on the modals, a definitions relay on the vocabulary, a phrasal-verb sprint — because the test samples three different kinds of knowledge and a single game would only rehearse one. Then the room changes shape, the three ghosts go on the board, and the papers go face down.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
Forty-five minutes to get all of it back, then the test on page 69 — thirty-five minutes, forty answers, everything written out. Before you start I'll show you the three places where this unit's marks disappear, so that they don't disappear from your paper.

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting at the door, then a standing retrieval round on the whole unit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what was the last thing you bought? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — rewind, everything. Quiz-Quiz-Trade with mixed unit cards: money words, idioms, phrasal verbs. Two rounds, standing.
  3. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Forty-five minutes to get it all back, then the test. Page 68.
📺 On screen: the mixed card set handed out at the door. Backup: no cards — run it straight from the vocabulary lists on SB p.68, partner A covering the word.
👦 Students do: stand, quiz, trade, move — three minutes of continuous retrieval.

5–19′
Grammar reference · the modal showdown
teams of four
Why: Simultaneous individual accountability is the opposite of a hands-up review, where three students answer and the rest revise nothing.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.68 Grammar reference turned into simultaneous individual retrieval

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two minutes of silent reading of the Grammar reference on p.68 — timed, and nothing else.
  2. Books closed. Showdown in teams of four: you read a prompt, all four write on mini whiteboards, the captain calls Showdown! and the boards go up together.
  3. Fifteen prompts: (1) It isn't necessary — two ways. (2) It's forbidden — three ways. (3) Advice — three ways. (4) «I must to go» — correct it. (5) Question form of «have to». (6) 'd better = ? (7) It wasn't necessary and I did it anyway. (8) It wasn't necessary and we don't know if I did it. (9) Past criticism: «You … told me». (10) I'm almost sure it's true — now. (11) I'm almost sure it isn't true — now. (12) I'm almost sure it was true — past. (13) Possible in the past — three modals. (14) «He can be at home» — right or wrong? (15) The form of past speculation, in three words.
  4. One point per correct board, and only if all four boards agree — that is what makes them coach each other.
  5. Do not explain during the game. Note the two prompts that failed most and reteach only those, in ninety seconds, at the end.
📺 On screen: the running score on the board; prompts read aloud one at a time. Backup: no mini whiteboards? torn paper raised in the air works identically.
👦 Students do: read, then answer every prompt individually and show simultaneously.

19–30′
Vocabulary revision · the definitions relay
open floor
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies. And it rehearses the one test format that cannot be guessed: producing a definition rather than recognising a word.

WHAT WE DO: the hardest part of the test — writing definitions — rehearsed on the feet

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stick eight money words on the walls before the lesson: refund · receipt · change · bargain · the sales · value for money · afford · get into debt.
  2. Pairs: one runner, one writer. The runner walks to a word, comes back and the pair writes a definition in English — not a translation. Swap roles after four.
  3. Six minutes, then two minutes checking against the vocabulary list on p.68 and against each other.
  4. Whole-class round: you read three of their definitions aloud (anonymously) and the class says the word. Any definition that works gets a point for the pair.
  5. This is the exact format of Vocabulary exercise 1 on the test. Say so once, at the end.
📺 On screen: eight word cards on the walls, prepared in advance. Backup: write them on eight sheets during the showdown — five minutes of your time, reusable all year.
👦 Students do: walk, retrieve, write English definitions in pairs, then identify each other's definitions.

30–40′
Phrasal-verb sprint + the three ghosts
teams → desks
Why: Three retrieval formats in thirty-five minutes, each matched to a different section of the paper — and the ghosts are the three marks the class would otherwise donate.

WHAT WE DO: the last retrieval block, then the three named error patterns that decide the marks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Sprint, six minutes. Teams of four, one column each on the board. You read a definition (survive on very little money · reduce how much you spend on something · give back money you borrowed · spend a lot on something unusual · compare prices in different shops · make somebody pay far too much · contribute money · have none left); one runner per team writes the phrasal verb and hands the pen on.
  2. Wrong particle is not a point, and neither is «pay back you». Say that before you start.
  3. Then sit down and write the three ghosts, and leave them up through the test: (1) mustn't ≠ don't have to. (2) modal + HAVE + third form — always have, never has. (3) pay YOU back, not «pay back you».
  4. Ninety seconds of silent personal revision from their own notebook page from Lesson 2.
📺 On screen: the team columns, then the three ghosts. Backup: the sprint works on paper at the desks with one sheet per team.
👦 Students do: run, write, argue about particles, then revise their own notes in silence.

40–45′
Room change + briefing
desks apart
Why: The transition is the fragile part of a test lesson. Naming it as a five-minute stage stops the test starting in a room still buzzing from a relay.

WHAT WE DO: the physical and mental switch from revision to test

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Desks apart, bags on the floor, phones in the box by the door, pencil cases out. Do it yourself, at speed, and do not negotiate.
  2. The briefing, once, calmly: Page 69. Six exercises: three grammar, three vocabulary. Forty answers, thirty-five minutes. Pencil. Spelling counts. If you don't know one, leave it and come back.
  3. Point at the three ghosts: these three cost the most marks in this unit. Check them at the end.
  4. There is nothing on this paper we haven't done in this room. Say it — it measurably lowers the noise in the first two minutes.
  5. Two minutes of silent reading of the whole page before pens move. No questions after that.
📺 On screen: the three ghosts and the finish time on the board; the timer set and visible. Backup: your phone timer on the desk, announced at 15 and 5 minutes remaining.
👦 Students do: move desks, put phones away, read the whole test silently before starting.

45–80′
Test yourself · SB p.69, in silence
desks apart
Why: Thirty-five minutes of quiet production is the point of the lesson; the checking instruction at the end is worth two or three marks per student and costs nothing.

WHAT WE DO: the Student's Book unit test: Grammar 1–3 and Vocabulary 1–3, 40 items, 35 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start the timer and write the finish time on the board.
  2. Sit down. Do not walk the aisles — it raises anxiety and helps nobody.
  3. Announce time twice: fifteen minutes remaining, and five.
  4. The only permitted question is about an instruction. Answer it in four words, aloud, for the whole room.
  5. Last three minutes: go back to the three things on the board. Check every past modal for «have». Check every «mustn't». Check the position of the pronoun in the phrasal verbs.
  6. Collect the papers face down, all at once, at the front.
📺 On screen: the finish time and the three ghosts stay on the board. Backup: nothing else is displayed — a test needs no technology, which is exactly why it is planned this way.
👦 Students do: work alone in silence for thirty-five minutes, then check against the three named traps.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The revision half overruns and the test starts late.The phrasal-verb sprint is the flexible item — cut it to four minutes. The test starts at 45 minutes exactly; write the finish time on the board so the clock is the authority.
Definitions come out as translations.The relay rehearses the exact format, and the whole-class round makes a bad definition audible without naming anybody. Give one model yourself first.
A student writes «should have went» throughout.The three-box visual has been on the board since Lesson 3. Keep the irregular-verb list on the desks during the revision half — but not during the test.
Somebody finishes in twenty minutes.The checking instruction is prepared in advance: past modals for «have», «mustn't» for meaning, phrasal-verb word order. Then sit and read.
December energy is low and the class is already on holiday.Three short retrieval games in three formats, all on the feet for the middle two. Nothing in the revision half is longer than fourteen minutes.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 and 32–36 — the grammar and vocabulary block of the written paper.

The unit test is productive from beginning to end: modal + have + past participle, written out, spelled right. That is the same demand tasks 19–25 make, and the same place careless candidates give marks away — should have went, must have be.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
