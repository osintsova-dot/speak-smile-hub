🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A long text frightens new students, so the way in has four steps: the words BEFORE the text → prediction from the photo (you cannot be wrong) → reading in chunks in crews → tasks with the “finger on the line” support. I give the subskill of the unit straight and simply: underline the key words in the question, then look for them in the text. The product is modest: 4 opinion sentences.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at the photo. A girl on a skateboard. She is thirteen — like you. And she is a WORLD champion. But in the evening she does homework and watches YouTube, like everyone. Today: six new words, then we read her blog — in parts, your crew takes one part — and at the end you write four sentences: is her life good or too hard? Your opinion, your words.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: a 13-year-old world skateboard champion who still does her homework in the evening: we learn six words, read the blog in chunks (one chunk per crew) and write four sentences — is her life good or too hard?

Reading in chunks
crews

WHAT WE DO: track 14: listen and follow with your eyes → the text in 3 chunks, one per crew (Jigsaw) → each crew prepares 2 facts → we put it together → we check the sticky notes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. First we listen to the whole text (track 14) and follow with a finger — don't stop at new words.
  2. ex.4: Which summary is best? TB key: b — an ordinary girl with a special talent.
  3. MODEL FIRST: I take the first paragraph and show out loud how I pull two facts out of it and write them as short lines on the board — that is the report I expect from every crew.
  4. Kagan Jigsaw: Your crew owns ONE part. Find two facts. You will teach the class. Each crew reads only its own third — but takes the whole text home, because the other two thirds are told to them by their neighbours. If you don't tell it, the class has a hole in the text.
  5. Putting it together: the crews report in order and I build “Brighton's day” on the board.
  6. Checking the sticky notes: Which guesses came TRUE? — we circle the ones that matched.
📺 On screen: SB pp.26–27 (the text in 3 chunks) · audio track 14 · board (Backup: I read the script aloud twice, at the same pace)
👦 Students do: they own their chunk and listen to the others. Expect: putting it together gives the whole picture without fear of the length

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can find specific information in a blog with support (key words → search → line), match 6 new words to definitions, and write 4 opinion sentences by frame.
  • Subsidiary. Identifying the genre (ex.3) and the best summary (ex.4) with discussion; ex.5–6 in pairs with a prompt sheet; Word work before the text; Critical thinking in a simplified form (one plus and one minus).
  • Personal. I cut the text into 3 chunks — one per crew, then we put it together. Vocabulary BEFORE the reading, not during it. I read the answers out one at a time, not as a list.
By the end, students can…
  • underline key words in a question before reading
  • find an answer and point at the proof line
  • match 6 new words to definitions
  • write 4 opinion sentences with a frame

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
champion — is that a competition or a person? → a person who wins.
junior — is that about age or about level? → competitions for young people, not for adults.
nervous — does it mean “bad-tempered and angry”? → no: worried, anxious before a performance.

Assumptions: SB pp.26–27, KTP pair 14. They read more slowly than the “ours” line — the text in chunks, tasks with frames, answers one at a time.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They are scared of how long the text isThe text goes into 3 chunks, one per crew; each crew owns its own and tells the others — everybody reads a third but knows the whole thing.
New words stall the readingWord work BEFORE the text, with pictures and gestures; a mini-glossary in the margin.
“I don't know what to think” at the product stageOpinion frame: I think… because… + a bank of reasons on the slide.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
