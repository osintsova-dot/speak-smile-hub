WHAT WE DO: into the lesson + rewind P1: role and bank roll-call; a bridge to the anatomy of a talk

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: Your topic in ONE word!
  2. Rewind: Stand up if… you're a director! Say your job! Editors! Writers! Speakers! — movement + the roles are alive.
  3. Bank-check in pairs, 60 sec: Read your partner two phrases from your bank.
  4. Bridge: You have topics and facts. Today we turn them into a TALK. Watch me flop first.
📺 On screen: board: TED = hook → 3 facts → conclusion (covered for now)
👦 Students do: Standing role roll-call, bank-phrase swap. Expected: 80% have their bank with them; for those who forgot — spare P1 station sheets.

The anatomy of a talk: bad/good demo
Brief · anatomy of a talk (a «bad/good» demo)
screen

WHAT WE DO: two live teacher models: a boring talk vs a talk with a hook; the class works out the difference itself → the formula goes on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Demo 1 (deliberately bad, 40 sec): I read off the sheet, monotone: «I like dogs. Dogs are good…» Rate me: one to five?
  2. Demo 2 (good, 60 sec): Did you know a dog can learn 200 words? … — a hook, 3 facts with examples, a conclusion. Now rate. What changed?
  3. The class names the difference — I assemble the formula: HOOK → FACT+EXAMPLE ×3 → CONCLUSION + Thank you!
  4. ICQ: Do I read my script? — No! Keywords only.
📺 On screen: two live demos · the formula on the board (stays up the whole lesson)
👦 Students do: Students compare and formulate the rules of a good talk themselves. Expected: «the second one is more interesting, it has a question!» — the formula is accepted from experience, not handed down.

14–24′
Hook workshop (THINK)
THINK · hook workshop
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: hook workshop: 2 options per talk (a question / a shock fact), a hook duel with voting

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Bank of hook-starters: Did you know…? · What do you do when…? · Imagine… · [number]! That's how many…
  2. Task: TWO hooks for your talk — one question, one shock fact. Four minutes, the writer writes, everyone thinks.
  3. ▶ Hook duel: teams read out both — the class votes with a fist/open palm (wow/ok). One hook wins.
  4. Pause training: Say your hook… then FREEZE two seconds. Let the room feel it. — in chorus, team by team.
📺 On screen: hook-starters slide · timer (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: Students write hooks and duel with them, drilling the pause after the hook. Expected: shock facts win more often. Typical error: a hook-question with no ↗ intonation — a choral intonation drill.

24–36′
Script in keywords (PREPARE)
PREPARE · keyword script
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: everyone builds THEIR OWN part of the script on the template: keywords, max 4 words per line; connectors are compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand out templates: Your talk, keyword lines: HOOK / FACT 1 + example / FACT 2 + example / FACT 3 + example / CONCLUSION. Max FOUR words a line!
  2. Distribution rule: EVERYONE in the team speaks — the speaker leads, but everyone has a minimum of 3–4 sentences of their own part.
  3. I go round with a scissors gesture: a sentence longer than 4 words per line — we cut it down to keywords.
  4. For fast finishers ⭐: a second «rich words» column — 1 adjective for every fact.
📺 On screen: TED-script templates (asset) · P1 phrase banks
👦 Students do: Students assemble personal keyword scripts — everyone has their own speaking part, nobody hides behind the speaker. Expected: 12 min is enough for a draft. Typical error: full sentences — I cut them down to keywords on the spot.

Voice warm-up (standing)
Voice gym

WHAT WE DO: voice and body warm-up before the run-throughs: projection, pace, pauses — movement after a long sit-down

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands in a circle. Say ‘Did you know?’ — whisper… normal… BACK ROW voice! (3 volume levels).
  2. Pace drill: one bank phrase — slow like a sloth… normal… newsreader fast… now PERFECT pace.
  3. Pause game: a phrase + a 2-sec freeze, whoever twitches sits down. The last 3 standing get +1.
  4. Shake it out. Rehearsal mode: radio whisper in trios.
📺 On screen: —
👦 Students do: Students run volume/pace/pauses through body and voice — everyone standing, the energy is back. Expected: laughter at the sloth pace.

WHAT WE DO: the rules of the trio run-through: speaker–judge–timer, rotation; the judge works from the rubric

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Diagram on the board: Speaker → Judge (rubric) → Timer (phone/stopwatch), rotation round the circle.
  2. To the judge: You listen for THREE things: pauses over 3 seconds, clean forms, rich words. One tick each.
  3. ICQ: Does the speaker hold the script? — Yes, but EYES UP on the audience!
📺 On screen: judging cards (asset)
👦 Students do: Students sort out the run-through roles. Expected: the rubric is familiar from the year — a quick start.

45–60′
Run-through 1: trios with judging
PRACTISE · run-through 1 (Timed Pair Share in trios)
trios

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Timed Pair Share (+timer-judge): everyone performs for 90 sec — 2 min under the stopwatch, the listener-judge ticks the rubric; rotation ×3

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Timed Pair Share: Speaker talks for your time — judge LISTENS, no interrupting. Timer keeps the seconds. When time's up: one star, one wish. Rotate!
  2. Launch: Round one — speakers, GO. Radio whisper: three talks at once, nobody shouts.
  3. Rotation every ~4 min (talk + 60 sec of feedback): the judge speaks using the formula One star: … One wish: …
  4. I move between the trios: I fix only structural issues (no hook / no conclusion) and time.
  5. After 3 rotations: Hands up — who survived the first run? THAT was the hardest one. It only gets better.
📺 On screen: judging cards · timers · scripts
👦 Students do: Everyone performs, judges and keeps time — three roles in 15 minutes, everyone involved at once. Expected: the first run-through is rough — that's normal, we track the growth. Typical error: eyes stuck on the sheet — the judge signals «eyes up».

WHAT WE DO: an upgrade from feedback: everyone fixes 2 weak spots + adds 1 connector and 1 opinion phrase from the bank

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Task: Look at your judge's card. Fix TWO weak spots. Then ADD: one connector (first/also/for example) and one opinion line (I think… because…).
  2. The team's writer checks the joins: who speaks after whom, the hand-offs (And now — Misha with fact two!).
  3. Spot-check: 2–3 «before/after» phrases out loud to the class (+1).
  4. 💪 Support: a connectors-bank card for anyone who's got nothing.
📺 On screen: judging cards with feedback · phrase banks
👦 Students do: Students fix their weak spots precisely — the trio's feedback goes straight to work. Expected: richness grows visibly. Typical error: fixing everything at once and losing time — «only TWO spots!»

Carousel run-through + volunteer air time
Run-through · run-through 2 (Inside-Outside Circle)
two circles → stage

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Inside-Outside Circle: a carousel of run-throughs — the inside circle performs, the outside judges; rotation = everyone runs their talk to 3 new listeners

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Inside-Outside Circle: Two circles, face a partner. Inside circle — SPEAK your talk (short version, 60 sec). Outside — judge: one tick, one tip. Outside circle — rotate two steps! New partner — again!
  2. 3 rotations → SWITCH! Outside speaks, inside judges. 3 more rotations — everyone has performed three times, to different people.
  3. I listen to the carousel from outside: I track growth against run-through 1 (Better than round one? Tick means points.).
  4. Finale: 2–3 volunteers go «on air» for the class — the full version; the class judges with a thumb across the three axes. Growth = +2, gold run = +5.
📺 On screen: two circles (chairs away) · judging cards · season leaderboard
👦 Students do: Students run their talk THREE TIMES to fresh listeners — a rehearsal conveyor belt: fluency grows visibly, nobody waits in a queue. Expected: by the 3rd circle they're speaking without the paper. Typical error: rushing — the judge-partner slows them down with a gesture.
🏆 The season bar (judging rubric 🚀): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — forms are clean (1–3) · Richness — linkers, adjectives, 2+ tenses (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the season leaderboard. 📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can deliver a draft 2-min TED-talk from a keyword script: hook → 3 facts with examples → conclusion, with 2+ tenses and connectors.
  • Subsidiary. The talk's structure is absorbed through a live model; the script is in keywords (not a read-out!); a richness upgrade (connectors, adjectives, opinion phrases from the year's bank); the first run-through is in trios with peer review against the rubric.
  • Personal. Hold the «keywords, not essay» bar: continuers tend to write an essay and read it. We drill the actual PERFORMANCE: eyes on the room, pauses under control.
By the end, students can…
  • build a hook (question / shock fact)
  • turn 3 facts into talk keywords
  • upgrade sentences with connectors and opinion phrases
  • run the draft talk twice in a trio

Target language
Lexis
Hello everyone!Today I want to tell you about…Did you know that…?First… · Also… · For example…The best thing is…I think… because…That's all about…Thank you for listening!Any questions?
Structure
Did you know that sloths sleep 20 hours a day? · First… Also… For example… · I think… because… · That's all about my topic — thank you for listening!
Form
hook = a question or a shock fact (1 sentence); fact + example = a couple of sentences; the connectors first/also/for example are compulsory; conclusion + thanks.
Pron. watch
the hook-question intonation ↗ · a pause after the hook (2 sec — «let the room be surprised») · thank you for LISTening.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Is the script a full text?» → No: keywords only. If you're reading, you're not performing.
«Is the hook a greeting?» → No: hello first — then the hook: a question or a fact that grabs attention.
«How many facts?» → Three. More — the room gets tired, fewer — it's empty.

Assumptions: Topics were approved in P1, facts were brought in from homework. The THINK-PREPARE structure is familiar from the Skills boost format in every unit — today it works towards one big product.

A quiet voice at the run-throughVoice gym before the run-throughs + the rule «the back row must hear»: the trio's judge sits FURTHEST away.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
