🎤 Give Me Five! 4 · Speaking Exam — End-of-Year Oral Assessment (Units 1–9)
A2 young learners (~10 y.o., readers · continuing line) · 60 min · warm 1-to-1 / paired mini-interview · exam corner + quiet desks
Week 17.05–23.05 · Lesson 70🎤 Speaking Exam — warm & encouraging~10 pupils · ~4–5 min eachYLE Movers/Flyers speaking format
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. Each pupil demonstrates spoken English from the year in a friendly oral exam — three parts (dialogue · description · question & answer) — assessed on fluency, vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation.
  • Subsidiary. Interaction skills: greet, take turns, answer in full where they can, ask back, self-repair. Familiar YLE Movers/Flyers speaking shape so the format is not a surprise.
  • Personal. Warmth first — a nervous child underperforms. Easy opening question, smile, wait-time, prompt/rephrase freely; grade the English, not the nerves. Keep the waiting class calm & productive.
By the end, pupils can…
  • hold a short dialogue about themselves & the year’s topics;
  • describe a picture / topic card in 3–5 sentences;
  • answer “Have you ever…?” and unit questions with short answers.

Structure of the exam · assessed language
Part 1 · Dialogue
~1.5 min. Warm interview / paired chat: name, age, family, likes, this year. What’s your name? How old are you? What are you like? What do you like doing? What did you do at the weekend?
Part 2 · Description
~1.5 min. Pupil picks a topic/picture card & describes it in 3–5 sentences: a job, a machine, a city / transport, a country, a meal, space. This is… It’s used for… It’s made of… A … is a person who…
Part 3 · Q & A
~1.5 min. You ask, pupil answers (short answers ok): Have you ever been to…? Have you ever eaten…? Would you rather… or…? Will there be… in the future? Do you have to… in your job card?
Format
Examine in pairs for Part 1 (turn-taking), then each pupil solo for Parts 2–3. ~4–5 min per pupil / ~8–9 min per pair. YLE Movers/Flyers speaking style.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is this a scary test?” → no — it’s a friendly chat; I just listen and smile.
“If you don’t know a word, do you stop?” → no — try another word, or ask “How do you say…?”
“Short answers — are they allowed?” → yes; a full sentence is a bonus.

Assumptions: All units taught & reviewed; Final Test done (lesson 69). Pupils have met all three part-types in Talk Partners routines through the year. Waiting class can self-direct a quiet task.

Assessment criteria — band descriptors (tick per pupil)
  • Fluency & interaction. Strong = speaks readily, takes turns, minimal prompting · Developing = answers with some prompts, short pauses · Emerging = one-word answers, needs each question re-asked.
  • Vocabulary. Strong = uses unit words accurately & a range · Developing = core words, some gaps · Emerging = few words, much L1/pointing.
  • Grammar. Strong = target structures mostly correct (present perfect, passive, will, have to…) · Developing = correct with slips · Emerging = memorised chunks only.
  • Pronunciation. Strong = clear, easy to understand · Developing = mostly clear, some L1 sounds · Emerging = often hard to follow.
  • ✔ Record: one tick per criterion on your class-list grid, + a one-word note (“strong present perfect” / “watch th-”). Grade English, never nerves.
Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Pupil freezes / goes silentDrop to the easiest question (“What’s your name?”), smile, give wait-time, offer a choice: “Cinema or bowling?” — never leave them stuck.
Very shy childLet them go in a pair with a friend for Part 1; sit beside, not across; start with a picture they choose.
One-word answers onlyPrompt to extend: “Tell me more… Why?” Accept short answers — don’t penalise, just note the band.
Waiting class gets loudQuiet self-directed task ready before you start (see stage 2); volume arm; a helper monitor.
Pupil asks “How do you say…?”Tell them warmly — that’s good exam behaviour; note it positively, continue.
Runs over timeSoft close: “Lovely — thank you!” Keep to ~4–5 min; better all pupils calm than a few perfect.
Classroom management — signals
“Class? — Yes!” — you: “Class!” → pupils stop & echo “Yes!”
“WiFi” 🙋 — say “WiFi” + raise a spread hand → pupils silently mirror & tune in.
Volume control — raise/lower your arm = louder/quieter → settler.
“Give me five” — eyes · ears · mouth · hands · feet.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1… pencils down.”
“Freeze!” — statue, mid-movement reset.

10–52′
Speaking Exam — pairs rotate
exam corner

Call pairs to the exam corner (~8–9 min/pair, 5 pairs). Sit beside them, warm tone. Run the 3 parts:

Part 1 · Dialogue (together). Hello! What’s your name? How old are you? What are you like? What do you like doing? What did you do at the weekend? Let them answer in turn; nudge them to ask each other one question.

Part 2 · Description (each solo). Choose a card. Tell me about it. Prompt if needed: What is it? What’s it used for? / What’s it made of? / Who works there?

Part 3 · Q & A (each solo). Have you ever been to another country? Have you ever eaten sushi? Would you rather play the drums or the guitar? Will cars fly in the future? Short answers fine.

Close each pupil warmly: Thank you — that was great! Well done!

✔ Assess live: tick the 4-criteria grid + one note per pupil the moment they finish, before the next pair. Send finished pupils back to their poster silently.
