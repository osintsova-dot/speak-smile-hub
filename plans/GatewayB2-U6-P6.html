🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Answer thirteen exam listening items — seven with pictures (Part 1) and six with written options (Part 2) — using two habits that transfer to every listening in both exams: predict before the recording starts, and use the second hearing to check the rejected options, not to start again. Then finish the review spread's two reading tasks.
What is genuinely hard
In Part 1 all three pictures are mentioned in the recording, and in Part 2 the exact words printed in the options are usually NOT the words that are said. Both traps punish the same behaviour — listening for a word instead of for an answer. And thirteen items in one lesson is a stamina problem: concentration drops after item eight unless the lesson moves in between.
Typical error to expect
Choosing the picture that was mentioned rather than the one that answers the question. All three get mentioned; that is the design.
Waiting for the printed word. If the option says «stressed», the recording will say «I couldn't relax» — and a candidate who waits for «stressed» hears nothing.
Why the lesson is built this way
Both tasks are run exactly as the exam runs them, but with the preparation stage that the exam does not give you: before Part 1, predicting the words you expect to hear; before Part 2, producing SYNONYMS for the three options, which is the single most transferable exercise on the page. Between the two listenings there is a genuine move break, because thirteen items of concentration in one sitting is not a real thing. The last third of the lesson finishes the review spread's exam corner — a multiple-choice cloze about water clocks and an open cloze about a school with no books — so the block closes with reading in the same lesson as listening, which is what a real paper feels like.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“In part one, all three pictures are mentioned. In part two, the words printed in your options are exactly the words you will NOT hear. That is not unfair — it is the whole test. Today: thirteen items, two hearings each, and the two habits that make them answerable.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, marked papers back with specific feedback, and the switch into the exam page

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — how did you get to school today? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the papers come back. Hand back both tests with section totals written on them. One specific positive sentence about the class (The past perfect section was the strongest — twenty-one of you got all eight participles right) and one specific instruction for block 7.
  3. Sixty seconds in pairs: find ONE mistake on your paper that you can explain to your partner. Not all of them — one.
  4. If the compound-noun spelling caused trouble, put three of them on the board and count the letters in public. Ninety seconds.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): change gear: Papers away. Page one hundred and twenty-eight. Thirteen listening questions today — and in every single one, the wrong answers are mentioned too.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2 = B1 Listening Part 1: seven recordings, three pictures each

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the exam information aloud: Listening Part 1 — seven short recordings (monologues and conversations), one question each, three PICTURES to choose from. Every recording is heard twice.
  2. ex.1 first, and it is the technique: look at the three pictures in question 1 and predict the words you expect to hear. Take them onto the board — jeans, material, stripes, bottom, leg, pocket. Then play track 82 and let them hear their own predictions arrive.
  3. Before the task itself, two minutes silent: look at all seven questions and the twenty-one pictures. Ask What is the question actually asking? for three of them aloud.
  4. ex.2, track 83: play the whole task once. Ninety seconds of pair comparison — reasons, not letters.
  5. Play it a second time, exactly as the exam does. Pens down between the two plays for ten seconds, deliberately.
  6. Take the answers: 1 C, 2 A, 3 B, 4 C, 5 B, 6 C, 7 A.
📺 On screen: the class's predicted words on the board before the first recording. Backup: if the audio fails, read the seven extracts from the audioscript (TB p.306–307) twice, at normal speed — the task is unchanged and you are the recording

Part 1 · how the traps worked
pairs → class
Why: Part 1 · how the traps worked

WHAT WE DO: the autopsy: what was mentioned, and what answered the question

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the exam tip aloud, because it is now obvious rather than abstract: Look at the three pictures before the recording starts and predict the words you expect to hear. Choose the picture that answers the QUESTION, not the picture that gets mentioned. Use the second hearing to check, not to start again.
  2. Take three of the seven items and dissect them in public. For each: what did the speaker say about picture A? About B? And why does only one of them ANSWER the question?
  3. Put two columns on the board — WHAT WAS MENTIONED · WHAT ANSWERED THE QUESTION — and fill them for those three items. This is the board work of the lesson.
  4. Then the audioscript, three minutes, in RallyCoach pairs: find the exact line that proves each of the three answers. Reading the script after listening is worth more than a third play.
  5. Close with the honest count: Twenty-one pictures. Seven answers. Fourteen of them were mentioned on purpose.
📺 On screen: the two-column table filled for three items — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: explain three items in pairs and find the proving line in the script

Move break · say it another way
standing, whole class
Why: The one stirrer in this block that is also the preparation stage for the next task — the class stands up, moves, and arrives at Part 2 already thinking in paraphrase.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that IS the preparation for Part 2

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands. You say a common exam word — worried · excited · disappointed · tired · surprised · expensive — and pairs have ten seconds to produce TWO other ways of saying it.
  2. Six words, six exchanges. After each one take two answers aloud and write them on the board.
  3. The transition says the point out loud: That is exactly what part two does to you. It prints one word and says another.
  4. Freeze and sit: Page one hundred and twenty-nine.
📺 On screen: the class's synonyms collected on the board as they are produced — nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everybody produces twelve synonyms in four minutes, on their feet

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3 = B1 Listening Part 2: six conversations, three written options each

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the exam information aloud: Listening Part 2 — six conversations, one question each, three WRITTEN options. Heard twice. The exam tip on the page is the whole task: you may not hear the same words that are printed in the options.
  2. ex.1: look at question 1 only. Who will you hear, what will they talk about, and what do you need to listen for? Answer: a boy and his friend, a cooking course, how he FELT before it started.
  3. ex.2, and this is the exercise that transfers: what other words might you hear for stressed, confident and worried? Take them onto the board — worried, unable to relax · certain, sure · anxious, unhappy, nervous. Then play track 84 and let them check.
  4. Before the task, three minutes silent: read all six questions and eighteen options, underline the key word in each option, and write ONE synonym above at least six of them.
  5. ex.3, track 85: play once, whole. Ninety seconds of pair comparison. Then play it a second time.
  6. Check: 1 C, 2 A, 3 B, 4 B, 5 A, 6 C.
📺 On screen: the class's synonyms written above the options on the board. Backup: if the audio fails, read the six conversations from the audioscript (TB p.307–308) twice, taking both voices — it works, and the class has heard you do it before

Why: Part 2 · the paraphrase column

WHAT WE DO: the autopsy: what was printed against what was said

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three of the six items, dissected in public, in the same two columns as before: WHAT WAS PRINTED · WHAT WAS SAID.
  2. Item 4 is the one to slow down for — a girl describing the buildings her father designs. All three options are opinions about the same buildings, and the deciding word is one adjective.
  3. Kagan Numbered Heads Together for the other two: the group agrees on the paraphrase before it says the letter.
  4. Then the transferable summary, written on the board and copied: In part two, the answer is a MEANING. You will never hear the printed words.
  5. Close the listening half with the count: Thirteen items today, every one heard twice. That is the whole listening paper.
📺 On screen: the printed-against-said table for three items — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: produce the paraphrase before the letter, three times, in groups

Reading Part 5 · the water clocks
individual → pairs → class
Why: Reading Part 5 · the water clocks

WHAT WE DO: the exam corner of the Review 3 spread: a six-gap multiple-choice cloze

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Change gear out loud: Books to page seventy-five. A real paper doesn't do one skill for ninety minutes, and neither do we.
  2. One minute, silent: read the whole text about water clocks with the gaps ignored. One question — what is good about the Eco-friendly Water-powered Clock?
  3. The method, which the class already owns from block 3: Not what it means. What it goes WITH.
  4. Five minutes, individually: the six gaps. Rule — write the partner word in the margin.
  5. Check with Kagan Showdown: 1 C, 2 A, 3 D, 4 B, 5 C, 6 D. Take the partner word before the letter every time — at least nine metres, researched HOW, the invention OF, plugged IN, a digital display, makes it easier.
  6. Two minutes on the rejected options for gaps 3 and 6, which are the two that split the class.
📺 On screen: the six partner words listed on the board as the check proceeds — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: read for gist, then solve six collocation gaps with a margin note for each

Why: Reading Part 6 + the strategy card

WHAT WE DO: the open cloze about a school with no books, then the card is completed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. One minute, silent: read the text about the West Philadelphia School of the Future — no books, everything on computers.
  2. The method from block 4, restated: What KIND of word first. Which word second. The twelve-word list goes back on the board: the, a, of, in, to, as, than, it, are, is, have, if.
  3. Four minutes, individually: six gaps, one word each. Rule — the part of speech goes in the margin before the word.
  4. Check: 1 on (on purpose) · 2 at (at first) · 3 about · 4 are · 5 to · 6 will. Take the label first, then the word.
  5. Then say the thing that makes the class sit up, because it is true: Two of those six answers are phrases you learned this month — on purpose and at first.
  6. Last four minutes: the strategy card (SB p.120) gets its listening entries — PART 1 — all three are mentioned; choose the one that answers the question. PART 2 — predict synonyms before you listen. BOTH — second hearing is for checking. The card now covers reading, writing and listening.
  7. One personal line each: which of the three listening parts is MY weakest.
📺 On screen: the twelve-word list and the three new card entries on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: solve an open cloze with a part-of-speech label for each gap, then complete a strategy card that now covers three papers

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«All three pictures are mentioned.» So what do I listen for? → the one that answers the QUESTION, not the one that is named.
The option says «worried». What might I hear instead? → anxious, nervous, unhappy, I couldn't sleep. Meaning, not words.
Second hearing — do I start again? → no. I check the two I rejected.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Thirteen listening items in one lesson exhausts the classThe move break sits exactly between the two tasks and is not optional. If concentration still drops, do Part 2 items 1–3, stand everybody up for thirty seconds, then do 4–6.
Students want a third hearingSay it once, kindly, and hold it: Twice is what the exam gives you. We do it twice, then we look at the audioscript. The audioscript afterwards is far more useful than a third play.
«I heard the word, so I chose it»In the check, ask what was actually said, then what it MEANT. Write the two side by side on the board for two or three items — the column of paraphrases is the lesson.
The reading half feels like an afterthoughtName it as the point at the start: a real paper mixes skills, and the last thirty minutes are the reading half of the same exam. Two tasks, both short, both already familiar formats.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
