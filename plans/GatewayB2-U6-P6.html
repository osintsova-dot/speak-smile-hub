🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Put the whole of Unit 6 in one place — the grammar reference in their own words, the word list in their mouths — and then find out what is still missing while there is still a night to fix it.
What is genuinely hard
Not the content: the transfer. A student who can do each exercise when it is labelled falls apart when six exercises sit on one page with no labels. The second difficulty is honesty — a practice paper only works if nobody hides the score from themselves.
Typical error to expect
«full of beans» written as «full of bean», «out of the shape», «on top of world» — an idiom is a fixed string and the Test yourself page scores it as one word: one wrong article or plural and the mark is gone. A right idiom remembered wrong scores nothing.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is revision that looks nothing like a test: the Check it page is answered, not read, and the vocabulary comes back through games where everybody writes at once. Then the room turns quiet for a timed run of the book's own Test yourself — for practice, with the marks belonging to nobody — and the students mark their own papers and write the reason for every error. The lesson ends on the real test: seven sections, a hundred points, the camera and the choice of version, all named a day early so that the paper measures the language and not the surprise.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Everything from this unit, on your desk, in one lesson — and one run against the clock that costs you nothing. Tomorrow it counts; today it only tells you where to look.”

Why: Everyone speaks English inside the first two minutes, and the rewind pulls the previous lesson's language back into working memory so that today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of self-diagnosis across the whole unit, and the first half of the lesson aimed at the weak spot

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — how did you sleep last night, and how do you feel about it? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.
  2. 2 minutes — the unit in six sentences. Kagan RallyRobin in pairs: take turns, six sentences in ninety seconds, and each one must use a different piece of the unit — a part of the body, a medical word, an idiom, a conditional, a connector, a wish.
  3. 1 minute — the count. Ask how many pairs got all six. Then take the ONE that everybody found hardest — it will be the connector or the wish — and put two examples of it on the board. That is where the first half of the lesson starts.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): point at the two examples: That is the part we spend the next forty minutes on. Then the page — forty points, thirty minutes.
📺 On screen: nothing to project in the first five minutes — paper and voices only
👦 Students do: produce six sentences each covering six different parts of the unit, then name the hardest one

The four-conditional table, built then checked
pairs → class → individual
Why: The four-conditional table, built then checked

WHAT WE DO: Grammar reference p.80 used as a source, not as a text: produce first, verify second

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books SHUT. Pairs, four minutes: fill the four rows of the table with the form and one example each. Nothing is looked up.
  2. Collect on the board row by row, taking one pair's version and letting the room challenge it. Correct only what is wrong, and let them say why.
  3. Confirmed: zero — if + present simple, present simple · first — if + present simple, will + infinitive · second — if + past simple, would + infinitive · third — if + past perfect, would have + past participle.
  4. NOW open p.80 and check, two minutes. What did we miss? Add it in a different colour. The Grammar reference is a verification tool today, never a reading text.
  5. Same treatment for the second box, three minutes: the four connectors with their meanings — unless (if not, except if), as long as and provided/providing that (if, only if), in case (because maybe). Produce from memory, then check.
  6. And the third box, three minutes: the three uses of I wish / If only, with one example each, from memory, then checked.
📺 On screen: the four-row table filled on the board by the class and then verified against p.80 — chalk only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: reconstruct the whole grammar of the unit from memory in pairs, then verify it and correct their own version in colour

20–26′
Move break · conditional showdown
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: three minutes standing between two long sitting stages

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone stands with a mini-whiteboard. Kagan Showdown: you read a sentence with the verb in brackets, they write the form, and on your signal every board goes up at once.
  2. Eight items, taken straight from the shape of the test: two zero, two first, two second, two third, mixed order and no warning of which is coming.
  3. After each showdown the class also calls the NAME of the conditional in chorus. That is the half of Grammar 1 they would otherwise lose.
  4. Then four rounds of the connectors on the same boards: you say a meaning, they write the connector.
  5. Nobody watches, nobody waits, and you get a live map of who is still guessing thirty minutes before the paper.
  6. Freeze and sit: Now the words.
📺 On screen: nothing to project — the items are read aloud and every board answers simultaneously
👦 Students do: answer twelve items on a mini-whiteboard standing, naming the conditional aloud each time
mini-whiteboards and pens · board

Vocabulary games · everybody answers every time
standing → pairs → class
Why: Vocabulary games · everybody answers every time

WHAT WE DO: the three vocabulary groups of p.80 recycled through two games with no spectators

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade, five minutes, on feet: each student holds a card with a word from p.80 on one side and its definition on the other. Ask, answer, coach, swap cards, find a new partner. Twenty exchanges each in five minutes.
  2. Then the definition drill in pairs, three minutes, because Vocabulary 2 of the test is production: one student says a word from the medical group, the other defines it in ONE sentence that would work for someone who does not know it. Ten words, then swap.
  3. Then the idiom spelling round, three minutes, scored and public: you say the meaning, they write the whole idiom on the whiteboard. One point per complete string, minus one for a missing article or preposition. Seven idioms — exactly the seven in the test.
  4. Last minute: the scrambled-letters format from Vocabulary 1, four words on the board, thirty seconds — phi, gnusl, ghiht, tiswa. It is trivial and it tells them that this section is about spelling.
  5. Say plainly what just happened: Every format on the page has now been done twice. Nothing on that paper is new.
📺 On screen: the four scrambled words written on the board for the last minute — chalk only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: make twenty vocabulary exchanges on their feet, define ten words aloud in pairs, and spell seven idioms for points

38–47′
Doubt clinic · their questions, not yours
desks
Why: The revision so far was ours. This eight minutes is theirs, and an anonymous slip is the only way the weakest student in the room asks the question that has been in the way for two weeks.

WHAT WE DO: the last honest questions before the paper — asked by the students

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand every student a slip. Write one question about this unit you still cannot answer. Anonymous. One sentence. Ninety seconds, everybody writes — a student who has no question writes the hardest question they CAN answer, for the class.
  2. Collect, shuffle, read aloud. Answer the first three yourself, fast. Then hand the rest to the class: Numbered Heads Together — the group agrees the answer, one number answers.
  3. Anything nobody can answer goes on the board under the heading ASK BEFORE THE TEST and is answered in the first two minutes of the next lesson, before the papers go out.
  4. Do not turn this into a new lesson. If a question needs twelve minutes of teaching, say so honestly: it is not on the paper in that form, and here is the one line that covers what is.
📺 On screen: the slips, then the board list ASK BEFORE THE TEST. Backup: none needed — paper and voice only.
👦 Students do: write one anonymous question, then answer other people's questions in groups

🎯 Test yourself · against the clock, for practice
desks
Why: The first time they meet the unit under time pressure must not be the day of the real test. Everything that goes wrong here is still repairable — and it is repaired by them, tonight.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.81 — the book's own Test yourself page, done to the clock — done to time, but the mark belongs to nobody

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say what this is, once, and mean it: This is practice. I am not writing these marks down. What you find out in the next 21 minutes is what you revise tonight.
  2. Clock it on the board. Same rule as the real paper: no book, no phone, no talking, and you may go back at the end if you have time.
  3. Monitor with a notebook, not a pen over their shoulders. Write down which exercise the room slows on — that is the section you re-teach in the last minutes, not the one you had planned to.
  4. Two minutes before the end, warn them once. Students who finish early do NOT sit and wait: they turn the page over and write three sentences of their own using the unit's grammar.
📺 On screen: the timer on the board, big enough to see from the back. Backup: a phone stopwatch face up on your desk.
👦 Students do: work in silence to the clock, then stop when the time stops

68–78′
Self-check and the error map
desks → pairs
Why: A score without a reason teaches nothing. Writing WHY the answer was wrong is the whole point of the practice run, and doing it while the paper is warm is worth three exercises done cold.

WHAT WE DO: keys go to the students; every wrong answer becomes a line to work on

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Answers on the board, or the key page open on the projector. Students mark their own paper — not a partner's. This is the one time where nobody else sees the score.
  2. Every wrong answer gets one line in the error log: what the answer was, what the right one is, and why — in their own words. «Careless» is not a reason and does not count.
  3. RallyCoach in pairs: take one error each and explain it to your partner. If you cannot explain it, it goes on the ASK BEFORE THE TEST list on the board.
  4. Take a show of hands by section — grammar, vocabulary, transformations — and write the three weakest on the board. That list IS tonight's homework, personalised, without you writing twelve different tasks.
📺 On screen: the answer key, then the three weakest sections on the board. Backup: if the projector fails, read the key aloud twice, slowly, and let them mark as you read.
👦 Students do: mark their own paper, write the reason for every error, then explain one error to a partner

The test explained · format, level, camera
board
Why: Format panic costs more marks than gaps in the language. A student who knows the order, the totals and the camera walks in measuring the unit, not the room.

WHAT WE DO: exactly what happens next lesson, so nobody is surprised by the paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Put the shape of the test on the board and leave it there: Listening 12 · Vocabulary 20 · Grammar 25 · Use of English 8 · Reading 15 · Writing 10 · Speaking 10 = 100. Ninety minutes, the whole lesson.
  2. Name the order and the reason: listening first, while the ears are fresh; writing last; and speaking taken in pairs in the corner while the room writes — the only honest way to fit five skills into one lesson.
  3. Say the camera out loud now, not on the day: Your speaking answer is filmed. The mark is for the test, the video is for December — so that you can hear for yourself how much better you have got. Nobody watches it but you and me.
  4. The choice of version. Standard or Higher, same seven sections, same hundred points, harder wording. Ask them to decide tonight, not at the door: Which version measures you honestly? Anyone who cannot decide gets Standard and may swap in the first five minutes.
  5. Last line, and say it plainly: Bring a pen that works and the sleep of somebody who has already done the revision.
📺 On screen: the seven sections and the totals, written up and left on the board. Backup: none — this is board and voice.

WHAT WE DO: self-check against today's aim, then the homework said once and written on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two minutes — one thing you now know. Kagan Stand Up–Hand Up–Pair Up: find a partner and say one thing you can do today that you could not do before this unit. Two partners each.
  2. One minute — the aim, checked honestly. Fingers on the desk: three if the four conditionals are now automatic, two if you need the table, one if the third one still slips. No names, no comment.

Structure
all four conditionals in one table · unless / as long as / provided (that) / in case · I wish + past simple, past perfect, would(n't) · plus the definition frame: «X is when you …» / «X is a person who …» / «X is the thing you use to …»
Form
zero: if + present simple, present simple · first: if + present simple, will + infinitive · second: if + past simple, would + infinitive · third: if + past perfect, would have + past participle · the idioms are fixed strings, scored whole.
Pron. watch
the long and short i of this set is what makes it hard to hear: heel /iː/ against hip /ɪ/, knee and sneeze and fever long, ribs and lips and sick short, and kidney and disease carrying one of each · silent letters where Russian expects sound: muscle /ˈmʌsl/, chemist /ˈkemɪst/ with a hard k · stress on the FIRST syllable in PHYsiotherapy is wrong — it is physioTHERapy · and the idioms live on connected speech: up◡and◡about, blacked◡out, back◡on◡his feet, full◡of◡beans, out◡of shape, with of reduced to /əv/ or /ə/, which is the reason students never catch them in the recording
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«If I had studied, I would have passed.» Which conditional, and did I pass? → third, and no. Now change one word so that it is about today. → If I studied, I would pass.
«Define chemist in one sentence.» → a shop that sells medicines, or the person who works there. Would somebody who does not know the word understand your sentence? That is the mark.
«Full of beans — how many words, and what is the article?» → three words, no article. Write it. That is one point, and it is the commonest one lost.

Unit 6 test — what is on the Test yourself page (SB p.81), section by section
SectionContent
Test yourself · Grammar 1 — Conditionals (8 points)eight sentences: put the verb in the right form AND name which conditional it is. The naming is half the mark and it is where the group will lose points — items 2, 3 and 6 are third conditionals hiding behind the contraction 'd.
Test yourself · Grammar 2 — unless, as long as, provided/providing (that), in case (6 points)six sentences rewritten with the word given, the sentence stem already printed (You …, Take …, They …, We …). Meaning decides the connector; a grammatically perfect sentence with the wrong connector scores nothing.
Test yourself · Grammar 3 — I wish / If only (6 points)six situations turned into wishes, again with the stem given. Open answers: the mark is for the tense shift, so a present wish written with a present tense is zero however sensible the idea.
Test yourself · Vocabulary 1 — Parts of the body (4 points)eight scrambled words re-ordered into parts of the body (phi → hip, gnusl → lungs, ghiht → thigh, nidkye → kidney, tiswa → waist, reedhofa → forehead, alin → nail, lemscu → muscle). It looks trivial and it is a spelling section.
Test yourself · Vocabulary 2 — Health and medical care (9 points)nine words defined in one sentence each of the student's own writing (stiffness, A and E, GP, chemist, prescription, operation, fever, ache, pain relief) — production, not recognition, and the sentence has to make the meaning clear to someone who does not know the word.
Test yourself · Vocabulary 3 — Idioms connected with health and illness (7 points)seven sentences rewritten with the idiom whose key word is given (top, down, last, shape, out, pull, picture). The idiom is scored as one fixed string: one wrong preposition or plural and the mark is gone.

The book's own Test yourself page, 40 points in about 30 minutes, which leaves a quarter of an hour for checking and collecting inside the second half of the lesson. Three honest heads-ups for the briefing: Grammar 1 asks for the NAME of each conditional as well as the form, so half the marks there are about recognition, not production; Grammar 3 is open writing and a wish without the tense shift is zero however sensible; and the whole Vocabulary test is spelled, not chosen — forehead, prescription and the seven idioms have to come out of the head. Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«We didn't revise» on the day of the testSay it once, calmly: the revision was the homework, and the whole of today. Then hand out the error map — it names three sections each, which is a night's work, not a fortnight's.
A student treats the practice run as a real test and panicsSay the sentence before the timer starts and again at the halfway point: nobody is writing these marks down. If a student freezes anyway, let them work from the book for the second half — the diagnosis matters more than the conditions.
Fast finishers hand the practice paper in after eight minutesNobody hands anything in — it is their own page. The rule is announced beforehand: finish early, turn the page over and write three sentences of your own with the unit's grammar. Those get checked in the self-marking stage.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 and tasks 32–38 — the two sections the unit test rehearses in miniature.

Test day is not the day to add anything. The point of the note is for the teacher: the Test yourself page mirrors the two sections of the Russian paper where marks are lost silently — a grammar item where the form is right and the conditional is wrong, and a vocabulary item where the word is right and the spelling is wrong. Both are scored zero in both exams, and both are trainable by naming them out loud beforehand.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
