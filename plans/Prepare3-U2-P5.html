🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that two units work without the book: 42 words, the past simple of irregular verbs, the choice between the two past tenses, and correct spelling.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the gap between oral and written. Out loud the class has the irregular verbs; on paper bought loses its u and cupboard loses its p. And the two past tenses are still chosen by feel rather than by the timeline.
Typical error to expect
becouse · tomorow · alot · wich · beatiful · intresting — the most frequent misspellings; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam In our block that means bougth, recieptionist, cieling, confortable.
I goed to Moscow / I maked two friends instead of went, made — the regular ‑ed ending pasted onto an irregular verb [high priority, A2/B1]. The coursebook itself flags I maked with its «common error» icon on SB p.21 ex.7.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is retrieval practice, not re-teaching: the two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it and what shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which forms still break. This time the stations have a book to lean on — Review 1 on SB p.30–31 covers Units 1–4, so the vocabulary and grammar exercises there are used as the station material rather than something invented. The game sits between the stations and the briefing on purpose: it is the settler that turns revision into a reason to speak, and a class that has just laughed walks into a test calmer. The briefing comes after the stations because only by then do you know which five words belong in the «watch out» column. Nothing new is introduced anywhere today.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. Everything on this test you have already done in this room, and page thirty of your own book is the proof — look at it, it is all Units one to four. First forty-five minutes we play it back. Then forty-five minutes you show it. Your score is not the point: your own list of five mistakes is.”

0–5′
Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast round of the block's words and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last lesson rewind. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade as a hot round: cards for all 42 words of the block, two trades each. Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest: Everything you just said is in today's test.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, read the words out from a list and students write them in pairs
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety dropping. Typical problem: they stumble on receptionist and cupboard — exactly the two that go into the «watch out» column later

5–8′
Lesson frame
board
Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: put the test specification up in advance — transparency is what takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the nine parts of the test on the board, one line each: ways of travelling · holiday words · homes · adjectives · past simple · questions in the past · past continuous · pronunciation by ear · a short story.
  2. Look at the board. Have we done all of this in class? Yes or no? The class answers chorally — the answer is always yes.
  3. Then open the book at p.30 for ten seconds: This is Review one. Units one to four. Same material.
  4. The rule of the day: Your score goes in the register. Your five mistakes go in your notebook. The second one matters more.
📺 On screen: the nine specification lines in chalk — the board is the medium and it stays up for the whole first half of the lesson, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the specification and answer chorally. Expect visible relief when they see Review 1 in their own book. Typical problem — they do not believe «we have done all of it»: ask them to name the lesson for each line

8–20′
Review station 1 · words
teams
Why: Retrieval practice rather than re-teaching: they pull the block's vocabulary out of memory in short bursts, which is what makes it stay.

WHAT WE DO: the block's vocabulary: 42 words, against the clock, orally and then in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, oral, 4 minutes: SB p.30 Review 1 ex.1 — five sets of words, name the category. Then ex.2, eight gapped sentences. Books open, teams answer on the buzzer.
  2. Round 2, written, 6 minutes: Kagan Showdown — you say the word, the team writes it in English and reveals on command. Spelling counts.
  3. Put the traps into round 2 deliberately: receptionist · guidebook · ceiling · cupboard · comfortable · bought.
  4. Every misspelling goes into a «watch out» column on the board and stays there right up to the test.
  5. Two minutes on the sets in ex.3 p.30 if there is time — the nature words are last block's and revising them costs nothing.
📺 On screen: SB p.30 Review 1 on the screen so the whole class works off the same exercise (Backup: everybody works from their own book on p.30 and you dictate the round 2 words from a list; the «watch out» column goes on the board either way)
👦 Students do: answer orally, then write on mini-whiteboards. Expect almost everything orally and a visible drop in the written round. Typical error: cieling — which is exactly why the column is needed
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare3_Block2_Units3-4_Match_2up_A4 + Prepare3_Block2_Units3-4_Translate_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — Russian sentences to be joined to their English pairs (ways of travelling, past simple, holiday people and things, homes, past continuous), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same sentences written out in English from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score in the margin — that is the block's exit line.
When: ~8 min at the start of the “Review station 1 · words” stagePrep: 1 × A4 = 2 pupils (pre-cut, one of each sheet per pair). Glue sticks, pencils.

20–32′
Review station 2 · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar — and it shows you, twenty minutes before the test, exactly which form still breaks.

WHAT WE DO: the block's grammar: the past simple, the question, and the choice between the two past tenses

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. SB p.30 Review 1 Grammar ex.1: four sentences to choose and four to correct, 4 minutes in pairs. Two of them are last block's tenses — leave them in, they cost nothing.
  2. Review 1 Grammar ex.3 — the text about Gabby, past simple and past continuous, 5 minutes. This one is the heart of the station.
  3. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the REASON, not the answer — Long or short? Which one is the background?
  4. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Gap four — number twos, boards up!
  5. Boil every explanation down to the timeline: while = the long arrow, when = the short one crossing it.
📺 On screen: SB p.30 Review 1 on the screen, one exercise at a time (Backup: everybody works from their own book and you draw the two timelines in chalk — they are all the explanation this station needs)
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with a reason, then report by number. Expect the Gabby text to sort the class into two halves. Typical error: past continuous chosen for a completed action because the sentence is long
SB p.30 · mini-whiteboards · board
32–40′
Review game
groups of 4–5
Why: A game that hides revision inside a reason to speak; nerves before a test drop when the last thing they did was laugh.

WHAT WE DO: the game «I think you're lying! (Review game Units 1–4, Cambridge, PHOTOCOPIABLE 2019)» — truths and lies about yourself with the block's words

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Groups of 2–5, one game sheet, one dice. The first roll gives the column, the second the row. The player reads the sentence in that square as if it were true of them («I live on the ground floor») — the others decide whether it is true or a lie. Guess right — 1 point; fool someone — a point for every person you fooled.
  2. ⚠️ Which squares are ours: ⚠️ The sheet covers Units 1–4, and this time most of it is ours. The squares from our block, word for word off the sheet: In the future I want to travel by motorbike · I know someone that travels by boat · When I travel, I always take a suitcase · Last year I travelled by ship · My family takes a lot of luggage when we travel · I think it's easy to follow a map · I come to school on foot · Last year I went on a tram · I think helicopters are dangerous · I have a scooter · I went on holiday by plane last year · I don't enjoy going to school by bike · I was lying on the sofa last night · My house hasn't got a balcony · My house has a garage · There are two lamps in my bedroom · I live on the ground floor · There is always lots of food in our cupboard · We have a gas cooker at home. The Units 1–2 squares (personality, animals, geography) are last block's — keep them for the warm-up round, they revise too.
  3. Use the first variation from the teacher's page: Three ready variations printed on the teacher's page, and the first two are worth using: Explain — the player has to add one more fact after the sentence (I live on the ground floor. My flat has three bedrooms.), inventing it if they are lying; Ask me! — before guessing, the others may ask one question, which turns the game into real speaking.
  4. Set the bar: Your sentence must be a full sentence, and the extra fact must be a full sentence too.
  5. Extension from the game book: at the end, write down as many TRUE sentences about classmates as you can remember.
📺 On screen: one printed game sheet per group, run off in advance — paper is the medium here, so no backup is needed; with no printer, write ten of our sentences on the board and roll the dice for the line number
👦 Students do: play, guess and argue. Expect the liveliest stage of the review half. Typical error: they answer with one word instead of a sentence — the «Explain» variation removes it

40–45′
Test briefing
class
Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions for the test

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go back to the «watch out» column: the words that were misspelt. Look at these words for thirty seconds. Then I close them.
  2. Instructions: 45 minutes, nine parts, 78 marks; phones in the bag; finished early — part 9, the short story. Part 8 is done TOGETHER at the start: you read the words out, so nobody can be late for it.
  3. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  4. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; everything else in the room is switched off
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect silence to settle in 20 seconds if the flat hand goes up before you start talking

47–85′
Block test
solo, silent mode
Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats. Everything in the paper has been done in class, so the test measures learning, not surprise.

WHAT WE DO: the block 2 test: nine parts, 78 marks, 38 minutes (the first 5 are the part 8 dictation)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. You have thirty-eight minutes. Start.
  2. Walk round the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  3. Write the time left in chalk: 20 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  4. Part 8 (pronunciation) comes first and runs to your dictation — 5 minutes, after which students work on their own. Anyone who finishes early writes part 9.
  5. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; nothing at all is displayed in the room while the test is running
👦 Students do: work in silence for 38 minutes. Expect parts 1–3 to be closed fast and part 7 (the two past tenses) to slow them down. Typical error: they skip part 9, taking it for an optional extra — which is why it is on the specification on the board

85–90′
Test wrap-up
circle
Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip of paper, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the feedback lesson.
  3. Homework: rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly once the papers come back; for now — the Vocabulary or Grammar worksheet Unit 3–4 if anything is still open.
  4. Teaser: Next lesson we open page one hundred and twenty-three — one long text, five questions, and one of them is not about a paragraph at all.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is full, read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to be about the past continuous most often — that is the plan for the next feedback clinic

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
