🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): Here will finally gets its form — but it is presented not as “the topic of the lesson” but as the working language of a forecasting bureau. Students have already seen will in the blog in Pair 2 and argued about teleportation, so the table on p.100 answers a question that has already come up: “how does this actually work?”. The error clinic for Pairs 1–2 (will to go, will goes, we staying) runs here — everything logged in silence is finally worked through. The Brain teaser and Pronunciation p.117 work as two different kinds of challenge: logic and ear.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Bureau of Predictions, 2100 desk. A real expert from the University of Glasgow says that one day we will travel by teleportation. Underwater hotels by 2030. Planes with 360° windows by 2050. Your crew writes six predictions about holidays — and then defends them, because the room will attack every one. One tool only: will and won't. Wrong form, prediction rejected. The future opens in ninety minutes.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: a forecasting bureau: a real scientist from the University of Glasgow predicts travel by teleportation. The crew writes six predictions about holidays of the future and defends them — the class attacks every one. One tool only: will and won't. A broken form means the prediction is rejected.

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: as many predictions about their life in 10 years as possible; spelling and form both count

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

⚡ Base sprint · the prediction desk
crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 complete the aff/neg table → ex.2 four predictions → ex.3 the question tables → ex.5 build the questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1: Copy the table and put these two in: 'We will eat VR food in the future' and 'It won't cost a lot of money'. Which column? (key, TB: 1 We will eat VR food in the future. · 2 It won't cost a lot of money.)
  2. ex.2 against the clock (key, TB: 1 will have · 2 won't need, will have · 3 won't take, will use · 4 will use). TB tip: with less confident students do the first affirmative and the first negative together.
  3. ex.3 the tables of questions and short answers (key, TB: 1 Will it be expensive? · 2 No, it won't. · 3 What will this cost?).
  4. Build the tables through Kagan RoundTable: one sheet per crew, passed round the circle — you get the sheet, write in ONE line and pass it on. While the sheet is with you, only you write; you may not pass it on empty. By the end of the circle the table has been completed by everybody's hands, not by one strong student.
  5. ex.5 building questions from words (key, TB: 1 Who will you speak to next on the phone? · 2 What will the weather be like tomorrow? · 3 What time will you go to bed tonight? · 4 What job will you have when you leave school?) — then straight into ex.6 in pairs, Kagan RallyRobin.
📺 On screen: SB p.100 ex.1–3, 5–6 · mini-whiteboards · one sheet per crew for RoundTable · timer
👦 Students do: they complete the tables themselves from the examples. Expect: the short answer “Yes, they will” with no verb feels odd at first — CCQ “what is missing here and why is that fine?”

Challenge: the 2100 forecast bureau
🔥 Challenge · 2100 bureau
crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.7 the text “How will holidays change in the future?” against the clock + a duel of predictions, 8 seconds per turn

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pre-teach from the TB: teleportation = moving people or objects instantly from one place to another.
  2. Before reading (TB routine): In your crew, predict how holidays will change in 10, 30 and 80 years. Thirty seconds. Then check against the text.
  3. ex.7 against the clock (key, TB: 1 will become · 2 will look · 3 will have · 4 will we travel · 5 won't take · 6 how will we travel).
  4. The duel between crews: One prediction each, eight seconds, and the next crew must answer with a SHORT answer: 'No, they won't.' Then hit back. The constraint: repeat somebody else's idea and you lose your turn.
  5. Inside the crew the turn moves by Kagan RoundRobin: predictions go strictly round the circle, one each, no jumping the circle. Whoever has spoken stays quiet until the next round, so all six go into the duel, not just the captain.
  6. ex.4 as fuel for the argument: Do you agree with the predictions in exercise 2? If not — what WILL happen instead?
📺 On screen: SB p.100 ex.4, 7 · 8-second timer
👦 Students do: they race through the text and duel with short answers. Expect: teleportation causes a real argument — hold it for 60 seconds and bring them back into format

Error clinic · Pairs 1–2
board · crews

WHAT WE DO: what you have been logging silently for two pairs: eight sentences with errors — fix them against the clock through Showdown

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two lessons I said nothing. Here's what I collected. No names — these are OUR sentences.
  2. Eight lines from their real work go on the board (typical examples): People will to travel by teleportation. · She will goes to the beach. · Will you to come? · He willn't come. · I want go to the beach. · We will to stay in a hotel. · It will be maybe expensive. · Will people goes to the Moon?
  3. The crews fix them against the clock through Kagan Showdown: you give a line — everyone fixes it on their own mini-whiteboard in silence, on Showdown! they all go up at once, and only then do you explain WHY. That way everybody does the fixing, not whoever shouted first.
  4. One picture for all of it: will + clean verb. No 's'. No 'to'. Ever.
📺 On screen: the clinic slide · mini-whiteboards (Backup: write the eight lines on the board by hand, students fix them in their notebooks and come out to correct them with a marker)
👦 Students do: they fix other people's errors and recognise their own. Expect: relief — “I thought it was only me”

PRODUCT · the 2050 forecast
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: six crew predictions (two each for 2030 · 2050 · 2100) + a defence under questions from the class

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes to prepare: Six predictions. Two per year. Each one: a year, will or won't, and ONE concrete detail — a number, a place, a thing.
  2. Roles: every member of the crew delivers two predictions.
  3. Then the room attacks. Two questions per crew — and they must start with 'Will' or a question word.
  4. The defence rule: Answer short first — 'Yes, we will' — then explain.
  5. Judges use the rubric; a separate award: the boldest prediction the room couldn't break.
📺 On screen: timer · judges' cards · the rubric
👦 Students do: they perform and hold the line. Expect: the questions from the class are the most valuable part; that is exactly where the question form falls into place

WB Unit 8 · the error clinic stays on the wall until the end of the unit
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can make, deny and question predictions with will/won't (affirmative, negative, Yes/No and Wh-questions, short answers) and defend six predictions under attack from the class.
  • Subsidiary. The tables in ex.1 and ex.3; ex.2 four predictions; ex.5 building questions; ex.7 the text “How will holidays change in the future?” (2030 · 2050 · 2100); ex.8 Brain teaser; Pronunciation p.117 — 'll and telling I go from I'll go (tracks 79–81); the error clinic for Pairs 1–2.
  • Personal. The word “grammar” is never said: this is “the language of the bureau”. Students complete the tables themselves from the examples — you do not dictate. During the defence a broken form is flagged by the CLASS, not by you.
By the end, students can…
  • make positive and negative predictions with will/won't
  • ask Yes/No and Wh-questions with will and answer them short
  • hear the difference between 'I go' and 'I'll go'
  • defend six predictions against questions from the class

Structure
People will have holidays in space. · We won't need a passport. · We will eat VR food in the future. · It won't cost a lot of money. · Will we visit the Moon? — Yes, we will. / No, we won't. · Will it be expensive? — No, it won't. · Where will we travel? · What will this cost?
Form
will/won't + the infinitive WITHOUT to, one form for every person · question — (Question word) + will + subject + verb · short answer Yes, they will. / No, they won't.
Pron. watch
'll: I'll /aɪl/ · he'll /hiːl/ · she'll /ʃiːl/ · we'll /wiːl/ · they'll /ðeɪl/ · and the trap won't /wəʊnt/ vs want /wɒnt/ — tell them apart by what comes next (after want the infinitive keeps to)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«People will to travel by teleportation» — what is the extra word? → to: will + verb without to.
«Will she goes?» — correct it. → Will she go? — after will always the bare form.
«I'll go» and «I go» — do they sound the same? → no: in I'll you hear /aɪl/, the vowel is longer.

Assumptions: SB p.100 + Pronunciation p.117, syllabus pair 64. They have heard will both in the Pair 2 blog and in A1+; what is new is the question form, the short answers and hearing 'll.

“Will to go” and “will goes”The clinic in the middle of the pair: the errors collected in Pairs 1–2 go on the board with no names, and the crews fix them against the clock. One picture for the rule: will + a clean verb.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
