Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /r/ sound spelt wr (Ron the robber writes on the red wrapper — “wr says r, the w is silent”), talk about an underground town in Australia, and read & plan a script — three mini-blocks, one detective thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that wr spells /r/ with a silent w (recognise → say → sort); culture — an underground town in Australia (My town blog) + Channel 21 video, 21C: paying attention; literacy — text type a script (characters · stage directions · exclamation marks), read «The missing necklace» & plan a scene.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (chant/drum bridges); the robber tongue-twister is play; cap each block hard so the script plan actually gets written. Silent w in wr — cross it out, don't say it.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the robber /r/ twister and sort wr / r words;
  • say 2–3 facts about the underground town & compare with our homes;
  • find characters & stage directions in a script and plan a scene.

Structure

Form
/r/ = r (red, robber) AND wr with a silent w (write, wrist, wrong, wrapper). Script: NAME + (stage direction) + line; exclamation marks show feeling — Hands up!
Pron. watch
silent w in wr — write /raɪt/, wrist /rɪst/, wrong /rɒŋ/; contrast red /r/ vs write /r/ (same sound!); Australia /ɒˈstreɪliə/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“write and red — same first SOUND? Same first LETTER?” → same sound /r/, different letters (w is silent!).
“A script — is it a story to READ quietly, or words for actors to SAY?” → words for actors to say, with actions.

Assumptions: was/were, belongings & the story are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from earlier phonics is enough to enjoy the wr / r noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short script lines with support.

Block 1 · Phonics /r/ = wr
carpet → desks

Listen, read and say (act. 1, CD3 35). PB p.77: wr says r — the w is silent! Ron the robber writes on the red wrapper. Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort r / wr. Two hoops/columns: r (red, robber, ring, rain) · wr (write, wrist, wrong, wrapper). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card.

Notice the rule. Same sound /r/ — but wr has a SILENT w. Cross it out! (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more wr word (wrap, wren).

✔ Assess: who still says the silent w? Note them — extra RallyRead next unit.

Block 3 · Literacy · a script
carpet → desks, pairs

Before you read (act. 1–3, CD3 37). PB p.78 «The missing necklace: Scene 1»: Who are the characters? What are they talking about? Read/listen — a police officer investigates Lady Muck's missing necklace.

Investigate the script (PB p.79, act. 3). Find: Where are the characters' names? How many ways do they speak? What actions do they do? Notice stage directions (whispering) (nervously) (angrily) & exclamation marks — Hands up!

▶ Perform a snippet (Talk Partners). Pairs read 4 lines of Scene 1 aloud WITH the stage directions acted out. Swap roles. 2 pairs show the class.

▶ Plan a script (Cooperative learning, AB p.76 format). Pairs plan a short mystery scene: Characters · What's missing? · Where was it? · A funny line. Make notes; 2 pairs share one line aloud.

💪 Support: fill the script plan template with word support. ⭐ Extension: write the full scene with 2 stage directions + an exclamation.
