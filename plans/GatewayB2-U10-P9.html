🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Close the course's language work: repair Units 6–10 at the Reach higher stations (SB p.138–140), present the Collaborative projects launched two lessons ago, and recycle the year as one system through a team quiz and a speaking carousel — so that nobody walks into the mock paper having last spoken English a week ago.
What is genuinely hard
Everything today is retrieval, and retrieval after eight months feels like failure to a student who was fluent in March. Expect the passive of Unit 8 and the reported speech of Unit 7 to have gone soft, and expect the relative clauses of Unit 9 to come back as two sentences joined with and. The organisational difficulty is different: five projects, a quiz and a carousel in one lesson only fit if every stage starts on the minute, so the clock is the real lesson plan today.
Typical error to expect
«I wonder where is he», «Can you tell me what time does the train leave?» — in an indirect question the subject comes BEFORE the verb and the auxiliary disappears. Russian keeps the same word order in both, so nothing warns the student. This is the most frequent single error in the whole unit and it is worth five points on the Test yourself page.
Why the lesson is built this way
The same station machine as last lesson, so no instructions are needed and the fourteen minutes are all work. Then the projects — presented and evaluated on the book's own five categories, which keeps the feedback specific and short. Then the year as a team quiz, which is the only competitive element of the block and is placed where energy dips. Then fourteen minutes of Inside–Outside Circle in which every student speaks continuously and answers a different question every ninety seconds. The last ten minutes are the mock briefing: what the paper is, how long each part runs, what to bring, and what happens if you are ill — said calmly, because a briefing given now removes the panic that would otherwise arrive next lesson.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“This is the last lesson of the course where we learn language. After today it is papers. So today we finish the repairs, you show the projects you have been building, we run the whole year as one quiz, and everybody speaks for a solid quarter of an hour. Then I tell you exactly what next Monday's paper looks like — no surprises, ever.”

Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that also sets up the quiz: one sentence per unit, round the circle

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands in one big circle, books left on the desks.
  2. Round the circle, one student per unit, in order 1 to 10: one true sentence containing the grammar of that unit. If a student is stuck, the person on their left gives a clue — not the sentence.
  3. Second lap, faster, and this time the sentence must be about somebody in this room. Laughter is fine and is part of the point.
  4. Straight into project teams from the circle, and the first group is already at the front.

👦 Students do: produce one grammar sentence each in a fast circle, twice round

WHAT WE DO: each group presents its project on the press and the news in Russia, and every other group evaluates it on the book's five categories

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three minutes per group plus one minute of feedback. Hold the clock visibly — with five groups this stage is exactly eighteen minutes and it has no slack.
  2. Before the first group, set the audience task, because an audience with no task is a corridor: every student marks each project from 1 to 5 on the five categories from p.135 — Content, Presentation, Design, Language — and writes ONE sentence of specific praise.
  3. The rule for a group that is not ready: three minutes orally from notes, evaluated on Content and Language only. Announce it before the first presentation.
  4. After each group, take two pieces of feedback from the audience, and require the shape: One thing that worked was … One thing I would add is …
  5. Your own feedback is one sentence per group and it is about LANGUAGE, not about the poster: a collocation used well, a structure from the unit that appeared, one error that appeared twice.
  6. Collect the evaluation sheets at the end — they are the mark for the project and they took the class no extra time.
📺 On screen: the groups that prepared a presentation or a video message will want the projector. The year in one quiz · ten rounds, ten units
teams of four
Why: The year in one quiz · ten rounds, ten units

WHAT WE DO: a fast team quiz across all ten units, run with Numbered Heads so that every team member has to be ready

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Teams of four, one point per correct answer, and the mechanic that keeps everybody in: twenty seconds of team discussion, then you call a number and THAT student answers. No hands.
  2. Ten rounds, one per unit, two questions each, mixed grammar and vocabulary. Unit 1: DO or MAKE — an effort? · Give me a sentence with the present perfect continuous about this class. Unit 2: Somebody who has lost their job because it no longer exists is …? · Past perfect: why were her hands dirty?
  3. Unit 3 travel and future forms · Unit 4 personality adjectives and comparatives · Unit 5 money idioms and past modals · Unit 6 health and wish · Unit 7 film and reported speech · Unit 8 disasters and the passive · Unit 9 technology and relative clauses · Unit 10 headlines and question tags.
  4. One bonus round at the end, worth three points and worth the time: Which unit did you enjoy most, and give one sentence of its language to prove you remember it. Every team answers; every team scores.
  5. Keep score on the board. Announce the winner in one sentence and move on — the quiz is a retrieval machine, not a tournament.
📺 On screen: the running score written on the board — the questions are read aloud, so there is nothing to display and nothing to fail
👦 Students do: discuss every question as a team and answer individually when their number is called

Inside–Outside Circle
Why: Speaking carousel · fourteen minutes on their feet

WHAT WE DO: the year's speaking functions recycled with a new partner every ninety seconds, everybody talking at once

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Inside–Outside Circle: half the class in an inner circle facing out, half in an outer circle facing in. Ninety seconds per pair, then the outer circle moves two places to the left.
  2. Eight rotations, one function each, taken from the year's Developing speaking pages: comparing two photographs · agreeing and disagreeing · negotiating a decision · giving advice · telling a short story · presenting an opinion with a concession · asking for and giving information · reacting to news.
  3. Every card has a question AND a follow-up printed under it, so that a pair who finish early are never stuck: Which of these two photos shows a better way to travel? → And which would YOU choose, and why?
  4. Model one exchange with a strong student before the first rotation. Twenty seconds, and it doubles the length of every conversation that follows.
  5. You listen, you do not correct, and you write down five things for the last stage. Two of them should be good.
  6. Last rotation, and say it as you start it: This is the last thing we do as a class before the exam papers. Make it a good one.
📺 On screen: the eight function cards laid on the desks in advance — no equipment at all, and nothing that can break
👦 Students do: speak continuously for fourteen minutes with eight different partners across eight different functions
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains the EGE oral section, and tasks 1–38 as a whole. Answer, then reason, then example: the two-part answer rehearsed at every rotation is the difference between a thin response and a full one on the Russian speaking card, and the follow-up printed on each card is the second question a Russian examiner will ask.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: in rotations seven and eight, replace the card with one Russian-format monologue plan: ninety seconds of silent preparation, then ninety seconds of delivery to the partner. It replaces two rotations and costs no extra time.

Structure
the five grammar lines of Units 6–10, produced not recognised: conditionals and wish · reported speech and reporting verbs · the passive and impersonal passive · relative clauses · indirect questions and question tags
Form
no new form. Every station prompt is bare — rewrite using the passive, use the person as the subject — and the student supplies everything else. In the carousel the form is whatever the function needs, which is the only test that matters at the end of a year.
Pron. watch
in the quiz and the carousel the group will speed up and lose the ends of words: the -ed of reported verbs (claimed /kleɪmd/, insisted /ɪnˈsɪstɪd/), the weak forms in the passive (it is said to /ɪts sed tə/), and the falling tag against the rising tag. Correct these two or three times in public during the carousel, briefly, then let them run.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Toads are said to sense earthquakes» — who says it? → nobody in particular, and that is the whole point of the structure.
«Remote controls, which first appeared in 1956, were called space commands» — can you remove the part between the commas and still have a sentence? → yes. So is it defining or non-defining? → non-defining, and the commas are compulsory.
«I'd like to know whether you enjoyed it» — where does the question mark go? → nowhere; it is a statement. And «Could you tell me where it is?» → question mark at the end, because the first half is the question.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
One group arrives with nothing prepared for the projectThey present orally, in three minutes, from notes made in the first station block, and they are evaluated on Content and Language only. Announce that rule at the start of the presentation stage so that it looks like a policy and not a punishment.
The quiz becomes loud and two teams stop workingNumbered Heads, not hands up: every question is discussed by the whole team for twenty seconds, and then you call a number. A team never knows who will answer, so nobody switches off. Points go to the answer AND to the whole team being ready.

When the first answer ends, the follow-up is already there. Model it once with a strong student before you start — twenty seconds, and it doubles the length of every exchange.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
