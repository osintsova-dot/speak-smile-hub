Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /s/ sound (Cecil snake sang danced city — “sometimes c says s”), talk about Canada Day celebrations, and read & plan an email about a festival — three mini-blocks, one “celebrate” thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that s and soft c spell the same /s/ sound (recognise → say → sort); culture — Canada Day (parade, Mounties, lumberjack show) + Channel 21 school-fete video, 21C: working in a group; literacy — text type an email, using but for opposite information, plan & write a festival email.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (chant/drum bridges); the Cecil tongue-twister is play; cap each block hard so the email plan actually gets written.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the Cecil /s/ tongue-twister and sort s / soft-c words;
  • name 2–3 Canada Day activities and compare with ours;
  • find the key facts in an email and plan one with a partner.

Structure
(Cecil) the (snake) (sang) and (danced) at the (city) (celebration).  ·  On Canada Day people (watch a parade).  ·  email: Do you know that we celebrate (May Day)? Last festival, I…
Form
/s/ = s (snake, sang, sun) AND soft c before e/i/y (Cecil, city, celebration, danced, circle). Email: greeting → introduce festival → describe activities → question → sign off; use but for opposite info.
Pron. watch
/s/ clean in Cecil/city/celebration; contrast c /s/ (city) vs c /k/ (car); lumberjack /ˈlʌmbədʒæk/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“city and snake — same first SOUND? Same first LETTER?” → same sound /s/, different letters!
“An email — is it for a quick message to a friend, or a long book?” → a quick message to a friend.

Assumptions: Attractions, past questions & time are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from earlier units is enough to enjoy the s / soft-c noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short email texts with support.

Block 1 · Phonics /s/
carpet → desks

Listen, read and say (act. 1, CD4 23). PB p.97: Sometimes ‘c’ says ‘s’. Cecil the snake sang and danced at the city celebration! Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort s / soft-c. Two hoops/columns: s (snake, sang, sun, summer) · c-says-s (Cecil, city, celebration, danced, circle). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card.

Notice the rule. When does c say s? Look — city, Cecil, celebration… c before e, i, y! (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more /s/ word to a hoop (e.g. face, ice, pencil).

✔ Assess: who still reads soft c as /k/? Note them — extra RallyRead next term.

Block 3 · Literacy · an email
carpet → desks, pairs

Before you read (act. 1–3, CD4 25). PB p.98 Jodie's email (May Day): Do you send emails? What do you think this email is about? Read/listen to find out.

▶ Email scavenger hunt. Race in pairs to find in the email: What festival? What did she do in the morning? …the afternoon? …the evening? First hands up read the answer aloud — everyone scans.

Investigate a text (PB p.99, CD4 26). Mary's reply (Burns Night): the order (hello → introduce festival → describe → question → goodbye). Writing tip: We use ‘but’ for opposite information — I sang songs, BUT I didn't dance!

▶ Plan an email (Cooperative learning, AB p.96 format). Pairs choose a festival & make notes: Name of festival · Date · I went to… · I saw… · I ate… · It was… 2 pairs share one fact aloud.

💪 Support: fill the plan template with word support. ⭐ Extension: write the full email with a but sentence.
