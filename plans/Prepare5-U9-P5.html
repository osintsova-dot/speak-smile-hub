🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Consolidate the block: the communication verbs, the twelve adjectives, reported questions and have something done — and then produce all four under test conditions, on the two official Cambridge papers.
What is genuinely hard
The test is not hard because the language is hard; it is hard because two of the eight sections run in a direction the class has not practised. Unit 17 Grammar 3 goes BACKWARDS, from the reported question to the direct one, and habit will make half the room report it again. Unit 18 Grammar 4 changes the tense of have from item to item, so the pattern has to be rebuilt eight times rather than copied once.
Typical error to expect
«He asked me where was I», «She asked what is the time» — a reported question is not a question any more. The word order goes back to subject + verb, and the question mark disappears with it. Russian keeps the inversion, so this one is produced by every single group until it is drilled aloud.
«I cut my hair yesterday» for a haircut at a hairdresser's, «I did my car repaired» — English marks the difference between doing it and paying someone: have + object + PAST PARTICIPLE, in that order. Russian has one verb for both, so the structure is learned as a pattern.
Why the lesson is built this way
Forty-five minutes of review, forty-five of testing, and the review half is built around the block's own review game rather than around a worksheet, because the game rehearses vocabulary AND grammar inside a sentence rather than in isolation. Before the game, the four Review 5 sections that belong to this block are done fast and checked publicly — vocabulary first because it warms the room, then the two grammar sections, then the mixed-option section, which is the closest thing on the page to the test. The briefing before the papers names the two traps out loud, by section number, because a surprise on a test day is a lost mark, not a lesson.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Everything on today's two papers has been done in this room, and I will tell you exactly where. First forty-five minutes: we play the block back. Second forty-five: you write it down on your own.”

standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer between the review and the game

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands in one line down the room, in any order.
  2. You call a word from the block — apologise, delighted, unless, stressful, despite, wonder, professional, instead of. The line passes it along: each student in turn says a WHOLE sentence containing it, and it may not repeat anyone else's.
  3. Two passes down the line, eight words, everybody speaks at least twice. Anyone stuck says Pass and gets the next word — no shame, no waiting.
  4. Freeze and sit in groups of three: Now the same words, but nobody knows what the next person is going to write.
📺 On screen: no technology in this stage — voice and feet only, and nothing can fail
👦 Students do: every student produces at least two whole sentences aloud, standing

Why: Review game · Crazy Sentences

WHAT WE DO: the block's own review game, played with the Units 17–18 cards only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. «CRAZY SENTENCES!» — PREPARE_5_Review_game_Units_17-20.pdf, the consequences game, and it is the best fifteen minutes of the block. Groups of three or more, one set of cut-up sentence cards per group, one blank A4 sheet and a pen each. One player reads the FIRST part of a sentence card aloud — The charming ______ told me… — and everyone secretly writes a word to fill the gap at the top of their own sheet, folds the paper over so it cannot be seen, and passes it to the left. The next part is read, everyone writes, folds, passes. Three gaps per card, three passes. Then the sheets are unfolded and each crazy sentence is read out.
  2. Which cards to use today. The pack covers Units 17–20, so pre-sort it: keep only the cards whose gaps live in Units 17–18 (the communication verbs, the adjectives of feeling, reported questions, have something done, the prepositions and conjunctions) and put the Units 19–20 cards aside for lesson 68. Scoring is optional and the Teacher's Notes say so: at the end each group reads out one sentence and the class awards points for originality, humour, vocabulary and — this is the part that makes it a language game — grammar and spelling. The printed follow-up is worth three minutes: with only the gaps in front of them, students rebuild the whole sentence from memory in their notebooks.
  3. Demonstrate one full card with the whole class first — read part one, everybody writes, everybody folds, everybody passes. Sixty seconds of demonstration saves five minutes of confusion.
  4. Play four cards, about eight minutes. Then two minutes of reading out and voting.
  5. Award the points out loud and name the reason each time — originality, humour, vocabulary, and grammar. The grammar point is what makes it a review rather than a party.
📺 On screen: no technology in this stage — cut-up cards and folded paper only, and nothing can fail
👦 Students do: everybody writes on every card, everybody reads a finished sentence aloud, and the group decides together

WHAT WE DO: four minutes that decide the marks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Papers face down, one Unit 17 and one Unit 18 per student, in that order.
  2. Take the frames off the board now, in front of them, and say so: The support was there for four lessons. From now it is in your head.
  3. Say the shape: two papers, four sections each, eight items per section, sixty-four items in about forty minutes. Nothing is new.
  4. Name the two traps one final time, in one sentence each, and point at the two board examples from the frame stage before rubbing them out.
  5. Rules: silence, pens only, read the rubric of every section before you start it, and if you finish early you CHECK — you do not turn over.
  6. Say the honest thing about marks, because it changes behaviour: This tells me what to reteach. It does not go on a report.

👦 Students do: put everything away, take the two papers face down, and hear the rules once

WHAT WE DO: thirty-four minutes of silent testing, both papers, no support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Start both papers together and write the finish time on the board. Unit 17 first, Unit 18 second; anyone who finishes 17 early moves straight on to 18.
  2. Halfway call at seventeen minutes, said once and quietly: Half the time. You should be starting the second paper.
  3. Circulate slowly and read over shoulders, but say nothing about answers. The only thing you correct is a misread rubric — if somebody is writing sentences where the paper wants one word, that is worth a whisper.
  4. Five-minute warning, then a two-minute checking call: Two minutes. Nobody is writing anything new. Read your reported questions and check the word order.
  5. Collect face down, in one pile, by row. Do not discuss answers in the room afterwards — the four minutes at the end belong to the next lesson, not to this one.
📺 On screen: the finish time written on the board, and nothing else — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: sit two official papers in silence and check their own work in the final two minutes

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can use the language of Units 17–18 accurately under test conditions — eight sections, sixty-four items, no support.
  • Subsidiary. Self-assessment against a topic list; playing a sentence-completion game that recycles both units; exam-day routine — reading the rubric, watching the clock, checking at the end.

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«She asked him if he had come by bike» → back to a direct question. What do you say? → Did you come by bike? The reverse direction is on the paper and it is worth rehearsing once.
«Someone is going to record Anna's new song» → Anna is going to have her new song recorded. Which word changes tense? → have, never the participle.
Test rubric check: how many words in each gap? Read it aloud before you start. Every year somebody writes two.

Block 9 test — what is in the two official papers (Cambridge 2019, both in the course folder with answer keys)
SectionContent
Unit 17 test · Vocabulary 1eight definitions matched to the communication verbs (remind, disagree, promise, complain, wonder, apologise, warn, joke). Pure meaning — no context to lean on.
Unit 17 test · Vocabulary 2eight sentences rewritten with the adverb of degree dropped into the right SLOT (fairly good, pretty nice, reasonably early, quite expensive, do fairly well, reasonably happy, quite busy, pretty easy). This section tests position, not meaning.
Unit 17 test · Grammar 3eight reported questions turned BACK into direct ones (Did you come, Where's, Can you start, Do you know, Why were you, Where are you going, Will Maria like, Who are you texting) — the reverse direction, which is harder and which is why it is worth naming in the briefing.
Unit 17 test · Grammar 4eight direct questions reported, with the verb supplied (where I was, if he had spoken, whether the plane would arrive, if we could swim, whether I had seen, why she had said, how they had managed, if I was coming).
Unit 18 test · Vocabulary 1eight adjectives of feeling dropped into contexts (delighted, shy, nasty, charming, rude, lonely, professional, mad).
Unit 18 test · Vocabulary 2eight three-option items on the prepositions and conjunctions (instead of, because of, despite, According to, despite, Besides, instead of, besides) — the section that separates a noun-taker from a clause-taker.
Unit 18 test · Grammar 3eight sentences built from jumbled words in the have-something-done pattern (Sonia has had her hair cut; Charlie had his camera repaired last week; Mike likes having his face painted; Jill is going to have her car fixed; Jack is having his dinner delivered; Ann often has her picture taken; Ian has had his bike mended; Helen may have her nails painted).
Unit 18 test · Grammar 4eight sentences rewritten from «Someone cleans Sandra's windows» into have something done, across five different tenses — the section where the marks actually go.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The block test is built the way the Russian exam block is: produce the form and spell it right. A reported question with the wrong word order, or an adjective of feeling swapped for its neighbour, scores nothing at all — which is why the briefing names both ghosts aloud before the papers go face down.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
