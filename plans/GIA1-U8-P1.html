WHAT WE DO: 10 definitions from the slide → write the word in 3 min + 3 sentences on the preposition; self-check against the key, private scoring

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Slide with 10 definitions (based on SB p.97 ex.3): It takes people from one city to another (a bus, but bigger) · It takes people to and from hospital · It's the system of trains under a city · It carries things on roads · It's similar to a train but travels on streets in a city…

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can deliver a 60-sec «transport report»: 6+ types of transport used accurately with by/on foot, 3 facts from the scooter article and at least one past simple negative (They didn't plan to start a sport).
  • Subsidiary. The gate exposes the real ceiling — coach/lorry/tram/underground and the preposition on foot; the 22-word set goes in at speed; Video skills (Nadiya, Bangladesh) is watched with a producer's tasks; the pp.98–99 text is taken as a quest with the subskill “asking questions before you read”; didn't is noticed in context (highlighted yellow), NOT explained.
  • Personal. Anti-“we already know transport”: everybody knows bus/car/plane, but coach vs bus, lorry, tram, on foot — no. The unit leaderboard starts today. Teaser: this is the last content unit of the year, next comes Unit 9 — the final review.
By the end, students can…
  • name 15+ types of transport and 6 journey words (driver, pilot, passenger…)
  • use by bus / by car BUT on foot without slips
  • ask their own question before reading and find the answer in the article
  • report 3 scooter facts + 1 negative to the 60-sec bar

Structure
I came to school by bus. · I didn't come on foot. · It's faster than a bike because it's got an engine. · Scooters didn't become popular until later.
Form
by + transport (by bus/car/plane), BUT on foot · ON — bike/bus/train/plane/tram/underground · IN — car/taxi/ambulance/lorry/helicopter (TB p.110 ABCD).
Pron. watch
/aɪ/ in pilot · /dʒ/ in passenger and journey · /ɪt/ in ticket · /əʊ/ in coach and boat (the same!) · /ʌ/ in bus.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I go to school by foot?” → No — ON foot. The one exception preposition.
“A coach and a bus — the same?” → A coach takes people from one city to ANOTHER; a bus — around a city.
“In the report, is ‘Scooters are cool’ a fact?” → No, opinion. Bar: 3 FACTS + 1 opinion.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: past simple affirmative and was/were (U7) are active, plane/car/bus have been known since primary school. The real gaps: coach/lorry/tram/underground/travelcard, the prepositions by/on foot, long connected speech on a fact. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (didn't + infinitive / question word order / superlative spelling), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
