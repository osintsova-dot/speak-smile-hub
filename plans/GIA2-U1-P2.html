Get Involved! A2 · Unit 1 “School life” · Pair 2 — Reading & Critical Thinking
A2 teens (~12–14 y.o., 6–8 grade, confident readers) · 90 min · Text-based skills lesson (predict → read → discuss) · SB pp.14–15
Week 21.09–27.09 · Pair 6 (lessons 11–12)Reading + Critical thinkingLine GIA2 «наши» · continuing A2~12 pupils · 3 teams of 4
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. Develop reading skills on the article “A day in the life of an online student” (SB pp.14–15): apply the sub-skill predicting from title & pictures, read for gist, then read for detail (True/False + evidence, sentence completion).
  • Subsidiary. Reactivate daily-routine lexis (Pair 1); teach 6 Word-work items in context (grades, subjects, library, log on, classmates, school trips); build a critical-thinking discussion — compare online vs traditional school, weigh advantages/disadvantages with an argument frame.
  • Personal. Keep TTT low; model the reading strategy with a think-aloud; make every learner accountable through Kagan structures (no “2 talk, 10 watch”); insert a stirrer at the ~45′ mid-point.
By the end, pupils can…
  • predict a text's content from its title & pictures, and read for gist and for detail;
  • find evidence in a text to justify a True/False answer;
  • use the 6 target words to talk about school life;
  • state one advantage & one disadvantage of online school with a reason.

Discussion frames
I think … is an advantage/disadvantage because … · Both online and traditional schools … · One difference is that …
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
grades — “Are these the numbers/letters that show how good your work is?” → yes. “Is a grade a class of pupils?” → no (that's a class).
log on — “Do you log on before or after you open the computer?” → you log on to START using it.
classmates vs class trips — “Classmates = people or places?” → people, in your class.
Predicting — “Do we read every word first, or look at the title & pictures first?” → title & pictures first.

Assumptions: daily-routine vocab is known from Pair 1; pupils are confident readers who can skim; Present simple met in Starter (be/have got) & will be formally studied next pair. Groups: teams of 3–4 (12 = 3×4 · 10 = 3+3+4 · 8 = 2×4). No notebook insert this lesson: P2 = reading pair — the interactive-notebook catalogue carries no cut-out sheet for it (Visual Takeaways sit on the vocab/grammar pairs).

Classroom management — signals
“Class? — Yes!” — you: “Class!” → pupils stop & echo “Yes!”
Countdown — “Reading time in 5…4…3…2…1 — pens down, eyes on the text.”
Volume control — raise/lower arm = louder/quieter for pair talk.
Numbered pupils — each team member is 1–4; call a number for accountable answers.
“Evidence, please” — hand out, palm up = quote the line from the text.
“Take a stand” — one wall = AGREE, other = DISAGREE for opinion moves.

Lead-in · activate
seats → pairs

Retrieval warmer (TB). Books closed. Yesterday we learnt the parts of a school day. In pairs, 60 seconds — write as many daily-routine actions as you can. Go! Then ▶ RallyRobin — partners take turns saying one action each until they run out. Quick board tally: which pair got the most?

Hook. Some teenagers never go to school — but they still study every day. How? Today's text tells us. Do you think online school is better or worse than ours? Keep that in your head.

Vocab into pictures · ex.1–2
seats, SB p.14

Ex.1. Open SB p.14. Match the words in the box with pictures A–J. Pairs. Check chorally. Key: A have lunch · B have a break · C get up · D go to sleep · E get dressed · F go to school · G start school · H wake up · I go home · J pack my bag.

Ex.2. Now order these into a typical school day — and add three more activities of your own. Pairs order 1–10, then add 3 (e.g. have a shower, do homework, finish school). Key order: wake up → get up → get dressed → pack my bag → go to school → start school → have a break → have lunch → go home → go to sleep. Two pairs read their extra three aloud.

18–28′
Sub-skill · Predicting
board → pairs

Model the strategy (teacher think-aloud). Books open at p.15, text covered by hand — only title + photos visible. Think aloud: The title says “A day in the life of an online student”, and the photos show a computer, someone at home, a clock… so before I read, I predict the text is about how a student works from home on a screen. I expect words like log on, lessons, computer. That prepares my brain. (reading-comprehension-strategy-selector: before-reading = predicting + activate schema.)

Ex.3. Look at the pictures on p.15 and the title. Answer the three questions with your partner — don't read the article yet. Key: 1 Monday 11 a.m. & Thursday 2:30 p.m. · 2 They're online students. · 3 They get up & get dressed, but they don't go to school / go home.

Elicit 2–3 predictions on the board: What do you think you'll read about? What words might appear? (We'll check them after reading.)

First read · gist
seats + audio

Ex.4 — read & listen for gist. Read and listen. One question only: which daily-routine activities do the students actually do? Don't stop at new words. Play audio 1 while they read (~2 min). (during-reading = purposeful single-focus read.)

Pair-check, then whole class. Key: have lessons, wake up, (have) breakfast, start school, get up, have lunch, do homework. Return to the board predictions: Were we right? Which of our words appeared? Tick them — this shows why predicting helps.

Second read · detail + evidence
teams of 4

Ex.5 — True/False + correct + evidence. Read again. True or false? And correct every false sentence. ▶ Numbered Heads Together (Kagan): teams agree an answer and the exact line that proves it; you call a number, that pupil answers + reads the line. Key: 1 F (they study when they want) · 2 T · 3 T · 4 F (they don't sit at the computer all day) · 5 T · 6 F (they do all the normal subjects like maths & history).

Ex.6 — complete the sentences. Individually, then pair-check. Key: 1 nine o'clock · 2 eight o'clock · 3 four lessons · 4 do homework and class projects · 5 the things he likes · 6 after-school clubs.

45–48′
⚡ Brain break · Take a Stand
stand, whole room

▶ Take a Stand (mid-lesson stirrer). One wall = TRUE / I'd like it, opposite wall = FALSE / not for me. Call fast statements about online school: You can study when you want. · You never see your classmates. · You need to be motivated. · I would enjoy online school. Pupils walk to the wall that fits — everyone moves every time. Two quick “Why there?” nominations reset focus for the discussion.

48–60′
Word work · ex.7–8
seats → mingle

Ex.7 — match definitions to bold words. Find these six words in bold in the text and match them to the definitions. Guess from context first, then confirm. CCQ + drill pronunciation of library / subjects / grades. Key: 1 grades · 2 subjects · 3 library · 4 log on · 5 classmates · 6 school trips.

Ex.8 — put words into gaps. Pairs. Key: 1 school trips · 2 library · 3 grades · 4 log on · 5 classmates · 6 subjects.

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (Kagan, 6 word cards each: definition on front, word on back). Stand up, hand up, pair up. A quizzes B (reads a definition → B says the word), swap, then trade cards & find a new partner. 4–5 rounds — active recall, everyone talking. (memory-science: retrieval practice on the new lexis.)

60–80′
Critical thinking · discuss
teams → Corners

1 Understand (compare). Find at least two things that are the SAME and two things DIFFERENT about online and traditional schools. Teams fill a quick Venn on mini-whiteboards. Frames on the board: Both online and traditional schools … · One difference is that … (EAL sentence-frame-generator: compare/contrast frames — model once, then remove.) Same: lessons, homework/projects, normal subjects, grades, school trips. Different: stay home, connect online, study when they want, don't see teacher/classmates daily.

2 Evaluate (argue). Each pupil writes ONE advantage & ONE disadvantage with the frame: I think … is an advantage/disadvantage of online school because … (argument-structure scaffold: claim → reason.) ▶ RallyRobin in pairs to share.

3 Create + vote (Corners). Think of three advantages of a TRADITIONAL school. Teams pool ideas; each reads one to the class. Finish with ▶ Take-a-stand class vote: Online school or traditional school — walk to your corner. Give me one reason. Nominate 3–4 reasons across corners.
