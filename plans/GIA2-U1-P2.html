🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The article about the online school is not read 'because it was set', but as reconnaissance: the crew delivers a verdict on whether their team would survive in an online school. Every True/False answer needs a line of evidence from the text — that is the subskill (predicting → scanning) without the word 'subskill'.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“New file: a school with NO building, NO classrooms, NO bell. Two students — Jack and Abi — live one day inside it. Your mission: go undercover, read their day, and decide — would YOUR crew survive one term online? Verdict at the end: yes or no, with proof from the text.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We read the day of two online-school students like an intelligence file. At the end the crew gives its verdict: 'would we survive online?' — with proof from the text.

WHAT WE DO: 3 minutes of skimming (checking the bets) → scanning: ex.5 True/False with a line of evidence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can mine an article for evidence (predict → skim → scan), correct false statements with a quoted line, and deliver a 60-second verdict on online schooling.
  • Subsidiary. Prediction from the title and the pictures (the unit subskill); Word work (grades · subjects · library · log on · classmates); a critical comparison of online school vs our school.
  • Personal. The fast readers drown out the slower ones — I hold the rule 'an answer only counts with its line of evidence', which levels the pace. I collect 3rd-person errors silently for Pair 3.
By the end, students can…
  • predict the content of an article from title and pictures
  • find and quote the line that proves an answer
  • correct false statements about a text
  • give a 60-second verdict with two pieces of evidence

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'True, I think so' — does that count? → no: True only with a line from the text.
'log on — does that mean going offline?' → the opposite: logging in, starting work at the computer.
My prediction didn't match the text — is that a mistake? → no, a prediction exists precisely to be checked by reading.

Assumptions: SB pp.12–13 (the article + tasks), KTP pair 6. They read confidently, but proving it with a line is a new bar. Word work — 5 words, all of them in the Wordlist.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They skim-read and then argue 'I'm right'The evidence-line rule: an argument is settled only by a line from the text, finger on the line.
The weaker ones can't manage the 3-minute skimSkimming in pairs: one runs a finger along, the other looks for the answer; the roles swap for the scanning.
The verdict turns into 'I like it / I don't'The verdict rubric: at least 2 facts from the text and 1 comparison with our school.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
