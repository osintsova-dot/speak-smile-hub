🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Systematise Unit 3 — the five future forms, the future continuous and the two future perfects, the transport vocabulary, the five journey nouns and sixteen prefixes — and demonstrate it under test conditions: Test yourself, six exercises, 40 points.
What is genuinely hard
Six exercises, none of them labelled, and three of them asking the student to PRODUCE rather than to choose: definitions of eight transport words, five journey nouns in context, and seven prefixed words built from scratch. The grammar half hides the two traps of the unit — a future form after «when», and «will finish» where «will have finished» is needed.
Typical error to expect
«When I will arrive, I will call you» — after when, before, after, as soon as, by the time and until, English uses the PRESENT: when I arrive, I'll call you. Russian uses the future in both clauses, so this error is universal and it is a mark in every exam.
«I had a good travel», «my travel to Moscow» — travel is uncountable and general; a single occasion is a journey, a trip, an excursion or, by sea and space, a voyage.
«unpossible», «disregular», «inlogical» — the negative prefix is fixed by the word, not by logic: impossible, irregular, illogical. English chooses the prefix by the first letter of the adjective more often than by meaning.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first forty minutes are revision that does not feel like a test: the Check it page is answered with the book covered, the prefixes come back as a speed drill, and the vocabulary is played as Showdown so that everybody writes at the same moment. The definitions are rehearsed out loud because exercise 1 of the vocabulary test asks for eight of them and students who have never said one in English freeze. Five minutes before the paper the three ghosts are named — when + will, «I had a good travel», «unpossible» — and then thirty-five quiet minutes.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Everything from three weeks is on two pages, and in forty minutes it is on page forty-three with your name on it. Forty points. And you will know your grammar score before you leave.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, the essays collected, one sentence of each read aloud, then straight into revision

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. One question each at the door, one full sentence back: One sentence — how did you get here today? Any content counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 2 minutes — the essays come in. Collect them by name at the door and look at each for five seconds: four paragraphs, no contractions. Nothing else today.
  3. 1 minute — one line each. Think-Pair-Share: read your partner the sentence from your essay that you are most pleased with.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Page forty-two. Three weeks of work on two pages — and in forty minutes it is on page forty-three with your name on it.
📺 On screen: nothing on screen — paper only. Backup: a student without an essay hands it in next lesson; do not spend lesson time on it.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.42: the reference page answered from memory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Cover the page. I ask, you answer, then uncover and check.
  2. Six questions: when do we use going to for a prediction, and when will? · which form for an arrangement? · which for a timetable? · what follows «as soon as»? · what follows «by the time»? · which form for a decision made at this moment?
  3. Then twelve fast oral items — you say a sentence, they call CORRECT or WRONG and fix it: «Look at the traffic, we will be late» ✘ · «I'm seeing the dentist at five» ✔ · «When I will finish, I'll call» ✘ · «The train leaves at 8.25» ✔ · «I think it's going to be a good year» (both possible) · «As soon as I get home, I'll text you» ✔.
  4. RallyCoach, two minutes: A gives a situation, B produces the sentence with the right form.
📺 On screen: SB p.42 on the board app, revealed one box at a time; otherwise the cover-and-answer routine. Backup: the page is in their hands.
👦 Students do: answer six questions with the page covered, judge twelve sentences aloud, then build sentences in pairs

Why: Exercise 3 of the paper asks them to judge and rewrite these three shapes, so the three shapes are said aloud first.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.42 + a short production drill

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Same routine, page covered. Four questions: what is the form of the future continuous? and the future perfect simple? and the continuous? · which of them answers «how long»? · what usually comes before all three? (by, by the time).
  2. Then a sixty-second chain: you say a time (at nine tomorrow · by 2040 · by the time I'm thirty) and a verb, and a named student produces the full sentence.
  3. Board the three model sentences the test will echo: At five o'clock next Saturday I'll be playing basketball. · I can write the summary tomorrow because I'll have read it by then. · I can't give you my assignment because I won't have finished it.
  4. Send anyone still unsure to p.42 to read the box themselves — naming the page is part of teaching them to revise.
📺 On screen: the three model sentences on the board. Backup: dictate them.
👦 Students do: answer four form questions from memory, then produce sentences on demand

32–40′
Vocabulary and prefixes
desks in fours
Why: The three exercises students lose most time on are the three rehearsed out loud here.

WHAT WE DO: the unit list, everybody writing at the same moment

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. «SHOWDOWN» on the unit word list (SB p.42): the leader reads a definition, everyone writes the word ALONE, then all four answers go up at the same moment. One point for the word, two for a correct sentence with it. Round two is prefixes: the leader says an adjective, everyone writes its negative.
  2. Then the definitions rehearsal, because the test asks for eight: board the frame It's a place where … / It's the people who … / It's a situation when … and take eight definitions aloud from eight different students (platform, vehicle, gate, off-peak, crew, delay, lift, launch).
  3. Then sixty seconds on the five journey nouns: you say a situation, the class calls the noun. Ten items.
  4. Finish with a sixty-second prefix drill: you say the adjective, they shout the negative. Twenty items.
📺 On screen: the definition frame on the board. Backup: if there are no cards, the leader reads a word from the list on p.42.
👦 Students do: write definitions alone and show them simultaneously, then say eight aloud and answer two speed drills

Test yourself · 40 points
desks
Why: A short, well-signposted paper on familiar material is an honest measure of the unit.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.43: six exercises, 35 minutes, silence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Thirty-five minutes. Grammar first, vocabulary second. If you are stuck, move on and come back. Write the finishing time on the board.
  2. Walk the room twice in the first five minutes, then sit down.
  3. At the halfway mark write 17 minutes left on the board without speaking.
  4. Fast finishers: go back to Vocabulary exercise 1 and write a second, better definition for each of the eight words.
  5. Collect at 83 minutes exactly, in silence.
📺 On screen: the finishing time and the halfway warning on the board. Backup: a wall clock and your own watch.
👦 Students do: sit the paper alone in silence for thirty-five minutes

«By 2035 I will finish university» — is that the same as «I will have finished»? → no; «by» needs the perfect.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The definitions exercise eats the clockBecause nobody has practised saying a definition aloud. Rehearse eight of them in the revision half with the frame: It's a place where … / It's the people who … / It's a situation when …
«We didn't revise»The revision was the homework and the first half of today. Say it once, calmly, and start the paper on time.
Fast finishers hand in after eighteen minutesThe rule announced before the paper: when you finish, go back to Vocabulary exercise 1 and write a second, better definition for each of the eight words.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The unit test is the exam block in miniature: produce the future form, build the word with the right prefix, choose between five travel nouns that Russian translates with two. A prefix error («unpossible», «disregular») and a wrong noun («I had a nice travel») cost exactly the same as an empty line.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
