🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): For new students the mission is simple and “about me”: choose a favourite animal and describe it in six lines using a frame. That gives a safe product (I speak from my notes, with support) and at the same time brings in 12 animals, 9 body parts and 6 adjectives. The mini-texts on p.72 are a ready-made model of a description: first we read someone else's, then we write our own to the same pattern.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at this page. Peacock. Turtle. Goat. Bee. Dolphin. Snake. Every one of them has a habitat and a description — that's all. Today two things: we learn to name animals, their body parts and how they look — and each of you writes your own animal, six lines, exactly like these texts. At the end you read it with your notes in your hand. This is not a test. Let's start with MY animal…” (and put the model on the board straight away)
The gist, if you need to restate it: we take the mini-texts about animals apart, learn to name body parts and describe how an animal looks, and then everyone writes their own animal — six lines to the same pattern. At the end we read it from our notes.

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 p.72: nine body parts on the photos + ex.4 p.73, six adjectives

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Nine body parts, nine numbers on the photos. Word bank is on the screen — use it! TB key ex.3: 1 shell · 2 feathers · 3 beak · 4 horn · 5 fur · 6 tail · 7 wings · 8 fin · 9 tooth (plural teeth).
  2. ex.4 together (TB key: 1 striped · 2 marine · 3 colourful · 4 poisonous · 5 dangerous · 6 spotted). The TB asks you to point out: spotted — two syllables, striped — one.
  3. TB routine: write dog, rabbit, owl, snail on the board — which body parts has each one got? Do the first animal (dog) out loud yourself as a model: “fur, tail, teeth — three parts, and I point at each on the photo”. Then the other three go through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: the crew puts heads together and agrees on one list, weaker students may keep a finger on the word bank; then you call a number and only that student answers. If they don't know, the crew coaches them and they answer again.
  4. Tick every body part YOUR animal has got. How many?
📺 On screen: SB pp.72–73 ex.3–4 · word bank (Backup: the nine words on the board)
👦 Students do: work with support and tick their own animal. Expect: slower than our own students — that is normal

WB Unit 6 (pp.44–51) · bring the 6 lines to P2
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 12 animals and 9 body parts, use 6 adjectives, and describe one animal in 6 lines (class · habitat · body parts · adjectives).
  • Subsidiary. The class table (ex.1–2) with support; body parts ex.3 from the photos; ex.4 adjectives; ex.5 track 36 by ear; ex.6 gap-fill; ex.7 a Yes/No guessing game; Video skills with a task set before watching.
  • Personal. Never call on anyone who has no draft. Every speaking task comes AFTER it has been written down. The word bank stays on screen all lesson. Drill the th sound with the TB routine (tongue between the teeth, hand on the throat).
By the end, students can…
  • say and write 12 animals in the right class
  • label 9 body parts
  • use 6 adjectives to describe an animal
  • describe my animal in 6 sentences

Structure

Form
the frame on SB p.73: “The tiger IS a striped animal”, but “It's GOT striped fur” — adjectives go with be, body parts with have got. Habitat: … / Description: … — a ready-made pattern taken from the mini-texts.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“tooths or teeth?” → teeth, it is an exception.
“It's got striped” — what is missing? → It's got striped FUR.
“A shark is a mammal?” → no, a shark is a fish; there is no column for it in ex.2.

Assumptions: SB pp.72–73, syllabus pair 46. Some of the words are familiar (snake, dolphin); the new ones are beak, fin, fur, horn, shell, feathers, the class names and the six adjectives.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Afraid to speak in front of people they don't knowThe rule of the lesson: first we write, then we read it to a partner, and we come to the board with the notes in hand.
27 words at once will not fitFeed them in three portions: animals first, then body parts, then adjectives, each one with a gesture; recognition first (point at it), naming second.
th comes out as /f/, /z/ or /d/The TB routine: tongue between the teeth; hand on the throat — tooth/three/think with no buzz, these/they/feather with a buzz. Thirty seconds before every read-aloud.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
