Structure
My favourite character is … · She was born in New Jersey. · Her father was a teacher and her mother worked in a restaurant. · She's also a good person. · I was top of my class. · Later, I opened a medical school for women.
Form
also goes AFTER be · BEFORE other verbs · BETWEEN can and the verb (SB p.92 ex.4) · the comic — first person (I was born…), past simple; §1 and §3 — present simple, §2 — past.
Pron. watch
also /ˈɔːlsəʊ/ — not “alsoh”; born /bɔːn/ — silent r · stress in biography /baɪˈɒɡrəfi/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“She's a good person also — is that right?” → No: She's ALSO a good person.
“She also had problems or She had also problems?” → She ALSO HAD problems (before an ordinary verb).
“Which paragraph is past simple?” → The second — family and early life.

Assumptions: After P2–P3 both past forms are active; they know also as a word but put it at the end of the sentence (interference from “too”). Project work in threes is familiar from U1–U6, they accept roles and timers. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (irregular forms / -ed spelling / was–were agreement), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
