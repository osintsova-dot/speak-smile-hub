📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can build affirmative and negative sentences with be (full + contracted, apostrophes right) — and use them with 8 new place adjectives to describe New Zealand after a real listening.
  • Subsidiary. Hour 1 (exactly 45′): inductive tables SB p.16 (ex.1, 5), contraction surgery, Fact Police, Quiz “Where am I?”. Hour 2: 8 adjective pairs with gestures, listening subskill (using visual clues), 2+1 passes of audio 12, a notes table. The final portion joins BOTH hours: be + adjective about NZ.
  • Personal. This is a systemic-grammar lesson — but the word “grammar” never sounds like a sigh: “today we build the machine, then flight-test it”. Every drill comes in a game wrapper; the table on the board lives the whole lesson; at minute 45 it's not a “new lesson”, it's the machine's test flight.
By the end, students can…
  • complete the be tables (full + contracted)
  • flip aff ↔ neg and fix wrong facts
  • say 8 opposite adjective pairs with gestures
  • catch key facts of a phone call and describe NZ with be + adjective

Target language
Lexis
'm / 're / 's'm not / isn't / aren'tbeautiful / uglyclean / dirtyempty / crowdedhot / coldinteresting / boringmodern / oldquiet / noisysafe / dangerous
Structure
I'm / He's / We're + (from)… · I'm not / isn't / aren't · Cem isn't from Istanbul. · Auckland is a modern city. · The beaches aren't crowded.
Form
contraction = apostrophe replaces the lost letter ('m/'s/'re); negative: 'm not · isn't · aren't; adjective + noun / be + adjective, NEVER plural (crowdeds ✗).
Pron. watch
weak forms /aɪm/ /wɪə/; isn't — one beat; crowded — 2 syllables; quiet vs quite.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He's — he IS or he HAS?” → today always he is.
“Can I say ‘I amn't’?” → No! I'm not — the only option.
“Two moderns cities — OK?” → No: two modern cities — adjectives never take -s.

Assumptions: Two pairs of contextual contact with be are done (P1 frames, P2 corrections) — the forms are familiar by ear; the SYSTEM (the table, the apostrophes) is new. The opposite adjectives and NZ are completely new; listening at a natural pace is scary — small goals take the panic away.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
