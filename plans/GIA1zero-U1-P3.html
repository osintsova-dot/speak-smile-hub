Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to make true affirmative and negative sentences with be about themselves and about places (I'm…, It isn't…, They aren't…), introduced from scratch with a model and a table.
  • Subsidiary. Learn 16 adjectives to describe places (8 opposite pairs) and use them correctly (adjective + noun / be + adjective, never plural); listen for specific information about New Zealand using the sub-skill using visual clues; recycle countries & nationalities from Pair 1.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low and model everything first; give sentence frames to every learner; run one clear brain-break at ~45′; slow down — lots of controlled practice before any freer talk.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say and write am / is / are and their contractions + negatives correctly;
  • describe a place with the 16 adjectives (a modern city / it isn't crowded);
  • catch key facts about New Zealand from a phone conversation.
Target language

Structure
be aff.: I'm · you're · he's/she's/it's · we're/they're · (I am / is / are).  be neg.: I'm not · isn't · aren't.  ·  adjective + noun / be + adjective.
Form
subject + be (+ not) + complement. am→I · is→he/she/it · are→you/we/they. Adjectives are never plural (a modern city ✓ / a city modern ✗ / crowdeds ✗).
Pron. watch
contracted 's = /z/ in he's, she's; weak are /ə/; isn't /ˈɪznt/, aren't /ɑːnt/; stress the adjective (a MOdern city).
Frames
I'm from ___ . I'm ___ .___ is/isn't ___ .It's a ___ city.My town is ___ and ___ .
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I am from Russia — one person or many?” → one → am / I'm.
“‘She's from Spain’ — is she's = she is or she has?” → she is.
“‘The beaches aren't crowded’ — are they crowded?” → no.
“‘a modern city’ — modern before or after the noun? Can I say ‘a crowdeds beach’?” → before; no, never plural.

Assumptions: countries & nationality adjectives met in Pairs 1–2; alphabet, numbers 1–100, international words from Starter. This is the first full introduction of be — treat learners as true beginners: model → table → heavy controlled practice → freer. Questions & short answers come next pair (P4) — do not introduce them today. Teams: 3–4 max; pairs for all speaking.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Wrong form (He are / They is)Colour-code the table by pronoun group; drill one column at a time (am · is · are).
Never contracting (I am everywhere)Back-chain: …Spain → from Spain → I'm from Spain; mark 'm 's 're in colour on the card.
Plural adjective (crowdeds / two moderns)Red cross on the card: “adjectives are never plural.” Choral fix.
Word order (a city modern)Gesture adjective → noun; frame a ___ city on the board.
Listening panic (too fast)Use visual clues first, tell them the task before they listen, play twice, pause between items.
Classroom management — signals
“3–2–1, eyes here.” — countdown → pens down, look at the board.
“Pens down.” — hands off, listen to instructions before starting.
Volume dial — raise/lower hand = louder/quieter pair work.
Silent thumb — 👍 agree · 👎 disagree · ✋ not sure (fast whole-class check).

“Freeze / pause.” — stop mid-task, reset, re-explain.

Lead-in & retrieval
standing

Recycle Pair 1. Greet, then a fast retrieval of countries & nationalities (spaced practice — sticks the new words). Stand up. Walk, and when I clap — find a partner.

▶ Mix-Pair-Share: in each new pair, say I'm from [Russia]. I'm [Russian]. using the frame I'm from ___ . I'm ___ . Swap partners twice. This is today's grammar in disguise — write one pupil's sentence on the board and underline I'm: This little word is our lesson today.

5–14′
Presentation · be affirmative (I DO)
board

I Do — model + think-aloud. Point to yourself: I am your teacher. I'm from Norilsk. Point to a pupil: You are a student. You're twelve. Point to one/two/all: He is… She is… It is… We are… They are… Build the table on the board as you speak, one pronoun group at a time, colour-coded:

am → I  |  is → he / she / it  |  are → you / we / they.

Then the short (contracted) forms, saying and writing each: I am = I'm. He is = he's. They are = they're. Model the SB p.16 examples: I'm 13 years old. We're from Zwolle. 25% of the Netherlands is under the sea.

CCQ: “He ___ from Spain” — am, is or are? (is) · “she's” — she is or she has? (she is).

22–33′
Controlled practice (YOU DO)
desks → pairs

You Do — write, then check. Practice moves from close-to-model to varied:

SB p.16 ex 6 — change affirmative ↔ negative (Cem is from Istanbul → Cem isn't…). ex 7 — correct the false sentences (Washington isn't the capital of the Netherlands. It's the capital of the USA.). Model item 1 of each on the board first.

Monitor. First 3 min: check the 4 weakest pupils' ex 6 no.1; wrong form → point back to the coloured card. Then ▶ RallyCoach in pairs: A does the next item aloud, B checks against the card and praises/fixes, then swap — every pupil both writes and checks.

CFU: Silent thumb — Could you do one more on your own? 👍 → move on · ✋ → one more together.

33–43′
Grammar game · Flip-It
teams, standing

▶ Flip-It Challenge (Kagan Showdown). Teams of 3, one mini-whiteboard each pupil. You read an affirmative sentence — They're from Brazil. — everyone writes the negative privately (They aren't from Brazil.). Call Showdown! → all boards up at once. Correct spelling of isn't / aren't / 'm not scores. 8–10 rounds, mix aff→neg and neg→aff.

Every learner writes every round (PIES — all engaged, no-one waits). Keep the be card visible as the answer key. Light, quick, no big prize (that's for the Unit review).

Vocab practice + game
desks → mingle

Controlled. SB p.17 ex 2 — match the yellow words to their opposites in the box (cold, dangerous, dirty, empty, interesting, noisy, old, ugly). Check with silent thumb. Read the Adjectives rule box aloud: Adjectives come before a noun or after the verb be. They are never plural.

65–77′
Listening · New Zealand
desks

Sub-skill — using visual clues (pre-listening). Before we listen, look. Titles, photos and maps tell us a lot. SB p.17 ex 4 — look at the photos (rugby ball, sheep, Māori carving): What do they tell you about New Zealand? Elicit predictions in pairs — no audio yet.

Listen 1 — gist/choose. Give the task first, then play Track 12 (a phone conversation). ex 5 — choose: New Zealand is/isn't a small country · Auckland is/isn't the capital · Māori is/isn't the only language. Check.

Listen 2 — detail. Play again. ex 6 true/false (1–6) · ex 7 complete the notes (Population · Capital city · Weather · Official languages · National sport). Pause between items; let pairs compare before whole-class check. fun fact: A person from New Zealand is a ‘Kiwi’ — a kind of bird!
