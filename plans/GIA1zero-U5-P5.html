Opening · check-in + rewind

WHAT WE DO: Showdown on repeat: check-in 2′ · rewind 3′ (Pair 4: comparatives — straight into the advert) · bridge 1′

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Check-in (2′). One word: something at home you never use.
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: everyone writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they all lift them together — I see everyone's answer, no hiding.
  3. Rewind P4 (3′). Boards up! Compare two things in this room: ‘The window is bigger than the door.’ Everyone writes; I read out 3, the class checks than and the form against the rule cards.
  4. Bridge (1′). That sentence is what SELLS things. Today you write a real advert — and the class decides what to buy.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · the five comparative rule cards (up)
👦 Students do: write a comparison at the same time. Expected: the forms hold up after Pair 4. Typical error: than is missing — I point at the card.

Hook · Hot seat with tech words

WHAT WE DO: warm-up, Warmer TB p.79: the Hot seat game on tech words — reviving the unit vocabulary

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Hot seat (Warmer TB p.79, Activities bank p.6): one person sits with their back to the board, I write a word (camera · charger · touchpad · speaker · laptop).
  2. The class describes it using the supports on the board: You can watch/listen to … on it. · It's small and light. · It's better than a …
  3. 4–5 rounds of 40 seconds, the person in the seat changes. A point to the team for a guessed word.
  4. Bridge: You just described things to make people understand. Now — to make people BUY.
📺 On screen: board: support phrases for Hot seat · tech words
👦 Students do: describe the word in chorus, the person in the seat changes — everyone takes part. Expected: 4 words in 6 minutes. Typical error: a hint in Russian — «English only».

12–21′
Introducing: how an advert is built
Discover · how an advert works
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin: ex.1–3 p.68: analysing two models through five questions; pulling out the advert skeleton onto the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1: What are the objects? What type of website is this? Key: 1 a laptop, a speaker, a charger, a games console · 2 a website to buy and sell things. I explain: advert = advertisement = ad.
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we alternate one answer each — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!
  3. Ex.2 in pairs: would buy / wouldn't buy. Support on the board: I'd like to buy the ___ because it's ___ . I wouldn't buy the ___ because ___ .
  4. Ex.3 — 5 questions on the laptop advert (who it's for · pluses · colour · age · price), pairs, 4 minutes. Keys TB p.80.
  5. We pull the SKELETON onto the board (stays up until the end of the pair): headline → who it's for → what you can do → comparison → colour/age/condition → price + hook.
  6. Fast finishers (TB p.80): write out all the comparisons from the adverts (smaller, lighter, better, bigger).
📺 On screen: SB p.68 · the advert skeleton on the board (stays up) · the seller's phrase bank from Pair 3
👦 Students do: analyse the models in pairs and pull out the structure. Expected: they notice for themselves that an advert starts with the benefit. Typical error: they answer with one word — I ask for a sentence from the support.

Introducing: the three spelling traps
Discover 2 · three spelling traps

WHAT WE DO: Subskill Spelling: three traps with examples + a “find the word in the unit” race

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three columns on the board: silent letters: cam(e)ra · Wed(nes)day · Feb(ru)ary · double letters: thinner · bigger · address · sound ↔ spelling /iː/: cheap · screen · piece · people · police.
  2. I go through each one with an example: camera — we say CAM-ra but we WRITE the e. thinner — double N, like ‘bigger’. cheap and screen — the same sound, different letters.
  3. ▶ A race (Fast finishers TB p.80, for everyone): Two minutes — find words from Unit 5 for each column. Pages sixty to sixty-nine! A point to the team for every word no one else has.
  4. Pronunciation /iː/ (p.117 if possible): 1 keyboard · 2 screen · 3 cheap · 4 piece · 5 police · 6 people · 7 niece · 8 complete (the letters ey, ee, ea, ie, i, eo, e = /iː/) — we read it in chorus.
📺 On screen: three columns on the board · SB pp.60–69 to search
👦 Students do: flip through the unit in teams and fill in the columns — everyone moves and searches. Expected: 12–15 words across the columns. Typical error: they put a word in the wrong column — we discuss it, which is more useful than a key.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4–6 p.68: an 8-word dictation (audio 38) → checked in pairs → checked against the book; ex.7 correcting

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed. Eight words. Write what you hear. (audio 38; no track — I dictate it myself, each word twice.) Words: 1 keyboard · 2 lighter · 3 touchpad · 4 beautiful · 5 February · 6 speaker · 7 bigger · 8 Wednesday.
  2. Ex.5: Compare with your partner — but do NOT change anything yet. (TB p.80)
  3. Ex.6: they check against the adverts on p.68. They count the clean ones.
  4. Ex.7 p.69 on mini-boards: 5 sentences, ONE mistake in each. Reinforcement (TB p.80): first we underline the wrong word, then we correct it. Key: 1 difficult · 2 fourteen · 3 address · 4 heavier · 5 photos.
  5. A point to the team for every clean correction.
📺 On screen: audio 38 (backup — I dictate from the key) · SB pp.68–69 · mini-boards
👦 Students do: write the dictation solo, check with a partner, then with the book. Expected: 5–7 out of 8; February and Wednesday trip up everyone. Typical error: «biger» — I link it back to the doubling rule from Pair 4.

WHAT WE DO: Skills boost THINK → PREPARE → WRITE (portion 1): choosing an object, making notes, writing 3 sentences

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK (1 min): Choose a real object to sell: a phone, a bike, a bag, a console, a toy.
  2. PREPARE (3 min): notes on the 5 questions of ex.3 — notes, NOT sentences (TB p.80).
  3. Support phrases on the board (SB p.69 PREPARE 2): This … is perfect for … · You can use it to … · It's a beautiful … colour. · It comes with … · It's only two years old. / It's in excellent condition..
  4. We write the first sentence TOGETHER on the board as a whole class (a model), then everyone writes their own.
  5. WRITE portion 1 (3 min): headline + 3 sentences (who it's for · what you can do · one comparison). Stop — the rest comes after movement.
📺 On screen: SB p.69 ex.8 · skeleton + support phrases on the board · notebooks
👦 Students do: make notes and write the first portion of 3 sentences. Expected: bright headlines, a clean portion. Typical error: they write full sentences during PREPARE — I bring them back to notes.

WHAT WE DO: the second portion (up to 5–6 sentences) + a marker CHECK against the SB p.69 checklist + peer review

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. WRITE portion 2 (6 min): colour/age/condition + price + hook. The bar for 🐣: 5–6 sentences, 1 comparison, can/can't, a price.
  2. CHECK (3 min) using ex.8: Marker one — underline ‘can’. Marker two — underline your comparative. Then check the spelling of every unit word. (TB p.80)
  3. If either underline is missing — they add it in.
  4. Peer review (ex.9) — exactly 2 questions: 1 Which object do you want to buy? Why? 2 Is the spelling correct? Swapped in pairs, 2 minutes each.
  5. Support: a skeleton card with 5 sentence starters.

👦 Students do: finish writing, mark up their own text with markers, read their partner's work against 2 questions. Expected: everyone has 5–6 sentences and a found comparison. Typical error: a price with no currency — «write ₽ or €».

WHAT WE DO: adverts on the walls (Extra activity TB p.80): a gallery, we walk round, read, “buy” with tokens

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I collect the finished copies with no names, put them up on the walls (Extra activity TB p.80).
  2. Two coins each. Walk, read, spend. You can't spend both on one advert — and you can't buy your own!
  3. The proofreader's role: If you find a spelling mistake, put a sticky note on it. The proofreaders get points too.
  4. We count the “sales”; the 3 leading authors read their advert out loud in a presenter's voice (20 seconds each).
  5. Points: 1 token = 1 point · a mistake-free advert = +3.
📺 On screen: adverts on the walls · 2 tokens per student · sticky notes
👦 Students do: walk round the gallery, read other people's adverts, spend tokens and catch mistakes — everyone moves and reads. Expected: 2–3 sales leaders, lots of laughter. Typical error: they vote for a friend — the rule is «tokens for the text».

WHAT WE DO: the grammar block of Quick review p.69 as a calm team quiz; I announce the test format

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three short volleys on mini-boards, 2 minutes each:
  2. can: 4 sentences to complete (aff / neg / question / short answer).
  3. comparatives: 6 forms for speed (big · easy · expensive · good · light · slow).
  4. possessive pronouns: 4 substitutions (this is my tablet → it's mine).
  5. After every volley — a key on screen and a personal note: Mark the part where YOU lost points. That's your homework tonight.
  6. I announce the test format (specification below): parts, points, what it mirrors — so that nobody is afraid.
📺 On screen: volley slides + keys · Quick review p.69 · mini-boards (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: solve by volley as a team, see the key straight away, mark their weak spot. Expected: everyone names a specific area. Typical error: «it's my» — volley 3 again.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write an advert of 5–6 sentences for an object they want to sell, using can, adjectives and one comparative, with correct spelling of unit words.
  • Subsidiary. The Spelling subskill is given as three traps with games; the advert model is analysed through questions; peer review — 2 ticks; the Quick review p.69 is closed with a calm team quiz; the test is announced and explained.
  • Personal. Their writing is slow — we write in two portions of 3 sentences, with obligatory movement in between. We make Spelling fun: “one letter — and nobody bought it”.
By the end, students can…
  • spot three types of spelling trap and fix them
  • answer five questions about a model advert
  • write an advert of 5–6 sentences with a heading and a price
  • review a classmate's advert with two concrete checks

Form
the advert skeleton: headline → who it's for → what you can do → one comparison → colour/age → price. Spelling: silent letters: cam(e)ra · Wed(nes)day · Feb(ru)ary · double letters: thinner · bigger · address · sound ↔ spelling /iː/: cheap · screen · piece · people · police.
Pron. watch
/iː/ is spelt differently: cheap · screen · people · piece (SB p.117) · bargain /ˈbɑːɡɪn/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“camera — how many syllables?” → two: CAM-ra, but we write the e.
“thiner or thinner?” → thinner — doubling, like in the comparative.
“Will an advert with no price work?” → no: a price is always needed.

Assumptions: All the language of the unit has been covered (Pairs 1–4). The advert as a genre is familiar from marketplaces and classifieds — we build on that. They can't write long pieces — portions of 3 sentences.

Anticipated problems & solutions

They don't know where to startThe advert skeleton on the board + a bank of support phrases; we write the first sentence together as a whole class.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
