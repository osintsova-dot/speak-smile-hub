0–7′
Warm-up + rewind
Opening · retrieval of be (+/−)
desks → standing

WHAT WE DO: a calm check-in, then whole-class retrieval of last pair's be forms (affirmative/negative) woven into the unit's countries; a picture warmer that plants today's question: “why did these sentences sound DIFFERENT?”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Greet at the door, calm start: Good afternoon! One country you'd like to visit — one word each.
  2. Retrieval on mini-boards (recycling P3, no marks — just recall): I say a subject, they write a full be sentence. Spain and Italy… → they write Spain and Italy are in Europe. · Now make it negative. → Japan isn't in Europe. On Boards up! everyone lifts together — I see every answer, nobody sits it out.
  3. Quick opposites recall with gestures (adjectives from P3): Crowded — Quiet! Busy — …?
  4. The warmer that opens the door: I write on the board New Zealand is a big country. The winters are cold. You are happy there. — Yesterday these were sentences. Today, in a phone call, they sounded DIFFERENT. Any idea how?
  5. I take a couple of guesses in any language and bridge: They became QUESTIONS. Today we learn exactly how to make them — step by step.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · the 3 warmer sentences on the board (TB warmer p.25)
👦 Students do: recall be sentences, flip to negative, lift boards together, guess the statement→question change. Expected: “It's a question!” Typical error: We aren't written as We arent — a quick apostrophe reminder.

10–22′
I Do → We Do: the question machine
Explicit input · model + table + etiquette
board · desks

WHAT WE DO: teacher MODELS the question rule out loud (the verb jumps to the front) and the short-answer etiquette; then we complete the table ex.1 TOGETHER. Calm and explicit — this is brand-new, so we go slowly.

👩‍🏫 I Do — teacher models with think-aloud (~5 min):
  1. I write a statement: You are happy. Watch my thinking. To ask a question, I don't add a word — I MOVE the verb 'are' to the FRONT. I physically draw an arrow: are → front → Are you happy?
  2. Two more, thinking aloud each time: It is big. → 'is' jumps to the front → Is it big? · They are from Japan. → Are they from Japan?
  3. Short answers, on the rule card: Question needs an answer. And English wants a POLITE full one. I show the trap: Yes, I am ✓ — but never Yes, I'm ✗. Yes, it is ✓ — never Yes, it's ✗. Negatives DO shorten: No, I'm not. / No, it isn't. (SB p.18 “contracted forms” box).
👩‍🏫 We Do — guided practice together (~6 min):
  1. Open page 18, exercise 1. We fill the table TOGETHER — no rush. I do the first row aloud, then cold-call for each next cell: Row 2 — the question: ‘… you happy there?’ Which verb jumps? … Are. So — Are you happy there? Class chorus, I write it in.
  2. Etiquette chant with two claps to make it stick: Yes-I-AM! No-it-IS-n't! ×2 — calm, rhythmic, not a shouting match.
  3. CFU before moving on — thumbs: Thumbs up if you can make a question by moving the verb; sideways if you want one more together. If many sideways → one more example.
📺 On screen: SB p.18 ex.1 table · the flip arrow diagram · the ✓/✗ rule card (stays up all unit)
👦 Students do: watch the model, then complete the table cell by cell in chorus, chant the etiquette, self-check with thumbs. Expected: they fill all cells with help. Typical error: Yes, it's. — I silently point at the ✓/✗ card.

22–30′
We Do move it: human sentences
Guided practice · inversion with the body
open space → desks

WHAT WE DO: we make the rule physical — word-cards stand in a line, the “verb” walks to the front, a question appears. Then, back at the desks, we do ex.2 and ex.3 together.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Five word-cards to five volunteers: your / city / is / big / ? — I line them up as the statement Your city is big.
  2. On my signal, the VERB walks to the front. I signal — the is student steps across: Is your city big? The class reads it with rising tone: Is your city big?↗
  3. We do 3–4 statements from ex.2 this way; new children hold cards each time. Calm, one at a time — the point is the move, not speed.
  4. Back to desks: Exercise 2 — order the words into questions. Do it with your partner, then we check together. Choral check. Then ex.3: Match each question to its answer, a–e. Check aloud.
📺 On screen: sets of word cards (ex.2 sentences) · SB p.18 ex.2–3 · space by the board
👦 Students do: build the sentence with their bodies, the “verb” walks forward, read the question with ↗; then order words and match Q–A in pairs. Expected: laughter, and the inversion sticks in the body. Typical error: the wrong word walks (“big”) — the class calls “Verb! Verb!”.

WHAT WE DO: the KTP drill game — short-answer cards on the desk; I ask a fact, pairs grab the correct answer and read the WHOLE thing aloud. The mechanic itself forces the full polite answer.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 6 answer cards per desk: Yes, it is · No, it isn't · Yes, they are · No, they aren't · Yes, I am · No, I'm not.
  2. Rules: I ask — you GRAB the right card and READ it WHOLE. Clean full answer wins the point.
  3. 6–8 unit-fact questions: Is Wellington the capital of NZ? Are the beaches in NZ crowded? Are you 11? Is Nepal's flag a rectangle?
  4. Close hour 1: The question machine works! Time for a stretch — then we MEET the new student.

WHAT WE DO: everyone secretly invents a new identity, then pairs build a meeting dialogue from a GAPPED skeleton (frames still visible) — this is the scaffolded version before the free take.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out identity cards: name · age · country/nationality · city.
  2. Step 1 THINK (solo, 3 min): Be anyone — a surfer from Australia, a gamer from Japan. Keep it SECRET for now!
  3. Step 2 PREPARE (pairs, 6 min): build the dialogue from the skeleton, using the Key phrases with the NEW identities. I run the turn-taking as Kagan RallyRobin: Ping-pong it — A one line, B one line, back to A. No monologues.
  4. ICQ: Your REAL name in the dialogue? — No! The mask name! Tick THINK and PREPARE on the tracker.

Structure
Am I…? / Is he/she/it…? / Are we/you/they…? · Yes, I am. / No, it isn't. · What's your name? Where are you from? How old are you?
Form
question = the verb JUMPS in front of the subject; affirmative short answers never contract (Yes, I am — not Yes, I'm); negatives do (No, it isn't). Key phrases p.19 taught as fixed chunks.
Pron. watch
rising intonation ↗ on yes/no questions; Wh-questions fall ↘ (TB ABCD); Nice to meet you — one smooth chunk.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“To ask a question, do I add a word or MOVE the verb?” → move the verb to the front.
“New Zealand is big? — can I just answer ‘Yes’?” → polite English wants Yes, it is.
“Yes, I'm. — OK?” → ✗! Affirmative short answers never contract.

Assumptions: the affirmative/negative be forms were introduced in P3 (still fragile — hence the retrieval warm-up). NEW here: making the question by inversion + short-answer etiquette + the meeting Key phrases. As true beginners, learners rely on the model, the grammar card and visible frames — these come out only when a task moves from controlled to freer. Made-up identities take the fear out of the stage.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
No inversion («You are from Japan?»)The human-sentence stage makes the verb PHYSICALLY walk to the front — one student IS the word and moves; keep the flip arrow on the board.
«Yes, I'm.» slipThe ✓/✗ rule card stays up all unit; a silent point at the card, class re-casts to the full form.
Too fast / overwhelmed (true beginners)Foundation pace: complete ex.1 TOGETHER, cell by cell; a CFU thumbs-check before any independent work; frames stay visible until the free take.
Shyness at PERFORM stepThe new identity is a mask; everyone performs simultaneously first, only volunteers go public; applause protocol.
Dialogue read roboticallyPRACTISE rule: the last run has the map face down, only the Key-phrases poster visible — forces recall, not reading.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise.
Visible timer — timer on = we work; timer off = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — re-cast and repeat.
