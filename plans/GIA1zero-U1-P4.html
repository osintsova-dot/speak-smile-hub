WHAT WE DO: Showdown as a rewind: “one good thing”; a lightning rewind of P3: is→isn't flips + a volley of opposites with gestures; a TB warmer — “3 sentences that sounded different”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: One good thing since last lesson — three seconds each, go!
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: everyone writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they all lift them together — I see every answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. Rewind of P3: 2 flips on mini-boards (We're at school → ? Thomas is from Spain → ? — Boards UP!) + a volley of opposites with gestures: Crowded! — Empty! Noisy! — Quiet!
  4. TB warmer (p.25): I write New Zealand is a big country. The winters are cold. You are happy there. — In the phone call these sounded DIFFERENT. How?
  5. Bridge: They were QUESTIONS! Today we build the question machine — because next week a NEW student joins us… and maybe they're an impostor.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · the 3 TB warmer sentences on the board
👦 Students do: write negatives, duel with opposites, catch the difference “statement → question”. Expected: “It's a question!”. Typical error: “We arent” — an apostrophe gesture.

WHAT WE DO: teams fill in the question/answer table ex.1; the Yes, I am ✓ etiquette; TB Common errors — a “catch the mistake” round

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: word-children stand in a line; on my clap the “verb-person” runs to the front — a question appears; then a blitz of ex.3

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I hand a group of five word cards: your / city / is / big / ? — I line them up as a statement.
  2. I explain: On my clap, the VERB runs to the front! I clap — “is” runs across: Is your city big?
  3. The class reads it in chorus with rising intonation: Is your city big?↗
  4. I run through 4 sentences from ex.2, new children hold the cards each time.
  5. Back to the desks, blitz ex.3: questions ↔ answers a–e in 90 sec, a choral check.
📺 On screen: sets of word cards (ex.2 sentences) · space by the board
👦 Students do: physically build the sentence, the “verb” runs forward, read the question in chorus with ↗; then match Q–A. Expected: laughter + the inversion sticks in the body. Typical error: the wrong word runs (“big”) — the class shouts “Verb! Verb!”

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask be-questions with inversion and give polite short answers — and act a «meeting new people» dialogue with Key phrases in a new identity.
  • Subsidiary. Hour 1 (exactly 45′): inductive table ex.1, human sentences (inversion with the body), the Lily/Erin dialogue, the flags Messageboard round-up, the “ask & grab” game. Hour 2: the Amy/Elena video model, Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM, peer review ex.5. TB Common errors — a “catch the mistake” round.
  • Personal. One storyline for the pair: we build a question machine to… meet the new students (and unmask the impostor). Inversion is the MAIN hard move for Russian speakers: we drill it with the body. A bare “Yes.” gets politely killed every time.
By the end, students can…
  • order words into a be-question
  • give short answers with the right pronoun+verb
  • catch the two TB common errors
  • act a first-meeting dialogue with Key phrases in a new identity

Target language
Lexis
Where / What / WhoflagrectangleWhat's your name? — My name's… / I'm…Good/Nice to meet you. — You too!Where are you from? — I'm from…How old are you? — I'm (12/13).
Structure
Am I…? / Is he/she/it…? / Are we/you/they…? · Yes, I am. / No, it isn't. · What's your name? Where are you from? How old are you?
Form
question = verb JUMPS before subject; affirmative short answers never contract; Key phrases p.19 as chunks.
Pron. watch
rising intonation ↗ on yes/no; TB ABCD: Wh-questions go DOWN ↘; Nice to meet you — one chunk.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is New Zealand big? — can I answer just ‘Yes’?” → polite English wants Yes, it is.
“Yes, I'm. — OK?” → ✗! Affirmative short answers never contract.
“The winters are cold? — is that a question?” → No! (TB common error) — Are the winters cold?

Assumptions: All the affirmative forms are automated (P3); questions have been heard receptively (T/F, the quiz). New: the inversion mechanics + short-answer etiquette + meeting Key phrases. Made-up identities take the fear out of the stage.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
No inversion («You are from Japan?»)Human-sentence game makes the verb PHYSICALLY jump — one student IS the word ‘are’ and runs to the front.
«Yes, I'm.» slipRule card: ✓ Yes, I am / ✗ Yes, I'm; buzzer when it slips, class corrects.

Shyness at PERFORM stepNew identity = a mask; perform simultaneously first, only volunteers go public; applause protocol.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
