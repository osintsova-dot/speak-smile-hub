🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Two real-world things in one pair: the phrases you can walk into any club with and sign up, and a first informal message. For new students the dialogue is built through shadowing the video (repeating after a voice takes the embarrassment away), and the writing follows a strict staircase: three questions → notes → the text from a model → checklist. I give and/or/but a physical anchor back in the warm-up.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today you learn the phrases that work in real life: I'd like to find out about your courses. What time does it start? How much is it? Can I sign up for it? First we watch Maria do it, then we do it — our own club fair right here. And then your first message: someone asks you three questions, and you answer. Step by step. The model shows the way.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: the phrases people really use to sign up for a course: we watch Maria do it, then we run our own club fair. After that — a first message: somebody asks you three questions and you answer them. Step by step, with a model.

WHAT WE DO: we prepare our own mini-dialogue from the model → the fair: half behind the desks, half hunting for their course with a form

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The pairs write a dialogue from the ex.4 model (their own course from the leaflet) — 5 minutes, phrase cards on the desks.
  2. The fair: Desks: photography, yoga, chess, movie-making. Visitors: sign up for ONE course!
  3. First I go round one desk myself, out loud, as a visitor — that is the model. Then Kagan Find Someone Who: each visitor has a form with four lines (course · day · time · price) and goes round the desks filling them in with questions. Two desks minimum — then choose.
  4. The constraint is soft: if you forget a phrase, take the card, that is fine.
  5. Roles swap after 5 minutes.
📺 On screen: desk cards · phrase cards
👦 Students do: they write first and speak after — the fear is gone. Expect: with a card in hand everybody speaks

WHAT WE DO: three messages A/B/C + the model reply → ex.4–5 on and/or/but

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: the staircase: choose A or B → write out the 3 questions → notes → 5 lines → checklist → swap

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1: Message A or B. Write down the three questions — NOTES only.
  2. Step 2: Five lines. Answer all three questions. Word bank is on the screen. Prompts from the book: Hi Akio, … I usually … in my free time. I like … but I don't like …
  3. Step 3: checklist on your own (three answers? adverb? -ing after like? and/or/but?) → swap with a partner (ex.7) through Kagan RallyCoach: first I check one message at the board against the checklist out loud as the model, then in the pair one reads their message out loud and the other is the coach: goes through the checklist point by point and praises each one that is there. Then they swap.
  4. The messages go on the unit wall!
📺 On screen: 3 questions · frame · checklist · word bank
👦 Students do: they write down the staircase with supports. Expect: 5 lines from everybody, the swap catches the linkers

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can ask for information about a course with 5 Key phrases and write a 5-line reply answering three questions, using and, or and but.
  • Subsidiary. The video with Maria + shadowing; ex.4 completing the dialogue; the model messages A/B/C and the model reply; ex.4–5 the linkers; checklist and peer review.
  • Personal. The writing happens in class with my support; at home they only copy it out neatly. To the board — volunteers only, and only with a finished dialogue.
By the end, students can…
  • use 5 Key phrases to ask about a course
  • act out a short sign-up dialogue
  • write a 5-line reply answering three questions
  • join ideas with and, or and but

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Can I sign up for it?” — is that about a signature? → no, about enrolling on a course.
and, or, but — which of them argues with the first idea? → but.
How many questions must the reply close? → all three, otherwise the message is not finished.

Assumptions: SB pp.31–33, KTP pair 18. The course leaflet gives you all the material; messages A/B are simple and short.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They are shy about acting out the dialogueShadowing the video in chorus first, then in a pair at their desk, and to the board — volunteers only.
“I don't know what to write” in the messageThe three questions in the original message are the plan: three questions, three answers, plus a greeting and a sign-off.
They stick the linkers in at randomThe stand-up-sit-down stirrer gives a physical anchor + a checklist with an example for each linker.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
