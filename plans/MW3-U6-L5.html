Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, children will be better able to order food politely like Alex, Lucy & Auntie Vicky — “Can I help you? — I want fish, please. / I don't want chicken.” — a life skill: ordering & being polite at a meal.
  • Subsidiary. Watch Lucy, Alex & Auntie Vicky at the restaurant (CD3 T10); set up a class café; play Waiter, waiter! & Genki The Koala Game (8); sing Genki «Ordering Food A–M» (68); match characters to what they want & draw & talk about lunch (PB p.41); Phonics Hero — tricky c · g · ph + air ear are, speed-read p.86–90.
  • Personal. First proper speaking focus of the unit — never force a shy child; pointing to the food + please counts. Model the waiter role with the puppet first. Set a gentle time-limit on each order so the café keeps moving. Keep each exchange ~20 seconds, then swap.
By the end, pupils can…
  • order food politely — “I want fish, please”;
  • ask “Can I help you?” as the waiter;
  • answer “Yes, please. / No, thank you. / I don't want…”

Phonics
Phonics Hero 2.0 — the tricky letters c · g · ph (hard/soft cat/city, go/gem; ph = /f/ phone) and the /eə/ spellings air · ear · are. Jolly actions → blend (ph-o-ne → phone, h-air → hair, c-are → care, b-ear → bear) → speed-read p.86–90.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“The waiter asks «Can I help you?» — do you say a food or a colour?” → a food I want!
“You don't want chicken — do you say «I don't want chicken» politely or grab it?” → say it politely!

Assumptions: All ten foods & What do you have for…? / Do you want…? are secure from L1–L4. Ordering politely at a restaurant (Can I help you? / I want… please) is today's focus; children meet Lucy, Alex & Auntie Vicky doing the same in the video. Reading moves to the tricky letters & /eə/.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Café gets chaoticOne café table at a time; the rest are «customers» in a queue with menus; Freeze on the tambourine.
Forgets «please / thank you»Puppet waiter only serves when it hears the polite word — a funny, clear rule.
Shy to speak to a peerOrder from the puppet waiter first, then a friend; pointing to the food + please is a full order.
Same food every timeGive each child a little menu-card of 3 foods — they must pick from it.
Long queue = waitingTwo cafés running at once; swap waiter/customer fast so nobody waits long.

Phonics Hero 🔤 ⚡
carpet, moving

① Recognise / review. Phonics Hero lesson 31 · tricky c·g·ph + /eə/ — sounds c · g · ph  ·  air · ear · are. 🦝 Speaky shows each with its Jolly action (gesture technique); children do the action and NAME the sound — they already know all 26. We don't drill bare sounds: teacher voices, children gesture.

② Blend / build. Slide the sounds: ph-o-ne → phone, h-air → hair, c-are → care, b-ear → bear — arm-sweep + a meaning picture; show c two ways (cat / city) and g two ways (go / gem). ▶ Sound hop — children hop to the spelling Speaky calls (air / ear / are), then blend a word with it.

③ Speed reading. open Phonics Hero 2.0 (our workbook) p.86–90 — finger under each word, read the row FAST as a group (whole words, playful, celebrate speed — real reading from zero, GMF1-style).

Tricky words: read father · after together as whole words (we don't sound tricky words out).

25–32′
Game · Waiter, waiter! (class café) ⚡
café tables

▶ Waiter, waiter! Set up 1–2 café tables. A child is the waiter with a tray, the others queue with menu-cards. Waiter: Can I help you? customer: I want salad, please! The waiter «serves» the food card. Swap roles fast — everyone orders & everyone serves.

💪 Support: point to the food + please. ⭐ Extension: order two things: I want fish, please. I don't want chicken.

▶ The Koala Game Pass a cuddly toy (Speaky's koala friend) round the circle. To pass it you must say the polite line — Do you want rice? — Yes, please! Here you are. — Thank you! the toy only moves on please / thank you. Calm, seated, everyone speaks in turn.
