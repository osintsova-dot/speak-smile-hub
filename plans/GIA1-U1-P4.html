🏁 PRODUCT · the 60-second scene
stage

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share: pairs play the 60-sec scene by the stopwatch; judges check the word card, answer peer review ex.5, and watch for Common errors

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. PERFORM: volunteer pairs first, then everyone “corridor-style”; stopwatch, pauses >3 sec — a minus.
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A speaks for their timed turn, B is the active listener (counts pauses on their fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. The judge checks the card BEFORE the scene: no more than 4 words!
  4. Peer review (ex.5): judges answer out loud: Key phrases correct? WHERE are the new students from? — and a flag for any “crime” from the Common errors poster.
  5. Rubric points to the leaderboard; a clean scene with no flags = gold run ×2.
📺 On screen: stopwatch · rubric · word cards with the judges · Common errors poster
👦 Students do: play live from keywords, judges listen with a double task. Expected: “Her cover is Brazil!”. Typical error: smuggling in a script — the judge confiscates it before the scene, that's part of the game.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — clean forms (1–3) · Richness — adjectives, connectors, bonus structures (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		stopwatch · rubric cards

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can run an interrogation with inverted be-questions and etiquette-perfect short answers (hour 1, exactly 45′) and improvise a “meeting new people” scene in a cover identity, 60 sec without a script (hour 2).
  • Subsidiary. Gate exposes the word-order ceiling in writing; short-answer etiquette; TB Common errors as a “catch the error” round; flags Messageboard round-up; GMF4 bonus (past interrogation); then Key phrases + Real-world grammar, improv limiters, peer review ex.5.
  • Personal. One story arc: officers in hour 1, burned agents undercover in hour 2 — the same questions they fired at travellers get fired at THEM in the corridor. Inversion is oral-automatic; the written gate proves where it isn't.
By the end, students can…
  • write 6 correctly-ordered questions under 90 sec
  • hold short-answer etiquette under 5-sec fire
  • catch both TB common errors in others' speech
  • hold a first-meeting conversation without a script

Structure

Form

Pron. watch
yes/no rise ↗ (TB ABCD: Wh-questions fall ↘); short answer rhythm Yes-it-IS / No-it-ISn't; Nice to meet you as one chunk.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“You've asked questions for years. Written, ordered, in 15 sec each — sure?” → gate.
“Officer answers just ‘Yes.’ — professional?” → No: Yes, I am.
“Reading your dialogue from paper at PERFORM?” → No. Keywords only — 4 max.

Assumptions: Inversion familiar (GMF4); ceilings: written word order at speed, sustained etiquette, IMPROV under limiters. Masks lower stage fear. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
