WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share on repeat: entry; rewind P4: three questions to a neighbour from a support; a bridge to texting

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Entry: Fist bump — what are you doing right now?
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A talks for their time against the timer, B is the active listener (counts pauses on their fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's feedback starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. Rewind P4 from a support on the board: Are you …ing? · What are you …ing? · Is your friend …ing? — pairs ask each other 3 questions, 60 sec.
  4. Bridge: Now your friend is NOT here. You can only TEXT. Same questions — but shorter.
📺 On screen: board: three-question support (from Pair 4)
👦 Students do: ask the questions from the support. Expected: the form holds up. Typical error: “What you are doing” — we count the carriages.

Input: the magic word so
Discover 2 · the magic word so
board · teams

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: the so rule picture → ex.3–4 (find 3 examples) → ex.5 rewrite 4 sentences with a check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rule picture on the board: REASON → so → RESULT. Example out loud: It's raining SO we can't go out. Chorus ×2.
  2. Ex.3, 30 sec: he wants to see the graffiti artists.
  3. Ex.4 a hunt in pairs: Find THREE sentences with ‘so’ in the chats! Key: We're studying for our French exam so I can't come. · Mum's at work so I'm looking after my little sister. · We're finishing now so I can be there at 12..
  4. Ex.5 one item at a time: the first together on the board, the rest in teams with a check. Key: 1 Jan's dad is from Berlin so he can speak German. · 2 It's raining so we can't go running now. · 3 Today's a holiday so we haven't got school. · 4 I haven't got any money so I can't go to the cinema..
  5. A mini-round on mini-boards (Extra TB p.93): so or because? — 4 sentences, boards up.
  6. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.
📺 On screen: board: the so arrow · SB p.80 ex.3–5 · mini-boards
👦 Students do: work out the rule from the picture, look for so in the text, tell so/because apart on the boards — everyone answers. Expected: 3 out of 4 in the mini-round. Typical error: so at the start of a sentence — point at the arrow.

Play · the “… so …” chain

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: ▶ Chain sentences (TB p.6): round the circle building on the result; then ex.6 in writing with a support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Chain sentences: I give a start — It's sunny today so … — each person round the circle adds their own result, without repeating. The team's streak on the board.
  2. Three circles: I'm hungry so… · I haven't got my phone so… · It's my birthday so…
  3. Ex.6 in writing, 3 min: their own endings to 5 starters (SB p.81), the support — examples on the board. Spot checks.
  4. I nail down: After ‘so’ — a SUBJECT and a VERB, not one word.
  5. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
📺 On screen: board: 5 starters for ex.6 + streak counter
👦 Students do: stand in a circle and build on it — everyone speaks; then they write their own. Expected: a streak of 8–12. Typical error: “so happy” instead of a sentence — the rule “subject + verb”.

WHAT WE DO: choose an event and a recipient; 4 stages of the dialogue; write the first 3 lines from a skeleton

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK: Pairs — one of you wants to go somewhere. Where? A film? A skate park? A graffiti event? 60 sec.
  2. 4 stages on the board: Say hello. · Invite your friend to do something with you / talk about a plan. · Agree or disagree with the idea; give a reason. · Say goodbye..
  3. Phrase bank (ABCD TB p.93) on the board: ABCD TB p.93 — inviting: Can you come? · Are you busy? · Are you doing anything now? · Are you free later? Turning down: I can't come. · Sorry, I can't today. · What about tomorrow? Other: No problem. · See you (Thursday/later).
  4. I hand out the chat skeleton as a card (line starters). They write the FIRST 3 lines, 5 min.
  5. Check by rows: I show a thumb up or point at the stage that's missing.
📺 On screen: board: 4 stages + phrase bank · skeleton cards
👦 Students do: choose an event and write the first portion from the skeleton. Expected: 3 lines from everyone. Typical error: an invitation with no question — the phrase bank is right there.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write a 6-line text message dialogue with an invitation, a polite refusal with a reason and at least one correct use of so.
  • Subsidiary. Text language is decoded and reassembled; so (result) is introduced with a picture and kept apart from because; invitation/refusal phrases are given as a bank; the writing goes in three portions with checks; Quick review p.81 — a team game with mutual help.
  • Personal. Handwriting is slow for them — only portions of 4–5 minutes. The correspondence skeleton support is legal the whole lesson: the goal isn't to “compose from scratch” but to assemble the right format and not lose so.
By the end, students can…
  • decode text language (R U · C U · missing words)
  • use so before the result
  • invite and refuse politely with a reason
  • check their own writing against 3 questions

Target language
Lexis
Hi. Are you busy?Do you want to meet?Sorry, I can't today.What about tomorrow?No problem. See you later.… so I can't come.
Structure
Hi. Are you busy? · Do you want to meet? · I'm looking after my sister so I can't come. · What about tomorrow? · OK. See you later.
Form
Subskill: so sits BETWEEN two parts; whatever comes AFTER so is the RESULT. Danny likes art SO he wants to go to the graffiti event. (because works the other way round: result first, then reason)
Pron. watch
chat tongue-twisters: What R U doing · C U later; invitation intonation — rising, refusal — softly falling.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“It's raining so we can't go — what comes after so?” → the result.
“so or because: I'm busy ___ I can't come.” → so (the result comes after).
“C U later — what's that?” → See you later, chat language.

Assumptions: Present continuous is active after Pairs 3–4, but written accuracy is still fragile — we keep the cheat sheet. Texting as a genre is native to them, the English format is new. so as a linker is introduced for the first time.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They mix up so and becauseAn arrow picture on the board: REASON → so → RESULT; check with the question “what came first?”
They write long and run out of timeThree portions of 4–5 minutes with a check after each; one line = 1–2 sentences.
A refusal with no reasonA phrase bank on the board: apology + reason + alternative. A line with no reason doesn't count.
Quick review = boringOnly a team game with the key on hand, mutual help allowed, a fast pace.
They're afraid to “compose”A correspondence skeleton with line starters — a legal support; creativity only in the content.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
