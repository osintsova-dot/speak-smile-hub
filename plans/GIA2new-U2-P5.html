🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Questions are the structure a new student needs most: without them there is no talking. So the pair is built as a staircase: three tables from examples → the question machine with prompt cards → a quiz with ready-made questions → their own interview. I separate the three “channels” (be / have got / do-does) by colour, so the choice is visible to the eye instead of being something you have to remember.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“How do you make friends? You ask questions. Where do you play? Why do you like it? Who is your favourite player? Today — the full question map: three ways, three colours. Blue for IS and ARE, yellow for HAVE GOT, green for everything else. We build questions together, play a sports quiz, and then you interview a partner for real. Ready to ask?”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: questions are the key to a conversation: three ways to ask one (be / have got / do-does), three colours. We build them together, play a sports quiz and take a real interview from a partner.

WHAT WE DO: we sort examples a–c from ex.1 into the three tables → finish the tables → chorus

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Cards in three colours: Who is the team captain? (blue) · When have you got your next match? (yellow) · Why do you like hockey? (green)
  2. I sort the first card myself and explain out loud (“I see IS — so it's the blue table”), then Kagan Think-Pair-Share: Which example goes in which table? And WHY? — 30 seconds alone in silence, a minute with a neighbour (they must say the “why” out loud), then the class answers. TB key ex.1: be — c · have got — b · other verbs — a.
  3. We finish the tables in the notebook (copied from the book, leaving space).
  4. Chorus with rhythm: Where DO you play? Where DOES he play?
📺 On screen: coloured cards · SB p.30 ex.1
👦 Students do: they work the rule out through the colours. Expect: the colour holds the choice better than the rule does

Pronunciation · do you /djʊ/
desks

WHAT WE DO: SB p.116: the linked do you + falling intonation, in chorus and in pairs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Listen: 'What sport d'you like?' Two words, one sound: /djʊ/.
  2. TB stress key: 1 What sport, like · 2 When, play · 3 Where, play · 4 Who, play with · 5 Why, like this sport.
  3. Five questions in chorus, then Kagan RallyRobin in pairs: A asks question 1 with the hand going down, B — question 2, A again — question 3. The partner listens not for the meaning but for the end of the phrase: hand goes down — I nod, hand goes up — I show “lower” with my palm.
📺 On screen: SB p.116 · audio 61–62 (Backup: I read the script aloud twice, at the same pace)
👦 Students do: they get the “grown-up” sound. Expect: the linked d'you lands as a trick — they ask you to say it again

WB Unit 2 · the coloured channel cards stay in the classroom
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can build Wh- questions with be, have got and do/does using colour support, and interview a partner with 5 questions.
  • Subsidiary. The three tables in ex.1, built from examples; ex.2 assembling questions; the sports quiz ex.4–5 (track 16) as safe practice; ex.6 their own questions; pronunciation of do you /djʊ/.
  • Personal. I drill the intonation of a Wh- question with my hand going DOWN (unlike Yes/No). The weaker ones get cards with ready-made questions; the stronger ones make their own questions without cards.
By the end, students can…
  • choose the right question channel (be / have got / do-does)
  • build a Wh- question with a card support
  • ask with falling intonation
  • interview a partner with 5 questions and write 3 lines

Form
question word + be/have-has/do-does + subject (+ got / + bare verb, no -s)
Pron. watch
the intonation of a Wh- question FALLS (unlike Yes/No): Why is hockey so important to you? ↘ · SB p.116: an unstressed do you in a question sounds like one word /djʊ/ — What sport do you like? · Where do you play?
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«Why she likes it?» — why doesn't that work? → you need does: Why does she like it? — and the verb is bare.
«How many cousins have you?» — what is missing? → got: How many cousins have you got?
«What you do in your free time?» → What DO you do in your free time?

Assumptions: SB p.30 + Pronunciation p.116, KTP pair 17. The Wh- words are familiar, but choosing the channel and the word order are new. A staircase from cards to their own questions.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
do and does get mixed upColour code: blue cards — be, yellow — have got, green — do/does; the green ones carry an extra note he/she/it → does. The colour holds all lesson.
They answer off the point because they did not understand the questionThe first rounds use questions whose answer is in the book (the quiz): understanding is checked by the answer, not by translation.
Rising intonation, as in Yes/No questionsHand drill: on a Wh- question the hand GOES DOWN. Three runs in chorus.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
