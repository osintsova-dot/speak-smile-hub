Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to ask & answer about someone else's health: “Has he got (a cold)? — Yes, he has. / No, he hasn't.  Does she feel (dizzy)? — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.”
  • Subsidiary. Understand Ravi & Lily's dialogue about Josh (CD2 02); discover the rule — the short answer keeps the SAME verb: Has…? → has/hasn't · Does…? → does/doesn't (Grammar clue); recycle the 12 health problems from L1; write questions & answers about pictures (AB follow-up).
  • Personal. Drill the matched short answer until it's automatic (Has→has, Does→does); keep the mime-interview strictly timed; model the full answer, accept a slip, recast with a smile.
By the end, pupils can…
  • ask “Has he/she got…? / Do(es) they/she feel…?” correctly;
  • give the matched short answer: “Yes, he has. / No, she doesn't.”;
  • act out a “What's the matter?” conversation.

Structure
Has he got (a headache)? — Yes, he has. / No, he hasn't.  ·  Does she feel (sick)? — Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
Form
Has he/she got…? → Yes, he has. / No, he hasn't.  ·  Does he/she feel…? → Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't. The answer verb MATCHES the question verb.
Pron. watch
weak has he /əz i/, does she /dʌʃ i/ at speed; hasn't /ˈhæznt/, doesn't /ˈdʌznt/ — the -n't is clear.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Has he got a cold? — do I answer with has or does?” → has (it matches Has!).
“Does she feel sick? — Yes, she has, or Yes, she does?” → Yes, she does!

Assumptions: 12 health problems are fresh from L1 (checked in warm-up). Have/has got and do/does are known, but keeping the answer verb MATCHED to the question is the new focus. Pupils can read the dialogue speech bubbles & the grammar table.

Tic-Tac-Toe · Has/Does
desks, 2 teams

▶ Noughts & Crosses — health. 3×3 grid of health pictures. To claim a cell, the team ASKS the other team about a person on the card — Has she got a temperature? — and the other team answers in chorus with the matched verb: Yes, she has! / No, she doesn't. Both sides speak every turn. Rotate the asker.

🎮 Projector/board option: draw the 3×3 grid on the whiteboard; a rotating child taps the winning cell as the WHOLE team says the question aloud.

ICQ: Has…? takes which answer — has or does? (→ has!)

A5 glue-in grammar map — CHOOSE THE RIGHT QUESTION PATH: HAVE/HAS GOT (a health problem) vs DO/DOES + FEEL (how someone feels), with the short-answer rule “short answers repeat the helper”
  • Glue the map facing the L1 illness pictures — problems on one side, question paths on the other.
  • Trace the two paths with a finger: A PROBLEM — have got. A FEELING — do you feel.
  • Everyone colours path 1 and path 2 in two different colours, then colours each short answer to match its helper.
  • During Tic-Tac-Toe a square is won only if the short answer repeats the same helper as the question.

27–37′
Mime interview · info-gap
mingle → desks, pairs

Model first. Give 2 pupils a secret problem card; ask publicly: Has he got a cough? — No, he hasn't. Does he feel dizzy? — Yes, he does! until you guess it.

▶ Talk Partners mime interview (act. 4-style). Each child holds a secret problem card. A asks Yes/No questions — Has she got a headache? Does she feel sick? — B answers & mimes; guess in 3 questions, then swap & trade cards. 5–6 exchanges, new partner each time.

✔ Assess: circulate with the class list — who keeps the answer verb matched? Recast, note names.

💪 Support: question stems printed on the card. ⭐ Extension: add: Poor you! You must go to bed.
