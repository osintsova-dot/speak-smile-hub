🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use verbs with two objects in both orders — she gave me the money and she gave the money to me — and know which order takes a preposition and which takes none.
What is genuinely hard
The rule takes ten minutes to understand and a lifetime to apply, because Russian marks the recipient with a case ending and English marks it with POSITION. A Russian speaker therefore imports a preposition to do the job the case ending used to do, and writes «tell to me», «send to me a photo», «explain me the rule» — three different errors from one cause. The second difficulty is that both English orders are correct, so the error is invisible to the ear that produced it.
Typical error to expect
«tell to me», «send to me a photo», «explain me» — with an indirect object FIRST, English uses no preposition at all: tell me, send me a photo. The preposition only appears when the order flips: send a photo TO me. Russian marks the recipient with a case ending, so the to is imported to do a job English does with word order.
Why the lesson is built this way
This is the last new grammar of the course and it is deliberately given a whole lesson, because the unit runs over three and because the error it fixes appears in every email a candidate writes. The rule is derived from two sentences in ninety seconds and then practised for fifty minutes, which is the right ratio for a point that is easy to grasp and hard to habituate. The identification stage (underline the direct object, circle the indirect one) is done on paper because the physical act of circling fixes which is which. The error-correction exercise is the heart of the lesson: all five items are the same mistake, and noticing that is the moment the rule becomes usable. The productive stage is a giving-and-receiving memory game that cannot be played without both orders.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Half of you wrote «tell to me» in an email this term. It is one mistake with one cause, and today it stops. English decides who gets what by WORD ORDER, not by a preposition — and once you see it, you cannot unsee it.”

Building the rule
pairs → class
Why: Building the rule

WHAT WE DO: the Teacher's Book warmer plus four real sentences from their own emails

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed. Write on the board: some flowers / for / they / bought / me. Mixed-ability pairs, ninety seconds, make the sentence. Answer: They bought some flowers for me.
  2. Write it up and label it together, out loud, using plain words first: who did it (they), what they did (bought), the THING (flowers), the PERSON (me).
  3. Then ask for the other version — They bought me some flowers — and put it directly underneath. Two sentences, same meaning, and one of them has a preposition in it.
  4. Now the four anonymous sentences from their own marked emails, on the board, unattributed: Tell to me about your school. · Please send to me a photo. · Can you explain me this rule? · I will show to you the pictures. Ask what all four have in common. Let them find it.
  5. That is the rule, and it is now theirs rather than the book's. Write the frame: PERSON first → no preposition · THING first → to / for.
📺 On screen: the two model sentences and the four anonymous real errors, side by side — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: build a sentence from jumbled words, produce the second version themselves, then diagnose four real errors as one error

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3: the technical words, then the objects marked by hand

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 as a class, ninety seconds: which of the two example sentences has two objects? Answer: You can give your favourite charity the money — the money is the thing, the charity is the person.
  2. Now bring in the technical words, because the reference page uses them: the THING is the DIRECT object, the PERSON is the INDIRECT object. Write both pairs on the board with the plain words in brackets underneath.
  3. Give two further examples of your own, slowly: I bought a new phone yesterday (one object) against She showed her holiday photos to her friends (two).
  4. ex.2 in mixed-ability pairs, four minutes: the two sentences and the four rules. Answers: photograph is direct and me is indirect in both; a a thing, b a person, c direct, d indirect.
  5. ex.3 individually, five minutes, and it is done BY HAND on paper: underline the direct object, circle the indirect one, in six sentences. Answers, indirect objects: 1 him, 2 his dad, 3 her mother, 4 me, 5 me, 6 each other.
  6. Check with Kagan Showdown, one item at a time — everybody holds up their marked sentence together.
📺 On screen: the two labelled pairs of terms and the frame, all on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: mark six sentences by hand, underlining and circling, then reveal them simultaneously

Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer on the order that carries the meaning

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two sides of the room: NO PREPOSITION and TO or FOR.
  2. You read a sentence with the preposition slot audible but empty — She gave ______ her brother the money. She gave the money ______ her brother. I bought ______ my sister a present. Everybody walks to the side that is correct.
  3. The first person there says the full sentence. If the class is split, ask which came first — the person or the thing — and the split resolves itself.
  4. Eight rounds. Then freeze and sit: Now you write both versions of everything.
📺 On screen: the two side labels only — chalk and paper, and nothing can fail
👦 Students do: everyone decides with their feet eight times and a different student produces the full sentence each round

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: five corrections that turn out to be a single error

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5 in pairs, four minutes: five sentences, correct the mistake in each. Do not say in advance that they are all the same.
  2. Answers: 1 Parents have to pay THEM a lot of money. · 2 I will show YOU the photographs. · 3 Tell ME everything about our last class. · 4 I'm going to tell YOU a story about my holiday. · 5 Can you lend ME a pencil, please?
  3. Then ask the question that makes the stage worth ten minutes: What did all five have in common? Answer: an extra to, or a person in the wrong place. One cause, five sentences.
  4. Point back at the four anonymous sentences from their own emails, still on the board from the warmer. Same error. Say so.
  5. Three minutes, individually, and this is the transfer: take out your marked email from lesson 64 and find every sentence with a person and a thing in it. Check each one against the frame. Repair anything that needs it, above the line.
  6. Take two repairs to the floor, volunteered, not nominated.

🎯 The giving game
groups of four → class
Why: 🎯 The giving game

WHAT WE DO: ex.6 turned into a memory game that needs both orders

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.6 first, four minutes in mixed-ability pairs, full sentences only: who did you send your last message to · what presents did people give you for your last birthday · has anyone promised you anything recently · who do you usually show your homework to.
  2. Take three answers to the floor and insist on the full sentence each time.
  3. 🎯 Then the game, and it runs for twelve minutes. Groups of four. Each student writes THREE cards: a person, a thing and a verb from the two lists. Cards go face down in three piles.
  4. Round one: a student turns over one of each and has ten seconds to make a correct sentence in EITHER order. The group judges against the frame on the board. One point for a correct sentence, TWO for producing both orders.
  5. Round two, harder and better: the person card must be replaced by a pronoun. That forces the no-preposition order and the class will discover why — a pronoun almost never comes last.
  6. Round three, three minutes: the memory version. Each student in turn adds a sentence to a chain and has to repeat everything said before it. It is the last game of the course and it should end loudly.
  7. Delayed correction, three minutes: five sentences you heard, on the board, repaired against the frame by the class.
📺 On screen: the frame, the two verb lists and a running score — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: answer four personal questions in full sentences, then produce dozens of two-object sentences under time pressure in a group where nobody can hide

Consolidation showdown
teams of four
Why: Consolidation showdown

WHAT WE DO: ten minutes that fix the rule in the form the test will use it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Teams of four, mini-whiteboards or paper.
  2. Round one, four minutes: you read a sentence with the preposition missing or wrongly added. Teams write the corrected version and hold it up together. One point each, and no discussion after the reveal — speed is the point.
  3. Round two, four minutes: you read jumbled words with no preposition among them and the teams build the sentence. This is exactly the block-test format, and say so: if to or for is not in the jumble, the person comes first.
  4. Two minutes: award the points and take the two items most teams got wrong back to the frame.
  5. Say the test warning six days early: on the block test this section forbids adding any words, so the prompt decides the order. Read the prompt before you decide anything.
📺 On screen: the running score and the frame on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: every team writes and reveals every round, in the exact format the block test will use

Structure
You can give your favourite charity the money. · My friend sent me this photograph. · My friend sent this photograph to me. · I bought my brother a book.
Form
verb + INDIRECT (person) + DIRECT (thing), no preposition · verb + DIRECT (thing) + to / for + INDIRECT (person) · to with give, send, show, tell, lend, teach, pay, throw, owe · for with buy, make, cook, bake, choose, fetch, find.
Pron. watch
achieve /əˈtʃiːv/ — two syllables, stress on the second, and the class will say a three-syllable version · imagine /ɪˈmædʒɪn/ keeps its stress in the middle · in a phrasal verb the PREPOSITION carries the stress and that is how a listener hears it: I missed OUT, we worked it OUT · and in verbs with two objects the sentence stress lands on the last word, which is why «she gave me a BOOK» and «she gave the book to ME» mean slightly different things
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«She sent me a photo.» Which word is the thing? → photo. Which is the person? → me. Which came first? → the person — so no preposition.
«She sent a photo ___ me.» What goes in the gap? → to. The thing came first, so the person needs a preposition.
«Tell to me everything» — right? → no. The person is first, so nothing goes in front of it. Tell me everything.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«It sounds fine to me» — the error is inaudible to the producerThat is why the anonymous real examples matter. Four sentences from their own emails on the board, unattributed, repaired by the class. Nobody argues with their own writing.
They learn one order and use only that oneThe frame has TWO lines and both get practised. The giving game is built so that both are needed — a pronoun forces one order, a long noun phrase invites the other.
Direct and indirect get confused as labelsDrop the labels for the first ten minutes and use THING and PERSON. Bring the technical words in afterwards, once the distinction is secure — the grammar reference uses them and they will meet them again.
Fast finishers throughoutThe Teacher's Book extension for exercise 6: answer the four personal questions in full sentences and add two more questions of their own for a partner.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text) and task 37.

Verbs with two objects are not a named line of the codifier, but they decide word order in half the sentences of a personal email — send me a photo, tell me about your school, show us your work. The Russian error is not the pattern itself, it is the extra to: «tell to me», «send to me», which is Russian case-marking leaking into English. The Review 5 error-correction section tests exactly that and so does every email a candidate writes.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
