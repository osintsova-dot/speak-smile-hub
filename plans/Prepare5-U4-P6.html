🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Complete an open cloze — six gaps, no options, one word each — by asking what KIND of word is missing before asking which one (B1 Reading Part 6), and write to the two exam formats: an email in which all four notes are answered and an article or a story of about a hundred words.
What is genuinely hard
The open cloze has no options, and that is exactly why it frightens people — there is nothing to choose between, so the student has to know what is missing. Almost every answer is a grammar word (the, are, if, it, as, of), which is precisely the class of word Russian does without: no articles, no auxiliaries, no empty subjects. The writing half is a planning problem: two tasks, one choice, and a story that must begin with somebody else's sentence.
Typical error to expect
Leaving an article or an auxiliary gap empty because it does not feel like a word. It is the word — half the answers on an open cloze are exactly this class.
Writing the story that ignores the given first sentence, or changing it. It is printed for a reason and it must appear untouched.
Why the lesson is built this way
Everything today is method made visible. The cloze starts with the gap the book has already filled in (1 the) and the class explains WHY it is the — which teaches more than any rule. Then five gaps under a clock with one extra requirement: write the PART OF SPEECH in the margin before the word. The writing half is split — Part 1 is written in full because it is the compulsory task, Part 2 is planned and started rather than finished, so that the choice between article and story is made consciously and the first paragraph is written with support. The strategy card gets its two new entries and the block closes.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Six gaps, and this time there are no options at all. Five of the six answers are words you would not translate: the, are, if, it, as. Then two pieces of writing — one you must do, one you choose. And in the story, the first sentence is already written for you.”

Part 6 · the text, and why gap 1 is «the»
individual → pairs → class
Why: Part 6 · the text, and why gap 1 is «the»

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2: gist under a clock, then the first gap dissected in public

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the scene in one sentence: «The Northern Lights» — where and when they can be seen, why they happen, and how many people travel north to watch them. The first gap is done for you: 1 the.
  2. ex.1, three minutes, silent: read the whole text with the gaps ignored. One question — where and when can you see them, and why do they happen?
  3. Kagan Think-Pair-Share, ninety seconds: answer the question in pairs, then two answers to the room.
  4. ex.2 in public and slowly: why is gap 1 the? Because there is only one set of Northern Lights, and because a plural noun with a unique reference takes the. Underline the noun on the board.
  5. Then the general move, said once: Look at the word AFTER the gap and the word BEFORE it. Those two decide the part of speech nine times out of ten.
📺 On screen: the class's gist answer and the dissected first gap on the board. Backup: the text is printed on the page and needs nothing else
👦 Students do: read for meaning under a clock, then watch one gap decided by grammar rather than by meaning

20–32′
Part 6 · the task and the autopsy
individual → pairs → class
Why: Part 6 · the task and the autopsy

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: five gaps under a clock, with a part-of-speech label for each

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the exam tip aloud: Read the whole text first for meaning. Then, at each gap, ask what KIND of word is missing before you ask which word — a preposition? an article? part of a verb? Finally read the whole text again and listen to it.
  2. ex.3 individually, five minutes, clock visible. Rule: in the margin next to each gap, write the label FIRST — article, verb, preposition, pronoun, quantity.
  3. Pairs compare — labels first, words second. Where the labels agree, the words nearly always do; where they disagree, the argument is short and useful.
  4. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: 2 are · 3 number · 4 if/when · 5 it · 6 as. For each, take the LABEL first from the student, then the word.
  5. Then the reasons, one line each: 2 are — the subject is plural and the verb is a present passive · 3 number — a number of is a fixed quantity phrase · 4 if/when — a first conditional, present simple + will · 5 it — the empty subject that pushes the real subject to the end · 6 as — the second half of an as … as comparison, which is exactly the structure Unit 8 built
  6. Close the task with the count that matters: Six gaps. Not one of them needed a word about the Northern Lights. This task is grammar wearing a reading hat.
📺 On screen: the six labels and then the six words listed on the board as the check proceeds — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: work alone with a part-of-speech label for every gap, then compare labels before words
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text). An open cloze and the Russian grammar section are the same task with one difference: the Russian one gives you the word to transform.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: after the class check, give three Russian-format items on the board (a short text with three gaps and three capitalised words) for five minutes. Take the time from the Writing Part 2 stage, which can end with the plan instead of the paragraph.

Move break · what kind of word is missing?
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Teenagers who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer that drills the cloze reflex physically

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Eight short sentences with one gap each are stuck around the walls — each one missing a different class of word (an article, an auxiliary, a preposition, a pronoun, a comparative marker…).
  2. Everyone walks. At each sentence they say ONE thing to a neighbour: the label, not the word. That one needs a preposition.
  3. Ninety seconds of walking, then freeze and take three answers aloud — with the word this time.
  4. Sit them down with the transition: Page one hundred and twenty-seven. Now you write — and there are four notes that all have to be answered.
📺 On screen: eight gapped sentences on the walls, printed or handwritten in advance — nothing to fail
👦 Students do: walk, read and label eight gaps aloud to a neighbour

Writing Part 1 · the four notes decoded
class → individual
Why: Writing Part 1 · the four notes decoded

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the four sentences matched to Sam's four notes, then planned

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the task information aloud: Writing Part 1 — an email of about a hundred words with four notes written on it. Every note must be answered. Answering three of the four is the commonest way to lose the communicative mark, in both exams.
  2. Read Sam's email aloud yourself, once. Then ex.1: match the four sentences to the four notes. Answer: 1 Say what · 2 Tell Sam · 3 Great! · 4 Suggest …
  3. Decode what each note DEMANDS, on the board, as verbs: react · give information · explain · suggest. Four verbs, four sentences minimum.
  4. Two minutes, in pairs: plan the email out loud, one note each, alternating. Nobody writes yet — this is deliberately spoken first, because a plan said aloud survives into the writing.
  5. Then everyone copies the four notes into the margin of their paper as a checklist. Walk the room and check every margin before the clock starts.
📺 On screen: the four notes decoded as four verbs on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: decode four notes, plan aloud in pairs, and build a margin checklist before writing

WHAT WE DO: ex.2: about 100 words in fourteen minutes, all four notes answered

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Fourteen minutes, silent, clock on the board. About 100 words. You sit down and do not answer language questions (Write the word you have. We fix it afterwards.).
  2. Announce the time at minute 9 and minute 12. Nothing else.
  3. At the end, two minutes: everyone reads their own email once and ticks the four notes in the margin. Fewer than four ticks means one more sentence, now.
  4. Then ninety seconds in RallyCoach pairs: swap and check ONLY the four ticks and the short forms. No other corrections.
  5. Read the Teacher's Book model aloud at the end, once, at speed — not as a standard to reach but as evidence that a hundred words is short: Hi Sam, thanks for your email … See you soon, Alice.
  6. Collect the emails. You mark them properly against the four criteria plus language.
📺 On screen: the running clock and the four notes on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write about 100 words alone against a clock, audit their own text, then check a partner's four ticks

62–74′
Writing Part 2 · choose, plan, and start
individual → pairs
Why: Writing Part 2 · choose, plan, and start

WHAT WE DO: the article or the story: the choice made consciously and the first paragraph written

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read both tasks aloud: question 2 is an article, «Being healthy» — what matters more, sport or food, and what you personally do; question 3 is a story that has to start with «Jake ran towards the building as quickly as possible.»
  2. Ninety seconds, in pairs, and this is the point of the stage: Which one would you choose, and why? Then the honest advice, said once: an article is safer because you control the content; a story is faster if you have an idea in the first thirty seconds.
  3. Everybody chooses and writes a three-line plan: beginning, middle, end (for the story) or opinion, reason, example (for the article). Three minutes, no sentences yet.
  4. Then six minutes: write the FIRST PARAGRAPH only. Story writers copy the given first sentence exactly — Jake ran towards the building as quickly as possible. — and continue from it.
  5. Ninety seconds in pairs: read your first paragraph to your partner. The partner says one thing only — what they expect to happen next. If they cannot, the paragraph is not doing its job.
  6. Say clearly what happens to it: Finish it at home if you want to. It is not homework — the Grammar Plus sheets are.
📺 On screen: the two tasks and the three-line plan shape on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: choose between two tasks with a reason, plan in three lines, write one paragraph, then test it on a partner

WHAT WE DO: the card gets Reading Part 6 and the two writing tasks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everyone takes out the strategy card (SB p.120) and adds today's three entries: PART 6 (open cloze) — what KIND of word first. WRITING 1 — four notes, four ticks. WRITING 2 — copy the first sentence exactly, then beginning, middle, end.
  2. The card now covers Reading Parts 1–6 and both writing tasks. Say that aloud, because it is genuinely most of the written paper and they have built it themselves across four blocks.
  3. One personal line each: which of the tasks is MY weakest, and what I do about it before the next exam page.
  4. Two students read their personal line aloud. No comment.
  5. Then close the block properly, in thirty seconds: Two units, one test, one exam page. You can now describe a building, compare two of them, talk about music professionally, and write two kinds of text. Say it as a list — teenagers underestimate how much a block contains.
  6. Say where these come back: Part six in Review three. The email in every block from now on.

Structure
The Northern Lights are seen in the far north. · A number of people travel north to see them. · If you go in winter, you will have the best chance. · It is not as difficult as it sounds.
Form
the grammar words that open clozes live on: articles, auxiliaries (are, have, do), prepositions, pronouns, the empty subject it, quantity phrases (a number of), and the second half of comparisons (as … as, than).
Pron. watch
reading and writing today; incidental work only — aurora /ɔːˈrɔːrə/, phenomenon /fəˈnɒmɪnən/, and the weak forms of are and as, which is why they are hard to hear and easy to leave out
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«___ Northern Lights are seen in Norway.» What kind of word is missing? → an article. Which one? → the: there is only one set of Northern Lights.
«It is not ___ difficult as it sounds.» Which structure is this? → as … as. So the missing word is as, and it is grammar, not vocabulary.
Four notes on the email. How many go in the margin as a checklist? → all four, before I write a single sentence.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Gaps are filled with content wordsThe margin rule is absolute today: part of speech FIRST, word second. A gap with no part of speech in the margin does not get checked.
«There's nothing to choose from, so I can't do it»Reframe it once, out loud: an open cloze has no options because the answer is nearly always one of about twelve words. Put the twelve on the board — the, a, of, in, to, as, than, it, are, is, have, if — and watch the fear drop.
The story ignores or rewrites the given first sentenceOne rule on the board: Copy it exactly. Then continue. Any draft that starts differently is handed back without comment.
Two writing tasks in one lesson feels like a marathonOnly Part 1 is finished today. Part 2 is chosen, planned in three lines and opened — and the class is told that at the start, so nobody paces themselves wrongly.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (the open cloze is the closest thing in the whole year to grammar in a text) and task 37 (the email).

Reading Part 6 gives a text with six gaps and no options at all: the missing word is an article, a preposition, an auxiliary or a pronoun, and it is found by asking what part of speech the sentence is missing. That is precisely the reflex tasks 19–25 reward. The writing half is the exam's other half: an email of a hundred words in which four notes must all be answered — the commonest single reason Russian candidates lose the communicative-task mark is that they answer three of the four.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
