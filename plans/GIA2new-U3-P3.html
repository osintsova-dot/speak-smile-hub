🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): With new students you MAY announce the grammar as the topic — they need clarity, not camouflage. But the delivery is discovery: the table is completed from examples in the feed they read last lesson, and they formulate the rule themselves. Lots of short written runs with a self-check against the key — confidence grows out of “I got it right”, not out of an explanation. The -ing spelling is done with hands (cards into baskets) BEFORE any writing.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Yesterday you read a whole text where every verb looked the same: is/are + -ing. Today we find out exactly how it works — and by the end you will describe what is happening in this room, without mistakes. Two little things decide everything: the word be, and the spelling of -ing. Let's find the rule — it's hiding in the Comic Con feed.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Yesterday every verb in the feed looked the same: is/are + -ing. Today we work out how it is built, and by the end we describe what is happening in this room, with no mistakes. The rule is hiding in the text — we will find it ourselves.

WHAT WE DO: examples from the feed on cards → sort them into columns → the ex.1 table → the rule chorally

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Example cards from P2: People are buying comics · He's wearing tights · The fans aren't listening · I'm sitting here.
  2. Two columns: with 'is' and with 'are'. Sort the cards. Who goes with 'is'? Who goes with 'are'?
  3. They formulate the rule themselves (Russian is allowed) → we check it against the ex.1 table and fill the gaps. Key: 1 'm · 2 's · 3 aren't · 4 isn't · 5 listening.
  4. Chorally, in colour: I AM sitting. He IS wearing. They ARE playing. She ISN'T sitting. — be in red, -ing in blue.
📺 On screen: the example cards · SB p.40 ex.1 · markers in two colours
👦 Students do: they discover the rule with their hands. Expect: “after he/she/it it's is” comes from them. Error: they lose be — I send them back to the colours

WHAT WE DO: sorting 12 verbs into three baskets by hand → the rule chorally → a written run with a self-check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The baskets: just +ing · drop the e · double the consonant. 12 cards: buy, wear, play, watch / take, come, write, dance / swim, run, sit, get.
  2. Sort them. Why does TAKE lose its e? Why does SWIM double the m?
  3. Checking through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: we do one basket together on the board as the model, then the crew agrees on the rest and makes sure every member could explain it; I call a number — Number twos: 'run' — which basket? That student answers, but the crew has already prepared them — that is the whole protection.
  4. The rule chorally + the poster on the wall (Spelling rules SB p.45).
  5. ex.2 in writing, then the key on the slide, and I check my OWN work: circle your slips — nobody sees. Key: 1 're taking · 2 'm playing · 3 aren't wearing · 4 's waiting · 5 isn't sitting.
📺 On screen: the verb cards · three baskets (backup — three columns on the board) · SB p.40 ex.2 · the key on the slide
👦 Students do: they sort with their hands before writing. Expect: swimming works for everyone from the second go; takeing disappears once the baskets have been done

Negatives and questions
pairs

WHAT WE DO: model → transformations on the frame → the question table ex.4 → short answers chorally

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The model: I'm sitting. → I'm NOT sitting. He's wearing a hat. → He ISN'T wearing a hat. Who takes isn't, who takes aren't?
  2. The transformation frame, 5 sentences (+) → (−): I flip the first one on the board myself, then Kagan RallyCoach — the first says the negative out loud, the second looks at the key on the slide and checks one thing: is be still there; after two sentences they swap.
  3. ex.4: we complete the question tables. Key: Wh- — What's this man doing? · Yes/No — Are they having a good time? / Is he wearing a costume? · short answers — Yes, they are. / No, he isn't.
  4. Short answers chorally with a rhythm: Yes-I-AM! No-he-ISN'T! And separately: never 'Yes, I'm' — always 'Yes, I am'.
📺 On screen: SB p.40 ex.4 · the frame on the slide (Backup: the frames written on the board in chalk)
👦 Students do: they turn sentences negative and build questions with support. Expect: “Yes, I'm” takes 2–3 repetitions to remove — that is normal

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use the present continuous in affirmative, negative and question forms with correct -ing spelling, checked by themselves against keys.
  • Subsidiary. Guided discovery from the P2 text; Spelling rules p.45 with hands (cards into baskets); ex.2 and ex.3 with self-check; ex.5 the Mia/Luke messages; the /n/ and /ŋ/ sounds (SB p.116); a mini-test with self-check.
  • Personal. The pace is half that of “ours” — and that is normal. Every stage ends with a self-check against the key: the mistake is found privately, before anyone else sees it.
By the end, students can…
  • complete the present continuous table
  • spell -ing forms: buying, taking, swimming
  • make negative sentences with isn't/aren't
  • ask and answer with short answers (Yes, I am / No, he isn't)

Structure
I'm buying comics. · You/We/They're wearing costumes. · He/She/It's drawing a comic. · I'm not listening. · You/We/They aren't listening. · He/She/It isn't listening.
Form
most verbs: + -ing (buy → buying, wear → wearing) · verbs ending in -e: drop the e + -ing (take → taking, come → coming) · some verbs with “consonant + vowel + consonant”: double the consonant (swim → swimming, run → running, sit → sitting)
Pron. watch
SB p.116 /n/ vs /ŋ/: thin–thing · win–wing · go in–going; the -ing ending is not “-ing” with a hard g, it is the nasal /ŋ/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He wearing a hat” — what is missing? → the form of be: He's wearing a hat.
“Yes, they're.” — can you answer like that? → no: a short affirmative answer keeps the full form — Yes, they are. / Yes, I am.
takeing or taking? → taking: verbs ending in -e drop the e.
swiming or swimming? → swimming: consonant + vowel + consonant → double it.

Assumptions: SB p.40 + Spelling rules p.45 + Pronunciation p.116, KTP pair 23. For some of them the form is genuinely new — I build on examples from a text they have already read (familiar context).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They drop be: “He wearing”Colour code: be — red marker, -ing — blue; every sentence on the board has both colours. No red — the sentence is not finished.
The -ing spelling gets muddledSorting with hands (cards into three baskets) BEFORE writing; a cheat-sheet poster on the wall until the end of the unit.
Fear of making written mistakesEvery written run comes with a self-check against the key, before anyone else sees it. I give no marks.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
