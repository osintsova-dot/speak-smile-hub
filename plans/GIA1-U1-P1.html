WHAT WE DO: “What's new? — one word!” at the door; a blitz-rewind of the Starter — first points; the bridge: “you're travel producers”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: What's new? One-word answers only! Finger-mood 1–5 — the first ritual of the year, easy.
  2. Rewind of the Starter: air-spelling 2 names in chorus; 4 numbers from the slide — shout them out in English; 3 internationalisms — thumbs up if English uses it!
  3. TB warmer “Two words — one country” (TB p.18): California, Oscars — ? Football, London — ? Pandas, Beijing — ? Flamenco, Madrid — ? — shout the country in chorus, tempo rises.
  4. I award the first points to the future crews.
  5. Bridge: Warm? Good. In one minute you're not students — you're travel producers with a job to win.
📺 On screen: mood slide · 4 numbers large on the board · the words pizza/taxi/football · TB-warmer word pairs (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: toss a word at the door, spell in chorus and shout out numbers, vote with fingers. Expected: an easy warm-up. Typical error: “hundred” without one — a “1” finger, they fix it themselves.

8–13′
Gate: nationality speed test
🎯 GATE · nationality blitz
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 3 minutes: write the nationalities for 8 countries — fast AND without mistakes; self-check, personal score in the margin

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I display 8 countries: China · Spain · the Netherlands · Brazil · Turkey · Japan · the UK · New Zealand.
  2. I say: WRITE the nationality for each. Fast AND spelled right. You've known these words for years — prove it in writing. Three minutes! Timer.
  3. At zero on the timer I open the key: self-check, score goes in the margin of the notebook.
  4. The reveal: 8 out of 8? Silence?.. Chinese with -ese, Dutch out of nowhere — THIS is our game: not ‘knowing’, but fast-accurate-written. Rematch in Pair 7!
📺 On screen: slide: 8 countries → key (Chinese · Spanish · Dutch · Brazilian · Turkish · Japanese · British · New Zealand); no slide — countries in a column on the board, key added alongside (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: solo, write 8 nationalities at speed, honestly count the clean ones, log the score in the margin (private, not a ranking). Expected: 4–6 out of 8 — the ceiling exposed. Typical error: “Chinish/Dutchland” — no shaming: “here's your growth zone, rematch in P7”.

22–31′
Challenge: the brand duel (5-second rule)
🔥 Challenge · Brand Duel
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: Student's Book questions with the 5-second rule; then teams write THEIR OWN and duel; the word “country” is taboo

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1 (ex.7): Full sentence in five seconds or the question bounces! Sony is a brand from…? — item by item, rotating.
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift them together — I see everyone's answer, no hiding.
  3. I switch on the limiter: The word ‘country’ is TABOO — say: It's a JAPANESE brand!
  4. Round 2 (ex.8): Write two YOUR OWN items — gaming, football, K-pop welcome. Duel the next crew! 2 min.
  5. I moderate the duels; the author's bluff (doesn't know their own answer) = −2.
📺 On screen: slides of ex.7 items (or I write on the board) · the suffix table on the board (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: answer in a full sentence within 5 sec, make up their own questions about favourite brands and drill their neighbours. Expected: “Minecraft is a Swedish game!” — delight. Typical error: “from Japanese” — a tap on the table: country or adjective?

40–49′
Bonus level: this is not in the book
🚀 Bonus level · not in the book
circle

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: a chain around the circle — “I've never been to…, but next year I'll visit it” — no repeated country, a class streak

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I raise the status: This exercise is NOT in this book. Beginners can't do it. You can — GMF4 grads only.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. I write the frame: I've never been to ___, but next year I'll visit it, because it's ___. I model it myself.
  4. I start the chain round the circle: no repeated country; if the streak breaks — the count restarts (the class against itself).
  5. +1 to the leaderboard for every clean bonus sentence.
📺 On screen: board: the bonus frame in large letters · a streak counter in the corner of the board
👦 Students do: build a sentence with perfect+will round the circle, watch for non-repeated countries. Expected: a streak of 8–12. Typical error: “I never was” — I point at the frame: exactly as written, that IS the level.

49–58′
Preparing the 60-second pitch
Prepare the pitch
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: crews choose their top-3 places and prepare the pitch: each person says ≥2 sentences, notes in keywords only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I set the task: Your crew's GLOBAL TOP THREE — real places. Hook opening + one line per place. Nine minutes.
  2. Rules on the slide: everyone says ≥2 sentences · at least 1 nationality adjective · 1 bonus structure = richness.
  3. ICQ: Full script to read? — No! Keywords only!
  4. I show the judges' rubric, help with hook phrases (the phrase bank helps).
📺 On screen: pitch rules slide · judges' rubric large · phrase bank on the board (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: choose 3 places, divide them up, write keywords (not a text!). Expected: arguments over places in English from the phrase bank. Typical error: writing a full script — I take the sheet away, leave 4 words.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — clean forms (1–3) · Richness — adjectives, connectors, bonus structures (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		prep sheets · rubric slide
58–78′
Product: pitches by the stopwatch
🏁 PRODUCT · 60-sec pitches
stage

WHAT WE DO: each crew pitches 60 sec by the stopwatch, the rest are judges with the rubric; after the pitch a judge justifies the score

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I start: Crew one — sixty seconds — GO! Stopwatch visible.
  2. I hand out judging cards: fluency/accuracy/richness, 1–3.
  3. After every pitch one judge: Defend ONE score in English! (Richness 3 — they used ‘I've never been’ AND three adjectives.)
  4. I average, log it live to the leaderboard, +1 for bonus structures on air.
📺 On screen: stopwatch on screen · judging cards · leaderboard (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: pitch from keywords without reading; judges count pauses on their fingers and score 1–3 on three scales, one justifies out loud. Expected: 1–2 crews close to a gold run. Typical error: a pause >3 sec — judges log it, the bar teaches itself.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can pitch a team «Global Top-3 places» in 60 seconds without pauses, using 13 nationality adjectives accurately (spelling included).
  • Subsidiary. Gate proves the spelling ceiling (-ese/-ish/-ian); suffix system fixed as a table; Video skills vlog watched ‘like content-makers’; GMF4 bonus: I've never been to… / I'll visit… as status structures.
  • Personal. Anti-“we know it all” frame from minute 1: the mission is speed + spelling + fluency. Respect their base loudly, raise the bar immediately. Launch the leaderboard ritual.
By the end, students can…
  • write 8+ nationalities correctly under time pressure
  • test another pair with own brand-quiz items
  • react to a vlog with viewer language
  • deliver a 60-sec team pitch to the bar

Structure
It's from (Japan) — it's (Japanese). · Our №1 is… because it's… · 🚀 bonus: I've never been to (Brazil), but next year I'll visit it.
Form
suffix columns -an/-ian/-ese/-ish + irregular; the + UK/USA/Netherlands; nationality always capital.
Pron. watch
moving stress: Ja-PAN → Japa-NESE; BRA-zil → Bra-ZIL-ian; pitch voice — project to the back wall.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“You KNOW countries. Does knowing = writing Chinese correctly in 10 seconds?” → that's the gate.
“He's Dutch — which country?” → the Netherlands.
“In the pitch, is a 5-second pause OK?” → No — bar rule: pauses under 3 sec.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: countries met in GMF4 U9, be automatic orally. Real gaps: nationality SPELLING, moving stress, sustained fluent speech. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
