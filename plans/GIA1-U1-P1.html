A1+ teens (~11–13 y.o., readers) · 90 min · WDYT? re-entry + word-formation + video literacy · pairs / teams · Student's Book pp. 12–13
Week 14.09–20.09 · Pair 5 · lessons 9–10🚀 ACCELERATED (after GMF4)~12 pupils · teams of 3–4WDYT? How are countries different?
Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name countries and build their nationality adjectives by suffix route (-an · -ian · -ese · -ish + irregulars) and say where a thing / a person is from — “It's from (China). It's (Chinese).” / “I'm from (Spain). I'm (Spanish).” — in a country quiz and a travel-vlog reaction task.
  • Subsidiary. Video literacy — watch a vlog with an active task and react like a blogger; word stress on nationality adjectives (SB p116); fast diagnostic re-entry of countries & be from GMF4; critical thinking (continents; “How are countries different?”); introduce be (aff/neg) in live speech, ready for the systematic lesson (P3).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; run a stirrer→settler rhythm with one clear mid-lesson brain-break (~45′); raise the bar for the accelerated group — full accurate sentences, not single words; all-hands engagement (mini-whiteboards, simultaneous pairs), not one voice at a time.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name 12+ countries and form their nationality adjective by suffix;
  • ask & answer where something / someone is from (It's from… / It's… · I'm from… / I'm…);
  • watch a vlog for specific information and give an opinion about it;
  • use be (affirmative & negative) in context to describe people & places.

Structure
Where's (paper) from? — It's from (China). It's (Chinese).  ·  Where are you from? — I'm from (Spain). I'm (Spanish).
Form
Suffix routes: -an Mexico→Mexican · -ian India→Indian, Brazil→Brazilian · -ese China→Chinese, Japan→Japanese · -ish Spain→Spanish, the UK→British · irregular Dutch, New Zealand. be: subject + am/is/are (+ not) + from + country (introduced in context).
Pron. watch
stress shift Japan → Japanese; Russian, Turkish; /ð/ in the Netherlands; nationality ≠ language (today = nationality only). SB Pronunciation: word stress → p116.
Pronunciation
Nationality-adjective word stress (SB p116) — clap-the-beat drill; no phonics strand at this age.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Spanish — country or nationality?” → nationality.
“I'm from ___ — do I put a country or a nationality here?” → country (Spain, not Spanish).
“Japanese food — is Japanese the country or an adjective?” → adjective / nationality.

Assumptions: from GMF4 they already know most country names and basic be — so this is fast re-entry, not first teaching. New = the systematic suffix word-formation, accurate spelling & capital letters, and the video-literacy / WDYT? format itself. Learners are readers → work with the printed quiz & table. Teams: 3–4 with rotating roles.

WDYT? opener. Project the unit question: Welcome to Unit 1 — Our world. Big question: How are countries different? Fast opinion vote: Food? Language? Sport? Move to the corner of the one you think matters most. 2–3 learners say why in one sentence — Countries are different because…

Video skills lead-in (SB p13). Freeze the title frame: This is Isabel. Her vlog: “Top three places in the world.” What three places do you think she'll choose? Write your guesses.

▶ Watch-bingo — set the task. Hand each pair a 6-square bingo grid of country/place images. Cross a square the moment you see or hear it in the video. First pair with a line shouts “Bingo!” ICQ: Do you cross a square before or after you see it? (→ after.)

Video skills · watch & react

Watch (SB p13, ex 9). Play the vlog — pairs play watch-bingo and note Isabel's top three places to visit. Second play if needed; check answers, award bingo points.

62–72′
be in context · introduce for P3
teams

Notice be from the vlog & quiz. Elicit onto the board straight from what they just watched/read: Isabel is American. She isn't from the UK. The lions aren't real — they're animation. Tapas is Spanish. Sushi and sumo are Japanese. Highlight am / is / are and the negative isn't / aren't. This is a first live look, not the full table (that's P3).

▶ True / False fast-fire (teams, mini-whiteboards): Sushi is Chinese. (F → It isn't Chinese, it's Japanese.) · Ajax is Dutch. (T) · Isabel is British. (F → She isn't British, she's American.) Teams write the corrected full sentence with be. Point per accurate correction.

CCQ: Negative — do we say “no is” or “isn't”? (→ isn't.)

 board · mini-whiteboards · T/F slides
72–84′
Communicative production · “My top three”
groups → present

Mini-task (blogger reaction). Make your own “Top three places in the world.” For each place: where it is, the nationality, and one reason. Frame on the board: Number one is [Japan]. It's [Japanese]. I want to go there because it's [amazing]. Groups prepare 3 places (3 min), rehearse.

▶ Timed Pair Share — 45 sec each: present your top three like a blogger; partner listens with a 2-point peer-review checklist: ✅ 3 nationalities used correctly? ✅ 1 reason each? Swap. Two strong bloggers present to the class for team points.

Fast finishers: add a fourth secret place and let the partner guess the country from the clues.

Whole-class feedback. Praise 2–3 accurate sentences; put right 1–2 common slips from the lesson (capital N, from + country). Tally team points on the unit leaderboard.

Reflection. One thing you can do now that you couldn't at the start? Turn to your partner — one sentence each.

Exit ticket (door). As they leave, each learner names a country and its nationality: Turkey — Turkish! Off you go.
