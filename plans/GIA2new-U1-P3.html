🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): With newcomers grammar CAN be announced as the topic — they need clarity, not camouflage. But it is delivered through discovery: the table is completed from familiar examples in the P2 text, and they word the rule themselves. Many short written runs with self-check — confidence grows out of «I managed it», not out of an explanation.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One small letter — s. It changes everything in English. I wake up — but Jack wakeS up. Today you get the FULL map of the present simple: when -s, when -es, when don't and doesn't. By the end you'll write six sentences about a friend's day — and they'll all be correct. Let's find the rule — it's hiding in Jack and Abi's text.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: One letter, -s, changes everything: today we get the full map of the present simple — when -s, when -es, when don't/doesn't. The rule is hiding in the text about Jack and Abi — we will find it ourselves.

Discovery · completing the table
pairs → board

WHAT WE DO: examples from the text on the board → pairs sort them into the columns I/you/we/they vs he/she/it (RoundTable) → the table in ex.1

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Example cards: I get up · Jack wakes up · They don't go · Abi doesn't see…
  2. I place two cards myself and say out loud why I chose that column — that is the model turn. Then Kagan RoundTable: one pile per pair, you take the top card, put it in a column, say one word of reason («Jack — so the right one») and pass the pile to your neighbour.
  3. Two columns. Sort the cards. What's different in the RIGHT column?
  4. They word the rule themselves (Russian is allowed) → we check it against the table in ex.1 and fill in the gaps.
  5. Chorally: he wakes, she goes, it starts — sssss!
📺 On screen: example cards · SB p.14 ex.1
👦 Students do: they discover the rule with their hands. Expect: they say «after he/she/it — s!» themselves

Spelling station · -s / -es / -ies
crews

WHAT WE DO: sorting 12 verbs into baskets by hand → the rule through Numbered Heads Together → a written run of 6 forms with self-check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Baskets: -s · -es · -ies. Twelve verbs. Sort them. Why does STUDY go to -ies?
  2. First I take STUDY apart on the board as a model of the reasoning, then Kagan Numbered Heads Together: everyone in the crew has a number 1–4, I ask a question («why does TEACH go to -es?»), the crew confers — heads together — and the number I call out answers. Which means everyone prepares to explain, not just the fastest.
  3. The rule chorally: -s usually · -es after s/sh/ch/x/o · -ies after a consonant + y · has!
  4. 6 forms in writing → the key on the slide → I check my own: circle your slips — nobody sees.
📺 On screen: verb cards · baskets · answer key
👦 Students do: they sort with their hands before writing. Expect: everybody gets studies on the second go

Negatives · don't / doesn't
pairs

WHAT WE DO: model → transformations from a frame → the game «a lie about me» through RallyRobin

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model: I don't get up at five. Jack doesn't go to school. Who takes don't, who takes doesn't?
  2. Transformation frame: (+) → (−) for 6 sentences, key on the slide.
  3. Game: Tell your partner a LIE about your day — partner fixes it: You don't…!
  4. I play a couple of turns with a strong student in front of everybody — that is the model — then Kagan RallyRobin: A tells a lie, B fixes it with a negative, then they swap; three turns each, with the frame in front of their eyes.

WHAT WE DO: an interview in pairs using prompt questions (Timed Pair Share) → 6 lines about the partner's day in the 3rd person → self-check → 2–3 read aloud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Prompt questions: What time do you get up? Do you have breakfast?… (on the slide).
  2. First I interview one student in front of everybody — a model of the pace and of what the notes look like. Then Kagan Timed Pair Share: two minutes for A to ask and write, the bell, two minutes for B. Equal time, which means both collect enough raw material for six lines.
  3. We write the 6 lines: Dasha gets up at seven. She doesn't have breakfast…
  4. Self-check against a checklist (3 points: -s? doesn't + bare verb? spelling?) → volunteers read to the class.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use present simple affirmative and negative with correct 3rd-person spelling, checked by themselves against keys.
  • Subsidiary. Guided discovery from the P2 text; sorting -s/-es/-ies; ex.5 personalisation; a mini-test with self-check (not a mark!).
  • Personal. The pace is half that of «ours» — and that is normal. Every stage ends with a self-check against the key: the error is found privately, before anyone else sees it.
By the end, students can…
  • complete the present simple table
  • spell 3rd person forms: -s, -es, -ies
  • make negative sentences with don't/doesn't
  • write 6 correct sentences about a friend's day

Structure
I/You/We/They get up at eight. · He/She/It gets up at seven. · I/You/We/They don't go to school. · He/She/It doesn't see her teachers.
Form
most verbs: + -s (read → reads, wake up → wakes up) · after -s, -sh, -ss, -ch, -x, -o: + -es (go → goes, teach → teaches, mix → mixes) · consonant + y: y → -ies (study → studies) · exception: have → has
Pron. watch
goes /ɡəʊz/ · studies /ˈstʌdiz/ · watches /ˈwɒtʃɪz/ — voiced, not «-es» as in Russian
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'He don't like maths' — what's wrong? → after he/she/it it is always doesn't.
'She studys' — is that right? → no: consonant + y → studies.
After doesn't, does the verb take -s? → no: doesn't see, not doesn't sees.

Assumptions: SB pp.14–15, KTP pair 7. For some of them the 3rd person is genuinely new; I build it on examples from the text they have already read (a familiar context).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They have heard the rule but do not apply itI do not explain — they complete the table from the examples and word the rule themselves; I only confirm it.
Fear of making written mistakesEvery written run comes with a self-check against the key, before anyone else sees it.
-es/-ies spelling gets mixed upHands-on sorting (cards into baskets) before any writing; a cheat-sheet poster on the wall until the end of the unit.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
