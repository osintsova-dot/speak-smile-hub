Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to form and use the present simple (affirmative & negative) — including 3rd-person spelling (+s / +es / -ies, have → has) and don't / doesn't + base verb — to talk about their own and other people's daily routines and school life.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle daily-routines lexis (P1) and present simple met in context (P2 reading); read a short text for the target form (Hogwarts) and check by listening; write controlled & personalised sentences; peer-check accuracy.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; run a clean I-Do → We-Do → You-Do release with sentence frames; use thumbs-checks to see the whole class, not just the fast hands.
By the end, learners can…
  • add the 3rd-person ending correctly (gets · goes · studies · has);
  • spell 3rd person by the four routes (+s · +es · -ies · irregular);
  • make present-simple negatives with don't / doesn't + base verb;
  • write true sentences about themselves and a family member.
Target language
Lexis
daily routines in the 3rd person: gets upbrusheswatchesgoesstudiesfinisheshasdoesmakesplays
Structure
I/you/we/they + base verb  ·  he/she/it + verb-s  ·  don't / doesn't + base verb
Form
Spelling routes: +s · +es after -s -sh -ss -ch -x -o · consonant + y → ies · have → has. Negative: doesn't already carries the -s → base verb next.
Pron. watch
3rd-person -s endings: /s/ works · /z/ plays, goes · /ɪz/ watches, finishes. doesn't /ˈdʌznt/.
Meaning
routines / facts / habits — every day, usually — not now, at the moment.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He gets up at seven — one person or many?” → one → verb + s.
“She doesn't see her teachers — do we say doesn't sees?” → No — doesn't has the -s already → base verb.
“study → he …?” → studies (consonant + y → ies). “Is this now or every day?” → every day.

Assumptions: daily-routines lexis is known from P1; present simple was met in context in the P2 reading — but this is the first explicit grammar rule of the year, so build it slowly and model everything. Learners are readers → work from printed examples and the notebook card. Groups: pairs for practice; teams of 3–4 for the sorting relay (10 = 3+3+4 · 12 = 3×4 · 9 = 3×3).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Drops the 3rd-person -s (He get up)Colour-code the -s on the card; choral drill with a “+s” gesture; thumbs-check.
Double marking (He doesn't gets)Rule on the card: doesn't already carries the -s → base verb. Cross out the red example.
Spelling studys / gos / watchsThe four spelling routes + the sorting relay; sort before you write.
have forgotten (he haves)Flag have → has as the ⭐ irregular; drill it separately.
Confuses with “now” / L1 transferCCQ “every day or now?”; anchor on time markers every day, usually, on Mondays.
Classroom management — signals (teens)
“3–2–1, eyes here” — countdown to refocus the whole class.
Thumbs check — up / sideways / down = how sure you are (formative, whole-class).
“Pens down, look up” — clean transition into whole-class feedback.
“Pair-check first” — compare answers with your partner before we go public.
Timer on board — visible countdown keeps tasks tight.
“Freeze & swap” — end the pair task, change roles / partner.

0–7′
Lead-in · retrieval
desks → standing

Retrieve P1. Welcome back! Let's warm up your day. ▶ Stand up if it's true for you — read routine sentences in the 1st person: I get up before seven. I have breakfast every day. I don't do homework on Saturdays. Students stand / stay seated — everyone reacts, every line.

RallyRobin. In pairs, take turns: Tell your partner four things you do every morning. Overhear and keep 2–3 lines on the board (I get up… I don't have…) — these become today's examples.

ICQ: Are we talking about right now, or every day? (→ every day).

 board · routine prompt list · timer
7–19′
Discovery · completing the table
desks

I Do — notice the pattern. Put the SB p.16 ex.1 examples on the board: I get up at eight. They don't go to school. Jack wakes up at seven. Abi doesn't see her teachers. I don't sit at the computer all day. Think aloud, underlining verbs: I / you / we / they → base verb. He / she / it → verb + s. Negative → don't / doesn't + base verb.

We Do — build the table. Open SB p.16, ex.1: copy and complete the affirmative / negative table together (get up · wakes up · doesn't see · don't sit · don't go). Elicit each cell; students say why.

CCQ. “He gets up” — one or many? Do we add -s to “see” after “doesn't”?

19–29′
Spelling lab
desks → teams, active

The four routes (SB Spelling rules, p.21). Model one of each on the board: +s: read → reads. +es after -s -sh -ss -ch -x -o: go → goes, watch → watches, mix → mixes. Consonant + y → ies: study → studies. Irregular: have → has.

You Do — SB p.16 ex.3. Write the 3rd person of finish · get up · go · have · make · play · study · watch → finishes · gets up · goes · has · makes · plays · studies · watches. Pair-check first.

▶ Sorting relay (teams of 3–4): four column headers on the wall — +s · +es · -ies · irregular. Each team gets a stack of verb cards; runners place each verb under the right route, fastest correct wall wins. Everyone holds cards — nobody watches.

Pron. watch. ▶ Take Off–Touch Down: say verbs — /s/ ending stand (works), /z/ ending touch down (plays, goes), /ɪz/ ending both hands up (watches, finishes). Fast, 8–10 verbs.

29–40′
Controlled practice
pairs, desks

We Do → You Do. SB p.16 ex.2 (choose the correct option, 6 items) and ex.4 (complete the sentences with the correct verb, affirmative / negative, from the photos, 6 items).

▶ RallyCoach (Kagan, pairs): A says the answer aloud and the reason (“doesn't watch — he, one person, negative”); B coaches and praises, then they swap for the next item. Same for ex.4.

Feedback. Pens down, look up. Nominate; whole class shows the answer on fingers (a / b). Thumbs-check confidence before moving on.

standing

Everyone up — 90 seconds. Quick non-language stir (stretch / “last one standing” follow-the-leader / cross-body reach). Reset focus, then 3–2–1, eyes here. Halfway point of a 90-minute lesson — this keeps the second half sharp.

44–56′
Negative focus · flip drill
standing, pairs

I Do — the flip. I don't go → He doesn't go. Show the -s “jumping” onto doesn't, so the main verb goes base. Cross out the trap: He doesn't gets ✗.

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (everyone moving): each learner holds a card with an affirmative sentence (She watches TV. · My brother studies French.). Pair up → read your card → partner flips it to the negative (She doesn't watch TV.) → praise → trade cards, find a new partner. Keep going for the full time — every learner speaks every round.

Support (new-starters). Frame on the board: [He / She] doesn't [base verb] … Weaker pairs keep the notebook card open.

Grammar in context · Hogwarts
desks

67–79′
Personalisation · freer
desks → pair share

You Do — make it true. SB p.16 ex.5: change the underlined words so the six sentences are true for you (I go to a … / My school day starts at … / My parents don't do my homework.).

Write 3 (sentence frames). On the notebook page write three true sentences: one about you, one about a family member (he/she + verb-s), one negative. Frames for support: [My mum / My brother] ______s every day. · [He / She] doesn't ______ … · I don't ______ …

▶ Timed Pair Share (40 sec each): read your three sentences; the listener hunts for the -s and the negative and gives one tick + one fix. Freeze & swap.

whole class

Error clinic. Four wrong sentences on the board: He get up early. · She don't like maths. · My dad studys English. · He doesn't goes to bed late. Class corrects each and names the rule (+s · don't/doesn't · consonant+y→ies · base verb after doesn't). Everyone writes the fixed version.

Fast-finisher challenge. SB p.16 ex.7 Brain teaser (Tom / Ana / Sara logic grid) — for pairs who finished early while others repair errors.
