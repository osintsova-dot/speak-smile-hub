🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Read for a purpose: match a statement to the person who means it, not to the person who repeats the words. Five short forum posts about five extremely odd clubs, five statements that paraphrase them; then a second, timed text in the exam's own multiple-choice shape. The lesson also lifts six chunks out of context — takes my mind off, have a bite to eat, reckon, current affairs, role-play, speeches — because guessing meaning from the sentence around a word is itself an exam skill.
What is genuinely hard
The matching task punishes exactly the strategy that feels safe: hunting the text for the word in the statement. Statement 2 says «doesn't care much about winning matches» and the right text says «we don't take them too seriously» — not a single shared content word. A student who scans for «winning» or «matches» lands on the wrong club every time. The reading has to be done for meaning, and every answer has to be proved with a line.
Typical error to expect
Choosing the text with the most words in common with the statement (word-matching, not idea-matching); reading the whole forum slowly on the first pass instead of skimming for the club names; giving an opinion with no reason — «I like the robot club» and stopping — when the exam speaking and writing both demand the reason and the example.
Why the lesson is built this way
Prediction first (the photos, then the club names) so the text is met with a question already in the head. One skim for gist — name the clubs — before any detail. Then the matching, taught by killing the wrong strategy publicly on item 1, so every later answer carries its proof line. Vocabulary from context comes after the reading, not before, because the point is to infer, not to be told. The lesson turns to opinion — which club is worth your time — because that is where the reading becomes speech. The workbook text is placed last and run against the clock: same skill, exam conditions, in the room where you can debrief it.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“One of these clubs plays hockey at the bottom of a swimming pool. One drinks cocoa and reads children's stories. One gives you 36 hours to get as far from school as you can with no money. Today you match five statements to five clubs — and here is the trap: the right answer never uses the words of the statement. Find the club that means it, and prove it with a line.”

WHAT WE DO: SB p.7 ex.1–2: describe the photos, then skim once to name five very odd clubs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1, 90 seconds. Look at the photos on the page. What can you see? What do you think these people do for fun? Two or three answers aloud — build the topic: extra-curricular activities, clubs, societies.

🎯 Reading for detail · the matching task
desks
Why: This is the exam skill in its pure form. Killing the word-hunt publicly on item 1, then demanding a proof line for every answer, is what makes the strategy stick — and it makes the checking self-evidencing rather than teacher-owned.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.7 ex.3: match five statements to five texts — and prove every answer with a line

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model item 1 out loud (think-aloud). Read statement 1 — «thinks the name of their club clearly reflects what people in the club do». Watch me. If I hunt for the word «name», I find nothing useful. So I read for the IDEA — a club whose name tells you what it is. The Model United Nations club: the name says exactly what they do — they play the United Nations. Underline the proof line.
  2. Set items 2–5, alone, four minutes. For every one: the answer, AND the line that proves it. Underline the line. If you cannot underline a line, it is not your answer.
  3. RallyCoach in pairs: one gives the answer and reads the proof line, the partner agrees or challenges with a different line. Three minutes.
  4. Open class check via Numbered Heads Together: a number is called for each item, that student gives the answer and the proof line — reporting is not volunteer-driven. Nail the paraphrases on the board: winning matches → «don't take them too seriously»; change your personality → «makes us feel kinder and more relaxed»; duration is important → «just 36 hours»; learn about subjects you enjoy → «I learn as much from this club as from my science lectures».
📺 On screen: statement 1 and the proof line highlighted, as the model. Backup: read the model aloud from the book and underline on your own copy held up.
👦 Students do: match five statements alone with a proof line each, coach a partner, then answer when their number is called

Vocabulary from context · guess before the dictionary
desks
Why: Inferring meaning from the sentence around a word is an exam skill in itself, and it only works if the guess comes first. Placed after the reading, the chunks are already met in context; the dictionary confirms, it does not teach.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.7 ex.5: six underlined chunks worked out from the sentence around them, then checked

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model one (think-aloud). «takes my mind off the assignments» — she plays the sport to stop thinking about her assignments. So «takes my mind off» must mean «stops me worrying about». I worked that out from the sentence, not from a dictionary.
  2. Students, in pairs, guess the other five from context: have a bite to eat · reckon · current affairs · role-play · speeches. Write the guess in English, not in Russian.
  3. Only then: Now check in your dictionary. Were you close? Confirm as whole chunks, and add the two best to the word wall as phrases, never as single words.
  4. Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the six chunks on cards, four minutes: everybody standing, everybody quizzing, the chunk produced in a sentence each time.
📺 On screen: the six chunks listed, meanings added beside them as they are confirmed. Backup: board the six and let pairs use their phones for the dictionary check.
👦 Students do: guess six chunks from context, check in the dictionary, then quiz partners standing up

WHAT WE DO: SB p.7 ex.4 + ex.6 Critical thinkers: opinion with a reason and an example

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4 — Timed Pair Share, 45 seconds each: Which of these activities would you like to do, and why? Answer, reason, example — never stop at «I like it». The listener says nothing, then reports the partner's choice to a new pair.
  2. Feed the frame from the speaking language of the unit: The club I'd join is …, because …, for example … Put it on the board.
  3. Ex.6 Critical thinkers — raise the stakes: In your opinion, how BENEFICIAL could each club be for you? Not «fun» — beneficial. Use ideas from the text. Think-Pair-Share: 60 seconds thinking with the text, then pairs, then two answers taken with a follow-up move each — What makes you say that? · Would that be true for everyone?
  4. Push one thin answer into a full one, publicly and kindly, so the class hears what «beneficial, because, for example» sounds like.
📺 On screen: the answer frame (choice → reason → example) on the board. Backup: none — this stage is voices and the frame.
👦 Students do: give a timed opinion with a reason and an example, report a partner's, then argue how beneficial each club is

WHAT WE DO: WB p.5 ex.1–2: predict from the title, then a timed multiple-choice read of «The end-of-school dilemma»

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 — predict, 2 minutes. Read only the title: «The end-of-school dilemma». What do you think the article is about? Who is it for? Two guesses aloud — this is the Workbook's own «Predicting content» tip, and it is a marked exam habit.
  2. Ex.2 — timed exam read, 10 minutes, silent, alone. Six multiple-choice questions. Ten minutes. No talking, no dictionary. Same rule as this morning: for every answer, the line in the text that proves it — underline it. Timer up; you monitor, you do not help.
  3. Peer-mark, 3 minutes. Swap books; you read the key aloud (see Answer keys card). Each pair discusses only the ones they disagree on — using the proof line, not «I just think so».
  4. Hands up: who got 6? 5? fewer? That number tells them how ready the reading section is — and the two follow-up exercises go home tonight.
📺 On screen: the timer only — this is exam conditions, screen off otherwise. Backup: the WB reading has an audio (track 01) — playing it quietly under a second read helps the weaker half, but is optional and never during the timed pass.

Feedback · the strategy, not just the score
desks
Why: A reading lesson is only learnt if students leave knowing HOW they found the answer. Debriefing the method — the proof line, the killed word-hunt — is what transfers to the next text.

WHAT WE DO: how each right answer was found — the line that proves it, not the word that matches it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Take the one WB question most pairs disagreed on. Ask the student who got it right: Not what — HOW. Which line told you? The method is the lesson, not the letter.
  2. Name the trap where it bit: any question whose wrong option repeats a word from the text. The exam does this on purpose. The repeated word is the bait.
  3. Thirty seconds back to the six-word rule on the board: Still true? The answer never repeats the question. That is your reading section, all year.
📺 On screen: the six-word rule, back up. Backup: none.
👦 Students do: explain how they found one answer, name where the bait was

WHAT WE DO: a short retrieval on the paraphrase skill, no re-reading

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read four statements about the five clubs, books closed. Two are true paraphrases, two are word-matches that are actually false. True or false — and if you can, which club.
  2. Answers on the board. The two false ones are the teaching point: they share words with a text but not meaning.
  3. Anything wrong goes straight into the error log as a full sentence, not a single word.

Workbook p.5

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
ICQ before ex.2: are you reading every word? → no. What are you finding? → just the five club names.
Statement 2 says «winning matches». The right text says «we don't take them too seriously». Do they share a word? → no. Do they share an idea? → yes. So which wins? → the idea.
«takes my mind off my assignments» — is she thinking MORE about her assignments or LESS? → less. So the phrase means…? → stops me worrying about.
«beneficial» — does it mean «fun»? → no, it means «good / useful for you». Can something be beneficial but not fun? → yes.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we read; timer stops = pens down. In the exam read, the timer is the boss, not you.
“Prove it” — your standing response to any reading answer: the student reads the line that proves it, or the answer does not count.
Silent mode — palm down = we read alone and in silence. This is the default for the timed WB text.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset after a stirrer.
English-first — a guess may start in Russian, but the point is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
