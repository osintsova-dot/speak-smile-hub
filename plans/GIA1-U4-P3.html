  1. Entry: Fist bump — one free-time word each!

👦 Students do: recall the P2 facts in the third person round the chain. Expected: in speech the -s gets lost for half the class — the ideal entry. Typical error: “he practise” — I note it silently on the board in the “error morgue”.

WB Unit 4 (pp.28–35) · notebook · points to the leaderboard next pair
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write present simple aff/neg with clean 3rd-person spelling (watches · studies · has · doesn't have) and complete sentences while listening (subskill) to the app conversation.
  • Subsidiary. Grammar = exactly 45′: gate → rules race → spot-the-mistake (Common errors TB) → saxophone relay → Brain teaser; pron -es /ɪz/. Lesson B: 16 free-time activities + app listening + the product “My real week” with a he/she-retelling (the -s again!).
  • Personal. Anti-whining: “you've been speaking present simple since GMF2 — but can you write watches with no mistake in 5 seconds?” Written accuracy = the mission. Spelling is their real ceiling.
By the end, students can…
  • write 3rd-person forms fast and clean (-es/-ies/has)
  • flip aff↔neg without ‘he don't’
  • name 16 free-time activities from icons
  • complete sentences while listening; retell a partner's week in he/she

Structure
He watches TV after school. · She doesn't live in a city. · My friends and I don't relax at the weekend. · On Thursdays, Flora goes swimming.
Form
he/she/it + -s; -es after -s/-o/-ch/-sh/-x; consonant+y → -ies; have→has; the negative don't/doesn't + infinitive WITHOUT -s.
Pron. watch
-es = /ɪz/: finishes · relaxes · watches (SB p.116 ex.1) · do you = /dʒʊ/ later in P4.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He watchs — OK?” → No: watch → watchES (-ch!).
“She doesn't likES?” → No — after doesn't, the infinitive with no -s.
“Studies or studys?” → consonant + y → -ies.

Assumptions: Oral PS is fluent (GMF2–4), written third-person spelling is the real gap; free-time lexis is partly familiar, but the 16-set as a system is new. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (time-in-words / -s spelling / question word order), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“Present simple? Again?!”A 3-minute gate closes the conversation: watches/studies/has in writing at speed — the score speaks for itself.
‘He don't’ from fluent speechA round of Spot the mistake from Common errors TB — the errors become targets, not shame.
-es/-ies get mixed up under the timerThree rules = three gestures (sibilants — the “snake”, y→ies — the “scissors”, has — the “crown”); a gesture instead of a prompt.
Listening: they write the wrong wordSubskill: first work out the TYPE of word in the gap (activity? day? noun?), then listen to the first part of the sentence.
The product with no -s in the retellingJudges listen for ONLY the -s: every clean he/she verb = a thumbs up; the system counts itself.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
