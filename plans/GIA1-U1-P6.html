Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can produce a short spoken & written description of an invented country (a poster + a 60-sec presentation) using the whole Unit 1 system — be in all forms, countries & nationalities, and adjectives to describe places.
  • Subsidiary. Consolidate the unit before the test (fluent retrieval of the target language); tighten written accuracy of be short answers and capital letters; practise collaboration key phrases and peer review against clear criteria.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; give an ICQ + a timer before every group stage; make the audience active during presentations (no passive listeners); run a create → present → review rhythm.
By the end, pupils can…
  • design and present a poster about a country;
  • use be (aff./neg./questions/short answers) accurately;
  • describe a place with the unit adjectives (a modern city / The city is modern);
  • use collaboration key phrases and give criteria-based peer feedback.

Structure
Where are you from? — I'm from Spain. I'm Spanish.  ·  It's a modern city. / The city is modern.
Form · be
Aff. I'm · he/she/it's · we/you/they're · Neg. 'm not · isn't · aren't · Q. Am/Is/Are + subject…? · Short ans. Yes, I am (full) / No, I'm not.
Pron. watch
weak are you /ə jə/; nationality stress — JapaNESE, BraZILian, RUSSian; capital letters on countries/nationalities in writing.
Project phrases
Creativity (Super skills): What's a good name/flag/national sport? · I like your/Sara's idea. · What about…? · That's a good idea! · No, that's not very original.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Yes, I ___. — can I say Yes, I'm.?” → No. Short answer = full form: Yes, I am.
“a beautifuls city — is that right?” → No. Adjectives are never plural.
“Turkey → Turkey-ish?” → No, Turkish — check the suffix route on the passport.

Assumptions: countries, nationalities, place adjectives and all forms of be were met across Pairs 1–5 — this pair recycles & produces, it does not present new language. Pupils read & write; groups-of-three routine and roles are known. Groups: threes for the project; if numbers don't divide, one four with an extra “editor” role (14 = 4×3 + 1 pair; adjust to 3–4 per group).

STEP 1 — THINK. Play the project video: students present “Calobia”. Where is Calobia? Which sections are on their poster?

Key phrases + roles

Super skills — Creativity. Drill the collaboration key phrases (chorus, then in pairs): What's a good name/flag/national sport? · I like your idea. · What about […]? · That's a good idea! · No, that's not very original.

Form groups of three; assign roles: Student 1 — flag & fun facts · Student 2 — map & important cities · Student 3 — language & currency. Sentence frames go on the board: [Name] is a […] country. · The capital is […]. · People are […]. · It's got […].

26–45′
PLAN & CREATE the country
groups of 3, desks

STEP 2 — PLAN (ex 4–7). In your group, choose FIVE sections for your poster. Use the Model and the Key phrases. Discuss & note each section (ex 6 model: FLAG: red – white – green (red = sun…)). Then choose one person to prepare each section. ICQ: How many sections? (5) In English or your language? (English!) Timer: 5 min.

STEP 3 — CREATE (ex 8–9). Prepare texts & visuals and build the poster. Compulsory language: be + at least three place adjectives. Circulate, feed language, hold pupils to the board frames. Timer: 12 min. Countdown: Finishing in 5… 4… 3… 2… 1… pens down.

45–48′
🧘 Brain break + set-up
stand up

Pens down — stand up, shake it out. 90-sec stretch / reset. Then pin the posters up as a gallery and decide who presents which section. In a moment your group meets another group: you present, they judge — then you swap.

48–63′
PRESENT · peer review
gallery, rotation

STEP 4 — PRESENT (ex 10). Group A presents to Group B; then swap; then rotate once more. Presenters use the board frames; every listener holds the ▶ judging card — Layout · Content · Language · Visuals (1–3 each) — and must ask the two set questions:

How is your new country different from your real country? · Do you want to visit this country? Why / Why not?

Rotate so every pupil presents at least one section and judges at least one poster. Reset the walk with Freeze! if it drifts.

67–80′
Revision quiz · Country Cup
teams

The last big points before the test. ▶ Numbered Heads Together — teams confer, a numbered player answers on a mini-whiteboard (▶ Showdown: reveal together). Points on the board.

Rounds (from Quick review SB p.21): ① country ↔ nationality · ② be gap-fill (aff./neg.) · ③ make a question + short answer (Are you from Japan? — No, I'm not.) · ④ catch the mistake — Yes, I'm. ✗ → Yes, I am. · ⑤ describe a place with two adjectives.

Fast, points-driven, team applause for the winners — this is the one competitive review flagship of the unit.
