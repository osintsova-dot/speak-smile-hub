👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: One word: your favourite café food?

10–20′
Input: there is/are (tables + gestures)
Discover · there is/are tables
board → desks

WHAT WE DO: Quiz-Quiz-Trade: ex.1 tables with gestures (is-finger/are-palm); TB traps as “mines”; ex.2 blitz

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. We fill in the ex.1 tables TOGETHER with gestures: is — one finger (one thing), are — a palm (many). Key: There's·There are·There aren't·Is there·there is·Are there.
  2. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the unit's printed cards (handout GIA1-QQT: the question on top, the answer on the back after folding; no printout — the kids write their own cards in 2 min).
  3. Command: Stand up, hand up, pair up! Quiz your partner — praise — TRADE cards — find a new partner! 3–4 swaps, EVERYBODY asks at the same time.
  4. Three TB mines on the poster: ① There're ✗ (are doesn't hide!) ② Yes, there's ✗ (a short answer — is full!) ③ a question = inversion (Is there…?).
  5. Anchor gesture from have got: I'VE GOT a pen (hand on chest). THERE'S a pen on the desk (hand towards the desk).
  6. Ex.2 blitz in chorus with gestures: 5 sentences is/are (key: are·is·is·are·are).
📺 On screen: the SB p.42 · the “3 mines” poster (stays up till the test) · gestures
👦 Students do: they fill in the tables with gestures, learn the 3 mines, flip the blitz. Expected: the gestures hold is/are. Typical error: mine ① — the poster is already up, I point at it silently.

WHAT WE DO: a drill game: a “+/−/?” card flips the sentence; mini-boards, 6 rounds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules: I say a sentence + show a card: − = make it negative, ? = a question, + = back to normal.
  2. There's a cinema… (a − card) … Boards up! (There isn't a cinema.) 8 sec.
  3. 6 rounds with rotation (2 singular · 2 plural with any · 2 questions + a short answer in chorus: Yes, there is!).
  4. The team with the most clean boards +3.
📺 On screen: +/−/? cards · mini-boards · timer
👦 Students do: they flip forms on their boards at the same time — everybody's engaged. Expected: by round 4 the speed doubles. Typical error: aren't without any — echo: “aren't ANY shops”.

WHAT WE DO: 8 Key phrases: who's speaking (C or W?), an intonation drill, a dialogue skeleton on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Key phrases one at a time: I read — the class repeats with intonation; we sort them — customer or server? (TB Extra; key: C: Can I have · I'd like · That's all · How much; W: Would you like any/anything · Anything else? · That's…, please.)
  2. US↔UK from the book: fries (US) = chips (UK).
  3. I build a dialogue skeleton on the board (5 lines with arrows): Hi! Can I have ___, please? → Would you like any ___? → Yes, please / No, thanks. → Anything else? → That's all, thanks. How much is that? → That's $___.
  4. A run-through of the skeleton in chorus ×2 with gestures (a tray, money).

👦 Students do: they sort the phrases by role, drill with intonation, run through the skeleton in chorus. Expected: the skeleton is almost learned by chorus already. Typical error: “I like a burger” instead of I'd like — the card I'd = I would.

WHAT WE DO: a step-by-step role-play: THINK the menu → PREPARE from the skeleton → PRACTISE in a whisper → PERFORM the scenes; role rotation

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK (2 min): pairs choose from the menu p.43: main + drink (+ dessert if they're brave). Budget: $10.
  2. PREPARE (4 min): a dialogue from the board skeleton, key words onto a card (not the text!). TB Reinforcement: I check a/an/some in the orders (a burger · some fries).
  3. PRACTISE (5 min): in a whisper (“radio whisper”), swapping roles — everybody tries being the waiter.
  4. PERFORM (7 min): 3–4 pairs at the “counter” (a desk + menu); the class = peer review on the book's 2 questions: ① What's the order? ② Are the Key phrases there? +2 for the scene, +1 to listeners for a correct ex.6 answer.

👦 Students do: they go through the 4 Skills boost steps with full scaffolding, perform prepared. Expected: even the quiet ones go up — the skeleton holds them. Typical error: reading straight from the card — the card = 4 words, the rest is the skeleton.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can describe places with there is/there are (aff/neg/questions) and order food politely with 4+ Key phrases in a scaffolded café role-play.
  • Subsidiary. Full input of there is/are (tables + gestures + TB Common errors as “mines”); practice on Lee Centre and our classroom; a round-up on Hong Kong cafés with support; a video model (Connor, $9.25); Key phrases step by step; the “café” role-play with a scaffold, in pairs.
  • Personal. The unit's climax for beginners — the café role-play. Full scaffolding: a dialogue skeleton on the board, whispered rehearsal, only then the scenes. Prices are simple, no calculator needed.
By the end, students can…
  • say/write there is/are in +, −, ?
  • ask Is there…? / Are there any…? with inversion
  • order food with Can I have… please / I'd like…

Structure
There's a cinema. · There are some cafés. · There isn't a bookshop. · Is there a baker's? — Yes, there is. · Can I have a burger, please? · How much is that?
Form
singular → there is/isn't · plural → there are/aren't (+ any); question = inversion Is/Are there; a short answer WITHOUT a contraction: Yes, there is (not Yes, there's!).
Pron. watch
rising question intonation: Is there…?↗ (TB) · Can I have…?↗ politely · prices: two seventy.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“There is two cafés?” → plural → There ARE two cafés.
“Yes, there's — is that OK?” → No: the full form in a short answer — Yes, there is.
“An order without please?” → The waiter “doesn't hear it”: politeness first.

Assumptions: have got (U2) and some/any (P3) — today's supports; there is/are — completely new as a system. The ordering Key phrases are new; prices = the numbers from Starter. Scaffolded role-plays have already happened (U1 meeting people) — the format is familiar.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
there is/are vs have got get mixed upAnchor: have got — SOMEBODY has it; there is — it's SOMEWHERE. The gesture “hand on chest” vs “hand towards the room”.
There're — they write it as they hear itTB trap poster: There're ✗ — “are doesn't hide!”; a hunt for traps as a game.
Scenes scare the quiet onesFirst a choral drill, then whispered in pairs, then volunteers; nobody goes up without rehearsing.
Prices slow the scene downA TB price warm-up before the scenes; round/simple prices on the menu.
No videoI play Connor's dialogue myself from the ex.4 text (it's in the book in full) — 2 voice positions.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
