Sprint: 4 -ed rules through gestures
⚡ Base sprint · 4 spelling rules = 4 gestures
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.1 the +-ed rule → 4 spelling rules (SB p.93) with gestures → ex.2 a race on 7 verbs → ex.3 gaps as a volley

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 in chorus (key TB p.103: end/ask/stay/watch/play; we add -ed; one form for every person).
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at once, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, no hiding.
  3. Four rules = four gestures: 1 +ed (the tail) · 2 -e → +d (the scissors) · 3 consonant+y → -ied (we twist the y) · 4 vowel+consonant → double it (the double fist). I show it → the class repeats the gestures.
  4. Ex.2 race, 2 min: chat · decide · plan · play · study · walk · want. Key TB p.103: chatted · decided · planned · played · studied · walked · wanted.
  5. The trap out loud: Why PLAYED, not ‘plaied’? — a VOWEL before the y.
  6. Ex.3 as a volley on mini-boards (key TB p.103: decided · planned · studied · chatted · walked · wanted · played).
  7. A clapping drill for /ɪd/: wanted-decided-chatted — clap the extra syllable; walked-played-asked — no clap (TB p.103 ABCD).
📺 On screen: SB p.90 + rules SB p.93 · board: 4 gestures · mini-boards
👦 Students do: act out the gestures, race through the forms, clap the extra syllable. Expected: the gestures lock the rules in for good. Typical error: “plaied” — the “vowel before y” gesture settles it.

Intonation drill + limiters
Drill · “Really?” and two limiters
pairs · standing

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share: pronunciation drill (weak was, Really? with two intonations) → practising the sceptic role → PERFORM rules

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Intonation drill (TB p.104): Really? ↗ — surprise; Really…? ↘ — disbelief. The class splits the room: half surprised, half sceptical; then they switch.
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A talks against the timer, B is an active listener (counts pauses on their fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. A drill of the weak form: How was (/wəz/) your weekend? — It WAS (/wɒz/) awesome. Chorus ×3.
  4. The sceptic role: every listener has a RED card. Red card for ANY present tense: ‘I go’, ‘we play’. The speaker must redo the sentence.
  5. The second limiter: Five seconds max between sentences. Silence over five — the detector wins.
  6. A trial run, 60 sec in pairs — just to feel the card.
📺 On screen: red cards for everyone · board: two PERFORM rules · stopwatch
👦 Students do: split the room into two intonations, practise the red card. Expected: the card immediately sharpens attention to the past. Typical error: they raise the card at everything — rule: only the present.

stage · whole class

WHAT WE DO: the finale: pairs act out the dialogue, the whole class votes with FAKE/REAL cards after each one; judges use the rubric; points for fooling AND for detecting

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I give everyone two cards: FAKE / REAL.
  2. Format: first ALL pairs perform at the same time in front of another pair (a carousel, 2×90 sec — everyone is engaged), voting for each other.

Structure
I watched TV and played video games. · We went to a party. · I had a great time! · How was your weekend? — It was awesome, thanks. · What about on Sunday?
Form
past simple is the same for every person · 4 rules for -ed (SB p.93) · irregulars — memory only (SB pp.126–127) · US→UK: movies → cinema, What about Sunday? → What about ON Sunday?
Pron. watch
-ed = an extra syllable /ɪd/ only after t/d: wanted · decided · chatted; elsewhere — just /t/ or /d/: walked · played · asked (TB p.103 ABCD) · Really? with two intonations: surprise ↗ and disbelief ↘ (TB p.104).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I goed — OK?” → No: go → WENT. Irregular.
“Studyed or studied?” → consonant + y → -ied: studied. And played? → vowel before y → just +ed.
“Stoped or stopped?” → one vowel + one consonant → double it: stopped.

Assumptions: Past simple as an idea is familiar from GMF4, regular -ed is recognised. Real gaps: irregular forms under the timer, spelling rules 3 and 4, and holding the past in connected speech (they slide into the present). Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (irregular forms / -ed spelling / was–were agreement), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
