Introducing: the five comparative rules, one at a time
Discover · five rules, one at a time
board → desks

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–3 p.66 step by step: the two/than rule → forms → rules a–e with cards; the traffic-light rule on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 together (key: 1 two · 2 than): We compare TWO things, and we use THAN.
  2. Ex.2 slowly: from the comparative back to the adjective (better–good, heavier–heavy, bigger–big, more expensive–expensive).
  3. I introduce the rules ONE AT A TIME, each one a card on the board + a volley of examples in chorus:
  4.   1) one syllable → + -er: cheap → cheaper · light → lighter · fast → faster · new → newer · old → older · slow → slower  2) one vowel + one consonant (not -w/-y) → double the consonant + -er: big → bigger · hot → hotter · thin → thinner  3) two syllables ending in -y → -y drops, + -ier: heavy → heavier · easy → easier · ugly → uglier  4) two or more syllables → more + adjective: expensive → more expensive · difficult → more difficult · interesting → more interesting  5) irregular: good → better · bad → worse
  5. Ex.3 — we match rules a–e with the forms from ex.2 (key: a heavier · b smaller · c more expensive · d better · e bigger).
  6. Pronunciation (TB p.77): smalle(r) — no R sound! THAN is quick and quiet: /ðən/. Chorus ×3.
📺 On screen: SB p.66 ex.1–3 · five rule cards on the board (stay up the whole unit) · the model sentence
👦 Students do: draw out the rules one at a time, repeat the examples in chorus. Expected: rules 1 and 4 sink in straight away, 2 and 3 need the game. Typical error: «goodest/more good» — a separate “irregular” card.

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: ex.6–7 compactly + ▶ a chain game with real things from the class; grammar closes at the 45th minute

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Demonstration with an object (TB p.77): I take a student's pen: This is MY pen. That's YOUR pen. → This is MINE. That's YOURS.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. The ex.6 table in chorus (key: 2 you – yours · 3 he – his · 4 she – hers · 5 we – ours · 6 they – theirs (his = both an adjective and a pronoun; all except mine end in -s)); I point out: his is the same, and the others end in -s — except mine.
  4. Ex.7 on mini-boards by volley, 4 items. Key: 1 mine, hers · 2 theirs · 3 his · 4 yours.
  5. ▶ Chain sentences (Activities bank TB p.6): a circle, the object is a phone. My phone is old, but yours is new. — Your phone is new, but his is smaller… Name the noun a second time — you sit down. Streak on the board.
  6. Stop: Forty-five minutes. Grammar closed!

WHAT WE DO: performing for ANOTHER pair: the listeners physically carry out the steps; a 2-tick peer review

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. PERFORM (TB p.79): a pair shows their dialogue to another pair. The listeners “carry out” the steps with their hands or draw them on paper.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can compare two objects (The tablet is lighter than the laptop · This printer is more expensive) and give a 4-step instruction with First/Then/Next/Finally.
  • Subsidiary. The five comparative rules are introduced step by step with rule cards; possessive pronouns through a chain game; Grammar round-up as a team quiz; the instructions' Key phrases are drilled right through to PERFORM in pairs.
  • Personal. ⚠️ Grammar is exactly 45 minutes. Two new topics on one page — don't rush: comparatives take the lion's share, possessive pronouns we give compactly and play with.
By the end, students can…
  • make comparatives with -er, -ier, doubling and more
  • compare two objects using than
  • say mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs instead of repeating a noun
  • ask for and give instructions in four steps

Structure
The tablet is lighter than the laptop. · Their camera is better than ours. · How do you make a website? — First, go to… Then, click on…
Form
one syllable → + -er: cheap → cheaper · light → lighter · fast → faster · new → newer · old → older · slow → slower · one vowel + one consonant (not -w/-y) → double the consonant + -er: big → bigger · hot → hotter · thin → thinner · two syllables ending in -y → -y drops, + -ier: heavy → heavier · easy → easier · ugly → uglier · two or more syllables → more + adjective: expensive → more expensive · difficult → more difficult · interesting → more interesting · irregular: good → better · bad → worse · possessive pronouns: I → mine · you → yours · he → his · she → hers · we → ours · they → theirs (NO noun after them).
Pron. watch
than is unstressed, sounds /ðən/: The laptop's HEAVier than the TABlet (TB p.77 ABCD) · -er is read /ə/, the R is silent: smaller /ˈsmɔːlə/ (TB p.77).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“more cheaper — is that ok?” → no: a short word only takes -er.
“big → biger or bigger?” → bigger: we double the consonant.
“Is this your tablet? — Yes, it's your.” → yours — no noun after it.

Assumptions: The comparative is a NEW topic for the 🐣 line (it wasn't in U1–U4). The adjectives from p.65 were introduced in Pair 3. The imperative for instructions is familiar from the Starter (p.9).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Five rules at once is a lotWe give them one at a time, each with a rule card and a volley of examples; the tricky ones (doubling, -ier) get their own game to consolidate.
‘more cheaper’The traffic-light rule: short = -er (green) · long = more (red) · together NEVER.
than → thenIn yellow: thAn = comparison · thEn = after that; then will come up in the instructions — we show the contrast explicitly.
Possessive pronouns don't stickA chain game with real things from the class: name the noun a second time — you're out.
A 2-step instructionSupport strip: First / Then / Next / Finally — all four are required, the partner physically carries it out.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
