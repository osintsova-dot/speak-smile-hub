Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name 12 sea animals and describe the underwater world in context: “There are (turtles) in the sea. There's a very big (shark) — and it's got (sharp teeth)!”
  • Subsidiary. Sing «Under the sea» (CD2 37) & the vocabulary chant (CD2 36); recycle has got + big/small from earlier units, woven into the new sea theme; preview the comparative bigger than (planted for L2); early literacy — read the animal words & solve the AB crossword.
  • Personal. First lesson after the winter break — warm reunion, big energy, low teacher-talk. ICQ before every task; sea-creature mimes are stirrers — plan the freezes. Insist on the plural: fish → fish, shark → sharks.
By the end, pupils can…
  • name the 12 sea animals;
  • say “There's a … / There are …” about the underwater picture;
  • read the animal words and match word ↔ picture.

Structure
There are (turtles) in the sea.  ·  There's a very big (shark). It's got (sharp teeth / a hard shell).  ·  preview: A (whale) is (bigger) than a (fish).
Form
There's a + singular · There are + plural. Irregular plural: one fish → two fish! Describe with It's got … (has got, recycled).
Pron. watch
squid /skwɪd/; octopus /ˈɒktəpəs/ (3 syll.); seahorse — stress the first part (SEA-horse); jellyfish /ˈdʒelifɪʃ/ (soft j); turtle /ˈtɜːtl/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“fish — one fish, and two…?” → two fish! (same word).
“A shark is very big — is it bigger or smaller than a little fish?” → bigger! (plants L2).

Assumptions: Pupils are back from the winter break — routines need a quick reboot. There is / There are and has got are known from earlier units; the 12 sea animals are largely NEW. Readers use word cards alongside pictures.

Presentation · 12 sea animals
carpet → board

Present with flashcards + chant (act. 2, CD2 36). Each card: What's this? It's… a dolphin! Choral ×3 + one gesture (shark = fin on head, octopus = wiggle 8 arms, crab = pinchers). Contrast held together: WHALE — the biggest! CRAB — small!

There's a / There are (act. 4-style). Board frame over the big picture: There's a whale. There are two jellyfish. Point to the aquarium scene — class makes the sentence; watch fish → fish.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: Stand up if you can see this animal in the picture… a seahorse! — flash cards fast, everyone moves every time.

💪 Support: answer with the gesture + word. ⭐ Extension: full sentence — There's a very big shark. It's got sharp teeth!

28–36′
Word ↔ picture · Memory
carpet, pairs

▶ Memory · There's a … — in pairs. Each pair: 6 picture cards + 6 WORD cards (readers!) face-down in two rows. Turn one of each; a match is claimed only with a sentence: There's a seahorse! Wrong / no article → cards go back. Most pairs wins.

ICQ: Can you keep the cards if you forget the “a”? (→ no — There's A seahorse!)

✔ Assess: stand behind one pair at a time — is the animal word automatic yet?

 6+6 sea-animal cards per pair
36–44′
Sea-creature mime & guess
carpet, 2 teams

▶ Slow-motion sea mime. One player mimes a sea animal (shark fin, octopus arms, crab claws); their team guesses together: It's a shark! Mimer confirms. Point per correct guess; rotate the mimer every round — everyone mimes once.

Whole team must guess together — no solo shouting: One voice, one team!

Our aquarium · speak & write

Speak. ▶ RallyRobin: Take turns: There's a… / There are… — about the sea picture.

Write (notebooks / AB p.48 format). Board frames: There's a ___. There are ___. Each child writes 3 true sentences about the underwater picture, copying animal words (watch fish!). Fast finishers add It's got….

💪 Support: copy the frame + choose from 3 word cards. ⭐ Extension: add a description: There's a big whale. It's got a long body.
