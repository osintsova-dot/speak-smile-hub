🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Grammar is NEVER the announced topic — the audit is. It's a double pair by the KTP: hold the break exactly in the middle, the “mall” is a change of scenery, not a loss of pace. Collect live errors silently — the clinic comes at the rematch.

Gate: the 60-second lunchbox audit
🎯 GATE · 60-second lunchbox audit
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 60 seconds: the maximum of TRUE sentences about food at home with some/any/a lot of; only the clean ones count

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I say: Write TRUE sentences about food at YOUR home: I've got some… / We haven't got any… / There's a lot of… Sixty seconds — GO!
  2. At zero: Pens down! Rule card: some=+, any=−/?, does C/UC agree?
  3. We count ONLY the clean ones, score in the margin.
  4. The turn: Four clean? Two? You've SAID some/any for a year — writing it fast and clean is the licence. Rematch at the end of the lesson.
📺 On screen: 60-sec timer · rule card (some + · any −? · countable?) on the board
👦 Students do: solo, they write at speed, honestly filter the clean ones. Expected: 2–5 clean — the writing ceiling is exposed. Typical error: “I have some breads” — it doesn't count, and they can see it themselves.

12–21′
Sprint: sorting C/UC + the quantifier rules
⚡ Base sprint · sort + rules
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: TB warmer “odd one out” + sorting C/UC in a race + rules ex.3/ex.5 assembled for points

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB warmer: Odd one out: bananas–biscuits–meat–peas? bread–cheese–apples–rice? Why? (meat, apples — C/UC surfaces on its own).
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.
  3. Race ex.1: Ten words, two columns, countable and uncountable — three minutes, crews! Key: C banana·biscuit·orange·potato / UC bread·cheese·coffee·juice·soup·tea. Jake's lunchbox (ex.2): uncountable = juice, water.
  4. Rules ex.3 + ex.5 as a match on mini-boards: Some describes…? Any lives in…? (1b·2c·3d·4a). +1 for a correct board.
  5. Respect: Base cleared at double speed. Now — the crime scenes.
📺 On screen: the SB p.40 · board: the test “can you count it 1-2-3?” · mini-boards · keys
👦 Students do: they find the odd word out with a reason, sort at speed, assemble the rules by matching. Expected: money/rice questions — the “1-2-3” test settles it. Typical error: juice → countable (“there are 2 juices!”) — “a bottle of juice is countable, juice — isn't” (TB note).

WHAT WE DO: the subskill “notes, not sentences”; pass 1 — the order of the questions, passes 2–3 — the notes grid

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Subskill: Real scouts never write sentences — no TIME. Words. Letters. Symbols. ‘+’ means ‘and’. I model one cell: clothes shops → I write “clothes” (TB: TWO words max).
  2. Pass 1 (ex.5): Just the ORDER of questions a–d. Nothing else. Key: 1c·2d·3a·4b.
  3. Passes 2–3 (ex.6): a grid (Westfield · Mall of America × shops/eat/other). Notes only!
  4. Checking as a battle for the answers: TB key — Westfield: clothes+shoe shops · restaurants, cafés, food stalls · cinema; Mall of America: sports shops, games shop · restaurants · cinema, concerts, mini golf. +1 per cell.
📺 On screen: audio 26 (Backup: I play both interviews myself from the TB script pp132–133, two voices/positions) · the notes grid on the slide/board
👦 Students do: they listen three times with a growing task, write notes in words, fight for the cells. Expected: 70–90% of the grid filled. Typical error: full sentences → the next answer is missed — the subskill is proven on their own skin.

Product: street interviews with notes
🏁 PRODUCT · street interviews
stage · rotation

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share: pairs play out the interview for 60–90 sec with partner rotation ×3; judges catch some/any accuracy; notes are keywords only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I start round 1: half are reporters, half are shoppers, the “street” = a walk through the classroom. 90 sec per interview.
  2. The format is Kagan Timed Pair Share: A speaks for their time on the timer, B is the active listener (counts pauses on fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. Rotation: reporters move on 1 position, rounds 2–3; in round 3 the roles swap.
  4. Live judging: I and 2 appointed judges catch clean quantifiers (+1) and any-in-a-question (+1).
  5. Final: the 2 best pairs go “on TV” (in front of the class), the full rubric applies.
📺 On screen: stopwatch · judging cards · leaderboard
👦 Students do: they interview with rotation, answer in full from their notes, the best perform for the class. Expected: everyone talks at once ×3 rounds — maximum engagement. Typical error: “Have you got some shops near…” — the judge logs it, swapping roles teaches it.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — clean forms (1–3) · Richness — quantifiers, connectors, bonus structures (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		stopwatch · rubric cards

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use some/any/a lot of with countable/uncountable nouns accurately in writing and run a «street interview» about a shopping centre from their own notes (subskill: taking notes).
  • Subsidiary. Gate exposes the written some/any ceiling; C/UC + rules closed in a base sprint; quantifier duels + “catch the mistake”; Brain teaser race; 45′ switch: 16 shopping-centre places at speed + street interview (audio 26) as scout tradecraft; the GMF4 bonus is woven into the interview (I've never been to…).
  • Personal. Grammar is NEVER the announced topic — the audit is. It's a double pair by the KTP: hold the break exactly in the middle, the “mall” is a change of scenery, not a loss of pace. Collect live errors silently — the clinic comes at the rematch.
By the end, students can…
  • sort C/UC and prove the rule
  • write 6+ clean some/any/a lot of sentences fast
  • name 12+ shopping-centre places
  • take notes from natural-speed audio and interview a partner from them

Structure
I've got some water, but I haven't got any tomatoes. · There's a lot of sugar in juice. · What's your favourite place to go shopping? · 🚀 bonus: I've never been to a food stall.
Form
some = affirmative · any = negative and question · a lot of = a lot, with C and UC; possessive 's: baker's, chemist's (“the baker's” shop); bookshop as one word.
Pron. watch
restaurant /ˈrestrɒnt/ 2 syllables, ‘au’ silent (TB) · chemist's /k/ · stress on the 1st syllable in every place: BOOKshop, CLOTHES shop.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Have you got any juice — why any, not some?” → question. “I've got some juice?” → affirmative.
“Two breads?” → No — bread is uncountable: some bread.
“Notes = full sentences?” → Never — words, letters, symbols (the subskill).

Assumptions: some/any met in GMF4 orally; the real ceiling is written accuracy + the C/UC logic behind it. Shopping-centre set partly new (chemist's, stationery shop, food stall). Note-taking is a NEW skill — model it. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
