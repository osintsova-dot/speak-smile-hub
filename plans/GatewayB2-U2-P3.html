🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Choose between the past simple and the past continuous — the event against the scene it happened in — and use used to and would for past habits while knowing the two places where would is impossible; then use ten phrasal verbs of work (set up, take over, turn down, fill in, put off, keep at, get ahead, keep up with, work on, put somebody off).
What is genuinely hard
The tense pair is a Russian problem: one Russian past covers both, so the student has to ask a question English speakers never ask — was this a scene or an event? The used to / would pair is harder still, because would looks like a free synonym and is not: it cannot describe a state, and used to cannot take a stated duration. Both restrictions are exam items.
Typical error to expect
«When I came home, my mother cooked dinner» for an action already in progress — English puts the background in the past continuous: my mother was cooking. Russian has one past tense and orders events with words, so the choice has to be made consciously every time.
«I would be shy when I was small», «I would have a cat» — would works for repeated past ACTIONS only, never for past states. Used to covers both, which is why it is the safer choice under exam pressure.
«I used to live there for ten years» — used to cannot take a stated duration. With a period, the past simple: I lived there for ten years.
«set on a company», «turn off an offer», «fill up a form» — the verb is right and the particle is wrong, which in the lexical section scores exactly the same as writing nothing.
Why the lesson is built this way
The grammar is discovered from five sentences about the two women the class read about yesterday, so the examples carry meaning the students already own. The controlled practice is a text about the Black Mambas, an all-woman anti-poaching unit in South Africa, which is worth reading in its own right and gives the continuous something real to describe. The used to / would half is built on a discovery grid (which sentence shows a state? which shows one single event?) rather than a rule, and lands in a Victorian text about chimney sweeps and rat catchers, where both forms are used naturally. The phrasal verbs close the lesson inside one story: a boy who set up a company at sixteen.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“In 1840 a nine-year-old boy would climb up the inside of a chimney, and if he refused, someone lit a fire under him. Today: the two English past tenses, the two ways of describing what people used to do — and the difference between them, which costs a mark in every exam.”

Structure
One day, as Lidia was preparing a meal, she stopped and noticed their excited faces. · They used to cook for rich mountaineers. · They would cook for rich mountaineers. · He set up a company at sixteen and then took over another one.
Form
past continuous = was/were + -ing for the scene, the longer action, the interrupted one · past simple for the event and for finished actions · used to = repeated actions AND past states · would = repeated actions only, never states, and not usually in the negative · a comma after a fronted while/as clause · transitive phrasal verbs: set the company up / set up the company, but keep at your work only.
Pron. watch
the -er / -ee pair moves the stress and changes the person: employer vs employee, trainer vs trainee, interviewer vs interviewee · the past-perfect contraction is one syllable: I'd been /aɪd bɪn/ · linking in the negotiating phrases: «I agree_up to_a point», «What_about_you?»
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«While I was walking home, I saw an accident.» Which is longer? → the walking. Which interrupts? → the accident.
«They would be cooks» — possible? → no. Being a cook is a state, and would does not do states.
«I used to live there for ten years» — possible? → no. A stated duration needs the past simple: I lived there for ten years.
«He turned the offer down» and «he turned down the offer» — both correct? → yes. «He turned down it»? → no, a pronoun goes in the middle.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«Would» is used for everything, because it feels elegantTwo crosses on the board, permanently: would ≠ state, would ≠ negative habit. Every wrong would gets pointed at rather than explained again.
The Black Mambas text turns into a vocabulary lessonPre-teach exactly one word — poach — and let the rest go. The text is a grammar exercise with a good story attached, not a reading lesson.
Phrasal verbs are memorised without their objectsEvery answer is said as a whole phrase: «set up a company», «turn down an offer», «fill in a form». A bare particle is not accepted as an answer.
Fast finishers in the phrasal-verb blockThe Teacher's Book task: write three or four sentences summarising the David Eisman text, using at least four of the ten verbs.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a narrative) — past simple against past continuous is the most frequent single item in that block.

Every year the Russian paper gives a short story with gaps, and every year the same decision has to be made: the background of the scene goes into the past continuous, the events into the past simple. Add used to and would, which the paper likes because Russian expresses both with one imperfective verb. The phrasal verbs are a different kind of mark: set up, take over, turn down and fill in appear in the lexical section, where a wrong particle scores zero.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
