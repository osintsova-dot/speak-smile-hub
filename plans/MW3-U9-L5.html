Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, children will be better able to ask & answer to guess a bug — “Has it got legs? Yes, it has. Has it got wings? No, it hasn't. It's got spots. It's black and yellow.” — a life skill: describing something so a friend can guess it.
  • Subsidiary. Play the bug quiz (as Alex, Lucy & Auntie Vicky in the video, CD4 T25); count bug legs; mingle-and-ask; invent & draw a bug and describe it; play Guess the bug! & Mingle! (04); sing Genki «How Many?» (30); Phonics Hero finale — final speed-reading of all sounds & spellings.
  • Personal. First proper speaking focus of the unit — never force a shy child; pointing or a yes/no nod counts. Model with the puppet first. Keep each exchange ~20 seconds, then swap partner. Celebrate the inventing — every bug is a good bug.
By the end, pupils can…
  • ask “Has it got…?” to guess a bug;
  • describe a bug — “It's got spots. It's black and yellow.”;
  • invent, draw & describe my own bug.

Structure
Has it got (legs)? — Yes, it has. / No, it hasn't.  ·  It's got (wings). It's black and yellow. It's a (bee)!
Form
Guessing routine: Has it got + feature? → yes/no → clues (It's got… It's + colour) → name the bug.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions

“To help my friend guess — do I show them, or DESCRIBE it?” → describe it!

Assumptions: All six bugs, the four features & Has it got…? are secure from L1–L4; today they use them freely to describe & guess. Children meet Alex & Lucy doing the same quiz in the video. Reading reaches its final speed-read celebration.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Guesses without askingPuppet models: first ASK Has it got wings?, get a clue, THEN guess — one question at a time.
Forgets the short answerPuppet models Yes, it has! (nod) / No, it hasn't! (shake) — children copy the gesture.
Invented bug = just scribbleFrame it: How many legs? Spots or stripes? What colour? — describe as they draw.
Shy to speak to a peerAsk the puppet first, then a friend; a yes/no nod is a full answer.
Over-excited minglingSmall space, walking feet, Freeze on the tambourine.
Classroom management — signals
“Class? — Yes!” — you: “Class!” → pupils stop & echo “Yes!”
“WiFi” 🖐 — say “WiFi” + raise a spread hand → pupils silently mirror & tune in.
Volume control — raise/lower your arm = louder/quieter → settler.
“Give me five” — eyes · ears · mouth · hands · feet.
Countdown — “Finishing in 5…4…3…2…1… pencils down.”
“Freeze!” — statue, mid-movement reset.

25–32′
Game · Guess the bug! (pairs) ⚡
carpet / floor space

▶ Guess the bug! In pairs: child A secretly picks a bug card; child B asks Has it got wings? Has it got spots? to guess. Swap. Every pair playing at once — nobody watches.

💪 Support: ask one question & guess. ⭐ Extension: give clues as child A: It's got stripes. It's black and yellow!

32–39′
Game · Mingle! (04) + count the legs
whole room

▶ Mingle! Music on — children mingle as bugs; music off — find a partner & ask Has it got wings? — Yes, it has! Then count together: How many legs? 1-2-3-4-5-6! (a bug has six legs!).

Count & sing. Sing Genki «How Many?» (30) — count legs, spots & wings while we move.

39–54′
Table time · Pupil's Book p.95 🧘
tables

Name & number. Children name the bugs in the quiz & number the photos in order 1–4 (CD4 T25).

Invent a bug. children invent & draw their own bug — you prompt as they draw: How many legs? Spots or stripes? What colour? Then colour it.

Show & describe. in pairs, describe their invented bug: Look at my bug! It's got six legs. It's got wings. It's got spots!

✔ Assess: who asks Has it got…? & gives full clues (It's got… It's black and yellow)? who needs the puppet? Celebrate every answer.
