🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A grammar pair, but the topic is NOT «adverbs of frequency» — the topic is «the lie detector». For the lie to be hard to spot, the statement has to be PRECISE: always/usually/hardly ever in the right place and love/like/don't mind + -ing. An error in the form = the crew spots the lie instantly from the crooked phrasing rather than from the meaning — the cost of an error works better than a rule. The text about the chess player Carissa Yip is the final calibration.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Welcome to the Lie Detector. Each of you writes three statements about your week. Two true, one fake. The crew has sixty seconds to find the fake one. But here is the trap: a badly built sentence gives you away instantly — the class hears the wrong word order and knows. So today we build sentences that are impossible to read. Precision wins. Calibration test in three minutes.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The lie detector: everyone writes three statements about their week, one of them false; the crew hunts for the lie. A crooked sentence gives you away at once — so today we learn to build a sentence nobody can read.

WB Unit 2 · points on the leaderboard
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can place adverbs of frequency correctly (after be, before other verbs) and use love/like/don't mind/don't like/hate + -ing or noun at speed, in statements the class cannot decode.
  • Subsidiary. The always → never scale is drawn out of the examples in the P2 text; they discover the position rule themselves (ex.1–2); ex.3 rewriting against the clock; ex.6–7 written runs; Brain teaser ex.8; the error clinic on P1–P2.
  • Personal. The first grammar pair of the unit: I do NOT explain the rule — the students build the scale and word the position rule themselves (guided discovery). The error feedback is anonymous.
By the end, students can…
  • build the frequency scale from always to never
  • place the adverb correctly with be and with other verbs
  • use like/love/hate/don't mind + -ing and + noun
  • write three statements the crew cannot decode

Structure
We're never late for class. · I'm sometimes nervous before exams. · We usually watch TV in the evenings. · My brother hardly ever does sports. · I love skateboarding. / I love sports. · He likes practising the piano. / He likes music. · I don't mind drawing. / I don't mind art. · We don't like playing chess. · They hate watching TV.
Form
with the verb be the adverb goes AFTER the verb: She is sometimes nervous. · with all other verbs — BEFORE the verb: She always practises in the skate park. · after love/like/don't mind/don't like/hate comes either a noun or a verb with -ing; don't mind = «it's OK with me», love/hate = strong feelings
Pron. watch
often — two legal versions: /ˈɒfən/ and /ˈɒftən/ (TB) · hardly ever is said as one chunk, with no pause
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«She plays often chess» — what's wrong? → often goes BEFORE the main verb: She often plays chess.
«He always is late» — and here? → with be it's the other way round, AFTER: He is always late.
«I like play football» — correct it. → I like playing football: after like comes -ing.

Assumptions: SB p.28, KTP pair 15 (both halves are grammar). The adverbs are familiar from A1+, like + -ing partly; the load is in speed, position and writing, not in understanding.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«Adverbs again, we know this»I don't argue: a gate on writing speed shows the real ceiling in 60 seconds. After that it is the thrill of the detector doing the work, not the rule.
She plays often chess — the classicA separate station on position + the detector rule: a phrase with a misplaced adverb is automatically «suspicious» and gets examined.
like + -ing gets mixed up with like + nounI give it as «two legal lanes»: I love sports / I love skateboarding — both correct, only I love skateboard is illegal.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
