WHAT WE DO: opening; a rewind of comparatives with transport — a springboard to superlatives

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Opening: One word — the transport you took today.
  2. Rewind U5 (Warmer TB p.115) from a frame: A ___ is faster than a ___ . Comparison pairs in chorus: train–bike · plane–car · taxi–bus.
  3. I add a third object: A bike, a train AND a plane. Which one is number one? — I wait for an attempt; I accept anything (“the most fast”, “the faster”).
  4. You need a NEW form. Today you get it.
📺 On screen: the board: three pictures + the frame “… is faster than …”
👦 Students do: they compare in pairs, run into the need for a new form. Expected: they try “the most fast” — a great way in. Typical error: I don't correct it now, we'll come back to it in 5 minutes.

Discovery: three steps (class records)
Hook · class records
standing

WHAT WE DO: a physical warmer-discovery: we line up by height / hair length — and we need the word “-est”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up! Make a line — from the tallest to the shortest. No English words yet — just do it.
  2. I point at the two ends: He is TALL. He is TALLER than you. And he is… THE TALLEST! — I write three steps on the board: tall → taller → the tallest.
  3. Two more line-ups: the longest hair, the longest way to school (in minutes) — every time in chorus the …est.
  4. These are class records. In forty minutes you'll have a whole Records Desk.
📺 On screen: the board: the ladder tall → taller → the tallest (stays up)
👦 Students do: they line up — everybody moves; they say the third step in chorus. Expected: laughter, the form is remembered through the body. Typical error: “more tall” — I point at the ladder.

Input: the five rules of the superlative
Discover · five rules (traffic light)
board → desks

WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin: input of the rules step by step: ex.1 → ex.2 the table together → ex.3 matching rules; we build the traffic light

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 p.102 together: three or more things · we use THE · the opposite of most is LEAST.
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we alternate, one answer each — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!
  3. I build a traffic light on the board: 🟢 1 syllable → the + -est (old→the oldest) · 🟢 1 syllable, consonant+vowel+consonant → double it (big→the biggest; BUT slow→the slowest, -w stays untouched) · 🟡 2 syllables ending in -y → -iest (happy→the happiest) · 🔴 long ones → the most / the least · ⭐ good→the best · bad→the worst.
  4. Ex.2 p.102, we fill it in TOGETHER on the board, each one — naming the colour of the rule (the biggest 🟢 · the healthiest 🟡 · the most popular 🔴 · the most dangerous 🔴 · the best ⭐ · the worst ⭐).
  5. Ex.3 in pairs, 2 min: rules a–e ↔ examples 1–5 (key: a→3 · b→1 · c→4,5 · d→6,7 · e→2).
  6. Pronunciation (TB p.116): -est is /ɪst/ · least /liːst/ · worst — long /ɜː/, no r.
📺 On screen: the board: the RULES TRAFFIC LIGHT (stays up until the end of the pair) · SB p.102
👦 Students do: they fill in the table together, tie each form to a colour. Expected: the colour-rule works as a crutch and removes the chaos. Typical error: they count syllables wrong — we clap the syllables together.

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.4, eight adjectives on mini-boards with mandatory naming of the rule colour

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4 ▶ spelling duel with support: I dictate a word → the team writes the form AND the rule colour → Boards up!
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYBODY writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift them all together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can sit it out.
  3. 8 rounds: heavy 🟡 · hot 🟢 doubling · interesting 🔴 · light 🟢 · slow 🟢 (no doubling!) · small 🟢 · thin 🟢 doubling · thirsty 🟡.
  4. Reinforcement (TB p.116): I hint in advance — Three of these are one-syllable-with-doubling. Find them.
  5. A separate card on the board: slow → the slowest. NO double W.
  6. +1 to the team for the form, +1 for correctly naming the colour.
📺 On screen: mini-boards · the traffic light on the board · the SLOW exception card
👦 Students do: they write the form and the colour at the same time — everybody takes part. Expected: by round 5 they name the colour themselves. Typical error: “the hotest” — the doubling rule, in chorus.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can form superlative adjectives (the biggest · the happiest · the most expensive · the best/worst) and act out a ticket-office dialogue from a model using 5 Key phrases.
  • Subsidiary. Comparatives (U5) are reviewed as a springboard; the five rules are introduced with a traffic light and fixed with the “class records” game; Grammar round-up p.102 is taken half at a time with support; the video model dialogue is watched twice; Key phrases are drilled step by step THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; Peer review — 2 questions.
  • Personal. Superlatives are a rewarding topic — you see the result right away. The role-play “ticket office” must feel safe: a support card on the desk, we only close the book by the third run-through.
By the end, students can…
  • form superlatives of 8 adjectives with the right rule
  • answer superlative questions about their city from a frame
  • ask for information at a ticket office (single/return, price, platform)
  • give simple information politely

Lexis

Structure
A plane is the fastest. · It's the most expensive way to travel. · What's the fastest way to Liverpool? — Take the 10:15 train. · Is that single or return? — Return, please.
Form
the + -est (old→the oldest) · doubling the consonant (big→the biggest, but slow→the slowest, -w is NOT doubled) · -y→-iest (happy→the happiest) · long ones → the most/least · irregular: good→the best, bad→the worst. Always THE!
Pron. watch
-est = /ɪst/ · least /liːst/ · worst — long /ɜː/, the r is silent · a polite intonation in the dialogue (TB pp.116–117).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Two things — the fastest?” → No, for two — faster THAN. The superlative is for three or more.
“The most big?” → No: the biggest. Most is only for long words.
“A return ticket — is that only back?” → No: there AND back.

Assumptions: Comparatives were introduced in U5 and are still alive; superlatives are a new form. Past simple negative/questions — from yesterday's pair, still fragile (we support them, not overload them). Transport and collocations are active.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
