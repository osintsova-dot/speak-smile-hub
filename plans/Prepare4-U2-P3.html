🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Name sixteen sports with the right verb — do athletics, go jogging, play volleyball — and say what was happening at a past moment with the past continuous (was/were + -ing), including questions.
What is genuinely hard
Not the sports — the verbs in front of them: Russian covers all sixteen with «заниматься/играть», so do/go/play must be built as a system, not memorised word by word. In the grammar the new muscle is the QUESTION: was/were jumping in front of the subject while the -ing stays put.
Typical error to expect
make sport · go to jogging · play swimming — the do/go/play collocation guessed from Russian «заниматься» [high priority, A2/B1]
I read a book when my mum called instead of was reading — the action in progress pushed into the past simple; and the mirror error, I was going to bed at ten and was sleeping immediately [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
Vocabulary comes first because the reading and the grammar are both made of it. The do/go/play table is built by the students from the recordings, then stress-tested by the book's own five-minute quiz — nine filters over sixteen words is deep processing disguised as a race. The BMX article carries the unit's removed-sentences task, run as ordinary careful reading, and hands the grammar its two example sentences (I was taking photographs… They were riding…). Questions get their own workshop stage because they fuel the speaking; the productive stage is a timeline interview about REAL times yesterday — reported in the third person, because that is where was/were agreement actually breaks. One honest note: the TB page with the p.24 vocabulary keys is missing from our scan — ex.2 is confirmed against track 16 live, and the quiz key below is teacher-derived and safe.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“At 8.30 last night — what were you doing? Prove it. Today you meet a team of teenage BMX champions, learn sixteen sports and the three small verbs English hides in front of them — and by the end you'll question each other about yesterday like sports journalists.”

WHAT WE DO: name the aim and hang the two machines of the lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Draw the verb machine, empty: three columns DO · GO · PLAY with the two questions underneath: does it end in -ing? → GO · can you win it against someone? → PLAY · neither? → DO. Plus a corner: WAS/WERE + -ING, empty.
  2. Explain the aim in 40 seconds: sixteen sports that Russian covers with one verb and English with three — and the tense that films what was happening.
  3. Set the bar: Your answer needs a verb and two facts. One word is not an answer.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 (track 15): sixteen sports ↔ photos A–H; ex.2 (track 16): six interviews; ex.3–4: build the table

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. About you, 60 seconds in pairs: Which sports do you play regularly? Which do you watch?
  2. ex.1 in pairs: match eight of the sixteen box words to photos A–H, then play track 15 to check. Key: photos: A boxing · B cycling · C gymnastics · D ice hockey · E rugby · F surfing · G table tennis · H volleyball.
  3. Drill the sound traps by ear: athletics /æθˈletɪks/ (three syllables, th!) · gymnastics /dʒɪmˈnæstɪks/ · squash /skwɒʃ/ · volleyball /ˈvɒlibɔːl/ · rugby /ˈrʌɡbi/ — three choral repetitions.
  4. ex.2, play track 16: six speakers, match the halves 1–6 ↔ a–f. Check against the collocation logic AND the tape: the collocations decide most of it: We do → a athletics · play → d ice hockey / e tennis together · go → b cycling / c jogging / f windsurfing on the lake. ⚠️ The exact speaker-by-speaker key is confirmed with track 16 in class (the TB notes page for p.24 is missing from the scan).
  5. ex.3–4: pairs sort ALL sixteen sports into the machine, plus two of their own. Key: do: athletics, boxing, gymnastics · go: climbing, cycling, ice skating, jogging, surfing, swimming, windsurfing · play: ice hockey, rugby, squash, table tennis, tennis, volleyball; ex.4 adds e.g. play basketball, go snowboarding (teacher-derived: -ing activities → go · games → play · the rest → do). Fill the board columns as pairs report — Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Column GO — number threes!
📺 On screen: tracks 15–16 in the board app (Backup: read the six interviews from the audioscript, TB p.263 — the matching works identically by voice; the photo check runs from the book alone)
👦 Students do: match, listen, sort sixteen words through the machine. Expect squash and athletics to be the new words, ice hockey to land in PLAY only after the «can you win it?» question. Typical error: go boxing — no -ing in the sport's NAME, the machine says do

WHAT WE DO: the BMX team article: gist → five removed sentences → the bike words

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hook, books closed: A bike with tiny wheels, a low seat and NO gears. Why would anyone want that? Take two guesses, then: because small wheels are faster.
  2. Gist race, 2 minutes — ex.1: 1 teenage girls · 2 racing is the target. Pre-teach on the board: wheels · seat · fear · pedal (= push the pedals with your feet).
  3. ex.2 — five sentences removed, eight candidates. Do gap 1 TOGETHER as the TB says: She laughed… she was watching her older brother race — who is she? Olivia. D fits. Then pairs, Kagan RallyCoach: partner must hear the deciding words for one more gap. Key: 1 D · 2 G · 3 A · 4 B · 5 F · not needed: C, E, H. TB coaching: do the first gap together (D — she = Olivia, watching her older brother race); the sentences before and after the gap vote, related words (brother–boys) and pronouns decide.
  4. ex.3: the five highlighted bike words. Key: 1 gears · 2 stunts · 3 track · 4 helmet · 5 guards.
  5. Talking points if the pace allows: Should schools offer the same sports to girls and boys? · Is there too much sport on TV?
📺 On screen: track 17 is the recording of the article — optional (Backup: skip the audio entirely; the stage is built on silent reading)
👦 Students do: read for the two gist answers, then argue the gaps from pronouns and related words. Expect gaps 4–5 (B and F) to be the hard pair — B's «they» = the races. Typical problem: answers grabbed because a sentence «sounds nice» — the deciding-words rule blocks it

Grammar · discovery
class → pairs
Why: Guided discovery, not presentation: the forms are hunted down in a text the class has already read and argued about. Rules extracted from familiar sentences are rules they keep.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1: the two example sentences from the article → the two rules

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Warmer, books closed: TB warmer: «Yesterday» + times of day on the board (8.00, 10.30, 1.30…). At 8.00 I was driving to school. Hayley — what about 8.30? The answer goes on the board in the third person (At 1.30 Hayley was having lunch), verbs underlined, and the question passes down the row.
  2. ex.1 — the two examples straight from the article: I was taking photographs of the girls' BMX team. · They were riding around the track. Pairs complete the two rules. Key: rules: a … at a particular time in THE PAST · b PAST form of be + -ING.
  3. Fill the tense corner on the board: WAS/WERE + -ING = the camera is rolling on a past moment. Ladder drill, choral: I was, you were, she was, we were, they were — fist on was, flat hand on were.
  4. Concept checks from the plan header — all three, cold-calling by name.
  5. Signpost: Grammar reference page 141 — tonight, two minutes, if you want it in a table. ⚠️ If a student's book is missing p.26 (a known print defect in our scanned copies), hand them the TB facsimile print-out — the page is identical.
📺 On screen: the tense corner fills in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: extract the two rules from sentences they have already read. Expect the -ing part to be automatic and was/were agreement to be the real work — hence the ladder. Typical error: she were
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Unit4_Grammar_Past_Continuous_A5
A5 grammar card — PAST CONTINUOUS: AN ACTION IN PROGRESS IN THE PAST: the formula subject + was/were + verb-ing with the SUBJECT MAP (I/he/she/it — was · you/we/they — were), the negative (weren't playing) and the question flip (Was she cycling? — Yes, she was.), the contrast with the past simple (I trained yesterday. / At 6 p.m., I was training.) and the -ING SPELLING note (ride → riding · run → running · play → playing)
  • Glue the card in and draw the clock: the action is already going at that moment.
  • Colour was and were on the subject map so the pair never mixes.
  • Write three sentences about six o'clock yesterday: you, your family, the street.
  • Say a pair aloud: what you did, and what you were doing.
When: ~5 min at the start of the “Grammar · discovery” stagePrep: 1 × A5 per pupil. Scissors, glue sticks, pencils.

Why: Questions are the hardest form of the tense and the fuel of the speaking stage — so they get their own six minutes with the word order nailed to the board before anyone has to improvise one.

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: build the six questions — word order nailed to the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model on the board from item 0: Were you doing sports at 3.30 yesterday? — box was/were FIRST, subject second, -ing third.
  2. ex.4 solo, 3 minutes, pair check. Key: questions: 1 What were you doing at 8.30 yesterday evening? · 2 Were you reading at 10.30 yesterday evening? · 3 What were you wearing last Sunday? · 4 Were you sleeping at midnight last night? · 5 Were you having breakfast at 8.00 this morning? · 6 What were you doing five minutes ago? — TB tip: underline the time words — yesterday morning/evening but LAST night/Sunday.
  3. Kagan Showdown on two of the six: teams write the full question, show on three.
  4. TB detail worth stealing — underline the time words: yesterday morning · yesterday evening · but LAST night, LAST Sunday.

🎯 Speaking out · yesterday 8.30
pairs, then thirds
Why: The productive outcome: past continuous questions and answers about real times yesterday, then reported in the third person. Errors are logged silently — they open lesson 4.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5: the timeline interview — ask, answer, then report in third person

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. One minute solo: everyone notes what they were REALLY doing at three times yesterday (8.30 am, 3.30 pm, 8.30 pm) — one detail each.
  2. Kagan Timed Pair Share, 2 minutes each way: A interviews B with the ex.4 questions plus follow-ups (Were you sleeping at midnight? What were you wearing last Sunday?); B answers in full sentences. Swap.
  3. The reporting round — new pairs: report your OLD partner in the third person: At 8.30 Misha was gaming. He wasn't doing his homework. The listener hunts one thing: was or were, right or wrong.
  4. TB fast finishers: two more time prompts each, swap again.
  5. You walk with a paper: log agreement and word-order slips SILENTLY — they open lesson 4.
📺 On screen: the three times and the question skeleton stay in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: interview, answer, then report a third person. Expect the third-person round to expose was/were exactly as planned — the listener's hunt keeps it peer-corrected. Typical error: he were gaming — logged, not corrected

WB Unit 4 (p.16–19)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can use do/go/play with sixteen sports and build past continuous statements, negatives and questions about a past moment.
  • Subsidiary. Reading the BMX article for gist and detail with the removed-sentences task (SB p.25); the sports quiz as deep vocabulary processing; third-person reporting (was → were agreement).
  • Personal. Never say «Reading Part 4» out loud — it is just careful reading. SB p.26 is missing from some print runs of our scanned SB: teach the grammar half from the TB facsimile print-out if a student's book lacks the page.
By the end, students can…
  • say sixteen sports with the right verb — do, go or play
  • explain WHY a sport takes its verb (-ing activity → go; game → play)
  • say what was/wasn't happening at a past time (Kim was playing…)
  • ask What were you doing at…? and answer with detail

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I was taking photographs at 3 pm» — finished at 3 pm or in progress? → in progress: the camera was still in my hands.
«Were you doing sport at 3.30 yesterday?» — how is the question built? → was/were jumps before the subject, the -ing stays.
«Kim was playing tennis. She wasn't swimming.» — one time or two? → one time: one action in progress, one NOT happening.

Assumptions: SB p.24–26 (top), syllabus lesson 11. Past simple is solid; past continuous existed in Prepare 3 receptively but has not been produced this year. Of the sixteen sports the class owns half as words (tennis, boxing, surfing as loans) but almost none with the right verb. Comparatives are two lessons old — the reading recycles them (small wheels are FASTER).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«play swimming», «go to jogging», «make sport»Build the three-column table as a MACHINE, not a list: -ing activity → go; game you can win → play; the rest → do. Every slip: point at the machine and make the student run the two questions.
was/were agreement breaking in the third personThe reporting round exists exactly for this: before it, drill the ladder chorally — I was, you were, she was, we were, they were. Fist on was, flat hand on were.
The removed-sentences task answered by feelDo the first gap together as the TB says (D — she = Olivia): the sentences before and after the gap vote, pronouns and related words decide. Pairs must point at the deciding words for one more gap before you accept answers.
athletics/gymnastics pronounced from spellingDrill by ear before the words are seen: athletics /æθˈletɪks/ (three syllables, th!) · gymnastics /dʒɪmˈnæstɪks/ · squash /skwɒʃ/ · volleyball /ˈvɒlibɔːl/ · rugby /ˈrʌɡbi/. Three choral repetitions, then individually.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
