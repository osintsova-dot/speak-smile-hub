🎯 GATE · time-in-words blitz
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 6 times from the slide (in digits) → write them IN WORDS in 3 minutes; self-check against the key, personal score in the margin

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I display 6 times in digits: 2:15 · 11:50 · 4:00 · 7:30 · 9:40 · 1:25.
  2. I say: WRITE them in words. Quarter past two — like that. You've known numbers for years. Time in words is the REAL game. Three minutes! Timer.
  3. At zero — the key goes up on the slide: quarter past two · ten to twelve · four o'clock · half past seven · twenty to ten · twenty-five past one. Self-check, score in the margin.
  4. The turn: Six out of six? Silence?.. ‘Half past’ with silent L, ‘to’ after thirty minutes — THIS is our growth zone. Rematch inside the test pair!
📺 On screen: slide: 6 times in digits → key in words (the same numbers from TB p.57 Warmer/ex.2) (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: write 6 times in words solo, honestly count the clean ones, score privately in the margin. Expected: 3–4 out of 6 — the ceiling is exposed. Typical error: “half to eight” — I don't shame it: “that's the growth zone”.

Sprint: the TO/PAST system + a times dictation
⚡ Base sprint · TO/PAST + audio clocks
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: the system in 4 min: TO/PAST clock (ex.1) + a times dictation audio 29 (ex.2) with a “in words” challenge; a duel of pairs ex.3

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I draw the anchor-clock: right half PAST, left half TO. Minutes one to thirty — PAST. After thirty — TO the next hour. That's the whole system. We sort ex.1 together, out loud.
  2. Ex.2: Draw six clocks. Audio — write the times. Challenge level: in WORDS, not numbers! (audio 29; no track — I dictate myself from the TB key).
  3. Check against the key, +1 for a clean one; pronunciation drill: half /hɑːf/ · quarter · pas(t) two · /tə/.
  4. Ex.3 as a 90-sec duel: Draw a clock — partner says the time in words. Swap. Score your partner!
📺 On screen: board: anchor-clock TO/PAST (stays up for the whole lesson) · SB p.48 · audio 29 or dictation from the key (TB p.57): quarter past two · ten to twelve · four o'clock · half past seven · twenty to ten · twenty-five past nine (Backup: no track — I read the script aloud at listening speed.)
👦 Students do: sort phrases onto the sides of the clock, draw and label clocks to the audio, duel in pairs. Expected: the system clicks fast. Typical error: “quarter past” at 45 min — a gesture at the left half of the clock.

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share: preparing a 60-sec voiceover of your day: 6 times + 6 routines in key words; a run-through in pairs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Task: Your «day in my life» audio: six times IN WORDS + six routines + one adverb of frequency. Keywords only — no full script! 6 min solo.
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A says their time against the timer, B is the active listener (counts pauses on their fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. ICQ: Full sentences on paper? — No! Six times, six verbs.
  4. I show the judges' rubric; a run-through in pairs 2×60 sec, the partner counts pauses on their fingers.
  5. I circulate, fixing only time-in-words and collocations.
📺 On screen: product rules slide · judges' rubric large · phrase bank (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: write a keyword support sheet, rehearse in pairs with a stopwatch. Expected: arguments about “how to say 7:40” get settled by the anchor-clock. Typical error: a full script — I take the sheet away, leave the key words.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — the forms are clean, third person with -s (1–3) · Richness — times in words, adverbs of frequency, connectors (1–3). 9/9 = “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		prep sheets · stopwatch

WB Unit 4 (pp.28–35) · notebook · points to the leaderboard next pair
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can voice a 60-sec «A day in my life» over a photo-timeline with 5+ times said correctly IN WORDS (quarter to eight, half past five…).
  • Subsidiary. Gate exposes the real ceiling — time in words; TO/PAST system + 12 routine collocations (have/do/go) fixed at speed; Video skills sloth watched with maker-tasks; Present simple used in context (he getS — noticed, not taught).
  • Personal. Anti-“we know it all”: they know the numbers, but not “twenty-five to two” without a pause. Respect the base out loud, raise the bar straight away. The unit leaderboard starts today; teaser: the unit closes right before Mid-year.
By the end, students can…
  • say and WRITE 6+ clock times in words
  • use have/do/go collocations without ‘to home’ mistakes
  • compare their day with the boy's day in full sentences
  • deliver a 60-sec day-voiceover to the bar

Structure
What time is it? — It's twenty past ten. · I get up at quarter past seven. · He gets up at… (noticing only) · That's different — I…
Form
minutes BEFORE the half hour = past, AFTER = to; half past (not half to!); go home WITHOUT to; have + meals/lessons, do + homework/sport.
Pron. watch
half /hɑːf/ (the l is silent!) · quarter /ˈkwɔːtə/ (the first r is silent) · pas(t) two — the t is swallowed · to = /tə/, stress on the numbers (TB p.57 ABCD).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“7:40 — past or to?” → twenty to eight (after the half hour — to).
“I go TO school, I go TO home?” → No — go home, no to.
“In the voiceover, can you say ‘seven-forty’ all the time?” → Digital is OK in schedules, but the bar is times in words.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: numbers/days are automatic, present simple is there in speech. The real gaps: time IN WORDS (past/to, half), have/do/go collocations, long connected speech. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (time-in-words / -s spelling / question word order), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
