WHAT WE DO: hook-mapping: we open the wall pp.110–111, read the 9 square topics and COLOUR each one 🟢/🟡/🔴 by class vote

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. We open the spread: This is the QUESTION WALL — nine white squares around the page. Each square = one grammar boss of our year.
  2. I read the topic of each square (be · have got · some/any · present simple · can+comparatives · present continuous · past -ed · past questions · superlatives) — the class votes: 🟢 “we can do it ourselves” / 🟡 “need the table” / 🔴 “work it out together”.

WHAT WE DO: pairs work through squares 2–6 at their own pace: green on their own, yellow with the Quick review tables; I go round to the yellow ones

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Instructions: Squares two to six, your pace. Yellow square? Open that unit's Quick review column — the page is written next to it. That's your legal helper.
  2. Support pages on the board: U2 have got → QR U2 · U3 some/any → QR U3 · U4 present simple → QR U4 · U5 can/comparatives → QR U5 · U6 continuous → QR U6.
  3. I go round the pairs on yellow: leading questions, not answers. Water — can you count it? So…?
  4. Self-check keys (upside-down cards) on my desk: a pair finishes a square → checks itself → “finds” go up on the board in the frame.
  5. The ⭐ envelope for the fast ones: your own sentence on the square's model.
📺 On screen: SB pp.110–111 ex.2–6 · key cards (from TB pp.124–125) · the “We worked this out” frame
👦 Students do: they travel round the wall at their own pace with legal supports. Expected: a spread of pace — that's normal, the self-check keys keep everyone busy. Typical error: ex.6 spelling (sitting/having) — a double/drop-e hint card by the keys.

WHAT WE DO: past simple together: square 7 + ex.7 (the Wright brothers) sentence by sentence with the class → square 8 + ex.8, two examples

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: consolidation through a game: bingo on the wall via Kagan Numbered Heads + a gentle fix-it via Kagan Showdown

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Wall bingo + Numbered Heads Together: teams have a 3×3 grid with the square numbers. Number off one-two-three! I read a task — heads together, find the square, agree the answer. Then I call a number — that person answers for the team.
  2. Sample task: “say the negative: She likes football” → square 4 → doesn't like → cell closed. Three in a row = BINGO (+3).
  3. 8–10 tasks across all the squares, with supports on the board.
  4. ▶ A gentle fix-it = Showdown: sentences from the “We worked this out” frame (our finds from today!) — Fix it in secret on your board… Showdown — boards UP!
  5. See? The mistakes we CAUGHT today — you fix them in seconds now.
📺 On screen: 3×3 bingo grids · task list · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they play grammar bingo and fix THEIR OWN morning finds. Expected: the finds are easy to fix — growth is visible within the lesson. Typical error: they're wary of cells 7–8 — I give those tasks with the verb card.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can work through the 9-square grammar wall with colour-coded support (green squares = “I can do it myself”, yellow = “with the table”, red = “we work it out together”) and solve the colour exercises in pairs using Quick review columns.
  • Subsidiary. The wall's mechanics are learned calmly (square-rule → exercise); the unit support tables are legal for the whole double lesson; the hard spots (past simple, superlatives) are worked through TOGETHER, without a rush; “catch the error” in a soft version.
  • Personal. Per the syllabus: “a quest in pairs with support tables… working through the hard spots without stress”. No stopwatch pressure on the red squares. An error = a find to work through, not a minus.
By the end, students can…
  • explain the wall route (white square → colour exercise)
  • solve squares 1–6 in pairs with tables
  • work through past simple squares 7–8 with the class
  • spot 2–3 classic mistakes with support

Pron. watch
doesn't /ˈdʌznt/ · didn't /ˈdɪdnt/ · than /ðən/ — weak form · a clear chorus on the short forms.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Can we look at the unit table?” → Yes! Yellow square = “with the table” — that's strategy.
“There are some water?” → No: water uncountable → There IS some water.
“Did you went?” → No: after did comes the dictionary verb: Did you GO?

Assumptions: All 9 grammar points of the year have been introduced on the from-zero line; the freshest ones (past simple U7–8, superlatives) are still raw — for those we plan a joint walk-through, not a solo race. The wall's colour coding takes the fear away: you can see where you're on your own, where you need support, where it's a group job.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The wall looks scary (9 squares!)Hook: first the CLASS colours the wall in 3 colours — turns out there are only 2–3 reds. We eat the elephant one bite at a time.
past simple falls apartSquares 7–8 — only TOGETHER, with the went/did/saw/took card and choral repeats; I never leave them for pairs alone.
They argue over answers in pairsThe referee = the unit's Quick review column (the page is written next to each square); I teach them to check themselves, not argue.
Fast pairs get boredThe “star tasks” envelope: make up YOUR OWN sentence following the square's example — we'll play with them later.
They see a mistake as a failureThe “find” ritual: every caught mistake goes up on the board in the “We worked this out” frame — with a + sign.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
