WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Quiz-Quiz-Trade: a deck of questions from the whole year — the whole class asks and answers at once, cards circulate

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Quiz-Quiz-Trade: everyone has a card (a question from the year + the answer underneath). Find a partner — QUIZ them! Check with the card. They quiz you. TRADE cards, new partner!
  2. The deck mixes units: Have you got…? (U2) · Is there any…? (U3) · What time do you…? (U4) · Can you…? (U5) · What are you wearing? (U6) · Where were you…? (U7) · How did you get here? (U8)
  3. 6–7 trades in 8 minutes; I walk round and listen for full answers (short answer + a detail = the 🚀 standard).
  4. Finale: Hands up if you answered SEVEN different units today. THAT's your year in eight minutes.
📺 On screen: the deck of the year's QQT questions (asset)
👦 Students do: they quiz each other across every unit at once — a cumulative review with no desk and no queue. Expected: they recognise the questions from “their” fair stands. Typical error: a short answer with no detail — the card reminds them: +1 detail.

Quest: the year wall
Year wall quest
stations · crews

WHAT WE DO: the “year wall” quest: 8 unit cards around the classroom, each with 3 tasks (a word + a form + a phrase); crews with a sheet, everyone signs

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 8 year-wall cards around the classroom. On each card: A) name 3 words from the set · B) fix the sentence (the unit's Common error) · C) say the Key phrase for the situation.
  2. The sheet rule: whoever answers signs THAT item — round the circle, no strong student hogging it.
  3. Free rotation, 12 minutes for all 8; disputed answers come to me for arbitration.
  4. I put up the keys at the end — self-check, the score goes onto the marathon leaderboard.
📺 On screen: 8 unit cards (the “year wall” asset) · quest sheets · keys
👦 Students do: they walk the year on foot, fix errors, sign under their answers — everyone's contribution is visible. Expected: 7–8 cards out of 8. Typical error: one student writes everything — the signed sheet catches it.

MARATHON: Showdown with a prize
🏁 MARATHON · Showdown with a prize
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Showdown (mini-boards): the year's marathon quiz — 10 rounds, everyone writes, we reveal as a team; the champion opens the box

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Showdown: Question up → EVERYBODY writes alone → captain calls ‘SHOWDOWN!’ → boards up together → team agrees the best answer. The captain rotates.
  2. 10 rounds through the year: telling the time in words · he watchES · some/any · was/were · comparative · a Key phrase cloze · a spelling trap · question word order · object pronoun · a past question.
  3. A clean volley (every board correct) = +3; a streak of 3 volleys = a streak bonus of +2.
  4. Finale: the count → the champions open the box (a prize for the whole team) → special nominations for the rest (Best comeback · Best spelling · Best streak). Applause for everyone.
📺 On screen: mini-boards for everyone · marathon slides with the keys · the prize box (Backup: I call out each round from a printed card and reveal the key on the board)
👦 Students do: the year's final quiz: everyone writes every round — nobody sits it out; the prize closes the competitive season. Expected: volleys under cheers. Typical error: they peek before the showdown — “no talking till the call!”

WHAT WE DO: express reading format: strategy (task before text, easy ones first) + 1 mini-text in A2 Key format, self-check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

  2. A mini-text (5 true/false questions, U9 “festival fun” format) — 5 min solo, timer.
  3. Self-check against the key on the slide; the score stays private.

WHAT WE DO: express listening format: pictures a/b/c; I read the script twice; strategy “look at the pictures FIRST”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Strategy: Look at the pictures BEFORE you listen. Ask yourself: what's different in a, b, c? — 30 sec to mark them up.
  2. I read the mini-script (3 dialogues) twice, at a pace a little slower than natural.
  3. Self-check; we go over one trap: the answer comes AFTER the distractor.
  4. On Thursday I read everything twice. Missed the first time? The second run is your friend.
📺 On screen: mini Listening task (a/b/c pictures) · script with me
👦 Students do: they mark up the pictures before listening and catch the answers — the format strategy is in hand. Expected: the second listen takes the panic away.

WHAT WE DO: express writing: choose a genre from the year, a portion of 5 sentences; Kagan · Numbered Heads Together — a genre checklist as a team

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Choose a genre (message / email / blog) — we write a PORTION: the first 5 sentences of your text, 6 min.
  2. ▶ Kagan · Numbered Heads Together (a genre check): Heads together: what MUST an email have? … Number FOURS — answer! (Dear + a name · a reason/because · a signature · 40+ words). 2 rounds, by genre.
  3. A self-check of your own portion against the named checklist; 1 correction in a different-coloured pen.
  4. On Thursday you'll finish the whole text. Today you proved the start is easy.
📺 On screen: portion sheets · genre checklists
👦 Students do: they write the start of a real genre and check it against a team checklist — knowing the requirements = half the Writing marks. Expected: they choose genres confidently — they've written all year.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is the marathon already the test?” → No: it's a game. The test is tomorrow, and it's easier than the marathon.
“Is a target something I don't know?” → Something I'm unsure of. Knowing your targets = half the win.
“Can I ask the teacher during the test?” → About the task — yes; about the answer — no. That's why we learn strategies today.

Assumptions: Targets were written down at the P9 Showcase; the whole year has been covered and shown to parents. Anxiety is low — the Showcase proved to the kids that they can do it; today we only add “paper” confidence.

PartContents & marking
Part 1 · Vocabulary (12 pts)A) 6 word↔topic matches from the year’s sets (U1–U8). B) 6 collocation gaps: have got / go home / play the guitar / catch the bus / wear a uniform — traps from the units.
Part 2 · Grammar (16 pts)A) 8 gaps covering the year’s system: be · have got · some/any · present simple (3rd person!) · can · comparatives · present continuous · past simple. B) 4 order-the-words questions (Do/Does · Did · Is/Are). C) 4 “spot the mistake” items from the year’s Common errors.
Part 3 · Reading (10 pts)A short guide text in the style of U9 “A world of festival fun” (A2 Key format): 5 true/false + 5 multiple choice.
Part 4 · Listening (6 pts)Mini dialogues: picture choice a/b/c (U9/Exam trainer format) — 6 items; no track → I read the script twice.
Part 5 · Writing (6 pts)Genre of CHOICE from the year: message / email / blog post. 🚀 higher: 40+ words, 2 tenses, a connector; 🐣 standard: 25+ words using the prompt frame. Marked for: task complete (2) · grammar (2) · genre features (2).

Total 50 pts · the focus is not on the number but on the “I can” sheet next to the score. Lines: 🚀 higher (Wh-questions in writing, more traps, a story option 50+ words) · 🐣 standard (word banks + prompt frames in writing). The format of every task is familiar from the year’s lessons.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Anxiety: “the test is tomorrow”The frame for the day: “the test shows what you've already shown live.” Every format gets touched by hand today — no surprises.
The marathon overheats the rivalryThe prize is team-based and kind (a privilege/small treat for everyone on the champion team); special nominations for the rest; only calm blocks after the marathon.
Weaker students hide behind strong ones in the quizzesKagan mechanics: Showdown — EVERYONE writes on mini-boards; QQT — everyone has their own card; the quest — a sheet with everyone's signature.
“Targets” get written as a formalityI check the target format: a topic + an example of MY OWN error + how to fix it. No example — not a target.
Not enough time for 3 formatsFormats go express: 1 mini-task per format + a strategy. The goal is recognising the answer sheet, not drilling volume.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
