👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door — a high-five + one word: project mood?

Step 2: the list of people + the bar for descriptions
PLAN · the list + the bar
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: RallyRobin: ex.4–6: a personal list of 8–10 → paired discussion using ONLY Super skills phrases → choosing 6 with notes against the bar

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.4 solo (3 min): a list of 8–10 important people/groups — MORE than will fit: the choice will be conscious.
  2. I run the pair as Kagan RallyRobin: we alternate one answer each — Ping-pong it: A — one, B — one, back to A. No monologues, no lists!
  3. I hand out Key phrases tents (What do you think? · Have you got any cousins…? · Are you sure about that?): paired discussion — decisions ONLY in English.
  4. Ex.6: choose 6 people/groups + notes against the bar: who + age · looks (have got!) · WHY important · 🚀 ≥1 bonus structure per map (We've been friends since… / has never…).
  5. ICQ: Sentences in notes? — No! Keywords!
📺 On screen: Key phrases tents · bar cards · note sheets
👦 Students do: they trade phrases, choose consciously, write notes with an eye on the bar. Expected: “Are you sure about that?” with relish. Typical error: a decision in Russian — we replay it in English in 20 sec.

WB Unit 2 (pp. 12–19) · points to the leaderboard next pair
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners create and present a circle map of important people (bar: 60 sec each, every description = who + looks + why) and pass the gate REMATCH ×3 proving unit growth.
  • Subsidiary. Project cycle with Alex's model; Super skill Critical thinking («don't marry the first idea»); Key phrases as the negotiation currency; judging by Layout/Content/Language/Visuals (TB axes); Final reflection; rematch of all three gates.
  • Personal. Continuers' project trap = coasting («my mum, my dad, the end»). The bar card forces depth: looks with have got + WHY important + a bonus structure. Creativity praise > correctness praise today. Time ruthlessly.
By the end, students can…
  • choose people via critical-thinking tips, not first ideas
  • negotiate in English with the book's Key phrases
  • present their map 60 sec to the bar
  • beat their own three gate scores

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is the first idea usually the best?” → Book says no: write it, then think about other people too.
“Can a TEAM be in the map?” → Yes — Alex's basketball team is (SB p.35).
“Is ‘my mum is nice’ enough for the bar?” → No: looks + why + a real detail.

Assumptions: All unit language live; the stretch = sustained English negotiation + WHY-sentences (reasons). Rematch closes the unit arc with measurable growth. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
