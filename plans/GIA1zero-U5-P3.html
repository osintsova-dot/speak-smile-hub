Discover · the four rules

WHAT WE DO: ex.1–2 p.64 step by step with chorus: the aff/neg table → four rules → a ban-poster on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 together on the board: we fill in the table slowly, each item in chorus. Key: 1 You · 2 can · 3 meet a robot guide. · 4 Walker · 5 can't · 6 sing..
  2. I note the full form: can't = cannot. (TB p.74)
  3. Ex.2 — four rules, we discuss each one: Rule one: after can we USE another verb. Rule two: WITHOUT to. Rule three: no -s with he, she, it. Rule four: negative is can't. Key: 1 use (after can — a DIFFERENT verb) · 2 without (no to!) · 3 doesn't end (no -s on can) · 4 can't.
  4. I put up a poster: can + VERB · ✂ to · ✂ -s · negative = can't (cannot) — it stays up until the end of the unit.
  5. Drill volley on -s: I show he / she / they — the class says can (not cans!) ×6.
  6. Pronunciation (ABCD TB p.74): I /kən/ speak French — short. I /kɑːnt/ cook — long! Chorus ×3, then “fingers”: I say it — they show 1 finger (can) or 2 (can't).
📺 On screen: SB p.64 ex.1–2 · the ban-poster on the board (stays up) · timer
👦 Students do: fill in the table together, repeat in chorus, tell can/can't apart by ear using their fingers — everyone reacts. Expected: the rules are clear, the listening needs a second attempt. Typical error: they hear the weak can as can't — I repeat it slowly and exaggerated.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use can/can't in all forms — affirmative, negative, questions and short answers — and name 8 technology nouns with 4 adjective pairs.
  • Subsidiary. The rules of can are drawn out inductively from the tables on SB p.64 with a choral drill; a Brain teaser as light relief; listening to “a talk” with the completing a table subskill in 2 passes; pronunciation /iː/ (p.117).
  • Personal. ⚠️ Grammar is exactly 45 minutes (lesson A), the second half is vocabulary and listening. The course's first big modal grammar: let it breathe — table, chorus, game, writing, and only then freedom.
By the end, students can…
  • say the four can-rules in their own words
  • make Yes/No and Wh- questions with can from a frame
  • name 8 technology nouns and 4 adjective pairs
  • complete a listening table with help from the subskill steps

Structure
I can speak French. · He can't cook. · Can you fly a plane? — Yes, I can. / No, I can't. · What sport can you play?
Form
can + verb WITHOUT to · no -s in the 3rd person (he can play) · negative can't (= cannot) · question: (Wh-) + can + subject + verb?
Pron. watch
can in a statement is UNSTRESSED and weak /kən/ (I can speak French) · in a short answer — strong /kæn/ (Yes, I can) · can't is always strong and long /kɑːnt/ (TB pp.74–75 ABCD)
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He can to sing — what's extra?” → to.
“She cans dance?” → no: can never takes -s.
“Can you swim? — a one-word answer?” → Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

Assumptions: After Pairs 1–2, can/can't has been heard in frames, with no rules given. Tech nouns are partly familiar from life (laptop, tablet), but touchpad, charger, screen are new. The adjectives cheap/expensive/fast/slow are partly known from U3.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
