🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that the vocabulary and grammar of two units work without the book: the thirteen life events, sixteen sports with do/go/play, the eight double words, comparatives and superlatives with not as… as, too/enough, and the past continuous — spelled correctly.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the gap between oral and written: out loud the class knows almost everything, on paper licence loses a letter, athletics grows one, and comparatives sprout a second marker the moment nobody is watching.
Typical error to expect
becouse · tomorow · alot · wich — the most frequent misspellings; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam In our block that means athletiks · gimnastics · lisence · hapier · begger (for bigger).
Plus do/go/play collocations picked by feel instead of by the machine.
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built as retrieval practice, not re-teaching: two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it — and shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which form still breaks. The official Cambridge game sits between the stations and the briefing on purpose: it hides revision inside a lie-detector race, and a class that has just competed walks into a test calmer. Only the Unit 3–4 squares are called — the U1–U2 squares were played in block 1 — and the book's Review 1 spread is not touched at all: it is syllabus lesson 14, whole and honest, next lesson. Nothing new is introduced anywhere today.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. Everything on these two papers you have already done in this room — I can name the lesson for every line. First forty-five minutes we play it back. Then forty-five minutes you show it. Your score is not the point: the point is your own list of five mistakes to fix.”

0–5′
Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast card round of the block's words, and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — hot round. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the block's word cards (life events + sports + double words), two trades each: Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest: Everything you just said is in today's test.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, call definitions and pairs answer chorally
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety dropping. Typical stumbles: get a degree vs go to university, and athletics said with two syllables

5–8′
Lesson frame
board
Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: put the test sections up in advance — transparency takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the six sections of the two papers on the board, one line each: life events · too/enough · comparatives ×2 · sports with do/go/play · double words · past continuous ×2.
  2. Look at the board. Have we done all of this in class? Yes or no? The class answers chorally — the answer is always yes; if anyone doubts, name the lesson for that line.
  3. The rule of the day: Your score goes in the register. Your five mistakes go in your notebook. The second one matters more.
📺 On screen: the section lines in chalk — the board is the medium and it stays up for the whole first half, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the sections and answer chorally. Expect visible relief. Typical problem — they don't believe «we have done all of it»: point line by line at the lesson it came from

8–20′
Review station 1 · words
teams
Why: Retrieval practice rather than re-teaching: they pull the block's vocabulary out of memory in short bursts, which is what makes it stay.

WHAT WE DO: the block's vocabulary: 35+ items, against the clock and in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, oral: definitions and collocations at speed — you stop working at 65? (retire) · the paper from university? (a degree) · the sport with a racket and a wall? (squash) · the person who trains the team? (coach — NOT trainer!). Twelve items.
  2. Round 2, the machine: you call a sport, teams answer with the verb chorally — gymnastics? — DO! · windsurfing? — GO! · squash? — PLAY! All sixteen, fast.
  3. Round 3, written: Kagan Showdown — you say the meaning, teams WRITE the word. Load the traps deliberately: licence, athletics, gymnastics, happier, bigger, enough.
  4. Every misspelling goes into a «watch out» column on the board — it stays there right up to the test.
📺 On screen: definitions read aloud, answers on mini-whiteboards — voice and boards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: say, chant, then write the words. Expect almost everything orally and a visible drop in the written round — which is exactly why the column exists. Typical error: lisence
📓 Interactive notebook — Prepare4_Block2_Units3-4_Match_2up_A4 + Prepare4_Block2_Units3-4_Translate_2up_A4
two 2-up sheets (one A4 = 2 pupils each): MATCH IT! — 12 Russian sentences to be joined to their English pairs A–L (life events, comparatives and not as … as, too / enough, sports with do–go–play, past continuous, words with two meanings), then TRANSLATE IT! — the same 12 written out in English from memory
  • Glue MATCH IT! in and join the pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with a partner.
  • Glue TRANSLATE IT! on the next page and write the English without looking back.
  • Self-check against the matched sheet; every mistake is rewritten as a full sentence.
  • Write your score out of 12 in the margin — that is the block's exit line.
When: ~8 min at the start of the “Review station 1 · words” stagePrep: 1 × A4 = 2 pupils (pre-cut, one of each sheet per pair). Glue sticks, pencils.

20–32′
Review station 2 · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar — and it shows you, twenty minutes before the test, exactly which form still breaks.

WHAT WE DO: the block's grammar: ten mixed sentences with reasons out loud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ten mixed sentences, one at a time: three on comparatives/superlatives (Winters here are ___ (cold) than in Sochi · the ___ (happy) day), two on not as… as, two on too/enough (I'm not ___ (old) to vote), three on past continuous ± and questions (What ___ you ___ (do) at 8.30?).
  2. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the REASON, not the answer — Say WHY, not just the answer.
  3. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Sentence six — number fours, boards up!
  4. Boil every explanation down to the step: one marker per adjective; enough after adjectives, before nouns; was/were jumps first in questions; -ing survives everywhere.
📺 On screen: the ten sentences written on the board before the lesson and uncovered one by one with a sheet of paper — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with an explanation, then report by number. Expect too/enough position to be the wobble — slow exactly there. Typical error: the most happiest still standing — a line of its own in the watch-out column
board · mini-whiteboards
32–40′
Review game
groups of 3–5
Why: The official Cambridge game hides revision inside a reason to listen to each other; a class that has just competed walks into a test calmer.

WHAT WE DO: «I think you're lying!» — the official Cambridge game, our block's squares only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Groups of 2–5, one sheet per group, a dice: the throw picks a grid square (column + row). The player makes a TRUE or FALSE sentence from the prompt in the square; the group votes «I think you're lying!» or «I believe you» — points for catching a liar or for fooling the group. Variants on the sheet: Explain / Ask me! / Listen.
  2. ⚠️ The grid covers Units 1–4 and its U1–U2 squares were played in the block 1 review (the full list is in Prepare4-U1-P5). Today call only the Unit 3–4 squares — anything about sports and matches, life events (born, left school, got married, retired…) and too/enough. If a throw lands on a describing-people or clothes square, throw again.
  3. Follow-up if time allows: the group picks its best lie of the day and performs it to the class — the class votes one last time.
  4. Set the bar: A lie only counts if it is a full sentence. «I got my driving licence when I was nine» — beautiful. «Licence, nine» — nothing.
📺 On screen: one printed game sheet and a dice per group, run off in advance — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed; with no printer, call the prompts yourself from the sheet and groups play against you
👦 Students do: throw, lie or confess, vote, score. Expect the lying to produce better past continuous than any exercise — that is the game's secret. Typical stumble: verbs dropped in the rush — full-sentence rule holds

40–45′
Test briefing
class
Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to the «watch out» column: the five words that were misspelt. Look at these five words for thirty seconds. Then I close them.
  2. Instructions: two official papers, Unit 3 then Unit 4, about 40 minutes total; phones in the bag; finished early — five sentences about last weekend on the back.
  3. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  4. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect silence to settle in 20 seconds if the flat hand goes up before you start talking

WHAT WE DO: two minutes for water and a stretch before the test

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

📺 On screen: instructions by voice, nothing to put up and no backup required

47–85′
Block test
solo, silent mode
Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats, official papers. Everything in them has been done in class, so the test measures learning, not surprise.

WHAT WE DO: the official Unit 3 and Unit 4 tests, back to back — ~65 items in about 38 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. Unit 3 first. You have thirty-eight minutes for both papers. Start.
  2. Walk the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  3. Write the time left in chalk: 20 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  4. Anyone who finishes early writes five sentences about last weekend on the back and sits quietly.
  5. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling — licence, athletics, happier.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; nothing at all is put up in the room while the test is running
👦 Students do: work in silence for 38 minutes. Expect the vocabulary sections to be closed fast, the U3 rewriting (same meaning) and the U4 question conversation to slow them down. Typical error: ex.4 of Unit 3 rushed — mention it in the briefing

85–90′
Test wrap-up
circle
Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the feedback lesson.
  3. Homework: once the papers come back — rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly; for now, the Unit 3–4 vocabulary or grammar worksheet if anything is still open.
  4. Teaser: Next lesson — the book itself looks back: Review 1, all four units, teams, an auction and the unit videos. And your marked papers after that.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is full, read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to name the rewriting section and the questions most often — that is the plan for the feedback clinic

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students have shown control of Units 3–4 vocabulary and grammar on the official Cambridge unit tests and have their own list of five mistakes to fix.
  • Subsidiary. Revision through stations (vocabulary, grammar) and the official review game; keeping test anxiety low through transparency.
  • Personal. Give no new tasks and do not squeeze the weaker students during the review: the review half builds confidence. Decide BEFORE the lesson who (if anyone) gets the Plus version of the papers.
By the end, students can…
  • recall the block's 35+ words and phrases without the book — spelled right
  • run the do/go/play machine and the one-marker comparative law
  • produce was/were + -ing in statements and questions
  • write five of their own mistakes in the correct form (after papers return)

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Nerves before the testSay it out loud and early: nothing will come out of thin air, every format has been done. Put the section list on the board before you start.
Someone finishes both papers in 25 minutesThe rule: hand them in, turn the sheet over and write five sentences about last weekend — two continuous, three simple. It is read, not marked.
After the test they want to compare answers out loudCollect the papers in silence; the analysis is next lesson's opening. Today, once a paper is in, the only thing left is personal reflection.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
