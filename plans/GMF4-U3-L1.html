Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to name 12 prehistoric objects and ask & answer about the past: “Were there (tools) in prehistoric times? — Yes, there were. / No, there weren't.”
  • Subsidiary. Sing «In prehistoric times» (CD1 47) & the vocabulary chant (CD1 46); brainstorm museums (Learning to learn); meet the past of there is/are — Were there…? / there were / there weren't; hear past verbs in context (wore · ate · made · had); early literacy — unscramble & read the object words (AB).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; ICQ before every task; stirrer→settler rhythm — the memory game is high-energy, plan the freezes. Insist on were in the question and the right short answer (there were / there weren't).
By the end, pupils can…
  • name the 12 prehistoric objects;
  • ask “Were there any…?” and answer “Yes, there were / No, there weren't”;
  • read & spell the object words (unscramble).

Structure
Were there (tools) in prehistoric times? — Yes, there were.  ·  Were there (computers) in prehistoric times? — No, there weren't.  ·  in the song: They wore fur. They ate meat. They made fire. They had spears.
Form
Were there + noun (in the past)? Short answers: Yes, there were · No, there weren't. Notice past verbs in the song: wear→wore, eat→ate, make→made, have→had (irregular — met here, drilled in L2).
Pron. watch
axe /æks/; spear /spɪə/; fur /fɜː/ (not “foor”); fossil /ˈfɒsl/; there were /ðeəwə/ weak at speed.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Were there cars in prehistoric times? Yes or no?” → no → No, there weren't.
“Were there caves? — one word for many long ago: is or WERE?” → WERE (past, more than one).

Assumptions: Starter + Units 1–2 routines are solid; pupils met was/were and past adjectives in Unit 2, so Were there…? is a natural next step. The 12 objects are largely NEW; irregular past verbs (wore/ate/made/had) are noticed today, drilled in L2. Readers — word cards work alongside pictures.

10–20′
Presentation · 12 objects
carpet → board

Present with flashcards + chant (act. 2, CD1 46). Each card: What's this? It's… a cave! An axe! A spear! Choral ×3 + one gesture (axe = chop, spear = throw, fire = warm hands, fossil = trace a spiral).

Were there…? (act. 4 language). Board frame in bright colour: Were there (spears) in prehistoric times? — Yes, there were. vs Were there (computers)? — No, there weren't. Hold each card; class asks & answers truthfully.

▶ Take Off–Touch Down: Stand up if it was in prehistoric times… a spear! …a phone! …a fossil! — flash cards fast, everyone reacts every time.

💪 Support: answer with the gesture + Yes/No. ⭐ Extension: full answer — Yes, there were spears in prehistoric times.

20–28′
Song · In prehistoric times
desks

To your tables! Open your books at page 28. Sing (act. 3, CD1 47). Listen & point to the objects in the cave picture; 2nd run — sing with cave-life gestures.

Hear the past verbs. Read the song lines: They wore fur. They ate meat and fish. They made fire. They had spears. Underline the past verbs (wore · ate · made · had) — These are PAST — they show a long time ago! Notice, don't lecture (drilled in L2). Freeze between verses.

28–36′
Cave memory game
carpet → desks, pairs

▶ Memory game (Talk Partners). Pairs study the PB p.28 cave picture for 30 sec, then close books. A asks: Were there bones in the cave? Were there baskets? B answers from memory Yes, there were / No, there weren't. Open to check. Swap. Point per correct memory.

ICQ: Do you look at the picture while you ask? (→ no — from memory!)

✔ Assess: stand behind one pair — is it Were there…? + the right there were / there weren't?

Object trade
mingle

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade. Each child holds an object card. Music → mingle; off → pair: A shows the card, Were there (axes) in prehistoric times? B answers Yes, there were! then swap, TRADE cards, new partner. 5–6 exchanges — everyone speaks many times.

Whole-class check: Hands up if your object was a TOOL for hunting!
