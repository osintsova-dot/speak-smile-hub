🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The fifteen phrases are not given as a list but through three “engines” (go on / go to / go for) — that is the only thing to remember, the rest builds itself. The listening is split into three plays with one task each: mood first, then plans, then details. The product is personal and safe: a week for Ed — you may copy from the blog, and that is fine.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Meet Ed. His friend Karl is at summer camp. His friend Piet is in Scotland. And last night his parents told him the family isn't going anywhere this year. So this morning at ten o'clock he called a friend just to ask if they were awake. That's how bored he is. Today we learn fifteen things you can do in the holidays — and each crew builds Ed a week. Seven days. And we also learn something useful: how to hear that someone is sad before they say it. Ears on.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: Ed's summer has collapsed: his friends have gone away, the family isn't going anywhere, and he rings at ten in the morning out of boredom. We learn fifteen holiday activities and build Ed a week. And along the way we learn to hear how a person feels before they say it.

Activities on three verb engines
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: model → ex.1 the blog with a task → ex.2 seven definitions → ex.3 the three engines

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. MODEL: tell them about your own week: On Monday I go for a walk. On Tuesday I visit my family. On Saturday we have a barbecue.
  2. Preparing to read (TB routine): Look at the pictures, the title and the seven headings FIRST. Then read fast.
  3. ex.1 (TB key): go on a bus tour, go for a bike ride, have a barbecue, visit an art gallery, look after pets.
  4. THREE COLUMNS on the screen for good (ex.3, TB key: 1 go on · 2 go to · 3 go for): go ON a bus tour, a day trip · go TO a water park, a concert · go FOR a bike ride, a walk, a pizza. Every new phrase goes into a column out loud and with a gesture.
  5. ex.2 with support, work the first definition through together (TB key: 1 go for a pizza · 2 go on a day trip · 3 hang out with friends · 4 go sightseeing · 5 stay with a friend · 6 go for a walk · 7 sunbathe).
  6. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with cards: phrase — definition.
📺 On screen: SB p.101 ex.1–3 · the three columns on screen · cards (Backup: the three columns go on / go to / go for on the board, with every new phrase added there in marker)
👦 Students do: work with support and put the phrases into columns. Expect: “go for a pizza” surprises them — explain it as the outing, not the food

Feelings before the listening
Feelings BEFORE the listening
crews · standing

WHAT WE DO: they SAY it with emotion themselves first, then listen to track 50

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB routine (produce first, recognise after): one phrase — “The concert is tomorrow” — read it bored · confused · excited · surprised · nervous · sad. Crew by crew, standing, with the face.
  2. The marker words on the screen (TB prompt): bored = 'This really isn't interesting.' · confused = 'I don't understand.' · excited = 'I can't wait!' · nervous = 'I feel a bit scared.'
  3. ex.5 track 50 — six short lines, listen TWICE (TB key: 1 confused · 2 excited · 3 surprised · 4 sad · 5 nervous · 6 bored). Mini-whiteboards — Kagan Showdown. Do the first line as a model: listen, write confused on the board yourself and explain which word told you. After that the rule holds: everyone writes their own, holds the board with the text facing them, and all lift together on the command Showdown! The six feelings are on the screen — they choose from a ready list, nothing has to be invented.
  4. Talk it through out loud: Which WORD told you? — for every item.
📺 On screen: audio track 50 · mini-whiteboards · slide with the six feelings (Backup: read the six lines yourself from TB p. 136, with honest intonation, twice each)
👦 Students do: act the emotion first, recognise it after. Expect: after acting it themselves, recognition is noticeably easier

34–46′
Listening: three calls step by step
Listening · three calls step by step
audio · crews

WHAT WE DO: track 51 in three plays: mood → who is talking about what → details

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Play 1 — mood only (ex.6, TB key: a Sam — excited · b Nina — sad · c Ed — bored). One word each. Nothing else.
  2. Before play 2, talk through out loud WHAT we are after (TB routine): Number one — a walk. Number two — a water park. Which extract? Play 2 — ex.7 (TB key: 1 Extract 3 · 2 Extract 1 · 3 Extract 2 · 4 Extract 3 · 5 Extract 2).
  3. Before play 3 — the same walk-through: Question one asks WHEN and WHERE. Question two asks WHEN. Play 3 — ex.8 (TB key: 1 at the bus station at eight o'clock · 2 on Saturday at six o'clock · 3 tomorrow · 4 this afternoon · 5 go for a walk or play video games).
  4. The ex.7 answers come in through Kagan Numbered Heads Together, and you model the first item yourself: say Extract 3 and explain which phrase told you. Then the crew puts the other four together — the crew rule is simple: while even one of them cannot say the answer, the crew is not ready. Then you call a number: Number threes — number four, which extract?
  5. After the third play — pause phrase by phrase, and a fourth play if it is needed.
📺 On screen: audio track 51 (Backup: read the three calls yourself from TB p. 137, one after another, with different intonation; read Ed's message twice)
👦 Students do: listen three times with one task at a time. Expect: by the third play almost everyone gets the details — three tasks at once would have been a failure

WHAT WE DO: a stretch

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Stand up. I say a feeling — you show it with your face and one sound. Bored! Excited! Nervous!
  2. This is Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols: you do the gesture and the sound first, the class repeats after you, and over four feelings the gesture sticks. Then play it the other way round — you do the gesture only and students name the feeling in a word. One word is all they have to say, so even the quietest ones join in.

PRODUCT · a week for Ed
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: product: seven days for Ed, one activity a day; the speaker holds notes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame on the screen: On Monday he can … . On Tuesday he can … . On Wednesday … . I think he'll like … because … .
  2. Support — the seven blog headings: Be a tourist at home · Get wet! · Spend time outside · Make mealtimes special · Enjoy the arts · Help out · Organise visits. Copying from the blog IS allowed and expected.
  3. One condition: Use all three verbs — go on, go to, go for. And one day must work in the rain.
  4. Write for 6 minutes, then read it in pairs, then the crew comes out with notes.
  5. The class listens with a task: Which day would YOU steal for yourself?
  6. Praise something specific: Right verb! Good rainy day!
📺 On screen: the frame as support · the seven-box calendar · the three columns on screen · timer (Backup: the frame and the seven boxes drawn on the board, with the engine columns there too)
👦 Students do: speak with notes, the crew beside them. Expect: the “rainy day” is the most creative part

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can say 15 holiday activities with the right verb (go on / go to / go for), name a speaker's feeling from a recording, and present a seven-day plan from notes.
  • Subsidiary. The “staycation” blog (ex.1–4); ex.2 seven definitions; ex.3 the three engines; the listening-for-feelings subskill; track 50 (six lines); track 51 (three calls) — ex.6, 7, 8 across three plays; ex.9 about their own holidays.
  • Personal. The three engine columns stay on the screen all lesson. Audio gets one task per play only. Before every play, talk through OUT LOUD exactly what we are listening for.
By the end, students can…
  • say 15 holiday activities with the correct verb
  • name a feeling from a short recording
  • catch when and where people are meeting
  • present a seven-day plan of activities from notes

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“go to a walk” — is that possible? → no: go FOR a walk.
“sunbathe” — sunbathing or swimming? → lying in the sun.
How do you tell a feeling when you can't see the face? → by the WORDS and by the INTONATION.

Assumptions: SB p.101, syllabus pair 65. Some phrases are familiar (go for a walk, hang out); new are go on a day trip, go sightseeing, sunbathe, look after a pet, visit a museum or art gallery.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Fifteen phrases at once don't fitGive them in three portions by engine, with a gesture for each; the columns stay on the screen until the end of the unit.
The audio is “too fast”Three plays, one task each; before every play talk through out loud what we are after (a time? a place? an activity?) — a TB routine.
They guess the feeling at randomFirst they SAY one phrase themselves with different emotions (TB routine), then they recognise it on the recording.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
