Gate: joining sentences
🎯 GATE · and/but surgery
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: join 5 pairs of sentences with and/but in writing; self-check by the rule; score in the margin

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I show 5 pairs: The food is cheap + it's delicious · I like fish + I don't like meat · You cook it with rice + you serve it with bread · Pizza is common in Italy + in other countries · It's tasty + it isn't healthy.
  2. I say: Join each pair — and or but, comma where needed. Ninety seconds, GO!
  3. Key + rule: and = similar, but = different, a comma before but. Score in the margin.
  4. The turn: Commas missing? but everywhere? THIS is the editor's game — connectors make writing sound grown-up. Rematch inside your post!
📺 On screen: slide with the 5 pairs → key · rule line large (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they join them in writing, check against the key, score privately. Expected: 3–4 out of 5; the comma before but is news. Typical error: and between contrasts — the rule line fixes it.

Challenge: and/but with limiters
🔥 Challenge · and/but limiters
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.7 by the 5-second rule + their own “two halves” sentences as a duel; the comma counts for points

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.7 with rotation: and or but — five seconds! (TB key: and·and·but·but·and·but).
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.
  3. Duel: a crew writes 2 “first halves” (Our canteen is big, …) → the neighbours complete them with the right and/but in 10 sec.
  4. A comma before but = compulsory: miss it — half a point to the opponents.
  5. TB extra: and before the last word of a list — bread, cheese AND milk.
📺 On screen: the SB p.45 ex.7 · mini-boards
👦 Students do: they race through the gaps, duel with the halves, watch the comma. Expected: “…, but the food is terrible!” — the humour kicks in, so the form has landed. Typical error: “but” with no contrast — a neighbour disputes it, +1 for catching it.

Peer review by checklist + publishing
Peer review + publish
pairs → wall

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: swapping posts: 3 editor's ticks + 1 compliment; a 2-min upgrade; publishing on the blog wall

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Swap round the circle (not your desk neighbour): You're the editor now. THREE ticks: ① language correct? ② info correct — are they typical dishes? ③ two and + two but? Plus ONE compliment in English. (ticks = ex.9 of the book)
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. Upgrade: 2 min for fixes against the ticks.
  4. Publishing: posts go on the “blog wall” — a 3-min gallery walk: everyone gives ❤ to two other posts (not their neighbours').
  5. Wrap-up: The blog is LIVE. Editors, you shipped on deadline — that's what real writers do.
📺 On screen: blog wall · heart stickers · tick card
👦 Students do: they edit somebody else's post against 3 points, fix their own, publish and read others' with a ❤. Expected: compliments in English (“Great hook!”). Typical error: the editor fixes it themselves — “underline it, the AUTHOR fixes it”.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — clean forms (1–3) · Richness — quantifiers, connectors, bonus structures (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		blog wall · hearts

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write a 6–8 sentence blog post about food in our country — structured like the model, joined with and/but, checked against the editor's list (a/an/some/any/a lot of · there is/are · and/but).
  • Subsidiary. Gate: sentence surgery with and/but; model blog dissected at speed (title, structure, frames); writing in 2 portions with a visible timer; peer review = 3 ticks; Quick review as a team quiz — prep for the test in P6; GMF4 bonus: superlative hook “The best … I've ever eaten”.
  • Personal. Writing in portions — not a half-hour essay: 2 passes of 8–10 min with a clear brief. The “editor's” bar keeps up the quality, the ticks keep it kind. Quick review is the test's trailer — say that out loud.
By the end, students can…
  • join ideas with and/but correctly (comma before but)
  • reuse 4 model frames in own writing
  • draft, check and upgrade a blog post in portions
  • answer Quick-review questions at quiz speed

Structure
… is a type of stew. · You cook it with… , and you serve it with… · It's delicious, but it takes 24 hours to cook. · 🚀 bonus: It's the best soup I've ever eaten.
Form
and = a similar idea · but = a contrast, a comma before but (SB p.44 rule box); post structure: a general sentence → 4 dishes → descriptions.
Pron. watch
delicious /dɪˈlɪʃəs/ · stew /stjuː/ · feijoada — however it comes out, and that's fine (respect for languages).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“It's delicious ___ it takes 24 hours — and or but?” → but: a contrast (+ a comma).
“Blog post = a list of ingredients?” → No: a general opening → dishes → descriptions (the model).
“Peer review = find everything bad?” → No: 3 ticks against the list, be kind, be exact.

Assumptions: All the unit's language is already in hand (P1–P4) — today it comes together into a genre. and/but are known intuitively; the comma rule and “similar/different idea” are new as a SYSTEM. Typing is faster than writing — but we write by hand (the test is by hand too). Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
