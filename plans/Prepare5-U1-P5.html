Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will have consolidated and self-assessed the lexis and grammar of Units 1–2 (shopping vocabulary & any- compounds · determiners · personality adjectives & un-/dis- prefixes · -ing forms) through low-stakes retrieval, and demonstrated it in a timed block test under exam conditions.
  • Subsidiary. Retrieval practice (free → cued recall) to strengthen retention; peer explanation & error-correction; interactive-notebook translation as productive recall; exam-strategy rehearsal (read instructions, budget time, check answers) with links to ОГЭ / B1 Preliminary.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; run a stirrer→settler rhythm before the test; give a clear ICQ before every stage; make the test setup calm and confidence-building, not high-pressure.
By the end, learners can…
  • recall & use the Block 1 lexis in a sentence;
  • choose the right determiner and form -ing structures accurately;
  • self-check their own answers against a model;
  • work through a timed test to length and to time.

Grammar
Determiners: much / many · some / any · no · a lot of / plenty of (countable vs uncountable)  ·  -ing forms (after verbs of liking, after prepositions, as subject)  ·  any- compounds (anyone / anything / anywhere / any + noun)
Form
many + count. plural · much + uncount. (mostly ?/–) · some in +, any in ?/– · verb + -ing (like reading) · preposition + -ing (good at making) · -ing as subject (Learning is…)
Pron. watch
-ing = /ɪŋ/ not /ɪn/ · word stress on adjectives (reLIable · SOciable · THOUGHTful) · prefixes: un- /ʌn/ vs dis- /dɪs/ · exam spelling: receipt, a piece of advice.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Can I count advice? So — much advice or many advice?” → uncountable → much / a piece of advice.
“Is the sentence positive or a question? So — some or any?” → + = some · ?/– = any.
“What follows a preposition — make or making?” → making (preposition + -ing).

Assumptions: Units 1–2 were taught across Lessons 4–7 (P1–P4); WB Unit 1 (pp.4–7) & Unit 2 (pp.8–11) largely done. Learners are confident readers/writers, know Quiz-Quiz-Trade / Showdown routines, and can work silently in exam rows. Teams: 3–4 per team (10=3+3+4 · 12=3×4 · 14=2×3+2×4).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Weaker pupils feel exposed in retrievalFrame it as low-stakes: “not for marks — getting stuck helps you learn.” Start with pair recall before public answers.
much / many confusion with uncountablesCCQ “Can I count it?” + the exam-trap card: an advice ✘ → a piece of advice ✔.
-ing vs infinitive after prepositionsDrill the chunk: good at / keen on / interested in + -ing; Showdown to expose errors fast.
Over-competition in the reward quiz spills into the testEnd the quiz with a clear settle signal + a 2-min brain-break before the test starts.
Fast finishers on the test disturb othersInstruction on the board: “Finished? Check spelling & countability, then read silently.”

Lead-in · Brain dump
PART A · desks, in pairs

Frame the lesson. Today has two halves: first we review Units 1 and 2 together, then a short block test. The review is not for marks — it's how we get ready.

Free recall (memory science). Close your books. In 90 seconds, write everything you remember from Units 1 and 2 — words, rules, examples. Then ▶ RallyRobin: pairs take turns reading one item each until they run dry.

ICQ: Books open or closed? (→ closed). Quick board harvest — a few items into two columns: WORDS / GRAMMAR.

Vocabulary review
standing, mingle

▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade — each learner holds a card: one side a Block 1 word (refund · discount · loyal · reliable · sociable · disagree · unkind · anything…), the other a definition + a gap-sentence.

Stand up, hand up, pair up! A quizzes B (What does “reliable” mean? Use it in a sentence.), B answers, coaches if needed, then swaps roles → trade cards → new partner. 3–4 rounds.

ICQ: One word, or a whole sentence? (→ a sentence — context, not just translation). Settle: Class? — Yes!

Grammar review · Showdown
teams at desks

Cued recall. Rebuild the two rules on the board from the class, not from you: determiners (much/many · some/any · a lot of / plenty of, countable vs uncountable) and -ing forms (after verbs of liking · after prepositions · as subject). Elicit examples; log the exam traps (an advice ✘, I like to swimming ✘).

▶ Showdown (Kagan): read a prompt (There isn't ___ milk. — much / many? · I'm good ___ ___ new people. — at meeting). Everyone writes on a mini-whiteboard; on Showdown! all reveal at once → team compares → teammate explains the rule. 8–10 items, fast.

CCQ: Positive sentence — some or any? (→ some). After “interested in” — do or doing? (→ doing).

 mini-whiteboards + pens · Showdown prompt slips
25–34′
▶ Flagship review quiz + reward
teams, competitive

Team quiz with a scoreboard (the block's reward game). ▶ Numbered Heads Together: each team of 3–4 numbers off 1–4. Show a question (vocab, determiner, -ing, prefix, or a spelling trap). Teams huddle and make sure everyone can answer.

Call a number: Number 3s, stand and answer! Correct + a full sentence = a point on the board. Rotate numbers so all pupils answer across rounds. 8–10 questions, mixing both units (interleaving).

Reward. Top team = “Block 1 Champions” — small reward / bonus tokens. Then settle hard: Great — champions decided. Now we shift to test mode.

📓 Interactive notebook · Station 1: words, written
desks, glue-in

Match It! (Block 1 sheet). Hand out 13_Block1_Match_2up_A4 (1 A4 = 2 pupils, pre-cut). Glue MATCH IT! in and join the twelve pairs in pencil — first alone, then check with your partner. 12 Russian sentences → English A–L (shopping journey, determiners & any, personality adjectives, friendship chunks, -ing forms, dis-/un- prefixes).

Test briefing (model-first)
PART B · exam rows

Hand out Block Test 1 (lexis + grammar, Units 1–2) face-down. Name and date at the top. Don't turn over yet.

Walk through the paper types once with one worked example each (matching, gap-fill, choose the correct word, transformation) so format is never the obstacle: In this part you choose “much” or “many”. Example: “How ___ money?” → much.

ICQ: How many minutes? Can you ask me a question during the test? (→ ~35 min · no). Turn over — begin.

exam conditions
