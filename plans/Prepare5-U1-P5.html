🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Retrieve and repair everything from Units 1 and 2 — shopping words, determiners, any-compounds, personality adjectives, prefixes and -ing forms — and then produce it under test conditions in the two official Cambridge unit tests.
What is genuinely hard
The block test is a spelling test wearing a vocabulary costume: reliable, thoughtful, uninteresting and receipt all have to be written, not recognised. The other quiet killer is the determiner section, where a right meaning with a wrong word scores nothing.
Typical error to expect
«How much shops are there?» — much never meets a countable noun. Russian has one word for both, so the choice has to be made consciously every single time: count it or weigh it?
«disfriendly», «unagree» — un- sits on adjectives, dis- prefers verbs (disagree, dislike, disappear). And the stress moves: unKIND but DISagree — the ear helps here.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is retrieval, not revision: three timed stations where every student writes and speaks, then the official Cambridge review game, which is the only stage where the language is used for fun. The second half is the real paper, briefed honestly — what is on it, how long it takes, which two words the Student's Book never printed. Nothing new is taught today, and nothing on the paper is a surprise.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty-five minutes to get everything back, then forty-five minutes to show it. Everything on today's paper has been in this room already — including the two words that aren't in your book, which is why we're saying them out loud now.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of retrieval with a spelling penalty, and the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what's the best thing about today so far? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the block in twelve words. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs: one pen, one paper, ninety seconds — write twelve words from Units 1 and 2, taking turns, spelling each one aloud as you write it.
  3. Swap papers with the pair behind and mark. One point per word, minus one for a spelling error — announced before they start, because that is exactly how the paper works.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): name the day honestly: First half: get everything back. Second half: show it. Nothing new today.
📺 On screen: the two word walls, still up from the whole block — no gadgets, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write in turns, then mark someone else's list against the wall

WHAT WE DO: shopping words, adjectives and prefixes retrieved on paper, at speed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three tables, three sheets, four minutes each, groups rotate on your signal.
  2. Table A — shopping: thirteen definitions, write the word. Table B — people: twelve definitions from SB p.14 ex.3, write the adjective. Table C — prefixes: fourteen base words, write the negative (including unhappy and uninteresting).
  3. One rule at every table: Whole word, correct spelling, or it doesn't count. Groups check the previous group's sheet before writing their own — marking someone else's spelling is the fastest way to fix your own.
  4. Fast finishers at every table write two extra items for the next group.
📺 On screen: three prepared A4 sheets, one per table — printed before the lesson, so nothing can fail
👦 Students do: write, rotate, mark. Every student writes about forty words in twelve minutes

WHAT WE DO: determiners, any-compounds and -ing forms, in speech and in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Same three groups, three new tables, four minutes each.
  2. Table D — determiners: twenty nouns on cards; sort into countable and uncountable, then make one sentence with much, many, plenty of or several. Table E — any: eight prompts, build a sentence for each (anyone, anything, anywhere, any more, any longer, any better, any good, any). Table F — -ing: ten half-sentences (I'm good at… · I don't mind… · …is boring for me) to complete truthfully.
  3. Kagan Showdown at each table: everyone writes their answer privately, then all reveal together on the signal and settle disagreements before moving on.
  4. Monitor for the two block errors and repair on the spot: much with a countable noun, and an infinitive after a preposition.
📺 On screen: cards and prompt sheets at three tables — printed in advance, so no gadgets and no backup needed
👦 Students do: everyone writes, everyone reveals at the same moment. No student can hide behind a faster partner

Why: Review game · «I think you're lying!»

WHAT WE DO: the official Cambridge review game, filtered to our two units

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it up in ninety seconds: «I think you're lying!» — PREPARE_5_Review_game_Units_01-04.pdf. Groups of three to five, one game sheet per group, one dice. A player rolls twice (column, then row), reads the sentence in that square and says it as TRUE or FALSE about themselves, adding one detail. The others guess. One point for guessing right, one point to the speaker for every person fooled. Versions: Explain (the extra detail is compulsory) · Ask me! (others may ask one question first) · Listen! (the sheet is held so only the speaker can see it).
  2. Announce the filter and why: the grid covers Units 1–4, so today play only the squares from our two units: There are plenty of shops in my street · I sometimes buy clothes online · I'm going shopping on Sunday · I think going shopping is unpleasant · I think I'm an easygoing person · I love learning English · I give thoughtful birthday presents · Nothing unpleasant happened to me yesterday · I haven't got much time to be sociable · I don't know anyone who has got a medal · I think buying second-hand clothes is silly · I didn't play any computer games yesterday. Squares about tornadoes, tournaments and used to belong to Units 3–4 — skip them and say why
  3. Play the Explain version: every statement carries one extra detail, invented if the statement is false. That is where the block's vocabulary actually gets used.
  4. Circulate with the scoreboard on the board. Two minutes before the end, stop the game and take the best lie from each group — the class votes on the most convincing.
📺 On screen: one game sheet per group plus a dice. Backup: if you have no dice, the rule sheet's own «No dice?» version works — the player next to you chooses the square
👦 Students do: play in small groups; everyone speaks every round because everyone has to guess as well as speak

WHAT WE DO: the two-minute ritual that starts a test calmly

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand out both papers face down, Unit 1 on top.
  2. Read the rubric of section one aloud yourself. It costs thirty seconds and prevents the classic loss of four marks to a misread instruction.
  3. State the two rules: Silence. If you finish, check your spelling — you don't hand in early.
  4. One deep breath together, then: Turn them over.
📺 On screen: the clock on the board with the finish time written on it — no gadgets, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: sit, listen, breathe, start together

Official block test
exam rows, silent
Why: Official block test

WHAT WE DO: Unit 1 Standard and Unit 2 Standard, about 35 minutes with checking time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silence, and you sit down. Do not walk the rows for the first ten minutes — it makes nervous students slower.
  2. Answer only procedural questions (Do I write the whole word?), never linguistic ones.
  3. Write the remaining time on the board at 15 minutes and at 5 minutes. Say nothing.
  4. Stronger students who asked for the Plus versions sit those instead — the sections are identical, so the timing does not change.
  5. At minute 30, one sentence: Five minutes. Spend them checking section one spellings.
📺 On screen: the finish time and the two time-checks on the board — no gadgets, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: work silently for 35 minutes, then check rather than sit idle

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Revision turns into the teacher talkingStations are timed and written. Your job for the first 34 minutes is to hold the clock and answer nothing that the station sheet answers.
The strong students finish every station earlyEvery station has a fast-finisher line at the bottom: write two more items of the same type for the next group to solve. Their items get used, which is why they write them properly.
Test nervesThe briefing is specific and calm: sections named, minutes named, keys promised for the next lesson. Then the ritual — pens out, papers face down, one deep breath, start together.
Someone finishes the paper in fifteen minutesThe instruction is given before the papers turn over: If you finish, check the spelling of every answer in section one, then sit quietly. You do not hand in early.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The block test is built the way the exam block is: produce the form, spell it right. An unkind written with dis-, or a much where many belongs, scores nothing at all — which is why the briefing names both ghosts aloud before the papers go face down.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
