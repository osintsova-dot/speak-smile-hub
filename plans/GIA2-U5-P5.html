🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The second grammar pair, but the topic is NOT “Is there / How much”, it is “the interrogation of a city”: the crew sends out an investigator who has 5 seconds to ask a question — and a broken question burns together with the turn. It is the only way to drive Is there / Are there and How much / How many to automaticity without announcing any grammar. The Grammar round-up about Chicago (ex.9) runs as the bonus level, as “the file on the happiest city in the world”.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Interrogation room. A city is on trial and you are the investigators. Twenty questions, five seconds each. Is there a skate park? How many libraries are there? How much traffic is there? Get the form wrong and the question burns — the city walks free. Two traps, and I'm telling you honestly: things you can count and things you can't, and where 'are there' goes in the sentence. Then the final file: the happiest city in the world. It's not the one you think. Begin.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: the interrogation of a city: twenty questions, 5 seconds each. A broken question burns together with the turn. Two traps — what counts and what doesn't, and where “are there” goes. At the end — the file on the happiest city in the world.

Challenge: much or many in 5 seconds
🔥 Challenge · much or many
crews

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 rebuild the questions from the answers → ex.5 they work out the much/many rule themselves → ex.6–7 on a conveyor belt at 5 seconds each

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 as a race: You have the answer. Build the question. Use a or any. (TB key: 2 Is there an underground in your town? No, there isn't. · 3 Are there (any) fast-food restaurants? Yes, there are. · 4 Are there any juice bars? No, there aren't. · 5 Is there any good live music? No, there isn't). TB reminder: before a vowel it is an.
  2. ex.5 through Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Two sentences in the table. Which word goes with things you can count? The crew formulates the rule and checks that everyone can say it — then I call a number and that student says it out loud (TB key: 1 uncountable · 2 countable).
  3. ex.6 on the belt, 5 seconds each (TB key: 1 How many · 2 How much · 3 How much · 4 How many · 5 How many) — and we answer about our own town straight away.
  4. ex.7: five questions built from the words, the auxiliary verb is compulsory (TB key: 1 How many brothers and sisters have you got? · 2 How much homework do you do at weekends? · 3 How much time do you spend watching TV? · 4 How many comics or magazines do you read? · 5 How much fruit do you eat every day?).
  5. Constraint: 5 seconds per turn; a form error and the turn burns and passes to the next crew.
📺 On screen: SB p.66 ex.4–7 · 5-second timer
👦 Students do: they assemble the questions on the belt. Error: “How much time do you spend to watch TV” — CCQ “after spend it is -ing”

WHAT WE DO: an anonymous look at 5 questions that burnt during the pair

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Burnt questions board. Five real ones from the last thirty minutes. Fix them — five seconds each.
  2. Every question through Kagan Think-Pair-Share: 5 seconds thinking silently on your own, 10 seconds checking with your neighbour, and only then the class answers — that way everyone has time to think the fix through, not only the one whose hand goes up first.
  3. Are there a library? → Is there · How much museums? → How many · How many homework? → How much · How many are there cycle lanes? → How many cycle lanes are there? · Is there some street food? → any.

PRODUCT · the city interrogation
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: the interrogation: crew against crew, 20 questions about their home town at 5 seconds each; the answers must be short; the score counts the clean questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 5 minutes to prepare: Ten questions with Is/Are there, five with How many, five with How much. Write them. Check the nouns.
  2. The interrogation: Crew A asks, crew B answers — five seconds per question, short answers only. Then swap.
  3. The judges count: +1 for a clean question · +1 for a correct short answer · 0 for one that burnt.
  4. The finale: Look at your answers. So — is OUR town a good place to live in? One sentence, with a number in it.
📺 On screen: 5-second timer · the score sheet · the rubric
👦 Students do: they interrogate each other. Expect: by question 15 the form settles by itself — the speed has done its job

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can ask and answer Is there…? / Are there…? questions with correct short answers and choose How much or How many correctly, at speed (5 seconds per question).
  • Subsidiary. ex.1 the table of questions; ex.2–4 three passes at the form; ex.5 they work out the much/many rule themselves; ex.6–8 their own questions and interviews; ex.9 Grammar round-up (Chicago); the clinic of the questions that burnt.
  • Personal. I do NOT explain the rule first — they finish the table from the examples in ex.1. The word order (are there at the end) I show by running a finger along the line, as the TB advises. Error feedback is anonymous.
By the end, students can…
  • ask Is there…? / Are there…? and give short answers
  • choose How much or How many by the noun
  • put 'is there / are there' in the right place in the question
  • ask 20 questions about a city in 100 seconds without a form error

Structure
Is there a sports centre? — Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. · Is there any street food? · Are there five football teams? — Yes, there are. / No, there aren't. · Are there any cycle lanes? · How much traffic is there? — A lot. / Some. / Not much. · How many cycle lanes are there? — A lot. / Some. / Not many.
Form
Is there … ? — with a singular countable and with an uncountable; Are there … ? — with a plural · in questions it is usually any, not some: Are there any juice bars? · How much — with uncountables (traffic, homework, time, fruit, public transport) · How many — with plural countables (libraries, skate parks, cinemas) · word order (the TB asks you to point with your finger): How many cycle lanes ARE THERE? — is there / are there go to the end of the question
Pron. watch
Is there /ɪz ðeə/ — joined up, no pause · the intonation in the question RISES: Is there a skate park? ↗ · in How much/many it falls: How many libraries are there? ↘
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Are there a library?” — why doesn't it work? → one library: Is there a library?
“How much museums are there?” — correct it. → museums are counted: How MANY museums are there?
“How many homework do you do?” → homework is not counted: How MUCH homework do you do?

Assumptions: SB p.66, KTP pair 42 (both halves are grammar). Affirmative there is/are was closed in P3; what is new is the question form, the short answers and much/many. The Grammar round-up also revises Unit 4 (comparatives, must/can).

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
