🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Form and choose the passive across seven tenses and with modals (were being given · can be used · will be saved · has been created · were inspired · should be built), decide when it is the right choice rather than a stylistic accident, and handle the two-object verbs (give, tell, offer, teach, lend, promise, send, show) which make two possible passives — Survivors will be brought food and Food will be brought to survivors. Then the fourteen prepositional verbs of the Developing vocabulary page.
What is genuinely hard
Not the concept — Russian has a passive and the class will recognise it instantly. The difficulty is entirely mechanical and threefold: the third form (was build), the missing auxiliary (the town destroyed by the flood), and choosing the tense of be when the sentence gives only a time expression. The two-object half adds a fourth: Russian prefers the thing as subject, English strongly prefers the person.
Typical error to expect
«The bridge was build in 1890», «the houses were destroy» — the passive takes the third form, always. This is the commonest single error in tasks 19–25 and it is mechanical, not conceptual: the student knows the passive and writes the infinitive. Drill the three-part chant aloud — build, built, built · destroy, destroyed, destroyed · hit, hit, hit.
«The town destroyed by the flood» — the be is not optional and Russian gives no warning that it is missing, because Russian marks the passive on the verb ending. Write both versions side by side: The flood destroyed the town / The town was destroyed by the flood.
«killed with a hurricane», «covered by ash» — by introduces the doer, with introduces the instrument or the material. The village was destroyed BY a landslide; the streets were covered WITH debris.
Why the lesson is built this way
Guided discovery, not presentation. The class has just read a text full of passives (page 97), so the warmer sends them back into it to count seven — the rule is derived from a text they already understand. Controlled practice is exercise 2, where every item is decided by a time expression, so the sub-skill being trained is tense choice rather than participle formation. Then the Culture exchange text about California, where they hunt ten errors — error correction is the fastest possible diagnostic and the most engaging form of controlled practice at B2. The second half of the lesson is a different skill entirely (prepositional verbs), which is why it is separated by a stirrer.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
Read any news report about a disaster and count how many sentences tell you who did it. Almost none. «Three thousand buildings were destroyed.» By what? By whom? The report does not say, because nobody cares — the buildings are the story. That is what the passive is for, and today you get all of it: seven tenses, two modals, and the eight verbs that can be turned inside out two different ways.

WHAT WE DO: greeting at the door, then a passive hunt in yesterday's reading text

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — the last time the weather changed your plans. Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — rewind straight into the grammar. Books open at page 97, the paragraph headed Emergency lighting. Ninety seconds. Find and count every passive in this paragraph. Hands up with a number.
  3. There are seven: has been created · they were inspired · food and other supplies were being given out · nothing was being done · easy to be distributed · the lights have been distributed · is given. Take the count, then read them out.
  4. One question only, and do not answer it yet: Why is this paragraph written like that? Hold the thought.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Yesterday's text was full of it and you read straight past it. Today we take it apart. Page 98.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.98 exercises 1a and 1b — the rule derived from five sentences, not announced

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Exercise 1a. Five sentences on the page, four questions under them. Pairs, four minutes. Do not pre-teach anything.
  2. Take the answers and build the form on the board as they give it: a yes, all five are passive · b 1 past continuous, 2 modal can, 3 future with will, 4 present perfect, 5 past simple · c subject + be + past participle, and with a modal or will: subject + modal + be + past participle · d by introduces the agent — the person or thing that does the action
  3. Write the two shapes big and leave them up for the whole lesson: subject + BE + past participle and subject + modal/will + BE + past participle.
  4. Exercise 1b, three True/False statements about when we use it. Before they answer, put the Teacher's Book's three sentences on the board to think with: The film Avengers: Endgame was released in 2019. · I've been robbed! · There has been an earthquake. Many houses have been destroyed.
  5. Answers: 1 True · 2 True · 3 False (we use the passive when it is NOT important who did it). Then answer the question you left hanging in the warmer: The lighting paragraph is passive because the story is the lamps, not the people who moved them.
📺 On screen: the two form-shapes written large on the board and left up. Backup: no technology needed at any point in this stage.

Controlled practice · the tense is in the time expression
desks → pairs
Why: Isolating tense choice as the sub-skill is what turns a passive exercise from mechanical into diagnostic, and passive question forms are the thing no gap-fill ever practises.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.98 exercise 2 — seven items where the difficulty is choosing the tense, not forming the participle

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Demonstrate the sub-skill first, exactly as the Teacher's Book does. Write on the board: It's possible that more countries ______ (affect) by heatwaves in the next decade. Elicit will be affected, then ask what gave it away — in the next decade. Underline it.
  2. Every item on this page works the same way. Find the time expression FIRST, underline it, then choose the tense. Do not start with the verb.
  3. With a less confident group, read all seven aloud and elicit only the time expressions before anybody writes. That takes ninety seconds and roughly halves the error rate.
  4. Seven minutes, individually, then compare in pairs before whole-class check.
  5. Answers: 1 were cancelled · 2 is measured · 3 be built · 4 was hit · 5 will be controlled · 6 be seen · 7 have been destroyed.
  6. Extra activity, two minutes (Teacher's Book): pairs write the question for three of the seven — How is the strength of hurricane winds measured? · Which country was hit by a terrible earthquake in 2017? · What could be seen from space in 2019? Passive questions are where the auxiliary goes missing, so this is worth the two minutes.
📺 On screen: the demonstration item and its underlined time expression on the board. Backup: dictate the demonstration sentence.
👦 Students do: underline the time expression before choosing a tense, compare in pairs, then build three passive questions.

WHAT WE DO: four minutes on the one error that costs the most marks, done on their feet

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Everybody stands. You say the infinitive, the class says all three forms at speed, twice: destroy — destroyed — destroyed · build — built — built · hit — hit — hit · sweep — swept — swept · put — put — put · give — gave — given · take — took — taken · find — found — found.
  2. Then the game: you say a sentence with the wrong form — The bridge was build in 1890 — and the class shouts the correction and sits down. Last person standing does the next one.
  3. Six or seven rounds, no more. It is loud, it takes four minutes, and it is the single highest-value drill in the unit.
📺 On screen: nothing to show. Backup: voice only.
👦 Students do: chant three-part verb forms standing up, then correct wrong passives out loud and sit down.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.98 exercise 3 — error correction as controlled practice, with a real text behind it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed for twenty seconds. Write Natural disasters in California on the board: What kinds do you think are a real risk there? Take guesses.
  2. Books open, thirty seconds' skim to confirm: wildfires, droughts, landslides and earthquakes.
  3. Now the task: Ten of the verb forms in this text are wrong. Find them and correct them. In pairs. Eight minutes.
  4. Mixed ability, straight from the Teacher's Book: less confident pairs do the first paragraph only, which contains four of the ten. More confident pairs finish and then turn three of the corrected passives back into actives.
  5. Check on the board, one at a time, and make the class say why: the ten mistakes in «Natural disasters in California»: 1 terrible wildfires hit large parts of the state (not were hit) · 2 the fires were made worse by strong winds (correct as printed — leave it) · 3 more fires have been / are being caused · 4 twelve times as much is spent / is being spent · 5 landslides have sometimes been caused · 6 California has often been hit by earthquakes · 7 was felt by 30 million people · 8 nobody died (not was died) · 9 the buildings are usually constructed · 10 an earthquake … will happen one day (not will be happen)
  6. Two of the ten are worth an extra thirty seconds each, because they are the errors this group makes in their own writing: nobody was died (die is intransitive — it has no passive at all) and will be happen (same reason).
  7. Leave the culture facts on the board — they will come back in lesson 4: twelve times as much is spent on fighting wildfires today as in 1985 · the wildfire season is two and a half months longer than in the early 1970s.
📺 On screen: the text's ten corrections written up as they are agreed. Backup: the text is in the students' own book; nothing to project.
👦 Students do: correct ten verb forms in pairs and justify each correction; stronger pairs convert three back to the active.

Verbs with two objects · two passives from one sentence
desks → pairs
Why: This is the half of the passive Russian speakers get wrong by default, because Russian prefers the thing as subject. Making the person-subject version compulsory in exercise 5 forces the English preference.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.98 exercises 4a–4b and p.99 exercise 5 — the half of the passive that Russian gets backwards

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Exercise 4a: three sentences on the page. They give a free light to those people → A free light is given to those people / Those people are given a free light. One question: which is more common? 4a it is more common to use the PERSON as the subject · 4b the twelve verbs that take two objects: bring, buy, lend, offer, owe, pay, promise, refuse, send, show, teach, tell. Ms Jones teaches us maths → We are taught maths by Ms Jones (person as subject) or Maths is taught to us by Ms Jones (thing as subject)
  2. Do the Teacher's Book board work, because it is the clearest possible model: Ms Jones teaches us maths. Theo has lent me £10. Elicit both passives of each. Then the second version with the thing as subject: Maths is taught to us by Ms Jones. £10 has been lent to me by Theo. Note the to appearing.
  3. Exercise 4b: the twelve verbs — bring, buy, lend, offer, owe, pay, promise, refuse, send, show, teach, tell. Check meanings fast; owe and refuse are the two that need it.
  4. Exercise 5, page 99, eight sentences about NGOs, person as subject. Say the constraint before they start: The subject of your passive sentence must be the PERSON affected, not the thing. Pairs, six minutes.
  5. Answers: the eight NGO sentences, person as subject: 1 First responders are given immediate support by many NGOs · 2 Millions of people have been taught lifesaving skills · 3 Disaster victims have often been offered shelter by NGOs · 4 Survivors will be brought food · 5 Young people are told general safety information · 6 People in need are never refused medical help · 7 Evacuees are promised as much help as possible · 8 Volunteers are always being given training.
  6. One culture note, ten seconds, because they will ask about the photograph: The Red Cross and Red Crescent was set up in Switzerland in 1863, has won the Nobel Peace Prize three times and has over ninety-five million volunteers.
📺 On screen: the two worked examples (Ms Jones, Theo) on the board. Backup: dictate them.
👦 Students do: produce both passives of two board sentences, then rewrite eight NGO sentences with the person as subject.

Why: The first genuinely free use of the form, and the follow-up rule stops it being a survey. Reporting in the third person at the end recycles both the passive and Unit 7's reported speech.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.99 exercise 6 — the passive used to ask real questions of real people

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before anything, board the short answers, because this is where it breaks: Have you ever been given a lot of money? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. Not «yes, I was given».
  2. Exercise 6, eight questions on the page (Have you ever been given a prize? … bought a special gift? … taught a special skill by somebody in your family? … offered a job or special responsibility? … sent an interesting or surprise package? … told an important secret? … paid for doing something? … owed money or a favour?).
  3. Mix-Pair-Share, five minutes: mingle, ask one question, and — this is the rule that makes it a conversation — when the answer is yes, you must ask one follow-up question before you move on.
  4. Sit them down and take three reports in the third person: Tell us about somebody else. That produces the passive again, in report form: Artem was taught to ski by his grandfather.
📺 On screen: the two short-answer forms on the board. Backup: none needed — spoken stage.
👦 Students do: mingle, ask eight passive questions of different partners, ask a follow-up on every yes, then report on somebody else.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.99 exercises 1–3 — a different lexical system, taught after a stirring speaking stage

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The Teacher's Book warmer, thirty seconds, books closed: I'm looking ___ my mobile phone. I can't find it anywhere! Elicit for, name it as a preposition, frame the stage.
  2. Exercise 1, the text A FALSE TSUNAMI ALERT IN NEW YORK CITY, fourteen gaps a–n, each governed by the verb in bold before it. Look at the photograph first: What is happening? Do you think this really happened? (It did not — it was a false alert issued by the US National Weather Service.)
  3. Mixed ability, from the Teacher's Book: for a less confident group, write the pool of prepositions on the board first — about (×2), against, for (×2), in (×2), of, on (×3), to, with. A stronger group covers the text, predicts the preposition for each bold verb from memory, and only then reads.
  4. Pairs, seven minutes. Answers: «A false tsunami alert in New York City»: a on · b from / against · c to · d for · e for · f about · g with · h to · i in · j about · k on · l on · m of · n in.
  5. Two language notes worth writing up, both from the Teacher's Book: dream ABOUT = a dream you had while asleep, dream OF = something you want; arrive IN a city or country, arrive AT a building.
  6. Exercise 2, four minutes: ten sentence stems completed truthfully about themselves (I occasionally spend money … · I hate waiting … · One day I dream … · I know I can rely …). Point out before they write that a preposition can be followed by a noun or a gerund: Dan complained about the food. Mia complained about having to get up early.
  7. Exercise 3, three minutes, threes: compare sentences and find any you have in common. One member of each three reports the coincidences.
📺 On screen: the preposition pool and the two language notes on the board. Backup: the text and the exercises are in the students' own book — no display dependency.
👦 Students do: predict or select fourteen prepositions in a text, then complete ten personal sentences and compare them in threes.

WHAT WE DO: a short productive task that puts both halves of the lesson into one piece of language

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Fours. You are writing the sixty-second news bulletin about a disaster — real or invented. Six sentences. At least four must be passive, and at least two must use a prepositional verb from page 99.
  2. Give the opening line so nobody stalls: Reports are coming in from …
  3. Six minutes to write, in fours, one scribe but everybody dictating.
  4. Then each group reads its bulletin aloud in a newsreader voice while the other groups count the passives on their fingers. The counting keeps the listeners honest and turns the performance into a task.
  5. Take two or three errors from what you heard and fix them on the board — almost certainly a missing be or a second form.

WHAT WE DO: five minutes on the mechanics of tasks 19–25 for the students who will sit them

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say who it is for: This is for the people taking the EGE in May. Everyone else, thirty seconds of curiosity.
  2. Write one item in the Russian paper's exact shape: The electric light bulb ______ by Thomas Edison in 1879. (INVENT)
  3. The word is given to you in capitals. You have to decide two things: is it active or passive, and what tense. That is tasks 19 to 25 — seven marks — and the passive appears in almost every text they use, because the texts are about things being built, discovered, destroyed and found.
  4. Then the warning that is worth the whole five minutes: Half the lost marks are spelling. «Was invented» is a mark. «Was invented» spelled «was invented» with one N is zero. Write it out, do not abbreviate, and check the participle.
  5. Two more on the board from today's lesson, done chorally: The houses ______ (destroy) by the flood last March · The new warning system ______ (install) next week.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class

Structure
Supplies were being given out to the victims. · The drone can be used by relief workers. · Many houses have been destroyed. · Survivors will be brought food. · Have you ever been given a prize? · It doesn't belong to me — I'm waiting for the owner.
Form
subject + be + past participle; with a modal or will, subject + modal + be + past participle · by introduces the agent and is only included when it adds information; with introduces the instrument · questions put the first auxiliary before the subject: Is the news being given on TV? · intransitive verbs (die, happen, arrive) have no passive at all · two-object verbs give two passives, and English prefers the person as subject · a preposition is followed by a noun or a gerund, never an infinitive.
Pron. watch
the passive lives or dies on the auxiliary, and the auxiliary is unstressed. was destroyed → /wəzdɪˈstrɔɪd/, have been given → /əvbɪnˈɡɪvn/, are being built → /əbiːɪŋˈbɪlt/. Students who expect three clear words hear none of them and then leave the auxiliary out of their own writing. Drill whole sentences at speed, then slowly, then at speed again
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
the passive: «The village was destroyed» — do we know who or what destroyed it? — Not from this sentence. Is that a problem? — No, because the village is the news.
by vs with: «The street was covered with debris» — is the debris the doer? — No, it's the stuff. And «the town was hit by a hurricane» — is the hurricane the doer? — Yes.
two objects: «Survivors will be brought food» — who gets the food? — The survivors. Can I also say «Food will be brought to survivors»? — Yes. Which sounds more natural in English? — The first.
ICQ before exercise 2: What do you underline first — the verb or the time expression? Do you write the participle before or after you choose the tense?

Assumptions: Students met the passive at B1 in the present and past simple and will recognise it immediately. Perfect and continuous passives, modal passives and the two-object passive are new as production. The prepositional verbs are partly known and wholly unstable.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Everything becomes passive for the rest of the unit.Name the danger in exercise 1b and again in the news bulletin: the passive is a choice, and a text that is all passive is bad English. The bulletin task asks for four passives out of six sentences precisely so that two are not.
Exercise 3 (ten errors) takes twenty minutes.Split it: less confident pairs do paragraph one only (four of the ten), stronger pairs do all ten and convert three. Check all ten on the board regardless — the checking is the teaching.
«Nobody was died» is corrected but not understood.Give it thirty seconds explicitly: die, happen, arrive, fall take no object, so there is nothing to move to the front, so there is no passive. Write *was happened crossed out on the board and leave it there.
The prepositional verbs get four minutes because the passive overran.Then cut exercise 2 to five stems instead of ten and keep exercise 1 whole. The text is where the learning is; the personalisation can be finished in the Workbook, which sets exactly these on page 69.
Students write «is arrived to Moscow».The two language notes go on the board before exercise 1, not after: arrive in a country or city, arrive at a building, and arrive has no passive.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–25 (grammar in a text — the passive is a named line of the codifier) and tasks 32–36.

The passive is the single most reliable grammar earner on the Russian paper because it is testable in a text: BUILD → was built, DESTROY → have been destroyed. Two things lose the marks and both are mechanical — the wrong participle (was build) and the wrong auxiliary tense. The prepositional verbs in the second half of the lesson are tasks 32–36 in their purest form: four real prepositions, one collocation.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
