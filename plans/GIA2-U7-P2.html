🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The newspaper article is not read “because it was set” but as newsroom material: the crew produces a 60-second news bulletin and has to back every claim with a line from the text. The unit subskill (understanding new words) is sewn into the rule: an unknown word is not translated — it is worked out from context and the guess is defended. The Critical thinking from the book (“can we all be heroes”) becomes the end of the bulletin, not an add-on.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Newsroom, deadline in one hour. Texas. Storm Harvey. The streets are rivers, there's no water, no electricity. Thirty thousand people left — the rest stayed. And four teenagers went out in a small fishing boat. Fifty people. Even more pets. Your crew runs the story: read the article, build the bulletin — what happened, who, how, and the line that makes it a story. Sixty seconds on air. Every claim needs a LINE from the text. No line, no point.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the newsroom produces a story about Storm Harvey and the four teenagers with a boat: read the article, build a 60-second bulletin — what happened, who, how. Every claim is backed by a line from the text, or it does not count.

WHAT WE DO: ex.5 six sentence completions against the clock + Word work ex.6–8: guessing from context, 8 seconds to defend

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5: Six sentences, six lines in the text. Four minutes. (TB key: 1 … were under water · 2 … his car was under water, too · 3 … calls for help · 4 … alone · 5 … police officers and rescue services · 6 … took photos of the boys and posted them on social media).
  2. Word work through the subskill algorithm: Don't translate. What TYPE of word is it? Is it positive or negative? What fits? (TB key ex.6: electricity — noun, brave — adjective, citizens — plural noun, safe — adjective, neighbours — plural noun, rescue services — plural noun)
  3. A duel of guesses through Kagan RallyRobin, 8 seconds a turn: strictly in turns inside the pair — one takes a word, gives the guess and the line of evidence, the other takes the next word; six words, three defended each. Your guess and your evidence — go! The constraint: say “I don't know” and your turn burns.
  4. Check ex.7 and ex.8 (TB key: ex.7 — 1 safe · 2 citizens · 3 brave · 4 rescue services · 5 neighbours · 6 electricity; ex.8 — 1 electricity · 2 safe · 3 brave · 4 neighbours · 5 citizens · 6 rescue services).
📺 On screen: SB p.86 ex.5–8 · 8-second timer (Backup: write the six words large on the board and have students come up and defend their guess at the board)
👦 Students do: students duel with guesses, then check against the key. Expect: they read citizens through the Russian word “tsitadel” (citadel) — catch the false friend

WHAT WE DO: a second listening of track 45 + Critical thinking 1: what exactly made them heroes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

PRODUCT · the 60-second bulletin
crews → stage

WHAT WE DO: a 60-second bulletin: what happened · who · how · the number · the quote — plus a question from the floor that demands a line

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 7 minutes of preparation: Keywords only. Everyone owns one block: what happened · who · how · the number · the quote.
  2. The newsroom rule: I will ask 'Where is that in the text?' If you can't point, the claim is cut.
  3. Sixty seconds per crew. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness — cards up!
  4. The end of the bulletin — Critical thinking 2: Can we all be heroes in daily life? At school, in free time, in the holidays — give ONE real example.
📺 On screen: 60-second timer · judges' cards · the rubric (Backup: ask “Where is that in the text?” yourself, pointing at the book)
👦 Students do: students perform in their roles and point at the lines. Expect: the bar holds the format. Typical problem: “I think they was heroes” — the judges take accuracy off

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can find and prove specific information in a newspaper article, guess six new words from context and deliver a 60-second bulletin with an evidence line behind every claim.
  • Subsidiary. ex.3 choosing a headline out of three; ex.4 (track 45) six questions; ex.5 six sentence completions; Word work electricity · brave · citizens · safe · neighbours · rescue services; Critical thinking 1–3; the Culture note on Harvey.
  • Personal. The strong readers drown the weaker ones — hold the rule “an answer only counts with a line of evidence”. Do not analyse the past tense in the text: collect the examples for P3.
By the end, students can…
  • choose the right headline for a text
  • answer six comprehension questions and point at the proof line
  • guess a new word from context and say what type of word it is
  • deliver a 60-second news bulletin with evidence

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“brave” — what type of word is it? → an adjective: it describes a person.
“rescue services” — are they people or organisations? → organisations that help in dangerous situations.
“An answer with no line from the text” — does it count? → no, that is a guess, not reading.

Assumptions: SB pp.86–87, syllabus pair 55. The jobs from P1 are active. The storm topic can be sensitive — keep the focus on what people did, not on the destruction.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They read the whole thing and drownThe newsroom rule: underline the key words in the question first, then scan. The first read is for the headline only, 3 minutes, on the timer.
An unknown word → a hand up, “what's this?”Don't translate. Hand over the subskill algorithm: the meaning of the sentence → the neighbouring words → the type of word → positive or negative. The guess has to be defended aloud.
The bulletin turns into a retellingThe bulletin format: what happened · who · how · the number · one quote. Five blocks, 60 seconds, the judges count.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
