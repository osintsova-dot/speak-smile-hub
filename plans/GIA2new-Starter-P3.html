🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The third part of the card is the easiest one language-wise and the nicest one in its effect: “I can” works for everybody, and it is a good place for new students to feel success in English in front of strangers for the first time. So there is a bit more movement here (mime, Find Someone Who) and a bit less writing. The second half of the pair solves a separate technical problem that is critical for the “new students” track: teaching them to find everything in the book. For the “ours” track that is a speed game; for new students it is a survival question for the whole year, so we go through p.11 slowly and with the books open.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Part three: what you CAN do. Everyone in this room can do something the others can't — and today we find out what. Careful with one thing: 'I can swim' is fine, but 'I can swim quite well' tells us much more. And the second job today is boring but it saves you all year: we learn where everything lives in this book. Ten seconds to find anything.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Part three of the card: what I can do. Plus, separately, we learn to find everything in the book — boring, but it saves your nerves all year.

WHAT WE DO: mime → p.10 ex.1 verbs ↔ pictures → collocations on the board → audio 6

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Mime on your feet through Kagan Kinesthetic Symbols. The model first: I do the gesture and say the verb myself — through all ten, and they only copy the movement. The second run is already the structure: I do the gesture — they say the verb in chorus; the third — I say the verb and they do the gesture. cook · dance · draw · play · ride · run · sing · speak · spell · swim. The gesture stays attached to the word until the end of the pair: can't remember the word — do the gesture.
  2. ex.1 in pairs off the pictures: key 1 draw · 2 dance · 3 cook · 4 swim · 5 ride · 6 play · 7 sing · 8 speak · 9 spell · 10 run.
  3. The collocations go on the board (the support for the whole lesson, we have no cards for these words): ride a bike · play the guitar · speak Chinese · run fast · draw a picture · cook dinner · spell your name · swim 50 metres.
  4. Audio 6, twice: Six recordings. Which verb — and can they do it or not? Key: 1 sing – yes · 2 play the guitar – yes · 3 speak Chinese – yes (the first speaker), no (the second) · 4 swim – yes · 5 spell – no · 6 dance – yes.
  5. We add to the mini-glossary: 10 verbs + a collocation for each.
📺 On screen: SB p.10 ex.1–2 · audio 6 · the list of verbs and collocations on the board (backup with no sound: I act out the six scenes myself — I sing, I “play the guitar”, I “swim”)
👦 Students do: they mime and guess, then listen. Expect: mime brings even the quietest ones in. Problem: “play guitar” with no the — the collocation on the board fixes it

Grammar: can — I can / I can't
Grammar: can
board → solo

WHAT WE DO: I name the topic openly → model → ex.3 table → ex.4 in writing → mini-test with self-check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I announce it: Grammar today: 'can'. Three easy rules. The three rules go on the board: the same for every person (he can, not “he cans”) · a bare verb after can (no to) · the question has no do (Can you…?, not “Do you can…?”).
  2. ex.3 — we build the table together out of the four sentences: key 1 d (She can swim) · 2 a (He can't spell) · 3 b (Can they cook?) · 4 c (Yes, they can).
  3. Pronunciation as a separate run: 'I can /kən/ swim' — weak and short. 'Yes, I CAN /kæn/' — strong. Say both after me.
  4. ex.4 IN WRITING, on the frame. The Teacher's Book allows the less confident ones to work in pairs — I allow it. Key: 1 She can sing · 2 He can't play the guitar · 3 Can she speak Chinese? · 4 He can swim · 5 They can't spell · 6 Can you dance?
  5. A 6-line mini-test → self-check against the key → sticker.
  6. 🟢🟡🔴 Self-check stickers: after every mini-test the student sticks a sticker in the margin themselves: 🟢 got it · 🟡 so-so · 🔴 didn't get it. This is NOT a mark — it is my map of who to go to during the next task. I say this out loud every single time.
📺 On screen: SB p.10 ex.3–4 · the three rules and the frame on the board · slide with the mini-test key (Backup: I read the keys out and write the tricky ones on the board)
👦 Students do: they build the table, write on the frame, check themselves. Expect: can comes more easily than any other grammar in Starter — this is where new students catch a taste of success. Problem: “Do you can…?” — the rule is up there on the board

WHAT WE DO: p.11: ex.1–3 in pairs with the books open → ex.4 Classroom challenge at a calm pace

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. This book is your map for the year. If you can't find things, every lesson is harder than it needs to be.
  2. ex.1 in pairs (the Teacher's Book specifically suggests pairs for the less confident). Key: Quick review — at the end of the unit on the Writing spread before the project · there are eight projects · irregular verbs pp134–135 · the graphic organiser pp130–133.
  3. ex.2 through Kagan RallyCoach, one or two questions per pair. I take the first question myself, out loud, as the model: I read it, look in the contents, show the page. Then in the pair: the first one searches and says out loud where they found it, the second is the coach, checks that the page really is open, and praises; on the next question they swap. I show the contents page on the board and explain how to use it.
  4. ex.3 photos ↔ units together: key 1 Unit 7 · 2 Unit 6 · 3 Unit 4 · 4 Unit 8 · 5 Unit 2 · 6 Unit 3 · 7 Unit 6 · 8 Unit 1.
  5. ex.4 Classroom challenge — at a calm pace, not a race: an answer only counts with the page open in their hands (as the Teacher's Book says).
  6. The hook into Unit 1: Question one: in which unit do you plan an ideal school timetable? Unit One. That's where we go next week.

PRODUCT · my card: what I can do
solo → crews

WHAT WE DO: the product: 5 lines of abilities with qualifiers → read it in the crew → Test yourself p.10 ex.6 in crews

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 6 minutes of writing on the frame: Five lines. Three 'I can…', two 'I can't…'. Every line needs a qualifier: quite well, a bit, not very well, not at all.
  2. Checklist on the desk: can with no to? · is there a qualifier? · is the collocation complete (play THE guitar)?
  3. Kagan Timed Pair Share: 40 seconds reading to a partner → swap. With the sheet.
  4. Crew circle: everyone reads their five lines to their own crew; the speaker takes one surprising line out to the class.
  5. The finale — SB p.10 ex.6 “Test yourself!” in crews, for points: six micro-tasks (6 colours · 10 animals · 3 objects starting with “c” · counting 20→1 · spelling the teacher's name · the days of the week). One point for each, and the points go to the crew.
📺 On screen: the card frame (5 lines) · the 3-point checklist · SB p.10 ex.6 · timer
👦 Students do: they write, read in the crew, race through Test yourself. Expect: Test yourself gives everyone the feeling “I know a lot” — the perfect finish for new students. Problem: lines with no qualifier — the checklist catches them

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write and say 5 lines about their abilities with can / can't and a qualifier (quite well · a bit · not very well), and ask and answer Can-questions with correct short answers.
  • Subsidiary. 10 action verbs through mime (SB p.10 ex.1) and audio 6; the can table built from a model (ex.3); Find Someone Who as their first experience of moving freely round the room; navigating the book (SB p.11) — with the books open, not against the clock.
  • Personal. This is the first time I let them move round the room freely (Find Someone Who) — I announce the movement rules BEFORE, otherwise it turns into chaos. And I watch that nobody is left without a line: everyone must have at least one ability their neighbour doesn't have.
By the end, students can…
  • name 10 action verbs with their collocations
  • make can / can't sentences and questions with short answers
  • add a qualifier: quite well · a bit · not very well
  • find a page or a feature in the Student's Book

Structure
I can draw. · She can swim. · They can speak Chinese. · I can't sing. · He can't spell. · They can't speak French. · Can you play the guitar? — Yes, I can. / No, I can't. · Can he sing? — Yes, he can. / No, he can't. · Can they cook? — Yes, they can.
Form
can is the same for every person: he can (not «he cans») · after can comes the bare verb, no to: I can swim (not «I can to swim») · the question needs no do/does: Can you swim? (not «Do you can swim?»)
Pron. watch
can inside a sentence is weak /kən/, in a short answer it is full /kæn/: “I can /kən/ swim” but “Yes, I CAN /kæn/” · can't /kɑːnt/ — a long “ah” · verbs: draw /drɔː/ · swim /swɪm/ · dance /dɑːns/ · spell /spel/
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«He cans swim» — what's the extra bit? → the -s: can doesn't change with person.
«Do you can dance?» — why not? → can asks its own question: Can you dance?
«I can to cook» — what do we drop? → to: after can the verb is bare.
“I can play guitar” — what's missing? → the: play THE guitar.

Assumptions: SB pp.10–11, KTP pair 3 (lessons 5–6). can is familiar to almost everyone — this is the most accessible pair in Starter, and it is worth using it to give new students a feeling of success. ⚠️ There are NO flashcards for the action verbs in our set — we work with mime and the pictures in the book.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
There are no cards for the action verbs — nothing to lean onThe support here is different: mime + the pictures in SB p.10 ex.1 + the list of verbs on the board, which stays up the whole lesson. Knowing this in advance matters, so you don't go hunting for cards before the lesson.
Find Someone Who turns into chaosI announce the movement rules BEFORE: we stand up on the signal · one name per person · on the “freeze” signal everyone stops dead. I show the signal in advance and rehearse it once.
“I can't do anything” — the shy student's self-defenceI widen the frame to everyday things: Can you cook eggs? Can you ride a bike? Can you spell your surname? Everyone finds at least three lines.
Navigating the book is boring and falls flatAs the Teacher's Book says, an answer only counts with the page OPEN in their hands — the book gets remembered physically. And we go slowly, in pairs, not as a race.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
