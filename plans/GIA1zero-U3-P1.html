WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin on the rewind: I meet them at the door; a blitz rewind of U2 (have got + family); speed-spelling; a bridge into the topic of food

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door: Hi! One word — how are you? Finger mood-check 1–5.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. U2 rewind in a frame: I've got a … in my family. Sentence chain — go! 5–6 people in a chain.
  4. Spelling in the air: Spell MUM in the air — together!
  5. Bridge: Families eat together… Today we learn to talk about FOOD — and build the ideal meal by the end of the unit.
📺 On screen: mood slide (or smileys on the board) · the have got frame (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they answer at the door, pull the have got chain, spell in chorus. Expected: warm-up + a U2 rewind woven in. Typical error: “I has got” — echo question “I…?”, they fix it themselves.

WHAT WE DO: Quiz-Quiz-Trade: input of the set: 12 words ex.5 with photos + gestures + a hunt for silent letters; teams match against time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.5 whole-class with gestures: I say a word — the class repeats it ×2 with a gesture (soup — “a spoon”, tea — “a cup”, peas — “little peas with your fingers”…).
  2. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the unit's printed cards (handout GIA1-QQT: the question on top, the answer on the back after folding; no printout — the kids write their own cards in 2 min).
  3. Command: Stand up, hand up, pair up! Quiz your partner — praise — TRADE cards — find a new partner! 3–4 swaps, EVERYBODY asks at the same time.
  4. Hunt for the invisible ones: Secret silent letters! Find them: juice… biscuit… sandwich! — we underline them in yellow (i · u · d). +1 to whoever finds one.
  5. TB pronunciation: CHOC-late — two beats! VEG-ta-bles — three beats! We clap the syllables.
  6. Teams: match 12 photos↔words (ex.5) against time — 3 min, the key on a slide (1 chicken · 2 rice · 3 oil · 4 biscuit · 5 soup · 6 apple · 7 juice · 8 tea · 9 meat · 10 peas · 11 orange · 12 sandwich).
  7. TB language box: Chicken = the animal AND the meat. Fish too!
📺 On screen: the SB p.37 · board: words with yellow silent letters (stay up till the end) · the key
👦 Students do: they repeat with gestures, hunt for silent letters, match against time. Expected: the gestures hold their attention; the peas gesture gets a laugh and sticks. Typical error: orange as a colour — “fruit AND colour, English's favourite 2-in-1”.
💪 Support (TB Reinforcement): for a weaker team — physically lay the word cards onto the photos. ⭐ Stretch: +4 words from ex.2 (bread·cheese·coffee·water) onto their own list.		SB p.37 · word cards · the unit's QQT cards (handouts/GIA1-QQT-U3.pdf)

50–60′
First a/an/some — in frames, no theory
Try it · a/an/some in frames
standing circle

WHAT WE DO: a speaking circle with a ball: catch a food card — “This is a banana! / This is some bread!” from the frame, the class echoes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Circle. Frames on the board: This is a ___ . / This is an ___ . / This is some ___ .
  2. I model slowly ×2: This is a biscuit. This is some bread. WITHOUT a rule — only “the little music of the word”: Listen: A banana. SOME water. Feel it?
  3. I throw the ball + a food card; a phrase from the frame → the class echoes. I keep a cheat-sheet: some for bread/cheese/water/rice/soup/juice/tea/meat.
  4. Everybody catches it twice; I correct ONLY with the hint “a? an? some?” and a gesture at the frames.
📺 On screen: board: 3 big frames · food cards · a soft ball
👦 Students do: they catch the ball, speak from the frame, the class echoes. Expected: the intuition for some is built before any theory (that's the plan: the system comes in P3). Typical error: “a bread” — I point at frame 3, the echo fixes it.

WHAT WE DO: they write 4 sentences about their own food from the frames; the first peer check by 2 boxes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Model on the board: My favourite food is chicken. I eat rice and peas. I drink juice. I don't eat fish.
  2. I wipe everything except the frames: My favourite food is ___ . I eat ___ and ___ . I drink ___ . I don't eat ___ .
  3. Four sentences about YOU. Five minutes. Timer.
  4. Our style peer review: Swap notebooks. TWO boxes only: ☐ spelling from the board? ☐ don't in the negative? Tick or underline — be kind, be exact.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can name 15+ food and drink items and say simple true sentences about food: “I eat rice. I don't eat meat. My favourite food is chicken.”
  • Subsidiary. Meet the unit question WDYT? What's the ideal meal?; silent letters noticed (SB p.116: juice, biscuit, sandwich); first contextual contact with a/an/some (no theory — system lesson = P3); Video skills (Bokang) with an active visual task.
  • Personal. Hold the unit's routines: check-in, teams of 3, the points board, the timer, English-first. Instructions ≤ 2 sentences + ICQ. Don't talk down to them: they're “teams”, not “little kids”. New vocabulary always comes with a picture and a gesture.
By the end, students can…
  • name 15+ food/drink items with pictures
  • guess a food from a definition
  • say 3 true sentences about their food

Structure
I eat/drink… · I don't eat… · My favourite food is… · This is a banana. / This is some bread. (in frames, no rule)
Form
plurals: -es after -s/-sh/-ch/-x + tomatoes/potatoes (SB p.36 box); food often has no article: I eat rice.
Pron. watch
silent letters: ju(i)ce · bisc(u)it · sandwich /ˈsænwɪdʒ/ · me(a)t; chocolate — 2 syllables; vegetables — 3 syllables (TB).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is chicken only the animal?” → No: the meat too (TB language box: chicken/fish = animal + meat).
“Two potato?” → potatoES — the -es club (and tomatoes).
“Do we spell juice the way we hear it?” → No — the silent i: j-u-i-c-e.

Assumptions: After Starter and U1–2: be, have got, classroom English in the routines. Some food words are familiar from life (pizza, chocolate) — but as a SYSTEM the set is new; spelling and silent letters are new for everyone. We do NOT explain C/UC today.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
21 words is a lot to take in at onceWe input all of them, BUT the active bar = 15; the cards stay on the desks until the end of the lesson; weaker students may peek.
Silent letters scare themWe present them as “secret invisible letters” — a hunt for them as a game: find the invisible one = +1.
The video is fast for beginnersThe task is visual (bingo “what do you see”), not verbal; 2 viewings; success = noticing, not understanding everything.
Teams argue over pointsSlide rule number one: “Judge's decision is final. Protest = −1.” We apply it once, with a smile.
Answers in RussianAccept the idea, recast it in English, the team repeats the English version for a point.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
