🎯 GATE · which happened first?
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 5 sentences with before/after → write which event happened FIRST; key and private scoring

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Slide: 1 Before we arrived at the gate, we heard a message. 2 After we arrived at the station, my dad noticed his passport. 3 We had our first problem before we left home. 4 After I had a piano lesson, I did my homework. 5 Before we went to Sicily, we visited Rome.
  2. Which event happened FIRST? Write A or B. Two minutes. Sounds easy — it isn't.
  3. Key: 1 the message · 2 arrived at the station · 3 the problem · 4 the piano lesson · 5 Rome.
  4. Reveal: Half the class read 'before' as 'first'. It's the opposite — and the test loves this trap.
📺 On screen: gate slide + key (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)

The story model: the paragraph map
Model story · we go over it like editors
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share: ex.1–3: the competition announcement + the story model (Palma de Mallorca) + the paragraph map → ex.4 order of events as a race

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1, 60 sec in pairs: What do they want you to write about? What can you win? (best/worst journey; a helicopter ride for two).
  2. Format — Kagan Timed Pair Share: A speaks for their time against the timer, B is the active listener (counts pauses on their fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. Ex.2: Best or worst? → worst journey. Challenge (TB p.118): name the causes — how many problems? (four: the alarm clock, the passport, a football match and traffic, the flight delay).
  4. Ex.3 the paragraph map by crews: 1 introduction · 2 what happened at home and the start of the journey · 3 the road to the airport · 4 what happened at the airport. Four paragraphs — that's your skeleton tonight.
  5. Ex.4 ▶ a race: put the 7 events in order (key: b g a e c d f). +1 for the right order, −0 for speed without accuracy.
  6. Drama devices (TB p.118): exclamation marks in paragraphs 2 and 4, direct speech in the finale — 'We are sorry to inform you…' — that's why the story feels real.
📺 On screen: SB p.104 · board: the 4-paragraph map (stays up) · timer
👦 Students do: they go over the model like editors, race for the order of events. Expected: the paragraph map removes the fear of “what do I write about”. Typical error: they mix up paragraphs 2 and 3 — I tie it to the setting (home → the road → the airport).

Subskill: before/after and the comma
Subskill · before / after + the comma
standing → desks

WHAT WE DO: ex.5–6 find the first event by the timeline → ex.7 joining 5 pairs of sentences; the comma rule

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Human timeline: five come out with event cards from the model and STAND in a line by time; the class calls out who moves where. A living time arrow instead of a diagram on the board (TB p.119 draws it in chalk — we do it with bodies).
  2. I transfer the line to the board: ← past … present →, I place two events from ex.5. Look at the LINE, not the word order.
  3. Ex.5 key: 1 b · 2 a. Ex.6: we find one more example each in the text (We had our first problem BEFORE we left home → first = the problem; AFTER we got Dad's passport → first = got the passport).
  4. The comma rule on the board: Before/After at the START → a comma. In the MIDDLE → no comma.
  5. Ex.7 in pairs, 4 min, 5 pairs of sentences (key: Before we took the train, we got a bus · After my friend arrived, we watched a film · Before I had breakfast, I had a shower · After I had a piano lesson, I did my homework · Before we went to Sicily, we visited Rome).
  6. Extra activity (TB p.119) in a volley: flip 2 sentences, keeping the meaning.
📺 On screen: board: timeline + comma rule (stay up) · SB pp.104–105
👦 Students do: they work with the timeline, join the sentences in pairs. Expected: after the timeline the gate mistake disappears. Typical error: a comma in the middle of the sentence — a finger on the rule.

WHAT WE DO: Skills boost THINK/PREPARE/WRITE in a portion: notes on 4 events → paragraph 1 (introduction) + paragraph 2 — to the bar

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: Find Someone Who: STEP 2: everyone personally surveys 4 people — everyone moves and talks at the same time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rule (SB STEP 2): Each person in your group asks FOUR other students. Write the answers — no memory.
  2. This is Kagan Find Someone Who: a mingle around the class with a form: A DIFFERENT classmate for every line — full question, full answer, then they sign! Finished — sit down; we check 2–3 finds out loud.
  3. ▶ Mingle survey, 6 min, music in the background; stop signal — Freeze! How many answers have you got?
  4. Challenge (TB p.120): a follow-up question to every answer — Why? How long did it take? — will come in handy for the conclusions.
  5. Reinforcement (TB p.120): weaker students — ask only ONE question out of three, but to all four.
  6. They come back to their threes: 2 min to combine the numbers.
📺 On screen: survey forms for everyone · timer · music
👦 Students do: they walk around and survey classmates, write down the answers. Expected: 12 answers per three. Typical error: they only survey friends — the rule “4 different people, at least 2 not from your own three”.

Structure
Before we arrived at the gate, we heard a message. · After we got Dad's passport, my parents decided to take a taxi. · How did you travel on holiday last year? · Travelling by car is the most popular form of transport.
Form
before/after + an event; at the start of a sentence — a COMMA between the parts; in the middle — no comma. Stories — past simple (+ negatives). Survey conclusions — comparative + superlative.
Pron. watch
expressive reading: exclamations and direct speech (TB p.118) · presenting — a voice that reaches “the back row”.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Before we arrived, we heard a message — what happened FIRST?” → the message (before shows that the second event is earlier).
“After I had breakfast, I went out — do we need a comma?” → Yes: after is at the start.
“The survey — 3 questions per group or per person?” → 3 per group, but everyone asks 4 people.

Assumptions: Past simple (all forms) and superlatives were drilled in P2–P3; the “story” genre is new, but the model in the book is strong. Project roles are a familiar ritual from U1–U7. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (didn't + infinitive / question word order / superlative spelling), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
