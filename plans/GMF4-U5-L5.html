Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /uː/ sound spelt u_e and ue (a huge mule · a tune · a barbecue · a statue), talk about the WOMAD music festival in New Zealand, and read & plan a myth — three mini-blocks, one “fun & music” thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that u_e and ue both spell /uː/ (recognise → say → sort, next to oo & ui); culture — WOMAD in New Zealand & world instruments (bagpipes · steel drum · didgeridoo · sitar), 21C: making your own fun; literacy — text type a myth / a mythical creature (Ulysses & the Sirens · the Hydra), the possessive apostrophe, plan & write a mythical creature.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (chant/action bridges); the huge-mule tongue-twister is play; cap each block hard so the mythical creature actually gets planned.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the /uː/ twister and sort u_e / ue words;
  • name 2–3 world instruments from WOMAD and say where they're from;
  • find the parts of a myth and plan a mythical creature with a partner.

Structure
Can a huge mule play a tune at the barbecue next to the statue?  ·  The didgeridoo comes from Australia.  ·  myth: The (Hydra) lived in… It ate… It had…
Form
/uː/ = u_e (huge, mule, tune — split digraph, magic e) AND ue (barbecue, statue, rescue) — (also oo: moon · ui: fruit). Myth layout: appearance · size · habitat · diet · possessive apostrophe (Hydra's claws · Sirens' island).
Pron. watch
/uː/ (~/juː/) clean in huge/mule/tune; barbecue /ˈbɑːbɪkjuː/; statue /ˈstætʃuː/; didgeridoo /ˌdɪdʒərɪˈduː/; sitar /sɪˈtɑː/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“mule and barbecue — same /uː/ sound? Same letters?” → same sound, different spelling (u_e vs ue)!
“A myth — is it a true story or an old made-up tale with monsters & heroes?” → an old made-up tale.

Assumptions: Free-time activities, conditional-with-can, instruments & I'd rather are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from GMF3 phonics is enough to enjoy the u_e / ue noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short myth texts with support.

Block 1 · Phonics /uː/ (u_e · ue)
carpet → desks

Speak and spell (act. 1, CD2 43). PB p.55: Can a huge mule play a tune at the barbecue next to the statue? Remember! fruit · moon. Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort the /uː/ spellings. Hoops/columns: u_e (mule, tune, huge, cube) · ue (barbecue, statue, rescue) · (spares: oo — moon · ui — fruit). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card.

Notice the rule. Mule and barbecue — same sound /uː/, different letters! u_e has a magic e; ue is two letters at the end. (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more /uː/ word to a hoop (June, glue).

✔ Assess: who still reads u_e as short /ʌ/? Note them — extra RallyRead next unit.

Block 3 · Literacy · a myth
carpet → desks, pairs

Read a myth (PB p.56, act. 1–3, CD2 45). «Ulysses and the Sirens»: Which of these will be in a myth — a monster? a hero? a mountain bike? What do the Sirens do? Read/listen; the Sirens (part woman, part bird) sang songs that crashed ships.

▶ Myth scavenger hunt. Race in pairs to find on PB p.57 «the Hydra»: What did it look like? How big was it? Where did it live? What did it eat? First hands up read the answer aloud — everyone scans.

Writing tip · possessive apostrophe (PB p.57). The Hydra's claws — one Hydra, apostrophe + s. The Sirens' island — many Sirens, just an apostrophe! Chorus two examples.

▶ Plan a mythical creature (Cooperative learning, AB p.54 format). Pairs invent a creature & make notes: Name · What it looks like · What it eats · Where it lives · How big · What it's got (claws / wings / a tail). 2 pairs share one fact aloud.

💪 Support: fill the plan template with word support. ⭐ Extension: write the full description using the possessive apostrophe.
