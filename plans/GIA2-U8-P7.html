🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): The WDYT project is turned into a task with real friction: three people travel together, they want different things, there is little money — and the plan has to suit everybody. The unit's Super skill (communicating clearly) works as the condition for handing it in: if even one member was silent during the presentation, the project is rejected. The Quick review is stitched into the middle as stations — “checking the route before you set off”: revision for the test without the word “test”. And the year closes here too: the WDYT question opened in Pair 1, and the timeline of all eight units — because after the test there is only Unit 9, the final review.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Last real mission of the year. Three of you. Three days. One city. And here's the problem: one of you wants museums, one wants water slides, one wants to eat. Marta, Emily and Nathan solved it — they went to Glasgow, stayed in a B & B because it's cheaper, ate burgers on day one because everyone loves burgers. Your group builds a plan where nobody is bored and nobody is broke. One rule from me: if anybody in your group is silent during the presentation, the plan is rejected. Then we close the question we opened in Pair 1. What IS your idea of a good holiday? Show me, don't tell me.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: the last real mission of the year. Three people, three days, one city — and one wants museums, one wants the water park, one wants to eat. The plan has to suit everyone and not break the bank. The rule: if anyone in the group stays silent during the presentation, the project is rejected. And at the end we close the question opened in Pair 1: what is a good holiday for you?

WHAT WE DO: the project video + STEP 1 analysing the model plan + Model project notes as a checklist

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 with a task: Where do they want to go? Write down every place they name. (key, TB: Glasgow).

Design: agreeing as a three
Design · agreeing as a three
groups

WHAT WE DO: STEP 2: Super skills Communication + the Key phrases + choosing the place and the five criteria — the discussion runs on equal-participation chips

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The Super skills tips on the board: Look at your classmates when you speak. Give a reason with every opinion. Make sure all of you agree. Make sure everyone participates.
  2. The project Key phrases — drill them and count them: I think this is a good idea because … · Do we all agree? · You're very quiet. What do you think? · What do you think is best? · I think this is the best option because … · I don't think that's a good idea because …
  3. TB follow-up: What do you do if you DON'T all agree? (find a middle option · go with the majority · make two versions and compare them). And: How do you make sure the quiet person is heard? (everyone gets their own minute for an opinion · one person is appointed chair).
  4. Run the discussion itself through Kagan Talking Chips — that is the Super skill in action: three chips each (a paper clip, an eraser, anything), every contribution costs one, put it in the middle. Out of chips — you listen until the others have used theirs. No chips left? Then you listen. The structure stops the loud one and obliges the quiet one; the rule “silent means rejected” stops being a threat and becomes a mechanism.
  5. ex.5 — the five criteria for the plan: the type of accommodation · places and activities, at least one cultural and one outdoors · interesting for all three · different food for different meals · not too expensive.
  6. TB tip: let them search online and note down the places, hotels and food they liked.
📺 On screen: SB pp.106–107 · the Key phrase cards · three chips per person · tablets/phones for searching
👦 Students do: the three of them reach agreement while the Key phrases are counted. Expect: the line “You're very quiet. What do you think?” is the most needed and the most forgotten; remind them twice

Route check · Quick review stations
stations

WHAT WE DO: 4 stations of 4 minutes: the unit vocabulary · will · going to · present continuous (SB p.105)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before you leave, the route goes through checks. Four stations, four minutes each.
  2. Station 1 — the four lexical sets of Quick review p.105 (types of holiday · places to stay · facilities · holiday activities) through Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: a card in your hand, find a partner — ask, answer them, trade cards and move on. Four minutes of movement and the whole lexical set is run through several times.
  3. Station 2 — will: predictions aff/neg + a question + a short answer.
  4. Station 3 — going to: affirmative, negative, question, short answer.
  5. Station 4 — present continuous for arrangements: a calendar → sentences (a time and a second person are compulsory).
  6. Each station finishes with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: you give a checkpoint, the group builds the answer together and makes sure ANY member could say it, then you call the number. Number threes — boards up! You decide who answers, so “let the strong one say it” does not work.
  7. A personal gap list: Write down what YOU missed — that's your test prep list, and it's short.
📺 On screen: SB p.105 Quick review · the station cards · mini-whiteboards · timer (Backup: four A3 sheets in the four corners of the room and groups moving round)
👦 Students do: they go through the stations and log their gaps honestly. Expect: the gap lists differ — that is the point

WHAT WE DO: STEP 3: build the plan in the chosen format + How to… tips p.121 + sharing out the roles

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Notes first, beauty later. Fourteen minutes — the clock is the boss.
  2. Build the draft through Kagan RoundTable: one plan sheet per group, passed round the circle — each student writes in their own day and passes it on, two full circles. While the sheet is with you, only you write; once you get it you must add something. That way by the end of the second circle there are no empty lines left — and no “I didn't write anything” either.
  3. Formats to choose from (TB tip): a poster · slides · a leaflet · a timetable with a time column.
  4. Go through the How to… tips p.121 together: Start with a clear introduction: 'I'm going to talk about …' · Use first, after that, finally so listeners know you're moving on · Give people a chance to ask questions · Thank them at the end.
  5. TB tip: predict in advance what questions you will be asked and write down what you will answer.
  6. Sharing out (the TB asks for this): Decide NOW who says what — one person per day, or one for activities, one for accommodation, one for food.
  7. A form check before the stage: Where's your prediction? Where's your plan? Where's your locked arrangement? All three must be in there.
📺 On screen: poster paper/slides/markers · SB p.121 How to… tips · timer
👦 Students do: they build the plan and rehearse in their roles. Expect: without a hard timer the decorating eats everything — the timer settles it

WHAT WE DO: STEP 4: the presentation to another group + peer review + Final reflection

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Getting ready to go up (TB presentation tips): Look at your audience — or just above their heads if you're nervous. Speak slowly and clearly. Don't fidget. If you make a mistake, stay calm and start the sentence again.
  2. Ninety seconds per group. Judges: fluency, accuracy, richness. And I count how many of you speak.
  3. Peer review ex.8: Before each presentation, write down — would you go on this holiday? Is the information presented well? And prepare ONE question about the plan.
  4. Final reflection p.107: Is the plan well prepared, with activities for different types of people? Is the information clear? Does everyone communicate their ideas well in the group? Do you use language from the unit — give examples. TB tip: check yourselves against Quick review p.105 — which of the unit's language actually went into service.
  5. TB questions for the group's reflection: Did everyone have a chance to talk? Did you agree on things? What did you do when you didn't agree? Does your plan contain ideas from everyone?
📺 On screen: the groups' plans · timer · the rubric · SB p.107 Final reflection
👦 Students do: they present and answer questions. Expect: the question from the other group is the most valuable moment of the pair; and the rule “silent means rejected” really does work

WHAT WE DO: the unit's WDYT question + the timeline of eight units + a personal test-prep plan

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. WDYT: back to the question opened in Pair 1 — So: what's YOUR idea of a good holiday? Answer with your plan, not with an opinion. One line each.
  2. Go round the circle in Kagan Timed RoundRobin: 20 seconds each, in order round the circle, nobody may be skipped — you may only say it shorter. Exactly 20 seconds holds both the ones who would launch into a monologue and the ones who would get away with one word.
  3. The year's timeline on the board: School life · Me time · Dressing up · Extremes · Must try! · Animals in danger · Heroes · Summer fun. Eight units. Eight missions. This was the last new one.
  4. What's left? One test — and then Unit 9, where we put the whole year in one place.
  5. The final unit leaderboard + a gap list for everyone: Your test prep = YOUR gaps, not everything.
📺 On screen: leaderboard · the timeline of eight units on the board · the gap lists
👦 Students do: they leave with a sense of distance covered and with a personal list. Expect: the year's timeline makes a strong impression — many see the scale of it for the first time

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, groups can plan and present a three-day holiday using all three futures, argue for other people's preferences with the project Key phrases, and revise the whole unit through Quick review stations.
  • Subsidiary. The project video as the model; STEP 1 analysing the model plan (what went in, what did not and why); STEP 2 Super skills Communication + the Key phrases; STEP 3 the format and the How to… tips p.121; STEP 4 the presentation + peer review; Quick review p.105 as stations; Final reflection p.107; closing the WDYT question and the year.
  • Personal. The project is the quiet students' moment of glory: the rule “silent means rejected” protects exactly them. Never call the Quick review test preparation — it is “checking the route before you set off”.
By the end, students can…
  • build a three-day plan with accommodation, activities and meals
  • argue for what other people in the group want, using the Key phrases
  • present a plan clearly with first / after that / finally
  • pass all Quick-review stations of Unit 8

Form

Pron. watch
the signpost words the presentation rests on: first /fɜː(r)st/ · after that · finally /ˈfaɪnəli/ — say them with a pause after each
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Is a plan with museums on all three days, because YOU like museums, accepted? → no: the Super skill requires it to be interesting for everybody.
Do the exact time of every activity and a packing list belong in the plan? → no (key, TB ex.2): that is too detailed and the plan stops being readable.
Is the Quick review a test? → no, it is checking the route before you set off: find your gaps today, not in the test.

Assumptions: SB pp.105–107, syllabus pair 68. All the language of the unit has been covered; what is new is reaching agreement in a group and defending the plan in public.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
The project eats the revisionA hard frame: the revision stations take 16 minutes IN THE MIDDLE of the pair, before the plan is built — they are “checking the route before you set off”.
One talks, two stay silentThe hand-in rule is announced BEFORE the work: silent means rejected. Plus the TB routine: decide in advance who reads which day or which block (one takes the activities, another the accommodation).
They write so much that on stage they read it outTB Model project notes: write enough to SPEAK from, not to read from. Check the drafts before the stage — cross out the long sentences.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
