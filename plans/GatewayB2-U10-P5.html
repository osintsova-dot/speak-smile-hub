🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Put the whole of Unit 10 in one place — the grammar reference in their own words, the word list in their mouths — and then find out what is still missing while there is still a night to fix it.
What is genuinely hard
Not the content: the transfer. A student who can do each exercise when it is labelled falls apart when six exercises sit on one page with no labels. The second difficulty is honesty — a practice paper only works if nobody hides the score from themselves.
Typical error to expect
«I wonder where is he», «Can you tell me what time does the train leave?» — in an indirect question the subject comes BEFORE the verb and the auxiliary disappears. Russian keeps the same word order in both, so nothing warns the student. This is the most frequent single error in the whole unit and it is worth five points on the Test yourself page.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is revision that looks nothing like a test: the Check it page is answered, not read, and the vocabulary comes back through games where everybody writes at once. Then the room turns quiet for a timed run of the book's own Test yourself — for practice, with the marks belonging to nobody — and the students mark their own papers and write the reason for every error. The lesson ends on the real test: seven sections, a hundred points, the camera and the choice of version, all named a day early so that the paper measures the language and not the surprise.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Everything from this unit, on your desk, in one lesson — and one run against the clock that costs you nothing. Tomorrow it counts; today it only tells you where to look.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, and the whole unit produced aloud by the class in ninety seconds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — what is the last thing you read or watched that was NEWS? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.
  2. 2 minutes — the unit in four sentences. Kagan Round Robin in fours: one sentence each, no repeats, and between them the four must cover future in the past, a mixed conditional, a question tag and an indirect question.
  3. 1 minute — one four to the floor. Take the group that covered all four cleanly and let the room hear what a full score sounds like.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): That is the test. Four grammar points, three word lists, forty points. You have forty-five minutes of revision and then thirty minutes of paper.

WHAT WE DO: each group rebuilds one grammar point from Check it and teaches it in ninety seconds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four groups, one grammar point each, drawn at random: future in the past · mixed conditionals · question tags · indirect questions.
  2. Seven minutes with Check it p.132 and their own notebooks. Each group produces one A4 poster containing exactly three things: the FORM in one line, TWO example sentences of their own (not from the book), and the ONE mistake people make.
  3. The rule that makes it work: the examples must be about the news or about this class. No sentences about John and Mary.
  4. Four presentations of ninety seconds each, in order. The rest copy the three lines into their notebooks — that is their revision sheet.
  5. You add at most one sentence per poster, and only if something is actually wrong. The posters then go on the wall face down and are turned over only after the papers are collected.
📺 On screen: the four posters are made on paper by the students — nothing digital, nothing to prepare
👦 Students do: rebuild one grammar point in a group, teach it for ninety seconds, and copy the other three

teams of four
Why: Vocabulary showdown · three lists, no book

WHAT WE DO: a fast game on the three word lists, played on mini-whiteboards so that everybody answers at once

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan Showdown: teams of four, one mini-whiteboard per student. You read a prompt, everybody writes in silence, and on Showdown! all boards go up at the same moment. One point per correct board, so a team of four can score four.
  2. Round one, news sections: Where do you read about people who have just died? · Where do readers give their own opinion? · Where are the longer articles that go deep? · Where is the private life of a film star?
  3. Round two, headline words: you say the everyday word, they write the headline word — investigate, fire, resign, marry, promise, cut, support, request.
  4. Round three, collocations: you say a gapped sentence, they write the missing half — The story is hot off the ____ · They're going to hold a ____ conference · An ____ of news.
  5. Spelling counts. Say it once at the start and hold to it — that is what the test does.
📺 On screen: the prompts are read aloud, not shown — the only equipment is mini-whiteboards and pens
👦 Students do: write every answer on their own board and show it simultaneously, in three rounds

Move break · four corners of grammar
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: three minutes standing between two long sitting stages

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four corners, one grammar point in each — the same four as the posters.
  2. You read a sentence and everybody walks to the corner it belongs to: Nobody told me, did they? · She was about to leave when the phone rang. · Can you tell me where the meeting is? · If I'd revised, I wouldn't be worried now.
  3. Six sentences, six walks. After each one, one student from the correct corner says WHY.
  4. The last two are deliberately close: I wonder whether he called. / He called, didn't he?
  5. Freeze and sit: Nine minutes on the two sections that cost the most, then the paper.
📺 On screen: the four corner cards from the poster stage — reuse them, nothing new to prepare
👦 Students do: classify six sentences physically and justify two of them aloud

The two expensive sections
whole class
Why: The two expensive sections

WHAT WE DO: nine minutes aimed at indirect-question word order and the tag rules that collide

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Five minutes on indirect questions, mechanically, on mini-whiteboards. You call a direct question, everybody writes the indirect version, all boards up: Where does she live? · What time is it? · Did he call? · How much does it cost? · Is the news true? · Why did you say that? · Have they arrived? · What does it mean? · When is the test? · Who wrote it?
  2. Two rules go up in front of them and stay: subject BEFORE verb · if or whether for yes-no questions · the question mark belongs to the FIRST half.
  3. Four minutes on tags, same method: Nobody knows … · I'm right … · There's no news … · She can swim … · You've seen it … · They didn't come …
  4. Every board up at once, every time. Nobody hides, and you see in one glance who still needs the last two minutes.
  5. Close with the two hardest, said by the whole class in chorus: Nobody knows, DO THEY? · I'm late, AREN'T I?
📺 On screen: the two rule lines written on the board and left up until the papers are handed out — chalk only
👦 Students do: produce sixteen answers each on a mini-whiteboard, all shown simultaneously

45–53′
Doubt clinic · their questions, not yours
desks
Why: The revision so far was ours. This eight minutes is theirs, and an anonymous slip is the only way the weakest student in the room asks the question that has been in the way for two weeks.

WHAT WE DO: the last honest questions before the paper — asked by the students

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Hand every student a slip. Write one question about this unit you still cannot answer. Anonymous. One sentence. Ninety seconds, everybody writes — a student who has no question writes the hardest question they CAN answer, for the class.
  2. Collect, shuffle, read aloud. Answer the first three yourself, fast. Then hand the rest to the class: Numbered Heads Together — the group agrees the answer, one number answers.
  3. Anything nobody can answer goes on the board under the heading ASK BEFORE THE TEST and is answered in the first two minutes of the next lesson, before the papers go out.
  4. Do not turn this into a new lesson. If a question needs twelve minutes of teaching, say so honestly: it is not on the paper in that form, and here is the one line that covers what is.
📺 On screen: the slips, then the board list ASK BEFORE THE TEST. Backup: none needed — paper and voice only.
👦 Students do: write one anonymous question, then answer other people's questions in groups

53–71′
🎯 Test yourself · against the clock, for practice
desks
Why: The first time they meet the unit under time pressure must not be the day of the real test. Everything that goes wrong here is still repairable — and it is repaired by them, tonight.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.133 — the book's own Test yourself page, done to the clock — done to time, but the mark belongs to nobody

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say what this is, once, and mean it: This is practice. I am not writing these marks down. What you find out in the next 18 minutes is what you revise tonight.
  2. Clock it on the board. Same rule as the real paper: no book, no phone, no talking, and you may go back at the end if you have time.
  3. Monitor with a notebook, not a pen over their shoulders. Write down which exercise the room slows on — that is the section you re-teach in the last minutes, not the one you had planned to.
  4. Two minutes before the end, warn them once. Students who finish early do NOT sit and wait: they turn the page over and write three sentences of their own using the unit's grammar.
📺 On screen: the timer on the board, big enough to see from the back. Backup: a phone stopwatch face up on your desk.
👦 Students do: work in silence to the clock, then stop when the time stops

71–79′
Self-check and the error map
desks → pairs
Why: A score without a reason teaches nothing. Writing WHY the answer was wrong is the whole point of the practice run, and doing it while the paper is warm is worth three exercises done cold.

WHAT WE DO: keys go to the students; every wrong answer becomes a line to work on

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Answers on the board, or the key page open on the projector. Students mark their own paper — not a partner's. This is the one time where nobody else sees the score.
  2. Every wrong answer gets one line in the error log: what the answer was, what the right one is, and why — in their own words. «Careless» is not a reason and does not count.
  3. RallyCoach in pairs: take one error each and explain it to your partner. If you cannot explain it, it goes on the ASK BEFORE THE TEST list on the board.
  4. Take a show of hands by section — grammar, vocabulary, transformations — and write the three weakest on the board. That list IS tonight's homework, personalised, without you writing twelve different tasks.
📺 On screen: the answer key, then the three weakest sections on the board. Backup: if the projector fails, read the key aloud twice, slowly, and let them mark as you read.
👦 Students do: mark their own paper, write the reason for every error, then explain one error to a partner

The test explained · format, level, camera
board
Why: Format panic costs more marks than gaps in the language. A student who knows the order, the totals and the camera walks in measuring the unit, not the room.

WHAT WE DO: exactly what happens next lesson, so nobody is surprised by the paper

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Put the shape of the test on the board and leave it there: Listening 12 · Vocabulary 20 · Grammar 25 · Use of English 8 · Reading 15 · Writing 10 · Speaking 10 = 100. Ninety minutes, the whole lesson.
  2. Name the order and the reason: listening first, while the ears are fresh; writing last; and speaking taken in pairs in the corner while the room writes — the only honest way to fit five skills into one lesson.
  3. Say the camera out loud now, not on the day: Your speaking answer is filmed. The mark is for the test, the video is for December — so that you can hear for yourself how much better you have got. Nobody watches it but you and me.
  4. The choice of version. Standard or Higher, same seven sections, same hundred points, harder wording. Ask them to decide tonight, not at the door: Which version measures you honestly? Anyone who cannot decide gets Standard and may swap in the first five minutes.
  5. Last line, and say it plainly: Bring a pen that works and the sleep of somebody who has already done the revision.

WHAT WE DO: self-check against today's aim, then the homework said once and written on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two minutes — the unit in one sentence each. Round the room: everyone says one thing they now know about the news in English that they did not know two weeks ago. No repeats. It goes faster than you expect and it is the best possible closure for a unit.
  2. One minute — what happens next. Say it plainly: Unit 10 was the last unit of the course. From here it is Exam success, two reserve lessons, and then the mock paper.
  3. One minute — homework, said once and written up. Workbook p.93, the second half of the Cumulative review of Units 1 to 10. Say what it is honestly: This is the whole year on one page. It will take you thirty minutes and it will tell you exactly what you have forgotten.

Structure
the four grammar points of the unit, revised as meaning: a plan in the past · two times inside one conditional · a tag that asks against a tag that confirms · a question that is not a question in form
Form
revision only — no new form is presented today. Check it p.132 holds all four boxes and the three vocabulary lists; by the end of the first half every student should be able to find any rule on that page in under ten seconds.
Pron. watch
the headline words are short and stressed, and the group will swallow them: axe /æks/ with a clean final cluster · plea /pliː/ not «plee-a» · probe /prəʊb/ with a diphthong · blaze /bleɪz/ against blast /blɑːst/ · wed /wed/ · PM said as two letters, pee-EM, stress on the second · the collocations take a compound stress on the FIRST element (FRONT-page news · NEWS bulletin · PRESS conference · CHIEF editor) · and the question tag carries the whole meaning in its intonation: falling = I am sure, rising = I am asking. Drill the two contours physically, with a hand
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«There's no news today, is there?» — why there in the tag? → with there is and there are, there behaves like the subject.
«If I hadn't broken my old tablet, I wouldn't have a new one» — which half is past? → the breaking. Which is present? → having the tablet.
How do you spell the section where readers give their own opinion? → have your say, three words. And the one about people who have just died? → obituaries.

Test yourself · Grammar 1 — Future in the past (5 points)five sentences about future activities seen from the past, ONE word in each gap: he thought the Sun … shine · because it … going to leave · the interview was … start at 10 · they thought the party … starting at 9 · he was about … cry. One word only — a second word scores nothing.
Test yourself · Grammar 2 — Mixed conditionals (5 points)five sentences with the verb given in brackets. Every item crosses two times, which is the whole point: I … (be) tired now if I'd gone to bed late · Dylan would be studying at university if he … (pass) his entrance exam last June.
Test yourself · Grammar 3 — Indirect questions (5 points)five jumbled sets of words put in order. Word order IS the mark: subject before verb, no auxiliary, and a question mark only if the first half is a question.
Test yourself · Grammar 4 — Question tags (5 points)five sentences, choose the correct tag: does he/do they · do/don't · hasn't/isn't · am/aren't · it/there. Item 4 is aren't I? and item 1 is the nobody → they rule.
Test yourself · Vocabulary 1 — News sections (6 points)six «which section would you look at to …» questions — obituaries, gossip, weather forecast, entertainment, have your say, features. Production, not recognition, and it is spelled.
Test yourself · Vocabulary 2 — Words used in headlines (8 points)eight headline words matched to their everyday equivalents: axe, plea, wed, back, key, clash, quit, blaze. Eight of the twenty points on the vocabulary half sit here.
Test yourself · Vocabulary 3 — Collocations connected with the news (6 points)six sentences, choose the right half of a fixed expression: press conference/headline · events/news · article/item · press/press conference · aware/informed · front-page/bulletin.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
Where this goes next
🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar, word formation and lexis) — thirteen marks of the written paper.

The Test yourself page is a production test: forty points, no options in most of it, and every answer has to be spelled. That is the shape of tasks 26–31, where the right word in the wrong form scores exactly what a blank scores. The two sections that will hurt are indirect questions (word order, five points) and the headline-word matching, where axe, plea, wed and blaze have to be produced, not recognised. Naming that aloud before the papers turn over is worth ten minutes of revision.
