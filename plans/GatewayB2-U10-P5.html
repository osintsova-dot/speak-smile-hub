🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Write an article of 140–190 words for a magazine about the latest crazes — what is best right now and what is yesterday's news — with a title, a reader, a four-paragraph plan and linkers from all seven families (sequence, contrast, addition, consequence, reason, opinion, conclusion).
What is genuinely hard
Register. An article is not an essay: it has a title, it speaks to a reader, it may use I and it may ask questions — and school has trained the opposite for years. The second difficulty is that linkers are known PASSIVELY: students recognise moreover and nevertheless instantly but produce however four times and nothing else. The third is the choice between the ones that open a sentence and the ones that join two halves of it — despite against despite the fact that, although against however.
Typical error to expect
An article written like an essay — no title, no reader, no voice, and «In this essay I want to tell about…» at the top. An article speaks to a reader, asks questions, and may use I; an essay does not. The task on p.131 says «tell US» — that word decides the register.
Why the lesson is built this way
The content comes before the genre: ten minutes of brainstorming what is actually trending right now in series, films, music, games, fashion and sport, so that nobody has to invent an opinion at minute sixty. Then the task is taken apart word by word — the notice says «tell US», which decides the whole register — and the model article is read twice, once for content and once for the eight linker choices, each explained by why the OTHER option fails. The Writing bank becomes a seven-family map on the wall, the six stages of writing are put in order by the class, and then twenty-two protected minutes of writing.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“What is the best thing right now — and what is finished, over, yesterday's news? Today you write about exactly that, for a real magazine, in a hundred and ninety words. And the difference between an article and an essay is one word in the task: they want you to tell US.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of concession-and-response recycling, and the bridge into writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — what is the last thing you read or watched that was NEWS? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.
  2. 2 minutes — yesterday, in your own words. Kagan RallyRobin: partner A states an opinion about the press, partner B answers it using a Speaking bank expression, then swap. Four exchanges in ninety seconds — Tabloids are pointless. — It is true that they print gossip, but that doesn't mean nobody learns anything from them.
  3. 1 minute — one exchange to the floor, and one question: Did you record yourself last night? What did you hear? Take two honest answers; they are always interesting.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Yesterday you argued out loud. Today you argue in writing, for a magazine, about something completely different. Page one hundred and thirty-one.

WHAT WE DO: the Teacher's Book warmer, then the aim and the twenty-two protected minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed, ninety seconds. Four gapped headlines on the board, completed in pairs, as absurdly as they like: Man finds ____ in fridge · Coastguard called to help ____ · Skateboarding dog ____ · Burglar caught with five ____ pleads not guilty

WHAT WE DO: ex.1a–1b: the content bank built before any genre work, and left on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Six headings on the board: SERIES · FILMS · MUSIC · GAMES · FASHION · SPORT. The Teacher's Book asks whether students would add a category — take one or two (creators, food, apps).
  2. ex.1a, three minutes individually: under each heading write ONE thing that is hot right now and ONE thing that is over. Phones allowed for this stage only, and say when the stage ends.

  4. ex.1b, three minutes: pool it on the board. Anything with three or more mentions gets circled — those are the safe topics for a magazine article.
  5. Leave the whole board up. It is the content bank for the writing stage and it removes the single most common excuse.

WHAT WE DO: ex.2: the notice underlined word by word, because the register is hidden inside it

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2 in pairs, three minutes: read the «Articles wanted» notice and underline every piece of information you MUST include.
  2. Pre-teach the two expressions that decide the content, ten seconds each: crazes (things that are very popular right now) and yesterday's news (things that are not trendy any more).
  3. Check by nomination and build the list on the board: who or what the latest crazes are · what YOU think is best right now, and why · what you think is yesterday's news, and why.
  4. Then the register question, which is the point of the stage: Find the word that tells you this is not an essay. It is «tell US». There is a reader, and the reader is being spoken to.
  5. Write the three article requirements on the board and leave them: A TITLE · a question to the reader · at least one I.
📺 On screen: the underlined requirements and the three article rules written on the board — chalk only
👦 Students do: underline the task in pairs, name the two content halves, and discover the register from one word

Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute stirrer on the content bank, with the thermometer from the Teacher's Book

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Draw the Teacher's Book thermometer on the board: HOT at thirty degrees, NOT at zero. Elicit the two meanings — popular, and out of fashion.
  2. Everybody stands in a line down the middle of the room. HOT end, NOT end.
  3. You call an item from the class list — a series, a game, a singer, a sport — and everybody positions themselves along the line. Six items.
  4. After each one, take a reason from the two extremes only, in one sentence, and insist on a linker: Personally, I think … because …
  5. Freeze and sit: Somebody has already written this article. Read it.
📺 On screen: the thermometer drawn on the board — one drawing, thirty seconds
👦 Students do: position themselves physically on six items and justify two positions with a linker

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: the article «The Name of the Wind» read for content against the task

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say the rule for the first read: Ignore the underlined words completely. One question: does this article include everything the notice asked for?
  2. ex.3, four minutes silent. Then pairs compare for one minute.
  3. Check as a class: yes. It talks about the latest crazes in series and films, names what the writer thinks is best right now (hopepunk — optimistic, hopeful stories in a genre full of dark dystopias) and what is old news (endless remakes of superhero films with the same plots and different actors).
  4. Now pull the genre features out of the text with three questions: Where is the writer's voice? Who is being spoken to? How does the first sentence work? «Let me begin by saying that I'm really into fantasy» — that is an article, not an essay, in nine words.
  5. Fast finishers, from the Teacher's Book: write a short paragraph saying whether you would be interested in the writer's suggestions.
  6. Sixty seconds in pairs: hopepunk or dystopia — which do you actually want to watch, and why?
📺 On screen: nothing to project — the model is printed on p.131 and the genre features go on the board as they are found
👦 Students do: read a model against a task, then identify three genre features and react to its content

WHAT WE DO: ex.4: the linker choices worked through with a reason for every rejection

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4, four minutes individually: the eight underlined alternatives in the model.
  2. Pairs compare, two minutes, with one instruction: for each one say why the OTHER option is impossible. That is the whole learning.
  3. Check with Kagan Rally Coach pairs reporting, and give the reason every time: a Despite the fact that (despite alone needs a noun) · b and so (consequence; whereas is contrast) · c being (present participle for something happening now) · d However (although joins two halves of one sentence) · e As far as I'm concerned (opinion; furthermore is addition) · f so (consequence; as is cause) · g All in all (conclusion; moreover is addition) · h However (contrast; therefore is consequence).
  4. Then the Teacher's Book extra, three minutes in pairs: sort ten linkers into ones that OPEN a sentence and ones that JOIN two halves. Opens: nevertheless, to sum up, therefore, however, moreover, on the one hand. Joins: and so, despite, as, whereas.
  5. This distinction is the one that costs marks in written work all year — put it on the wall in two columns.
📺 On screen: the two columns — OPENS A SENTENCE and JOINS TWO HALVES — written on the wall card during this stage
👦 Students do: make eight choices alone, justify every rejection to a partner, then sort ten linkers by position

WHAT WE DO: ex.5–6: the Writing bank labelled, the stages ordered, then a personal plan

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5 in pairs, two minutes: put the seven titles onto the seven linker groups. Answers: 1 d Sequencing · 2 g Contrast · 3 a Addition · 4 b Consequence · 5 e Reason · 6 c Opinion · 7 f Concluding. Copy the map onto the wall as seven boxes.
  2. ex.6 in pairs, ninety seconds: order the six stages of writing. Answer: brainstorm ideas (e) · make notes (b) · organise the notes into paragraphs (a) · write the first version (d) · check and edit (c) · write the final version (f).
  3. Point out what the class has just done: stages one and two happened in the first twenty minutes of this lesson. They are starting at stage three.
  4. Two and a half minutes silent: the four-paragraph plan, in notes only. Paragraph 1 — introduction and my angle · 2 — what is hot, with a reason · 3 — what is not, with a reason · 4 — conclusion. Plus the title, written now, not later.
📺 On screen: the seven-box linker map drawn on the wall card as the class names the families — it stays up until the end of the course

WHAT WE DO: ex.7a: the article written in class, in silence, with the walls as the only support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say the frame once and then stop talking: A hundred and forty to a hundred and ninety words. Twenty-two minutes. Silence. Everything you need is on the walls.
  2. Point at the three supports without explaining them again: the content list on the board, the seven-box linker map, the three article rules.
  3. Announce the halfway mark once, at minute eleven, and the five-minute warning. Nothing else.
  4. Circulate with a pencil and put a small dot in the margin next to a repeated linker or a missing title. No comment, no conversation — the dot is the feedback.
  5. For anybody genuinely stuck at minute five, one question only: What is the last series you finished? That unlocks it every time.
  6. At minute twenty-two, pens down whether or not it is finished. Word count written at the bottom by the student.
📺 On screen: the timer is the only thing visible; the walls carry everything else. Backup: if the timer app fails, write the finishing time on the board in large figures and call the halfway mark yourself
👦 Students do: write 140–190 words in silence, in twenty-two minutes, using the wall supports and their own plan
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE task 38 (the project piece), criteria К2 and К3. The article's spine — a stated aim, one idea per paragraph, quantified claims, a conclusion that recommends — is the spine the Russian task 38 is marked on. The linkers taught today are the surface of that spine.
How to run it without losing the 90 minutes: three minutes at the planning stage on quantifiers only: put a fake figure on the board (61 % of teenagers get their news from video) and have the class say it four different ways. It fits inside the planning stage without extending it.

Structure
Let me begin by saying that … · As I said before, … · We already know that … · What we want is for people to … · It's time we had … · I'm a big fan of …
Form
seven linker families and where each one stands: sequence (firstly, secondly, lastly) · contrast (however, nevertheless, although, even though, despite, whereas, while, on the one/other hand) · addition (what's more, furthermore, moreover, in addition, not only … but also) · consequence (therefore, and so, as a result, consequently) · reason (because, as, since) · opinion (as far as I'm concerned, personally, in my opinion) · conclusion (all in all, all things considered, to sum up, in conclusion). DESPITE + noun, DESPITE THE FACT THAT + clause; ALTHOUGH joins two halves of one sentence, HOWEVER opens a new one.
Pron. watch
the linkers are said as single units with one stress and a clear pause after them: howEVer, … · nevertheLESS, … · as far as I'm conCERNED, … · all THINGS conSIDered, … The comma is audible in English, and students who do not pause after a linker sound as if they have lost the sentence
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Despite the rain, we went out» — is despite followed by a noun or by a clause? → a noun. And if I want a clause? → despite the fact that.
The task says «tell US what YOU think» — who is us? → the magazine and its readers. So can I write I? → yes. Could I in an essay? → much less freely.
«All in all» — does it add an idea or close the article? → close it. So how many times can it appear? → once, at the end.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«I have nothing to write about»That is why ex.1 comes first and takes ten minutes: six categories on the board, everybody contributes, and the pooled list stays up during the writing. Nobody has to invent content at minute sixty.
The article turns into an essayThree requirements stated before writing and checked in the peer check: a TITLE · a question to the reader somewhere in the first paragraph · at least one I. Any piece without all three goes back.
However, however, howeverThe linker map on the wall has seven boxes and the rule is one line: no linker twice. During writing, walk past and put a small dot in the margin next to a repeated one — no comment, they will find it.
Writing time gets eaten by the earlier stagesTwenty-two minutes are protected. If the linker stage overruns, cut ex.5 to four families instead of seven and put the rest on the wall as a reference — the writing is the lesson, everything before it is preparation.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Task 38 (the written project piece, criteria К2 and К3) and task 37 for the register work.

An article is not a Russian exam genre, but everything that decides task 38 is trained by it: a paragraph plan agreed before writing (К2), linkers of contrast, addition, consequence and conclusion used once each and correctly (К2), an opinion signalled with a fixed phrase rather than «I think» seven times (К3). The Writing bank on p.131 is a seven-family linker table — it is the most exam-useful half page in the unit, and it is worth photographing.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
