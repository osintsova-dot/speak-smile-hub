Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to make conditional sentences with can / can't: “If it's (sunny), we can (have a picnic). / If it's (wet), we can't (ride a mountain bike).”
  • Subsidiary. Understand Josh & Lily's dialogue about tomorrow's plans (CD2 35); discover the rule — If + present simple, we can + verb (two parts, order can swap, no comma); recycle the 12 free-time activities from L1; match weather to activities; write & act out a weather conversation (AB).
  • Personal. Keep can weak /kən/ and can't strong /kɑːnt/ audibly different; keep the frame “If it's ___, we can ___” visible; model the full sentence, accept short answers and recast.
By the end, pupils can…
  • make “If it's …, we can / can't …” sentences;
  • match weather (sunny/wet/windy…) to sensible activities;
  • ask & answer “What can we do tomorrow?”

Structure
If it's sunny, we can have a picnic.  ·  If it's wet, we can't ride a mountain bike.  ·  We can go windsurfing if it's windy. (order can swap — no comma)
Form
If + present simple (it's sunny) , we can / can't + verb (have a picnic). Two parts; the order can reverse (We can… if it's…) with no comma. Weak can /kən/ ≠ strong can't /kɑːnt/.
Pron. watch
weak if it's /ɪfɪts/ at speed; can /kən/ (weak) vs can't /kɑːnt/ (strong, long vowel); windsurfing.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“If it's wet, can we have a barbecue?” → no → we CAN'T have a barbecue.
“We can go bowling if it's raining — is that OK without a comma?” → yes — order swapped, no comma!

Assumptions: 12 activities are fresh from L1 (checked in warm-up). Weather adjectives are partly known from GMF3; the new focus is joining them with If… we can/can't… and hearing can/can't apart. Pupils can read the dialogue speech bubbles & the grammar table.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
can / can't sound the sameExaggerate: can /kən/ short & quiet · CAN'T /kɑːnt/ long & strong; thumbs up/down as you drill.
Comma / order confusionGrammar-clue box: two colours for the two parts; show both orders side by side, cross out any comma in the swapped one.
Weather–activity mismatch (barbecue if wet)Sense-check drill: hold a weather card + an activity card — thumbs if it makes sense, cross if silly.
Conversation = same partner againTrade weather cards after each exchange (new partner every time).
Answers too short (“picnic”)Recast to the full frame with a “stretch it” gesture; frame stays on the board.

27–37′
What can we do tomorrow? · info
desks, pairs

Model first. Show a weather forecast strip (sunny → windy → wet). Model publicly: If it's sunny, we can have a barbecue. But if it's wet, we can't ride our bikes.

▶ Talk Partners (act. 4-style). Pairs take a weather card each and plan tomorrow: What can we do tomorrow? — If it's windy, we can go windsurfing! Both write two If-sentences. 5–6 exchanges, trade cards.

✔ Assess: circulate with the class list — who makes a full “If it's…, we can/can't…” unaided? Recast, note names.

💪 Support: If-clause printed, pupil adds the activity. ⭐ Extension: add the swapped order: We can go bowling if it's raining.
