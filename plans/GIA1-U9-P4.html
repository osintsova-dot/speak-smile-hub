🔥 Challenge · build & rehearse (THINK→PRACTISE)
pairs

WHAT WE DO: situations A/B: choose → 6+ phrases → write 12+ lines → rehearse FROM MEMORY through the Skills boost steps

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK 3 min: the pair chooses A (a friend + a photo of an event) or B (the tourist office); picks 6+ Key phrases from the page and from the year (Phrasebook p.123 is legal).

Product B: 100 words
🏁 PRODUCT B · 100 words (THINK→WRITE)
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: writing: choose task A/B/C → a 3-paragraph plan (time-box) → 100 words → CHECK by the checklist; quiet work with the timer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK 4 min: choose A (a blog about a festival in your country — present!) / B (an email about the weekend — past!) / C (a story about meeting a friend — past+now). A skeleton plan: 3 lines = 3 paragraphs. No plan, no sheet handed out.
  2. A genre check for everyone: Blog? Present. Story? Past. Email? Opening and closing lines!
  3. WRITE 12 min, quiet, visible timer: A hundred words is three paragraphs of three sentences. You've written every piece of this during the year — today you assemble.
  4. CHECK 2 min by the book: grammar? punctuation? connectors (and/but/because)?
  5. 🚀 bar: 100+ words · 3 paragraphs · 4+ connectors · at least 2 tenses.
📺 On screen: SB p.114 task + Skills boost · skeleton plans · timer
👦 Students do: plan and write the longest text of the year in exam mode. Expected: 90–110 words for most. Typical error: a story in the present — the genre check catches it before the start.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can act out a 12-line dialogue from memory for situation A or B using 6+ Key phrases of the year and write a 100-word text (blog / email / story) organised in 3 paragraphs with connectors.
  • Subsidiary. The Key phrases table by function (weekend/info/photo/ticket); audio 62 dialogues with challenge restoration; Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; the year's genres identified (ex.1–2); a language hunt ex.3 (capitals/apostrophes/connectors); exam tips: read every word of the task · plan before you write.
  • Personal. The peak of productive skills before the finale: both speaking and writing «at exam level». The 🚀 bar: 12+ lines from memory, exactly 100 words, 3 paragraphs, different tenses.
By the end, students can…
  • sort the year's Key phrases by function
  • act a memorised dialogue with 6+ Key phrases
  • identify text genres and their unit language
  • write 100 words in 3 paragraphs with and/but/because/so

Target language
Lexis
How was your weekend?It was great!How can I get to…?How much does it cost?It's a great photo!Who's this?Buy your ticket on the bus.Take the bus from the station.
Structure
How was your weekend? — It was great. Jo and I went to… · Who's this on the bike? · How can I get to the balloon festival? — Take the special bus from Temple Meads station. · It costs £1.20.
Form
a dialogue = question→answer→follow-up (not two monologues); writing: plan → 3 paragraphs → check (grammar? punctuation? connectors?); an email = opening + closing lines.
Pron. watch
How was = /haʊ wəz/ · get to /ˈɡet tə/ · question intonation rises, It was great! — with enthusiasm, not read flat.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«A dialogue for situation A AND B?» → No — for just ONE (Exam tip: read every word of the task).
«100 words in one lump?» → No — 3 paragraphs by the plan.
«A blog about a festival — which tense?» → Present (habits) + can; a story — past!

Assumptions: All the year's Key phrases have appeared in the units; gathering them into a system of functions and producing them from memory is a new bar. The 100-word piece is the longest of the year: a plan is essential. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (mixed-year items), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our revision zone before the exam formats”.

Likely problemWhat you do

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
