🎯 GATE · food spelling blitz
desks · solo

WHAT WE DO: 3 minutes: they write 8 food words from picture prompts — fast AND accurate; self-check, personal score in the margin

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I show 8 pictures one by one: chicken · biscuit · sandwich · juice · orange · potato (2!) · bread · chocolate.
  2. I say: WRITE the word for each photo. Fast AND spelled right. You've known these words for years — prove it in writing. Three minutes! Timer.
  3. At zero — the key on the slide: self-check, score in the margin. Two potatoes — with -es!
  4. The turn: 8 out of 8? Silence?.. The silent ‘u’ in biscuit, the silent ‘d’ in sandwich — THIS is our game: not ‘knowing food’, but fast-accurate-written. Rematch in Pair 5!
📺 On screen: slide: 8 photos → key (chicken · biscuit · sandwich · juice · orange · potatoes · bread · chocolate); (Backup: I draw/write the prompts on the board, and add the key by hand)
👦 Students do: solo, they write 8 words at speed, honestly count the clean ones, score privately in the margin. Expected: 4–6 out of 8 — the ceiling is exposed. Typical error: “sandwitch/bisquit” — I don't shame it: “there's our growth zone”.

Challenge: the clue duel (5-second rule)
🔥 Challenge · clue duel
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: the three-minute challenge ex.6 by the 5-second rule; then teams write THEIR OWN clues and duel

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1 (ex.6): Eleven clues, answers from exercise 5 — five seconds per clue or it bounces to the next crew! Round the circle with rotation. Key: 1 tea · 2 soup · 3 chicken · 4 apple · 5 sandwich · 6 orange · 7 meat · 8 rice · 9 peas · 10 oil · 11 juice.
  2. I run it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face down, on the command No peeking… Boards up! they lift together — I see everyone's answer, nobody can hide.
  3. The book's twist: Which word is MISSING? First crew — plus three! (biscuit)
  4. Round 2: Write TWO your own clues — any food from today. Duel the next crew! 2 min.
  5. I moderate the duels; the author bluffing (doesn't know their own answer) = −2.
📺 On screen: the SB p.37 ex.6 · timer · the definition frame on the board: “This is… / You can make this from… / You eat this…”
👦 Students do: they answer the clues in 5 sec, find the missing biscuit, invent their own clues and race their neighbours. Expected: “The name of this fruit is the same as its colour — orange!”. Typical error: a clue in Russian — it doesn't count, the frame is on the board.
⭐ For the fast ones (TB Fast finishers): write a clue for the missing word (biscuit) — so your neighbours guess it in one go.		duel frames · mini-boards

🚀 Bonus level · not in the book
circle

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin: a chain round the circle “I've tried…, but I'd never eat it again / and I'd eat it every day” — no repeating food, a class streak

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I raise the status: This structure is NOT in this book. Beginners can't do it. You can — GMF4 grads only.
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. I write the frame: I've tried ___ , and I'd eat it every day. / but I'd never eat it again. I model it myself (honestly and funny).
  4. The chain goes round the circle: no repeating food; if the streak breaks — count starts again (the class against itself).
  5. +1 to the leaderboard for every clean bonus sentence.
📺 On screen: board: the bonus frame large · streak counter in the corner
👦 Students do: round the circle they build a sentence with perfect + would, watch for repeated food. Expected: a streak of 8–12, dramatic confessions. Typical error: “I tried” — I show the frame: exactly as written, that IS the level.

Prepare the pitch
desks · crews

WHAT WE DO: crews invent a signature pizza and prepare a pitch: everyone says ≥2 sentences, notes in keywords only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I set the task: Invent your SIGNATURE pizza: name + 4 ingredients + who it's for. Hook opening. Nine minutes.
  2. Rules on the slide: everyone says ≥2 sentences · minimum 4 food words · 1 bonus structure = richness · “nice/good” — taboo.
  3. ICQ: Full script to read? — No! Keywords only!
  4. I help with hook phrases (Bokang's phrase bank helps). A cultural note from the TB: in Sweden they put peanuts on it, in Japan — squid; your pizza can be bolder.
📺 On screen: pitch rules slide · judges' rubric large · phrase bank on the board (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they invent the pizza, split up the lines, write keywords (not text!). Expected: “Pizza Norilsk: reindeer meat!” — delight. Typical error: they write a full script — I take the sheet away, leave 4 words.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses no longer than 3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — clean forms (1–3) · Richness — quantifiers, connectors, bonus structures (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		prep sheets · rubric slide

🏁 PRODUCT · signature pizza pitches
stage

WHAT WE DO: every crew pitches for 60 sec against the stopwatch, the rest are judges with the rubric; the final — a secret vote “would buy / wouldn't buy”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I start it: Crew one — sixty seconds — GO! The stopwatch is visible.
  2. I hand out judging cards: fluency/accuracy/richness, 1–3.
  3. After every pitch one judge: Defend ONE score in English! (Richness 3 — they used ‘I've tried’ AND five food words.)
  4. Final: a secret sticker vote Would you BUY it? — the champion pizza gets +3.
  5. I average it and log it to the leaderboard live.
📺 On screen: stopwatch on screen · judging cards · voting stickers · leaderboard (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they pitch from keywords with no reading; judges count pauses on their fingers and score 1–3, one explains it out loud; they vote for other crews' pizzas. Expected: 1–2 crews close to a gold run. Typical error: a pause >3 sec — the judges log it, the bar teaches itself.

Structure

Form
plurals: -es after -s/-sh/-ch/-x + tomatoes/potatoes (SB p.36 box); nationality-style caution: bread/cheese/water usually singular.
Pron. watch
silent letters: ju(i)ce · bisc(u)it · me(a)t · sandwich /ˈsænwɪdʒ/; chocolate — 2 syllables; vegetables — 3 syllables (TB).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“You've eaten these words for years. Does eating = writing ‘biscuit’ right in 10 seconds?” → that's the gate.
“Two potato?” → potatoES — the -es club (tomatoes too).
“In the pitch, is ‘it's nice’ enough?” → No — say WHAT's on it and WHO it's for.

Assumptions: Post-GMF4: most of the 21 items met orally in GMF; real gaps: SPELLING (silent letters), -es plurals, sustained fluent speech about food. C/UC is NOT presented today — only heard in frames. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
