🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Consolidate the block's language — arts and entertainment, twelve jobs, both word-building families, reported commands and the second conditional — and sit the official Cambridge Units 17–18 tests under calm exam conditions.
What is genuinely hard
Not the content — the retrieval, and the spelling. This block is two-thirds word building, so marks leak letter by letter: guitarist, receptionist, historical, peaceful. The grammar ghosts are the two named all block: said me to, and would in the if-half.
Typical error to expect
Under time pressure the old ghosts return: said me to, to not go, if I would have, musicion. The briefing names all four aloud BEFORE the paper is turned over.
Why the lesson is built this way
The two-half test-day shape the class has known since block 5: 45 minutes of retrieval, then the papers. Three stations rather than two this time, because the third one has a job — the book's own Review 5 spread (p.118) contains three tasks whose language is Units 17–18, and they are played TODAY while the block is warm. Block 10 runs the full Review 5 spread at lesson 67; meeting these lines twice, two weeks apart, is spacing, not repetition. Then the official Crazy sentences game with the eight cards whose language is ours, a briefing that names the ghosts, a real break, and 40 minutes of paper.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Three weeks: who really writes the songs, twelve jobs, two grammar machines and about forty new spellings. First we play it all back — then you show it on paper. Everything in the test you have already done in this room.”

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a 90-second retrieval flood, biographies returned, and the day's map

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the block in one breath. Kagan RallyRobin: pairs alternate naming ANYTHING learned in Units 17–18 — a job, an adjective, a reporting verb, a rule — no repeats, 90 seconds.
  3. Hand back the biographies from lesson 2 while they do it, face down, no comments yet.
  4. Frame the day: Half one — we play everything back, including three tasks from the book's own Review 5. Half two — the official test. Nothing in it will surprise you. That's a promise with evidence.
  5. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): into the stations: Three stations, one game, one break, one paper. Go.
📺 On screen: voices only — the voice is the medium here, so nothing can fail and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: flood the room with the block's language in 90 seconds; take back their biographies; hear the day's shape and relax into it

WHAT WE DO: Quiz-Quiz-Trade across both units, then a spelling volley

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The full card set out: arts words, twelve jobs, -al/-ful adjectives, person nouns — definition or gesture on one side, answer on the other.
  2. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade, five minutes, maximum trades: quiz, coach if stuck, trade, move.
  3. Sit on a clap. Mini-whiteboard spelling volley, eight words chosen because the test asks for them: guitarist · receptionist · scientist · designer · historical · peaceful · sculpture · architect.
  4. Fix the casualties with a smile and no lecture. Designer isn't in your book — but the ending is, and it's in the paper. Now it's yours.
📺 On screen: printed card sets from lessons 1–5 plus mini-whiteboards — the cards are the medium; if a set is missing, read definitions aloud and rows answer chorally
👦 Students do: quiz, coach, trade, then spell the monsters. Expect the two word-building families to interfere (-ful versus -ist) — that interference surfaces best today, before the paper

WHAT WE DO: mini-whiteboard Showdown: name the rule, then write the form

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Eight items read aloud, one at a time — a gap or an error, drawn from the block's own exercises, never from the test.
  2. Kagan Showdown: everyone writes; on three-two-one — show! all boards up at once.
  3. Every check names the machine first: Which rule? Reported command. Now the form: warned us not to open.
  4. Keep a public tally of which rule misses most — it heads the briefing in twenty minutes.
📺 On screen: items by voice, answers on mini-whiteboards — the boards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write, show, name the rule. Everyone answers every item — no hiding, and the class SEES which rule still needs work

WHAT WE DO: the three tasks of the book's Review 5 whose language is Units 17–18

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Open at p.118 with one instruction: SB p.118 (Review 5, Units 17–20) — only the three tasks whose language is Units 17–18. The rest of the spread belongs to block 10, lesson 67; do NOT open p.119 today.
  2. Task one, three minutes — the jobs with their vowels missing plus the six Who…? questions. Vocabulary ex.2 — jobs with the vowels missing, then six Who…? questions. Key: babysitter, firefighter, architect, presenter, journalist, pharmacist → 1 the most dangerous job = firefighter · 2 works with children = babysitter · 3 writes about the news = journalist · 4 designs buildings = architect · 5 introduces radio and TV shows = presenter · 6 sells medicines = pharmacist.
  3. Task two, two minutes — word building. Vocabulary ex.4 — build the noun or adjective. Key: 1 vegetable → vegetarian · 2 blog → blogger · 3 nature → natural · 4 piano → pianist · 5 compete → competitors · 6 pain → painful. This is the exam's word-building line in miniature.
  4. Task three, three minutes — second conditional rewrites. Grammar ex.2 — second conditional transformations. The printed model is: If my cousins lived near me, I would see them more often. Five to rewrite, each one a real-into-imaginary flip. Teams check each other's answers before you confirm anything.
  5. Say the reason out loud, because teenagers notice: We're doing three tasks from a page you'll open again in two weeks. Meeting them twice, far apart, is how memory actually works.

👦 Students do: solve, cross-check, hear why the page is being half-used. Expect pianist in the word-building task to be the one nobody predicts — it is also a free -ist reminder

Why: The official Cambridge game hides revision inside a folded piece of paper: every player writes a reported command or a second conditional blind, and the class hears eight of them read out. A class that has just laughed walks into a test calmer.

WHAT WE DO: Crazy sentences! — the eight Units 17–18 cards from the official pack

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set up Crazy sentences! (Review game Units 17–20, Cambridge, PHOTOCOPIABLE 2019): Groups of three or more. Each group gets sentence cards with THREE gaps. The first player writes a continuation in the first gap, folds the paper so nobody can see it, and passes to the left; the next player fills gap two blind, folds, passes; the third fills gap three. Then the papers are unfolded and the crazy sentences are read aloud. There are no points in the official rules — award them for humour, originality, grammar and spelling if the group needs a scoreboard.
  2. Today's deck: Block 10 will play the full Units 17–20 deck at Review 5. TODAY'S legal subset is the eight cards whose language is ours: the firefighters who warned us not to… · the lawyer who advised her to… · the sculpture… · the advert that persuaded her to buy… · the vet who thought «If I had more…, I could…» · the pharmacist and the painful… · the lady in the audience who was asked to leave… · «What would you do if you saw…?» said the babysitter. Eight cards at two to three minutes each fills the slot.
  3. One rule added to the official ones, because it turns the game into revision: whatever you write in a gap must be GRAMMATICAL — a crazy sentence with a broken conditional scores nothing, however funny.
  4. File: course folder → материалы → Review Games Worksheets → PREPARE_4_Review_game_Units_17-20.pdf. Slower, more writing: the Dictation version — each player copies the WHOLE sentence before adding their part. Follow-up for any group: reconstruct the funniest full sentence in the notebook, correctly spelled.
📺 On screen: printed game cards — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: write blind, fold, pass, then hear eight ridiculous sentences read out. Every player writes in every round — the folding is what makes it impossible to hide

WHAT WE DO: name the ghosts, plant the recovery moves, answer questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The tally speaks first: the most-missed rule from station 2 gets 90 seconds of surgical repair on the board.
  2. The four ghosts named aloud with their fixes: said me to → TOLD me to. to not go → NOT TO go. if I would have → if I HAD. musicion → musicIAN.
  3. Paper mechanics: two papers back to back, 64 items, the verb in brackets may change form but not identity, margins are legal for notes.
  4. Two honest heads-ups, so nobody freezes: One reporting verb in the paper is encourage — we did it in lesson one. One word-building item is designer — the ending you drilled twenty minutes ago.
  5. Last questions taken. Break. Three minutes. Water, corridor, back.
📺 On screen: the ghost list in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: hear the repairs while there is still time to use them; ask the last questions now, not mid-paper

WHAT WE DO: a real break before a long paper

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Game adrenaline crosses into the test halfThe break is real and the room is reset during it: desks apart, papers face down, volume to zero with the standard signal. Ritual beats speeches.
A student blanks on the second conditional mid-testThe briefing plants the recovery move: draw the chalk line in the margin, put IF + past on the left and WOULD on the right, and rebuild from it. Margins are legal.
Early finishers fidgetThe rule announced with the paper: finished = check the trap list (every suffix spelled, every negative before its to, no would on the left) against YOUR OWN answers, then read quietly. Nobody leaves early.
Somebody is absent todayStandard make-up: they sit the same papers before the next lesson (both tests also exist as editable RTF, with and without answers); the game owes them nothing.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
