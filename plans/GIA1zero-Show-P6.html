WHAT WE DO: we fill in the windows: title + 4 facts from Quick review notes + pictures; a spelling check by swap

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Teams fill in the windows; EVERYONE can write (one window per person).
  2. I go round with prompts: a fact doesn't come on its own — we take it from Quick review and simplify it together.
  3. A spelling check by swap: teams swap stands for 60 sec, hunting for letters (+a smiley for a find).
  4. Pictures get glued on AFTER the text (the rule “language before beauty”).
📺 On screen: templates · pictures · glue
👦 Students do: they fill in one window per person — everyone's contribution is visible and manageable. Expected: 4 facts in 12 minutes for everyone. Typical error: a fact written in Russian — we simplify and translate it together, the notes help.

The stand game (from the menu)
Build · a game from the menu

WHAT WE DO: the stand game from a menu of 3 mechanics; a one-line rule; tested on the neighbouring team

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The menu: (1) guess from a slice of the picture · (2) find the pair (word-picture) · (3) a yes/no question (Is it a banana?).
  2. A one-line rule from notes: Guess the word! · Find the pair! · Ask yes/no!
  3. Props from what's brought in + craft materials; tested on the neighbouring team for 2 min.
  4. A gate: the guest READS/SPEAKS/LISTENS in English during the game — I check with every team.
📺 On screen: menu of mechanics · craft materials
👦 Students do: they pick a ready-made mechanic and test it — the game works the first time. Expected: half pick the guessing game — and it's great.

WHAT WE DO: a chorus drill of the 3 host phrases + pair mini-pitches; the first phrase is said together

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. In chorus with energy: Welcome to our stand! · Our stand is about…! · Well done! ×3, louder-quieter-just right.
  2. Pairs of hosts: the first phrase TOGETHER in chorus — that's how we start at the fair.
  3. Mini-pitches: every team — 15 sec to the class (from the phrase card).
  4. Praise for the guest with gestures: Well done! · So close! · Try again!
📺 On screen: the 3 phrase cards
👦 Students do: they drill the phrases on their feet and pitch in pairs — “not scary with two” takes the tension off calling out. Expected: voices switch on.

Q&A training: the year's familiar questions (QQT)
Practise · Q&A from the familiar deck (QQT)

WHAT WE DO: Kagan · Quiz-Quiz-Trade: a deck of guests' FAMILIAR questions — we rehearse the answers with the whole class at once

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ Kagan · Quiz-Quiz-Trade: everyone has a guest-question card with an answer hint: What's this? · Do you like…? · Can you…? · How many…?
  2. Ask — answer — TRADE — new partner! 5–6 trades.
  3. A safety-net formula on the board: Good question! It's a… (for “I forgot”).
  4. Wrap-up: These are the ONLY questions guests will ask. You just answered them all!
📺 On screen: the familiar-question deck (🐣 asset)
👦 Students do: they run through ALL of tomorrow's guest questions in advance — there'll be no surprises at the fair, confidence is rock solid. Expected: they recognise the cards (“this is our QQT from pair 61!”).

Run-through · a mini dress rehearsal

WHAT WE DO: a dress rehearsal from notes: half host (phrases from the card), half are guests with the deck; rotation

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Half at the stands (phrase cards in hand), half are guests with the deck of questions.
  2. The guest: play + ask 1 question FROM THE DECK; the host: 3 phrases + an answer + praise.
  3. Rotate roles every 6 min — everyone in both roles.
  4. I go round as a guest: playing, asking from the deck, praising specifically.
📺 On screen: stands · the deck · phrase cards
👦 Students do: a full cycle of the fair from notes, both ways round — tomorrow will feel “already done before”. Expected: the games work, the phrases come out. Typical error: the host stays silent and waits — the phrase card in hand is the reminder.

  3. ⭐ For the confident: an “upgrade” station — add a 5th fact-question to your stand for guests (Did you know…?).

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, teams have a fair stand from the template (a poster + a simple game) and can present it with 3 core phrases; simple Q&A is rehearsed.
  • Subsidiary. The stand content = a review of the unit from Quick review WITH NOTES; review stations for the year's weak spots run alongside (KTP); guests will ask FAMILIAR questions — that's exactly what we rehearse.
  • Personal. The template solves everything: a title window, 4 fact windows, a game corner. Q&A with no tricks: only questions from the familiar deck (What's this? Do you like…? Can you…?) — success is guaranteed by preparation.
By the end, students can…
  • fill the stand template with unit facts
  • run a simple guest game with a rule of one line
  • say the 3 hosting phrases
  • answer familiar questions with support

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Will guests ask something scary?” → No: guests will have a card of FAMILIAR questions — we all rehearse them today.
“Do we have to invent the stand?” → Fill in the template: the windows are already drawn. We only invent the game — from the familiar list.
“Does the game need to be complicated?” → Simple is better: a guessing game with 3 pictures is the perfect fair game.

Assumptions: Topics handed out (P5), pictures brought in; each unit's Quick review is the source of facts (I bring the pages to the desks). The “familiar questions” deck is ready in advance — for hosts and guests both.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Unit facts are forgottenThe unit's Quick review page on the team's desk + I go round: we collect 4 facts together with the notes “It is… · We like… · You can…”.
They invent a complicated gameA menu of 3 ready-made mechanics (guess from a slice · find the pair · a yes/no question) — they pick, they don't invent.
Too shy to call out to guestsA chorus drill of the calling-out line + rule: the first phrase is said TOGETHER (the pair of hosts) — it's not scary with two.
Review stations feel like a punishmentStations = “gearing up for the fair”: earn a smiley token for your team at every station.
Materials forgottenSpare craft materials + printed Quick review pages: the template gets filled in no matter what.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
