👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

🏁 PRODUCT B · photo dialogues
stage

WHAT WE DO: Timed Pair Share: Skills boost: THINK (their own photos) → PREPARE (a dialogue, a 4-word card) → PRACTISE ×2 → PERFORM with rotation; peer review ex.6

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK (2 min): 2–3 photos on the phone OR a quick sketch of three people (TB option).
  2. The format is Kagan Timed Pair Share: A speaks their time against the timer, B is the active listener (counts pauses on fingers, catches the best phrase), then Switch roles! The listener's response starts with My favourite bit was…
  3. PREPARE (5 min): a dialogue with an example from EVERY phrase group; a key card, max 4 words, the judge checks it BEFORE the stage.
  4. PRACTISE: two run-throughs, the second without the card; fillers are legal.
  5. PERFORM: pairs at the “stand” for 60–90 sec, partner rotation ×2, then 2 pairs for the whole class; peer review ex.6: which phrases are for people? for questions? for opinions? — judges answer out loud.
  6. Points by the rubric + bonus lines to the leaderboard.
📺 On screen: phones/drawings · key cards · rubric · stopwatch
👦 Students do: they act out live dialogues about THEIR OWN photos, judge by the three phrase groups. Expected: genuine “That's really funny!” over other people's photos. Typical error: smuggling a script — the judge confiscates it before the stage.
🏆 The bar for the product (the judges' rubric, every pair): Fluency — pauses ≤3 sec (1–3) · Accuracy — the form is clean (1–3) · Richness — adjectives, details, bonus structures (1–3). 9/9 = a “gold run”, +5 to the leaderboard.		phones/drawings · rubric cards

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can run inverted Have/Has…got? interrogations with etiquette-perfect short answers (Act 1, 45′) and perform a live photo dialogue with all three Key-phrase groups (Act 2, 45′).
  • Subsidiary. Gate exposes written word order; question tables + etiquette; grid drills; a/an vs any; Ask Alex TWINS round-up as a team quiz (closes P3 case); RWS video model; Skills boost THINK→PREPARE→PRACTISE→PERFORM; peer review; GMF4 bonus upgrades the dialogue.
  • Personal. Inversion is automatic orally — the written gate proves where it isn't. KTP boundary: lesson A (questions) = 0–45′, lesson B (Real-world speaking) = 45–90′. Push question VARIETY and opinion phrases with real enthusiasm (TB: say them like you MEAN them).
By the end, students can…
  • write 6 correctly-ordered Have/Has got questions in 90 sec
  • hold short-answer etiquette under 5-sec fire
  • win «20 questions» with varied questions
  • perform a photo dialogue with opinion phrases

Structure
Have you got any cousins? — Yes, I have. · Has she got curly hair? — No, she hasn't. · Who's this? That's my auntie. That's a great photo! · 🚀 bonus: This is my cousin — he's never lost a game of chess.
Form
inversion: Have/Has jumps left; short answers: Yes, I have (never «Yes, I've» — TB Common error!); a/an + singular, any + plural (Language note).
Pron. watch
questions rise ↗; short answer rhythm Yes-I-HAVE / No-she-HASn't; opinion phrases with enthusiasm: That's a GREAT photo!
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“You've asked questions for years. Written, ordered, 15 sec each — sure?” → gate.
“Yes, I've. — professional?” → Never: Yes, I have.
“Has she got ANY blue eyes?” → No — we KNOW she's got eyes; any = for unknown plurals.

Assumptions: have got statements systematised in P3; inversion familiar from GMF4. Ceilings: written word order at speed, etiquette under pressure, question variety, opinion phrases with real intonation. Gate is diagnosis, not humiliation: the ceiling must be REAL (spelling/speed/writing), results are private tallies, the reveal is “so THIS is our growth zone”.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
