🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Show that the vocabulary and grammar of two units work without the book: twelve feelings adjectives, the -ed/-ing switch, just/already/yet in their slots, twelve genres, seven film words, who/which/that/where by what the noun is — and correct spelling.
What is genuinely hard
Not the tasks — the gap between oral and written: out loud the class knows almost everything, on paper embarrassed loses an r, disappointed gains an s, already sneaks into negatives, and what creeps back in as glue under time pressure.
Typical error to expect
embarrassed — TWO r, TWO s · disappointed — one s, two p · exhausted — the h hides in the middle · soap opera is two words · science fiction — sci before ence; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
the jacket WHAT I bought · a man WHAT works here — Russian «что» pulled straight into English; the glue words are who for people, which/that for things, where for places — never what [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson is built as retrieval practice, not re-teaching: two stations make students pull the block out of memory, which is what fixes it — and shows you, twenty minutes before the paper, which form still breaks. The grammar station quietly borrows the Unit 13–14 lines of the book's Review 4 (the full spread gets its own lesson after block 8 — its U15–16 lines stay untouched). The official Cambridge game «List it!» sits between the stations and the briefing on purpose: its own scoring pays a point for correct spelling, which is exactly where this block's marks leak — and ⚠️ the U15–16 cards are REMOVED before class, not played as bonus: unlike December's game, those units are still unlearned. Nothing new is introduced anywhere today, and the five-mistake lists that leave this room are the entry ticket to block 8.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Nothing new today. Everything on these two papers you have already done in this room — I can name the lesson for every line. First forty-five minutes we play it back — and in today's game, SPELLING scores points, so embarrassed had better bring both of its r's. Then forty-five minutes you show it. Your score is not the point: the point is your own list of five mistakes — your ticket into the next block.”

Opening · check-in + rewind
door → desks
Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, a fast card round of the block's words, and the bridge into the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — hot round. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with the block's word cards (feelings + genres + film words), two trades each: Quiz, coach, trade — go!
  3. Sit them down on a clap and be honest: Everything you just said is in today's test.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): Two halves today: forty-five minutes to remember, forty-five to show.
📺 On screen: printed word cards for the block — the cards in their hands are the medium, so no backup is needed; if the set has gone missing, call definitions and pairs answer chorally
👦 Students do: trade cards round the room. Expect a fast pace and the anxiety dropping. Typical stumbles: embarrassed and soap opera — both spelling, both in the stations

Lesson frame
board
Why: Students hear the one aim and the standard their answers must reach. Naming the bar now means you can hold them to it later without it feeling like a punishment.

WHAT WE DO: put the test sections up in advance — transparency takes the nerves away

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Write the six sections of the two papers on the board, one line each: feelings adjectives · -ed/-ing · just/already/yet ×2 · genres + film words · relative clauses ×2.
  2. Look at the board. Have we done all of this in class? Yes or no? The class answers chorally — the answer is always yes; if anyone doubts, name the lesson for that line.
  3. The rule of the day: Your score goes in the register. Your five mistakes go in your notebook — and that list is your TICKET into block 8. Bring it.
📺 On screen: the six section lines in chalk — the board is the medium and it stays up for the whole first half, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: read the sections and answer chorally. Expect visible relief. Typical problem — they don't believe «we have done all of it»: point line by line at the lesson it came from

8–20′
Review station 1 · words
teams
Why: Retrieval practice rather than re-teaching: they pull the block's vocabulary out of memory in short bursts, which is what makes it stay.

WHAT WE DO: the block's vocabulary: 30+ words, against the clock and in writing

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Round 1, oral: definitions at speed — very tired after a marathon? (exhausted) · a film that makes you laugh? (comedy) · the music of a film? (soundtrack) · thanks-feeling when someone helps? (grateful) · a short advert for a new film? (trailer). Twelve items.
  2. Round 2, the switch: you name the situation, teams fire BOTH forms — six lessons in one day! (tiring / tired) · my little brother! (annoying / annoyed). Six items.
  3. Round 3, written: Kagan Showdown — you say the meaning, teams WRITE the word. Load the traps deliberately: embarrassed, disappointed, exhausted, soap opera, science fiction, documentary.
  4. Every misspelling goes into a «watch out» column on the board — it stays there right up to the test.
📺 On screen: definitions read aloud, answers on mini-whiteboards — voice and boards are the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: say, fire pairs, then write the words. Expect almost everything orally and a visible drop in the written round — which is exactly why the column exists. Typical error: embarassed

20–32′
Review station 2 · grammar
pairs → class
Why: Same logic for grammar — and it shows you, twenty minutes before the test, exactly which form still breaks. The sentences quietly come from the book's own Review 4, Units 13–14 lines only.

WHAT WE DO: ten mixed sentences with the reason every time — quietly borrowed from Review 4's U13–14 lines

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ten sentences, one at a time — four on just/already/yet (including My friend hasn't arrived already/yet and the Review 4 transformation I finished a few minutes ago → I've JUST finished), four on relative clauses (including She's the new actor WHO PLAYS Juliet and one what-plant — catch it!), two on -ed/-ing.
  2. Kagan RallyCoach: the partner asks for the REASON, not the answer — Say WHY, not just the answer.
  3. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Sentence six — number fours, boards up!
  4. Boil every explanation down to the step: negative → yet at the end; person → who; place → where, never that; the person feels → -ed.
📺 On screen: the ten sentences written on the board before the lesson and uncovered one by one with a sheet of paper — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: solve in pairs with an explanation, then report by number. Expect the what-plant to be caught with joy — that is the point of planting it. Typical error: already in the negative — the slot rule, one more time

32–40′
Review game
teams of 2–3
Why: The official Cambridge game hides revision inside a race to five — and its own scoring pays a point for correct spelling, which is exactly where this block's marks leak. A class that has just competed walks into a test calmer.

WHAT WE DO: «List it!» — the official Cambridge review game, U13–14 cards only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Rules in one minute: Teams of one to three; a pile of «List 5…» cards face down. A player draws a card and reads it out (List 5 situations where people feel embarrassed…). ALL teams race to write five valid items; the first to five shouts «Listed!» and everyone stops. The fast team reads its list out; other teams tick off items they also have. Scoring: 1 point for finishing first + 1 per item nobody else has + 1 if the whole list is spelled correctly — so spelling wins games.
  2. ⚠️ The pack covers Units 13–16, and Units 15–16 have not been taught yet — so before class REMOVE the thirteen U15–16 cards (computer files, passwords, things you install/switch on/share online, things you tie/shake/wrap/stir, experiments, apps). Today's legal deck is the eight U13–14 cards: situations where people feel disappointed / proud / embarrassed / stressed · soap operas · comedies · science fiction films · reality shows. Eight cards at two minutes each fills the slot exactly.
  3. Sharper groups: the teacher's «list unlimited» version — as many items as possible in 60 seconds, one point per unique item.
  4. Set the bar: The other teams judge your list — so listen like judges: five REAL items, spelled right? The spelling point decides games.
📺 On screen: one cut card set, U15–16 cards removed in advance — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed; with no printer, read the eight card texts from this plan aloud, one per round
👦 Students do: draw, race to five, judge each other, score — with the spelling bonus deciding at least one round. Expect «situations where people feel embarrassed» to produce the best list of the day; expect arguments about validity — referee impartially, arguing in English is the game working

40–45′
Test briefing
class
Why: The errors from the stations get named and fixed while the test is still ahead — feedback is only useful when there is still time to use it.

WHAT WE DO: feedback on what dropped at the stations, plus the instructions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Back to the «watch out» column: the five words that were misspelt. Look at these five words for thirty seconds. Then I close them.
  2. Instructions: two official papers, Unit 13 then Unit 14, about 35 minutes total; phones in the bag; finished early — five film riddles on the back. Two honest heads-ups: Paper one uses lonely and helpful as wrong options, and interested and shocking in the -ed/-ing part — you know them all. Paper two's film-word section has SEVEN questions, not eight. Nothing is missing.
  3. Silent mode — a hand held flat, palm down. From that moment, silence.
  4. Hand the papers out face down: Do not turn the paper. Names first.
📺 On screen: the «watch out» column stays in chalk and you wipe it just before handing the papers out — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: memorise the traps, put phones away, write their names. Expect silence to settle in 20 seconds if the flat hand goes up before you start talking

47–83′
Block test
solo, silent mode
Why: Exam conditions, familiar formats, official papers. Everything in them has been done in class, so the test measures learning, not surprise.

WHAT WE DO: the official Unit 13 and Unit 14 tests, back to back — 63 items in about 35 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Turn the paper. Unit 13 first. You have thirty-five minutes for both papers. Start.
  2. Walk the room in silence. To any question about content answer only Read the instruction again.
  3. Write the time left in chalk: 20 · 10 · 5 minutes — in writing, never out loud.
  4. Anyone who finishes early writes five film riddles with who/which/where on the back and sits quietly.
  5. Two minutes before the end: Two minutes. Check your spelling — embarrassed, disappointed, soap opera.
📺 On screen: the minutes remaining written on the board — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; nothing at all is put up in the room while the test is running
👦 Students do: work in silence for 35 minutes. Expect the two vocabulary sections to be closed fast, the U13 sentence-building and the U14 joining to slow them down. Typical error: what for which in the joining section — it was mentioned in the briefing, and it will still happen
printed test papers · timer
83–90′
Test wrap-up
circle
Why: Papers in, tension out: they name one thing they now know they can do, and leave with the next step rather than with a mark.

WHAT WE DO: papers in, personal reflection, homework

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Collect the papers in silence, in one pile, without comment.
  2. Personal reflection on a slip, not out loud: Write ONE thing you are sure you got right, and ONE thing you are not sure about. Collect the slips — that is your material for the next lesson's five minutes of feedback.
  3. Homework: once the papers come back — rewrite five of your own mistakes correctly; that list is the ENTRY TICKET to block 8, collected at the door. Plus one unfinished WB page of your choice from Units 13–14.
  4. Teaser: Next lesson: Unit fifteen — Digital life. Apps, viruses, passwords… and how English says «кнопка, которую нельзя нажимать». You'll survive. Probably.
📺 On screen: the two reflection questions in chalk — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed; if the board is full, read them out and students write on their slip
👦 Students do: hand the papers in and write the reflection. Expect «not sure» to name the U13 sentence-building and the joining most often — that is the plan for next lesson's feedback five minutes

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Nerves before the testSay it out loud and early: nothing will come out of thin air, every format has been done. Put the section list on the board before you start.
Someone finishes both papers in 25 minutesThe rule: hand them in, turn the sheet over and write five one-line film riddles with who/which/where. It is read, not marked.
After the test they want to compare answers out loudCollect the papers in silence; the analysis is next lesson. Today, once a paper is in, the only thing left is personal reflection.
The game deck feels thin with 13 cards removedEight cards at two minutes each fills the slot exactly — and the «list unlimited» variant stretches any card for a strong group. Do not deal the U15–16 cards «for fun»: unlearned words kill the race.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
