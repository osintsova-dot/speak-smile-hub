🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why (for the teacher): For a student who joined from outside, a midterm is more dangerous than it is for our own: they already compare themselves with the class in their head and they already know they will “be the worst”. Run the lesson in the usual way and the paper will measure their anxiety, not their language. So the whole design serves one aim: remove the unknown. The limits of the paper are announced in advance and narrowed down to three physical pages of the book. The scale is on the board from minute one. Before every section a model of one item stays on the board — not the answer, but the format, so that no time is lost on “what am I supposed to do here”. And the second half of the lesson matters more than the first: the student marks their own paper against the key, puts stickers on it and lays their September sheet next to it. A new student's growth since September is usually bigger than our own students' — and they have to see it with their eyes, not hear it from you.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Half the way. Today is a check — not an exam, a mark on the map: where am I now? Seventy points, forty minutes, and everything in it comes from three pages: 21, 33 and 45. Nothing else. Look — the scale is on the board already, before we start, so nobody guesses. First we warm up together, with models on the board. Then you work. Then YOU mark your own paper with the key and put a sticker on it. And at the end I give you back your September sheet — you'll see the difference yourself. Open page 21.”
The gist, if you need to restate it: today is a check — not an exam, a mark on the map: where am I now. 70 marks, 40 minutes, and everything in it comes from three pages: 21, 33 and 45. The scale is on the board from the start. First we warm up with models, then you work, then you mark yourself and compare with September.

WHAT WE DO: three blocks of about 4.5 minutes — one per unit: model on the board → mini-task → key → sticker

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Announce it directly, as always in this line: Today we check present simple, adverbs and likes, and present continuous. Three blocks, one per unit.
  2. Block 1 · SB p.21 (Unit 1). MODEL on the board: He gets up at seven. He doesn't study French. Does she study music? — Yes, she does. I like HER. Mini-task: 5 items on the form (two of them on -ies/-es) + 1 on an object pronoun. Key on the slide → self-check → sticker.
  3. Block 2 · SB p.33 (Unit 2). MODEL: She often plays chess. He is always late. I love skateboarding. The rule in one line on the board: “with be — AFTER, with every other verb — BEFORE”. Mini-task: 4 items on the position of the adverb (one with be) + 2 on -ing after like/hate. Key → sticker.
  4. Block 3 · SB p.45 (Unit 3). MODEL: I'm reading. He isn't taking photos. Are they having fun? — Yes, they are. Spell it out separately: take → taking, swim → swimming. Mini-task: 5 forms (two on spelling) + 2 on choosing simple/continuous from the marker. Key → sticker.
  5. The last 60 seconds: Books closed. Quick reviews closed. The two time columns stay on the board — they stay for everyone, all lesson.
  6. 🟢🟡🔴 Self-check stickers: after every block the student puts a sticker in the margin: 🟢 got it · 🟡 so-so · 🔴 didn't get it. It is NOT a grade — it is your map of who to go to. On this lesson the stickers matter even more: they turn a test paper into a tool instead of a verdict.
📺 On screen: SB p.21, p.33, p.45 · three slides with the keys to the mini-tasks · stickers (Backup: write the models on the board in marker and do NOT rub them out; print the keys, one sheet per desk — that way nobody waits for the class pace)
👦 Students do: students look at the model, do the mini-task, check themselves, put on a sticker. Expected: block 2 produces the most 🔴 — the position of the adverb and -ing after like sag for most new students. Error: “She plays often” — the one-line rule on the board catches it, provided it is up there and not just spoken

THE PAPER · half the way
solo

WHAT WE DO: 40 minutes: the 70-mark paper, eight sections (specification in the reference at the end of this plan)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The first minute — read the rubrics together, all eight sections, aloud. Only then start the timer. For new students this is not a lost minute, it saves five: they do not freeze on the wording.
  2. Forty minutes. Easy points first. Stuck? Mark it, move on, come back. A blank line is a guaranteed zero — always write something.
  3. Raise your hand for the TASK, never for the answer. I will explain what to do as many times as you need.
  4. Time on the board, updated three times in silence: 30 · 15 · 5 minutes left.
  5. The listening (Section G) at minute 25 of the paper, twice, with a 30-second pause: Pens down. Thirty seconds to read the questions. Then twice.
  6. The writing (Section H) is last, and remind them at the “15 left” mark: Fifteen left — start the writing now if you haven't.
  7. Once, halfway through, walk past every desk in silence — so that a quiet student can raise a hand without words. After that stop walking: sit down and do your own work.
📺 On screen: the papers (built from the specification at the end of this plan) · audio for Section G · a timer on the board · the two columns of time markers stay on the board (Backup with no equipment: read the listening text yourself, twice, at the same pace — it does not affect the marks, and the class knows this in advance)
👦 Students do: students work in silence for 40 minutes. Expected: sections A and E go fastest (familiar September formats), section D (the simple/continuous contrast) is the slowest. Error: they skip an item instead of writing something — repeat the blank-line rule before the start and once at the “15 left” mark

WHAT WE DO: students mark themselves against the key, circle every slip, put a sticker on every section and write three “to work on” lines

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Keys out. Mark your own paper. Circle EVERY slip — the -s, the apostrophe, the capital letter. Circling is not losing points. Circling is finding what to fix.
  2. A sticker on EVERY section, not one for the whole paper: that shows exactly where the hole is. 🟢 got it · 🟡 so-so · 🔴 didn't get it.
  3. They add up their own score and look at the scale — it has been on the board since minute one. No scores are said out loud.
  4. Now three lines at the top: what you work on before Unit 4. Not ‘grammar’. Write the real thing: ‘-s in he/she/it’, ‘adverb before the verb’, ‘-ing after like’. Write where to look next to each line yourself, as you walk the rows.
  5. Collect the papers only after the three lines are copied into the exercise book: The three lines stay with you. The paper comes to me.
  6. 🟢🟡🔴 Self-check stickers: after every block the student puts a sticker in the margin: 🟢 got it · 🟡 so-so · 🔴 didn't get it. It is NOT a grade — it is your map of who to go to. On this lesson the stickers matter even more: they turn a test paper into a tool instead of a verdict.
📺 On screen: printed keys, one per desk · stickers · the scale on the board (Backup: read the key aloud item by item and students tick — slower, but it is essential that the student marks their own paper, not their neighbour and not you)
👦 Students do: students mark themselves, put stickers on each section, write three lines. Expected: new students mark themselves more harshly than you would — they take marks off where you would give them; say out loud: “your score may turn out higher, I'll re-check”. Error: one sticker for the whole paper — ask for one per section

WHAT WE DO: the comparison with the September sheet + a clinic on exactly four errors of the term

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. September → December. Hand back the September entry-check sheets: Section E today is the SAME format as your September sheet. Same task. Put them side by side.
  2. One question, answered in writing, for themselves: What can you write now that you could not write in September? One sentence.
  3. Only volunteers read out, and you do not comment — repeat after them, louder, so the class hears it. For a new student this is the most important minute of the lesson.
  4. The clinic: four errors, 90 seconds each, anonymously. Write the wrong version on the board, the class fixes it chorally, write the correct version next to it plus the way to self-check (all four are in the reference at the end of this plan): the -s of the third person · the bare verb after does · the position of the adverb of frequency · time marker → continuous.
  5. Four. Not twenty. If you fix these four, your next paper looks completely different.
  6. Everyone marks in their own three lines which of the four are about them.
📺 On screen: the September entry-check sheets (kept by the teacher since Starter P4) · the board for the clinic (Backup: if the September sheets are missing — compare with the first page of the exercise book, where they wrote a mini-profile about themselves in September; the difference is obvious anyway)
👦 Students do: students put the two sheets side by side, see the difference and fix four errors chorally. Expected: for new students the gap between September and December is the most visible in the class — 3–4 short sentences in September, a connected text with two tenses now. Error: they compare marks instead of texts — bring them back with “what can you WRITE now?”

WHAT WE DO: team score, “I can…”, homework strictly from each student's own three lines, and the bridge to Unit 4

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Score out loud and for something concrete: for the warm-up · for honest self-marking · for writing something specific in the three lines rather than “revise everything”. No points for the mark on the paper, and say so out loud: the score is about work in class.
  2. The “I can…” slide for the first term: Count the lines that are true for you now. They count in silence.
  3. Homework comes from each student's own three lines, not from a common list (see the block below). Point at exactly where to look, for each of them personally, while they write it down.
  4. Bridge: Unit Four: Extremes. Weather — and comparing: hotter, the coldest, the worst. We'll compare Norilsk with places that think THEY have bad weather.
📺 On screen: the leaderboard sheet on the wall · the “I can…” slide (Backup: read the lines from a sheet and students count on their fingers; write the homework on the board and check that it has been copied)
👦 Students do: students get the score, count their own “I can” lines and write down their personal homework. Expected: they leave without a sense of failure — because the last impression was the September sheet, not a score. Error: they ask for their grade out loud — answer that everyone counted their own marks and that you will enter the register next lesson, with each of them in private

Homework

The core task (personal, different for everyone): 20 minutes strictly on the three lines you wrote today. Where to look is written next to each line on your paper: a line about present simple → Quick review SB p.21 + Workbook p.11 · a line about adverbs, like + -ing or Wh-questions → SB p.33 + WB p.19 · a line about present continuous and the contrast → SB p.45 + WB p.27 · a line about the base (be · have got · can · possessives) → SB pp.6–10. Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He don't like maths” — what is wrong? → after he/she/it it is always doesn't.
“Does she likes PE?” — why not? → does already marks the third person, so the verb is bare.
“I like she” — what has to change? → her: after a verb we use the object pronoun.
“She plays often chess” — where is the error? → often goes before the main verb: She often plays.
“Yes, they're” — is that possible? → no: a short answer takes the full form — Yes, they are.
“What if I don't finish?” → you will: eight short sections, and the writing comes last. If one section is unfinished, you hand in the rest — that is normal.

Midterm paper — specification (OURS, 70 marks / 40 minutes)
PartWhat is in it, how many marks, and which model to build it from
Section A · Vocabulary, Units 1–3 — 15 marksthree blocks of 5 items. A1 daily routines and school subjects: label the picture / give the word from a definition (format: WB p.11 Vocabulary review, sets from Quick review SB p.21). A2 free-time activities + sort 6 sports into do / go / play (format: WB p.14, set from Quick review SB p.33). A3 clothes and describing them: gap-fill and opposite (format: WB pp.20 and 22, sets from Quick review SB p.45).
Section B · Grammar 1 — Present simple + object pronouns (Unit 1) — 10 marks5 items: put the verb in the right form (aff/neg), two of them on the -es / -ies rule (format: WB p.11 ex.1). 3 items: build a Yes/No question and write the short answer (format: WB p.11 ex.2). 2 items: replace the words in bold with an object pronoun (format: WB p.11 ex.3).
Section C · Grammar 2 — adverbs of frequency · likes and dislikes · Wh-questions (Unit 2) — 10 marks4 items: put the adverb of frequency in the right place, one of them with be (format: WB p.19 ex.1). 3 items: build a sentence with love / like / don’t mind / hate + -ing (format: WB p.19 ex.2). 3 items: find and correct one error in a Wh-question (format: WB p.19 ex.3 — the same page where “Why do Patrick like surfing?” was worked through).
Section D · Grammar 3 — Present continuous and the contrast with present simple (Unit 3) — 12 marks5 items: the present continuous form, two of them on -ing spelling (take → taking, swim → swimming) — format: WB p.27 ex.1. 5 items: choose simple or continuous from the time marker (format: WB p.27 ex.2 and SB p.42). 2 items: a short answer with no contraction (Yes, they are — not “Yes, they're”).
Section E · Starter carry-over — 6 marks6 one-line items: be in a question and a short answer · a possessive adjective or the apostrophe ’s / s’ · have got in the third person · can + bare verb · country and nationality · one family member from a definition. Formats — SB pp.6–10, exactly the ones used in the September entry check: this matters, otherwise the “September → December” comparison means nothing.
Section F · Reading — 6 marksa short 90–110-word text about a teenager's school day or free time (built from the vocabulary already covered in Units 1–2, modelled on SB p.14 and WB p.8): 3 True / False items where the line of evidence must be quoted + 3 items answered in one sentence.

Error clinic after the paper — the four biggest errors of the term
What breaksHow to fix it and how to self-check
The -s of the third person goes missingHe go → He goes · She study → She studies. Self-check: try he/she/it — if it fits, the -s is compulsory.
The verb keeps its -s after do/doesDoes she likes PE? → Does she like PE? Self-check: does has ALREADY marked the person — we do not mark it twice.
The adverb of frequency is in the wrong placeShe plays often → She often plays · He always is → He is always. Self-check: with be it goes after, with every other verb before.
Present simple instead of continuous when there is a time markerAt the moment I wear → I'm wearing. Self-check: if you see now / right now / at the moment / today — use -ing.

Work through exactly these four, anonymously, 90 seconds each. Everything else goes into the student's personal error log: going through twenty errors with the whole class fixes none of them.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“I'll write worse than everyone anyway” — the main brake on a new student in this lessonAnswer it with the design, not with words: the limits of the paper are announced and small, the scale is up in advance, every task format is familiar, and at the end of the lesson they physically lay their September sheet next to today's. New students usually show the biggest growth since September — and it has to be shown, not described.
Gets lost in the rubric and spends minutes on “what am I supposed to do here”Before every section a MODEL of one item stays on the board — not the answer, the format. On top of that, spend the first minute of the paper reading the rubrics of all eight sections aloud together, and only then start the timer.
Goes quiet and does not raise a hand when the task is unclearAnnounce the rule before the start: Raise your hand for the TASK, never for the answer. And once, halfway through the paper, walk past every desk in silence — simply so that the quiet ones get a chance to raise a hand without words.
After marking they see only the score and shut downNobody says a score out loud. The section stickers, the three “to work on” lines and the comparison with September come AFTER the counting for exactly that reason: the last impression of the lesson must be the growth, not the number.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
