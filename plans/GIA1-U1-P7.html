Opening · calm entry
desks · calm

WHAT WE DO: a quiet, low-arousal entry that lowers test anxiety with the TRUTH — every part of today's test is language they have already used in Unit 1 (Bandura: reduce threat, cue prior mastery before a performance).

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Desks apart, quiet music off as they settle. At the door, calmly: Phones in the box, water on the desk, one deep breath.
  2. No surprise framing: Today is the Unit 1 test. Nothing new — every question is language you have already spoken and written this unit.
  3. Normalise nerves: A few nerves are normal and they don't mean you're not ready. You are. Do your best and it's enough.
  4. State the plan on the board so there's no uncertainty: walkthrough → Listening → written test (Speaking one by one) → self-check → done.
📺 On board: today's plan in 5 steps (Backup: write the 5 steps on the board before the lesson.)
👦 Students do: settle, breathe, see the whole shape of the lesson. Expected: calmer room; the reminder “you've met it all” does the heavy lifting. 💪 anxious pupils: seat near the front, reassure privately.

Validity briefing · test the language, not the format

WHAT WE DO: walk through each section with ONE teacher-made model item so the format is transparent — construct validity: we remove “puzzle difficulty” so the score reflects Unit 1 language, not confusion about what to do.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step (use YOUR OWN examples, not the test's items):
  1. Vocabulary: One part matches countries and nationalities, another gives place adjectives. Like: China → Chinese. A city with lots of people is crowded. That's the type — the words on YOUR paper may differ.
  2. Grammar (be): You complete, order or fix sentences with the verb be. Like: They ___ from Spain → They're from Spain. Trap: “Yes, I'm.” is wrong → “Yes, I am.”
  3. Reading: A short text about a place, then questions. Read the question first, then find the answer in the text — don't translate every word.
  4. Listening: You'll hear a description of a country twice. First read the task, underline what you must catch, then listen.
  5. Writing: A few sentences about you — name, country, nationality, your city and one adjective. Capital letters for countries and “I”, apostrophes in I'm / isn't.
  6. Strategy card on the board: 1) read the example · 2) skip & return if stuck · 3) two sweeps at the end (apostrophes → capital letters).
📺 On board: the 5 section names + strategy card (Backup: pre-write the section names and strategy on the board.)
👦 Students do: answer each model in chorus, so no one meets an unfamiliar format cold. Expected: “can we ask what a word means?” → “Instructions yes, answers no.”

WHAT WE DO: run the audio section first, while attention is fresh and the room is fully quiet — teacher-paced for everyone together.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Thirty seconds — read the Listening task. Underline what you must catch. Don't write answers yet.
  2. Play the track ONCE, full. Pause ~30 sec: Note what you caught.

  4. Close the section: Pens off Listening. Now work through the rest at your own pace, in silence.

Test · Vocabulary · Grammar · Reading · Writing (+ Speaking corner)

WHAT WE DO: the class works the written sections in silence; in parallel you take pupils ONE AT A TIME to the quiet corner for the Speaking task + our oral EN↔RU pairing check — so the whole cohort's speaking is assessed without dead time.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Circulate briefly, then run the corner. Call pupils by a quiet list order: Marta, bring your pencil to the corner.
  2. Speaking task (publisher PDF): run it as printed — the meeting-people / describe-a-place prompts; keep it warm, ~2–3 min each.
  3. Our oral pairing check: show 4–5 Unit 1 cards; pupil says the pair aloud — “Она датчанка.” — say it in English. → She's Danish. Note accuracy on your grid.
  4. Between pupils, scan the room; whisper-answer procedural questions only. Two-minute warnings for the written sections near 68′.
  5. Fast finishers: Two sweeps — apostrophes, then capital letters — then sit quietly.
📺 On board: visible timer · section order (Backup: keep the timer on the board; speaking grid on paper.)
👦 Students do: write independently; each leaves the desk once for a calm 1:1 speaking turn and returns. Expected: steady silence; the corner is private so shy speakers aren't on show. 💪 Standard pupils get the full time; ⭐ Higher pupils add a beyond-the-book line in Writing.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. Administer the Unit 1 Progress test (Macmillan Test Generator, Standard; Higher for the strongest) as a fair, calm summative check of Unit 1 “Our world” — Vocabulary, Grammar (be), Reading, Listening, Writing and Speaking — so scores reflect the pupils' language, not test-format confusion.
  • Subsidiary. Assess speaking for every pupil via the publisher Speaking task + our oral EN↔RU pairing check, 1:1, in parallel with the written test; protect confidence with a private self-efficacy close and no public scores.
  • Personal. Keep arousal LOW and clarity HIGH: name the whole plan up front, model every section format, run Listening while fresh, mark privately. No games, no competition today — the payoff is a fair, kind test.

  • a completed Standard/Higher paper per pupil across 5 written/aural sections
  • a 1:1 Speaking + oral-pairing record for every pupil
  • a private “nailed / to polish” slip from each pupil for next-lesson feedback

What is assessed — Unit 1 “Our world” (all previously taught; SB pp.12–23 / Quick review p.21)

Reading
a short text about a place + comprehension (Unit 1 “A guidebook / places” type).
Listening
a country description on the test's audio track, played twice.
Writing
a few sentences about oneself (keypal-style): capital letters + apostrophes as the accuracy targets.
Speaking
publisher Speaking task (Meeting new people / describing a place) + our oral EN↔RU sentence-pairing check.
Assessment notes · Assumptions
Validity: every format was met in P1–P6; the walkthrough removes construct-irrelevant difficulty so the score = Unit 1 language (Messick / Wiliam).
Reliability: use the printed key; Standard vs Higher chosen by need, not mixed within a section.
Fairness: Listening read aloud twice, identical wording, if no track; results returned privately.

Assumptions: P6 review + Match It / Translate It done last pair; pupils continue from GMF4 so be is revision — accuracy & spelling are the real targets. Quiet room, spaced desks, working speaker (or teacher-read backup).

Classroom management — signals (calm test-day set)
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1»; never talk over noise.
Visible timer — timer on = test time; the timer, not your voice, keeps the pace.
Hand up, I come to you — no calling out; procedural questions answered in a whisper at the desk.
Quiet corner order — a named list = who's next for Speaking; pupils don't queue or crowd.
Two-minute warning — a calm “Two minutes on this section” before each transition.
Private results — no scores read aloud, ever; feedback is 1:1, next lesson.
