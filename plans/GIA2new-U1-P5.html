🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Questions are the structure a newcomer needs most (without them you never start talking), so the pair is built as a staircase: a table made from examples → the question machine with prompt cards → a guessing game with ready-made questions → their own questions. Object pronouns are a short separate step with a «swap code» — I never mix them with questions in one exercise.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“How do you make friends? You ask questions. Do you play football? Does your school have drama? Today — the question machine: two little words, Do and Does, open every conversation in English. We build questions, we play a guessing game, and you interview a partner for real. Ready to ask?”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Questions are the key to any conversation: today we get the Do/Does question machine, a guessing game and a real interview with a partner.

The question machine
pairs · mini-boards

WHAT WE DO: a card (subject + verb + object) → the question written on a mini-whiteboard → Showdown, 8 rounds with self-check

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Card: she / like / PE → your board: Does she like PE? Up!
  2. The first round I build the question on the board together with the class, then Kagan Showdown: they write silently, each on their own board, and on the call Showdown! all the boards go up AT ONCE — there is nobody to copy from and nobody to hide behind.
  3. The key after every round; the intonation with a hand rising at the end.
  4. Weaker students get cards with the beginning ready (Do… / Does…).
📺 On screen: cards · mini-whiteboards
👦 Students do: they build questions on a conveyor belt, with support. Expect: by round 6 they no longer peek

Game · guess the subject
crews

WHAT WE DO: a guessing game with ready-made question cards (Fan-N-Pick): a subject/habit is chosen, questions are Yes/No only

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I'm thinking of a subject. Ask me: Do we have it on Monday? Does it need a ball?
  2. First my own riddles for the class (from the cards) — that is the model turn — then each crew has its own leader.
  3. Kagan Fan-N-Pick with the roles rotating: the first holds the cards in a fan, the second draws one and reads the question, the third (the one who is «it») answers Yes/No, the fourth praises or repairs the wording. After every card the roles shift — over the game everyone plays all four.
  4. A badly-formed question does not burn (we are not «ours») — we repair it together and ask it again.
📺 On screen: question cards
👦 Students do: they play with ready-made questions → then dare to make their own. Expect: excitement without fear

The swap code · object pronouns
pairs

WHAT WE DO: the subject→object table in pairs (RallyRobin) + ex.6 with the «name once» frame

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The table in pairs, chorally: I → me · he → him · she → her · we → us · they → them.
  2. I say the first pair myself, then Kagan RallyRobin: A says the word on the left — B answers with the one on the right, on the next line the other way round; the table is in front of their eyes and we drive the circle three times, until it starts bouncing back automatically.
  3. ex.6: we replace the highlighted words; the key one by one: PE → it · Mr Flynn → him · Sara → her…
  4. The frame: Name once — then a pronoun. I like Sara. I walk with HER.
📺 On screen: SB p.17 ex.6
👦 Students do: they swap by the code with the table in front of their eyes

PRODUCT · interview with support
pairs → stage

WHAT WE DO: 5 questions to the partner (Timed Pair Share, cards or their own) → notes → a mini-report of 3 lines with pronouns → volunteers read aloud

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. The interview: Five questions. Cards if you need them. Notes, not sentences.
  2. I run an interview with one student in front of everybody (five questions, notes on the board), then Kagan Timed Pair Share: two minutes for A to ask and write, the bell, two minutes for B. Equal time, which means both will have a report to write.
  3. The report: I asked Misha about football. He plays IT on Sundays. I like his answers — I agree with HIM.
  4. Checklist: Do/Does? is the short answer written down? is the name used once?
📺 On screen: cards · checklist
👦 Students do: the full cycle question → answer → report, with support. Expect: 3 lines from everybody

WB Unit 1
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can build Yes/No questions with Do/Does, give short answers, and use object pronouns to replace names.
  • Subsidiary. The ex.1 table built from examples; short answers drilled chorally; ex.6 replacing names; an interview with prompt cards.
  • Personal. I drill the question intonation with hands (hand up at the end). Weaker students get cards with ready-made questions; stronger ones make their own questions without cards.
By the end, students can…
  • build Do/Does questions with a card support
  • answer: Yes, I do / No, she doesn't
  • replace names with him/her/it/us/them
  • interview a partner with 5 questions

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
'Does she likes PE?' — why not? → does already marks the third person, the verb stays bare.
'I like she' — what has to change? → her: after a verb we use the object pronoun.

Assumptions: SB p.17, KTP pair 9. For some of them Do/Does is new; a staircase from cards to their own questions.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Do and Does get mixed upA colour code: Do — blue cards (I/you/we/they), Does — yellow (he/she/it); the colour holds for the whole lesson.
They answer in full sentences, with mistakesI drill the short answers chorally until they are automatic: Yes, I do! No, she doesn't!
Pronouns get tangled up with questionsA separate step after the break, with its own «name once» frame.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
