Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to ask & answer Present simple Yes/No questions (Do/Does + subject + base verb → short answers) and use object pronouns (me · him · her · it · us · them) to talk about their school day.
  • Subsidiary. Recycle school subjects & daily routines (pairs 3–4) inside the new structure; speaking fluency in a survey; accuracy self-correction with mini-whiteboards; a Visual Takeaway grammar card in the notebook.
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk low; give an ICQ before every task; model first, then release (I Do → We Do → You Do); alternate a stirrer with a settler; act on the hinge check, don't just move on.
By the end, learners can…
  • form Yes/No questions with Do / Does + base verb;
  • give correct short answers (Yes, I do. / No, it doesn't.);
  • replace a noun with the right object pronoun.
Target language
Structure
Do you / we / they + base verb…?  ·  Does he / she / it + base verb…?  ·  Short: Yes, I do. / No, she doesn't.
Pronouns
I→me you→you he→him she→her it→it we→us they→them
Form
Do/Does + subject + base verb (no -s). After DOES the verb loses its -s: Does she like art? (not likes). Object pronoun = after a verb / preposition: We like him.
Recycled
school subjects (art, maths, science, PE…) · daily routines (start school, do homework, wear a uniform…) — from pairs 3–4, now inside questions.
Pron. watch
weak Do you /djʊ/ & Does he /dʌzi/; rising intonation on the Yes/No question; contracted doesn't /ˈdʌznt/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“He / she / it — do or does?” → does. “Does she like or likes art?” → like (base verb).
“Answer: Yes, she ___?” → does. Match the helper to the subject.
“I like Sara — Sara is after the verb: subject or object?” → object → “I like her.” · him = boy, her = girl.

Assumptions: Present simple affirmative & negative are known (pair 3, SB p.16); school subjects (pair 4, p.17) & daily routines (pair 1, p.12) are in place. Newcomers line: weaker/newer learners → present slowly with a filled table, keep sentence-frames on the board through every task, extra choral modelling before any writing. No Wh-questions here — those come in Unit 2. Groups: teams of 3–4; pairs re-formed for the survey.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Keeps -s after does (Does she likes)Colour-code on the board: DOES already carries the -s, so the verb goes back to base. Cross it out in red on the notebook card.
Do / Does chosen by wrong subjectAnchor: he · she · it = DOES; everyone else = DO. Point at a subject strip on the wall.
Short answer mismatch (Yes, she do)“The helper in the answer copies the helper in the question.” Drill Q→A pairs chorally.
Subject vs object pronoun (I like she)Position rule: before the verb = subject (she), after the verb = object (her). Gesture arrow.
him / her gender mix-upTwo photo cards (a boy, a girl) held up as live cues during the drill.

0–6′
Lead-in · retrieval
desks → standing

Warm-up (recall the topic). Greet the class. Quick recall — three things about your school day. ▶ RallyRobin (Kagan, pairs, take turns): partner A says a school subject, partner B a daily routine — back and forth, 45 sec. Everyone talks at once, no waiting.

Board harvest: elicit 5–6 of their words (art, science, PE · start school, do homework, wear a uniform). Keep them on the board — we'll turn all of these into questions today.

Tease the lesson: point at an affirmative sentence — You wear a uniform. Now… how do I ASK it? Hands. This is the hook into today's grammar.

 board · timer · pairs
6–15′
Presentation 1 · Yes/No Qs (I Do)
board / seated

SB p.18, ex.1 — model & build the table. Show the three examples: Do you have history lessons? — Yes, we do. Does your school start later? — No, it doesn't. Think aloud: I want to ASK, so I put a helper FIRST. He/she/it → DOES; everyone else → DO. And look — after DOES the verb has NO -s.

Complete the ex.1 table together on the board (Do … I/you/we/they …? · Does … he/she/it …?) + the short-answer grid (Yes, I do. / No, it doesn't.).

Error inoculation. Write Does she likes art? — Is this right? Cross out the -s in red: DOES already took the -s. The verb goes back to base — Does she LIKE art?

Choral drill (newcomers). Back-chain the weak forms: …have history? → you have history? → Do you have history? Then Q→A pairs chorally (Do you…? — Yes, I do. / Does it…? — No, it doesn't.).

CCQ before release. He, she, it — do or does? · Does she LIKE or LIKES?

 SB p.18 ex.1 · board (colour markers) · subject strip on wall
15–25′
Controlled practice (We Do→You Do)
desks / boards

We Do — SB p.18 ex.2 (match). Do questions 1–2 together on the board (match to the short answers box), thinking aloud about the helper. Then learners finish 3–5 in pairs — ▶ RallyCoach (one solves, one coaches). ICQ: Do you write full sentences? — No, just match.

You Do — SB p.18 ex.3 (write questions with Do/Does). Learners write questions 1–5 on mini-whiteboards from the prompts (you / start school / eight o'clock → Do you start school at eight o'clock?). Frame stays on the board: Do/Does + [subject] + [base verb] …?

Circulate first 3 min; check the two weakest pairs. 3-2-1, boards up. Scan for the -s slip & do/does agreement; fix on the spot.

25–30′
Hinge check
boards up

Diagnostic hinge (Wiliam). Boards / finger vote A–D — Which one is correct?

A) Does your school has a swimming pool? · B) Does your school have a swimming pool? · C) Do your school have…? · D) Does your school having…?

Read the room. B is correct. A = kept the -s after does · C = wrong helper for “school (it)” · D = -ing slip. 80%+ B → move on. Many A → re-drill “does takes the -s, not the verb” with two more examples, then re-vote.

30–40′
Freer speaking · survey
open floor, mingle

SB p.18 ex.4 → Quiz-Quiz-Trade. ▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade (Kagan): each learner gets a card with one Do/Does question about school (Does your teacher use a computer? · Do you study French?). Stand up, find a partner: quiz them, they quiz you, swap cards, new partner.

Answers must be real short answers: Yes, I do. / No, I don't. / Yes, she does. Model one round with a strong student first (ICQ: Full answer or short answer? — Short!).

Everyone speaks every round — no audience. Freeze after ~4 swaps; spotlight 2–3 good exchanges you overheard.

40–47′
Brain break → bridge
standing

▶ Take Off – Touch Down (Kagan, whole class, on your feet): you read a statement — Stand up if it's TRUE for you. Sit if it's not. You do homework every day. · Your school has a library. · You like Mondays. Fast, physical — a mid-lesson reset at the ~45′ mark.

Bridge. Freeze on one: You like your maths teacher. Now — I don't want to say “the maths teacher” again and again. What short word can I use instead? → I like… HER / HIM. Straight into object pronouns.

47–55′
Presentation 2 · object pronouns (I Do)
board / seated

SB p.18 ex.5 — build the pronoun table. Show the live example: Mr Flynn is great. We like… him. Think aloud: “Mr Flynn” comes AFTER the verb — so I use the object pronoun. Complete the grid together: I→me, you→you, he→him, she→her, it→it, we→us, they→them.

Position rule (newcomers). Draw an arrow: Before the verb = subject (she). After the verb = object (her). Contrast pair on the board: She likes me. / I like her.

Photo-cue drill. Hold a boy card → class: him. Girl card → her. Group of learners → them / us. Fast, choral, everyone answers.

CCQ. “I help Sara.” Subject or object? Which pronoun? → object → her.

📓 Interactive notebook
desks

Glue-in — “ASK + POINT BACK” grammar card (A5). Hand out the Unit 1 Grammar 2 Visual Takeaway: Do/Does + base verb + short answers, the reminder “after DOES — base verb”, and the object-pronoun strip (I→me · he→him · she→her · we→us · they→them) on the model Mr Flynn is great. We like him.

Steps: ① glue it onto a clean page; ② cross out “Does she watches…?” in red — mark the trap; ③ build four Yes/No questions about your partner beside the card; ④ in each answer, swap the noun for a pronoun from the bottom strip. The card stays open for the survey next.

ICQ: Yes/No questions only today? — Yes. Wh-questions? — No, next unit.

71–83′
Production · interview + report
pairs → teams

Personalised survey (both structures together). New pairs. Each learner writes 4 Yes/No questions about school life, then interviews their partner: Do you like science? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't. Frame & pronoun strip stay on the board.

Report back — Numbered Heads. ▶ Numbered Heads Together (Kagan, teams): pool what you found. Called number stands and reports using an object pronoun: Masha likes PE — she loves it. Ivan and Sara don't do music — I don't do it either.

Light error feedback after each report; praise correct do/does + pronoun use. Newcomers may read from a frame — accuracy over speed.
