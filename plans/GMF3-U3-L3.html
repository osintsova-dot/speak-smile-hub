Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to follow, read and act out the story «The dolphin rescue» and talk about helping a hurt animal: “There's a dolphin on the beach. It's got some cuts. We must protect its skin. We mustn't shout.”
  • Subsidiary. Break Luke's code («DOLPHINS!») — recycles the alphabet code; Values — dealing with problems & telling someone; story skills: predict → listen → read along → Read & correct → act out; notice must / mustn't for the vets' rules (fully taught in L4).
  • Personal. Let the story breathe — big voices, pauses, prediction; must/mustn't is NOTICED today, drilled properly in L4 — don't over-correct. Give every child a story role.
By the end, pupils can…
  • retell 2–3 things that happen in the rescue;
  • read their role's lines from the book;
  • correct false statements: “No, they don't. They play outside.”

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“The dolphin's got some cuts — is it well or hurt?” → hurt, it needs help!
“We mustn't shout — is shouting OK or NOT OK near the dolphin?” → NOT OK; be quiet.

Assumptions: Has/Does questions and 12 health problems are active from L1–L2; the alphabet code was learnt earlier (recycled in the pre-reading). Act-out routine is familiar. Readers follow the text with a finger. It's got / must / mustn't appear here for the first time in context.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Code pre-reading too hardSolve the first two letters together (16=D, 60=O); give the ×4 key card — it's a warm hook, not a test.
must/mustn't over-analysedJust notice-and-mark when the vets say it; a “rule” gesture (finger wag for mustn't); no grammar lecture today.
Read & correct — pupils just say “false”Model the full correction: No, they don't. They play OUTSIDE.; sentence frame stays up.

Listen 1 — gist (CD2 04, books closed). Listen: what's the matter with the dolphin? Check ideas.

Listen 2 — read along (books open pp.30–31). Finger under the text. Pause per frame: Are you better now, Josh? — Yes, I am. Look at that dog! Why is it barking? Oh no! There's a dolphin on the beach. I think it's hurt. Raise a “rule!” card each time the vets say must / mustn't.

Values (act. 4). Who do the children tell when they see the dolphin? Who do YOU talk to when there's a problem? Why is it important to tell someone? 3–4 hands, Russian OK, recast key words.

20–28′
Read & correct · comprehension
carpet → desks

Read and correct (act. 3, Talk Partners). Statements: The children play inside… true or false? Pairs correct the false ones fully: No, they don't. They play outside. 5 statements (a cat is barking → No, a DOG is barking; Mum phones the police → No, she phones the VET…) — every pair speaks.

Who said it? (Showdown). Mini-whiteboards: you read a line — “We mustn't shout.” Who said it? — all write (the vet), Show me! 4 rounds.

✔ Assess: the boards are your check — who follows the story? The rest sit with you in the board game.

💪 Support: point to the right frame in the book. ⭐ Extension: add a second true fact about that frame.

28–40′
Act out
carpet, groups of 4

Model a frame. You + 3 pupils play the beach frame with big drama: Oh no! There's a dolphin on the beach. I think it's hurt. Quick! Let's tell Mum!

Groups of 4 (Josh · Lily · Ravi · Mum/Vet). Rehearse 2 frames with books as scripts. Rotate roles once.

Showtime. Each group performs ONE frame (30–40 sec — fast carousel, no long waits). Audience task: shout the vets' rule at the end (Protect the skin! Don't shout!).

💪 Support: shortest lines / the “Woof!” dog part for the shyest child. ⭐ Extension: add an extra line from the frame.

Rescue board game
desks, groups
