🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Write a formal report of 140–190 words with a title, section headings and four functions — introduction, current situation, recommendations, conclusion — using impersonal and passive structures, linkers of addition, contrast and consequence, and determiners and quantifiers (nearly all, a large number of, not all, none, most of).
What is genuinely hard
Register. Everything this class has practised for four lessons — discussion, opinion, personal example — is exactly what a report must not sound like. Contractions come out automatically after a speaking lesson, and «I think it's bad» has to become «It has been mentioned that the current situation is unsatisfactory». The second difficulty is quantifying: students write «students think» when they mean «nearly all the students we spoke to», and the vagueness costs the mark.
Typical error to expect
«I don't think the website's good» inside a formal report — contractions are the single fastest way to lose the register mark, and they are the hardest habit to break because spoken practice trains them in.
Why the lesson is built this way
The genre is taught backwards from a model that is on the page in full — the class reads a report about a school website before it writes a report about technology in school, so the shape is copied, not invented. The middle of the lesson is a language hunt: five features found IN the model, not listed on a slide. The planning stage uses the Teacher's Book paragraph plan (six paragraphs, four questions from the company) so nobody starts writing without a skeleton. Then twenty-two uninterrupted minutes of writing, in silence, in class — because a report written at home is a report written in Russian and translated. Peer check is against the book's own checklist, which is the same list the exam criteria are made of.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“An international company wants to know how students in your country use technology to study. They asked YOU. Not an essay, not an opinion — a report: a title, headings, no contractions, and numbers instead of feelings. Ninety minutes, and you leave with it finished.”

WHAT WE DO: ninety seconds of speaking recycling, a homework glance and the turn from discussion to report

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one full sentence back: One sentence — what is the last thing you used your phone for today? Any answer counts as long as it is in English and it is a sentence.

Lesson frame
board
Why: Defining the genre against two genres they have already written is faster and stickier than defining it from scratch.

WHAT WE DO: the genre named against the genres they already know, and the aim set

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three words on the board: ESSAY · REVIEW · REPORT. Elicit in thirty seconds what the class has already written this year (review in Unit 7, opinion essay in Unit 8).
  2. Name the difference in one line: an essay argues, a review judges, a report FINDS OUT and then recommends. That is why it has headings and no «I think».
  3. Say the outcome plainly: You leave this room with a finished report of a hundred and forty to a hundred and ninety words. Not a plan — a text.
  4. Warmer from the Teacher's Book if the room is slow, ninety seconds: the compound-noun skeletons on the board — ___load, ___phones, ___wall, ___proof, ___mark, ___bait, ___top, ___nav, ___cam, ___-drive, ___bud, ___less. It wakes the unit vocabulary up and it is going into the report.
📺 On screen: the three genres and the twelve compound-noun skeletons on the board — chalk only
👦 Students do: name the difference between the three genres and complete twelve compound nouns from memory

WHAT WE DO: ex.2: what the task actually asks, and what register it demands

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.2, two minutes: read the writing task on the page and decide — formal or informal? Answer: formal, and the class says why (the head of the school asked for it; it reports on other people's opinions).
  2. Underline the two bullet points in the task itself. Every report that misses a bullet loses marks in every marking system there is, and this is the moment to say so.
  3. Ask the question that will be asked again at minute fifty-four: Who is going to read this? What do they want to know?
  4. Write the two answers on the board — a head teacher, who wants findings and a recommendation — and leave them up.

👦 Students do: decide the register with evidence, then identify the reader and what the reader wants

The model report, read twice
individual → pairs → class
Why: The model report, read twice

WHAT WE DO: ex.3: the model read for shape first, then for the ideas it borrowed from stage one

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. First read, three minutes, silent: the model «Improvements to the school website». One job — what is each of the four sections DOING? (Introduction · The current website · Suggested improvements · Conclusion.)
  2. Second read, three minutes, in pairs: does the model mention any of the ideas the class produced in ex.1? Answer — a student vlog and a list of special events.
  3. Check the register question with two votes: is it formal? how do you know? Take three pieces of evidence — no contractions, passives, «As we have previously mentioned».
  4. Now hide the model and elicit the four headings back, in order, with books shut. If the class can produce the skeleton from memory, they can write one.
  5. Read the Exam tip on the page aloud, once: give the report a title, divide it into paragraphs, give each paragraph a heading.
📺 On screen: the four section functions written up as the class names them — this skeleton stays on the board until they write
👦 Students do: read a model twice with two different jobs, then reproduce its skeleton with the book shut

Move break · register sort
standing, whole class
Why: A stirrer that also recycles the new language. Seventeen-year-olds who have been sitting for half an hour need to stand before the next block, or the next block dies quietly.

WHAT WE DO: a four-minute physical sort that fixes the single most expensive habit

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Two sides of the room: FORMAL and NOT IN A REPORT. Twelve sentences read aloud by you, one at a time, everybody walks.
  2. The twelve: It's really bad. · The current situation is unsatisfactory. · Loads of students hate it. · A large number of students expressed dissatisfaction. · I think they should change it. · Our main recommendation is that the site should be updated. · The site's rubbish, to be honest. · It has been mentioned that the design is out of date. Add four of your own from the model.
  3. Every time the room splits, one student from each side says WHY in four words: contraction, opinion, passive, quantifier.
  4. Last thirty seconds, standing: convert one informal sentence into a formal one, in chorus, out loud.
  5. Sit down straight into the language hunt.
📺 On screen: the twelve sentences are read aloud, not shown — nothing to prepare and nothing to fail
👦 Students do: sort twelve sentences with their bodies and name the reason each time

The five features, hunted in the model
pairs → class
Why: The five features, hunted in the model

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 plus a quantifier drill: the language of a report found rather than listed

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4 in pairs, six minutes: find examples IN the model of five things — no contractions, passive and impersonal structures, linkers of addition/contrast/consequence, determiners and quantifiers, and the expressions that begin and end a report.
  2. Check with Kagan Numbered Heads Together and write each family up as it is confirmed: passives — could be improved, is designed, It has been mentioned, should be updated · linkers — in order to, Furthermore, so that · quantifiers — nearly all, Another, not all, a large number of, many, A few, most of · opening — The aim of this report is to outline … · closing — To sum up …, All things considered, our main recommendation is …
  3. The quantifier drill, three minutes, because it is the feature that separates a five from a seven: you say a vague sentence, the class rewrites it precisely. Students don't like it → Nearly all the students we spoke to disliked it. · Some people said → A large number of those interviewed said.
  4. Mixed ability from the Teacher's Book: stronger pairs add two more linkers of addition, three of contrast and two of consequence to the board; less confident pairs add one of each.
📺 On screen: the five families written up in five columns — this board is what they write from in twenty minutes
👦 Students do: hunt five language families inside a real text, then rewrite five vague claims precisely

Planning the six paragraphs
individual
Why: Planning the six paragraphs

WHAT WE DO: ex.5a: the task read, the four questions listed, the skeleton written before a word of prose

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the task on the page with the class and put the Teacher's Book questions on the board: who is the report for, and what FOUR things do they want to know? (Which technologies you use in and outside class · how you use them · students' attitude to using technology for studying · what the ideal use would be.)
  2. Elicit the paragraph plan and write it up: paragraph 1 introduction · paragraphs 2–5 the four areas · paragraph 6 conclusion with a recommendation. Six paragraphs, each with a heading.
  3. Four minutes, silent: everyone writes the skeleton only — a title, six headings, and two or three words of content under each. No sentences yet.
  4. Circulate and check ONE thing: does every heading say what its paragraph will do? A heading called «Technology» is refused.
  5. Remind them where the content is: the ideas from the discussion on p.118 and from the reflection work of this unit are legal and encouraged.
📺 On screen: the six-paragraph plan and the four company questions on the board, next to the five language families
👦 Students do: write a title, six headings and a content note under each, in silence
🇷🇺 EGE parallel — optional 5–7 minutes, teacher's choice: this stage already trains EGE task 38 (the project piece with a data table), criteria К2–К4. The report's skeleton — stated aim, sectioned body, quantified claims, conclusion with a recommendation — is the skeleton the Russian task 38 is marked on, and the quantifiers are how the data in the table gets commented on instead of retold.

🎯 Writing the report
individual, silent
Why: 🎯 Writing the report

WHAT WE DO: twenty-two uninterrupted minutes: the whole text, in class, in silence

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set the conditions out loud and then protect them: twenty-two minutes, silent, no phones, no dictionaries for the first ten. The board stays as it is — five language families, six paragraphs, four questions.
  2. The word count is written up: 140–190. Under 140 loses marks for task completion; over 190 loses time, not marks, and time is what they are short of.
  3. 🎯 They write. You circulate silently with the checklist and make a small mark in the margin where something needs a second look — no corrections, no comments, no conversations.
  4. At minute fifteen of writing, one quiet instruction from the front: Five minutes left. If you have no quantifiers yet, put three in now.
  5. At minute twenty: pens down for thirty seconds, count your words, write the number at the bottom. Then two more minutes to finish the sentence you are in.
📺 On screen: the board is the only support and it does not change during the writing. Backup: none needed — no media
👦 Students do: write a complete formal report of 140–190 words in silence, then count and record their own word total

Peer check against the checklist
pairs
Why: Peer check against the checklist

WHAT WE DO: ex.5b: the book's own checklist used as a marking scheme, by the students

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Swap papers. Kagan RallyCoach: the checker reads aloud, the writer listens without defending.
  2. The checklist is SB p.141 and it is used line by line. Five questions and a tick or a cross for each: title and headings? formal language, no contractions? linkers? quantifiers? an opening and a closing expression?
  3. One instruction that makes peer feedback useful: for every cross, the checker writes WHERE, not what. «No quantifiers in paragraph 3» is help; «be more formal» is noise.
  4. Papers back to the owners with two minutes to fix the cheapest cross. Then collect everything — finished or not.
📺 On screen: the checklist on SB p.141 is the marking scheme; nothing else is needed
👦 Students do: mark a partner's report against five criteria and repair the cheapest problem in their own

Structure
The aim of this report is to outline … · At the moment, mobile phones are banned in school. · It has been mentioned that … · Nearly all the students we spoke to said … · All things considered, our main recommendation is …
Form
no contractions · passive and impersonal structures (could be improved, is designed, should be updated) · linkers of addition (Furthermore, In addition, What is more), contrast (However, Nevertheless, On the one hand … on the other) and consequence (As a result, so that, because of) · determiners and quantifiers to grade a claim.
Pron. watch
not a pronunciation lesson, but two things are said aloud anyway: RECommendation has its stress on the fourth syllable, and «All things conSIDered» is one phrase with one stress — students who can say them stop avoiding them in writing
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«Our school's website isn't very good» — allowed in a report? → no. Why? → a contraction and a personal judgement. Give me the formal version. → The current website is not sufficiently attractive.
«Students think the site is boring.» How many students? All of them? → say it: nearly all the students we spoke to. Now it is a finding, not a rumour.
Does a report need a title? → yes, and every section needs a heading. That is the difference between a report and an essay, and it is the first thing an examiner sees.

Assumptions: SB p.119, syllabus lesson 58 — a full ninety minutes on one page. Unit 8 taught talking about statistics, so language for quantity exists and is recycled rather than introduced. The class has written an opinion essay (Unit 8) and a review (Unit 7), so paragraphing is secure; the report's headings and register are new. The Writing checklist on SB p.141 is used at the end, and the Writing reference in the Workbook (p.82–83) is tonight's homework, so the model is met a second time.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Contractions everywhere after a speaking lessonThe register sort at minute forty is physical and public, and after it every contraction found in a draft costs the writer nothing except the word CONTRACTION written in their margin by a partner. Peer marking fixes this faster than teacher marking.
Students write an essay with headings on topThe four functions are elicited from the model and written on the board as questions: what is this report FOR? what is happening now? what should change? what is the overall picture? A paragraph that answers none of the four is cut.
The report has no data in it because there was no surveySay it before planning: you are allowed to invent your findings, but you must quantify them. «A few students», «the majority», «none of the students we spoke to» — the numbers are fictional, the language is real.
Someone finishes in nine minutesTheir next job is on the board before writing starts: count your words, check the checklist on p.141 line by line, then underline every quantifier you used. If there are fewer than four, add them.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Task 38 (the written piece, all five criteria) and, for the register work, task 37.

A report is not a Russian exam genre, but every criterion that decides task 38 is trained by it: a stated aim in the first paragraph (К1), paragraphs with visible headings and linkers (К2), formal register with no contractions (К3), determiners and quantifiers that make a claim measurable — nearly all, a large number of, none (К4). Students who can write «The aim of this report is to outline…» stop starting their exam essays with «I want to tell about…», which costs a mark every time.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
