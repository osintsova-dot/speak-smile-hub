🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): A live feed is a rare text that explains by itself why the present continuous exists: everything is happening right now. So the pair is built as press-office work: first we read somebody else's feed like professionals (the subskill “spot the text type BEFORE reading”), then we publish our own. True/False only counts with a line of evidence — that levels the pace of the strong and the weak. I never name the grammar: the students notice themselves that every verb in the feed has the same form.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Press passes, agents. Comic Con: thousands of people, hundreds of costumes, and one reporter with a phone. His feed is on page 39 — but careful, it reads from the BOTTOM up. Your mission: work the feed like professionals — every answer needs a LINE from the text, no line no point. And then you run your own feed: three posts, live, from an event you choose. Press pass — accepted?”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We are a press office. First we work through somebody else's live feed from Comic Con (every answer needs a line of evidence), then we publish our own: three live posts from an event we choose.

Critical thinking: where a live feed works
Critical thinking · where a feed works
crews

WHAT WE DO: CT 1–3: why a live feed is only about “now”, which events it suits, think of two more of your own (CT-3 — RoundRobin round the crew)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. CT-1: Choose the correct sentence about a live feed. Key: a — a live feed reports actions and events AT THE MOMENT they are happening.
  2. CT-2 in crews: Which events could you describe with a live feed? Key: a football match · an election · an awards ceremony. The rest — no, and they have to say WHY.
  3. CT-3 through Kagan RoundRobin: round the crew — one event per turn, no repeats, the circle keeps going until they have two new ones. Think of TWO more events. TB suggestions: extreme weather, a royal wedding, a fashion show, another sports event.
  4. From here the crew picks ITS OWN event for the product.
📺 On screen: the CT slide (backup — the list of events on the board)
👦 Students do: they argue and choose the event for their own feed. Expected: “a biography” and “an animal factfile” drop out by themselves — that is the genre understood

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, crews can identify a text type before reading, mine a live feed for evidence (True/False with a quoted line), and write a 3-post live feed of their own.
  • Subsidiary. The unit subskill: identifying the text type (layout, headline, pictures, times and comments); Word work (artists · hero · costumes · autographs · enormous · excited); Critical thinking: which events a live feed suits.
  • Personal. The reading speed of the strong ones drowns the weak — I hold the rule “an answer only counts with the line”. Form errors (he wearing / they is) I log silently for the P3 clinic.
By the end, students can…
  • name a text type and say what gave it away
  • find and quote the line that proves an answer
  • correct false statements with evidence
  • write three live-feed posts about a real event

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“True, I think so” — does that count? → no: True only with the line from the text.
Does hero mean a character in a book? → no, here it is someone people admire.
Why is the feed read from the bottom? → because a new post goes on top; the very first one is at the bottom.
Is a live feed any good for a biography of Newton? → no: it is about what is happening AT THE MOMENT.

Assumptions: SB pp.38–39 (the live feed + the tasks), KTP pair 22. They read confidently, but “evidence by line” and naming the text type are a new bar.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
They skim and argue “I'm right”The evidence-line rule: only a line from the text settles an argument, finger on the line.
The order of the feed throws them: they start at the topFirst thing — ex.3 question 3: where does it begin? We pencil an arrow bottom-to-top straight into the book.
Their own feed turns into a retelling of the pastThe press-office rule: a post must have a time on it and say what is happening NOW; a post in the past tense is not published.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
