Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read & say the /z/ sound (“sometimes s says z” — zebra sneezes cheese cherries), talk about food in India, and read & plan a shape poem — three mini-blocks, one “creative food” thread.
  • Subsidiary. Phonics: notice that z and s can spell the same /z/ sound (recognise → say → sort); culture — food in India (Channel 2! video: being creative with fruit) + 21C: using your imagination; literacy — text type shape poems, adjectives with singular & plural nouns, plan a shape poem.
  • Personal. Three blocks — keep transitions crisp (rap/drum bridges); the zebra twister is play; cap each block hard so the shape-poem plan actually gets started.
By the end, pupils can…
  • say the zebra /z/ tongue-twister and sort s / z words;
  • name 2–3 facts about food in India and compare with ours;
  • find the adjectives in a shape poem and plan one with a partner.

Structure
Sometimes s says z: The (zebra) (sneezes) when it eats (cheese) and (cherries).  ·  In India people eat (curry) with (rice).  ·  shape poem: It's long and thin. It's yellow. Yum, yum — it's delicious!
Form
/z/ = letter z (zebra, prize, quiz) AND letter s that says /z/ (cheese, sneeze, cherries, please, nose). Poem: adjectives are the SAME with singular & plural nouns — A lollipop is sweet. Cherries are sweet.
Pron. watch
/z/ voiced — buzz like a bee (put a hand on your throat); contrast cheese /z/ vs peace /s/; cherries /ˈtʃeriz/; delicious /dɪˈlɪʃəs/.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“cheese and zebra — same LAST/first SOUND /z/? Same LETTER?” → same sound /z/, different letters (s / z)!
“A shape poem — is it in the shape of the FOOD or just straight lines?” → in the shape of the food; and it describes it.

Assumptions: Toppings, adjectives and prefer are active (L1–L4). Sound–letter awareness from earlier phonics is enough to enjoy the s / z noticing (по учебнику). Pupils can read the short shape-poem texts with support (curved text — read together).

Block 1 · Phonics /z/
carpet → desks

Speak and spell (act. 1, CD2 28). PB p.45: Sometimes “s” says “z”. The zebra sneezes when it eats cheese and cherries! Buzz the /z/ with a hand on your throat. Listen, then the speed ladder: slow → fast → whisper. Pairs test each other ×3.

Sort s / z. Two hoops/columns: z (zebra, prize, quiz, lazy) · s-says-z (cheese, sneeze, cherries, please, nose). Hold up word cards — every child points/steps to the right hoop BEFORE you place each card.

Notice the rule. Listen — cheese, sneeze, cherries… the letter s is buzzing like a z! (notice, don't lecture).

💪 Support: say the word, teacher shows the hoop. ⭐ Extension: add one more /z/ word to a hoop (e.g. easy, nose, roses).

✔ Assess: who still reads s-says-z as /s/? Note them — extra RallyRead next unit.

Block 3 · Literacy · shape poems
carpet → desks, pairs

Before you read (act. 1–3, p.46, CD2 30). Look at the shape of the poems. What food can you see? Think of words to describe it. Read/listen (turn the book to follow the curve) — do your words appear? Say the poems aloud.

▶ Adjective scavenger hunt. Race in pairs to find in a poem: a size word · a colour word · a taste word. (long, thin · yellow · sweet, delicious). First hands up read them aloud — everyone scans.

Investigate a text (p.47, CD2 31). Lollipop shape poem: what shape is it? Writing tip: Adjectives are the SAME with one or many — A lollipop is sweet. Cherries are sweet!

▶ Plan a shape poem (Cooperative learning, AB p.44 format). Pairs choose a food with a simple shape (banana / apple / pizza slice / lollipop) and make notes: Food · Taste · Size · Colour · Touch · Other. 2 pairs share one adjective aloud.

💪 Support: fill the plan template with the adjective word bank. ⭐ Extension: write two full poem lines in the food's shape.
