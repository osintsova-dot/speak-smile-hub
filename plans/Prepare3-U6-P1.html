🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Name twelve places in a city and choose between a, the and no article — plus all, both, another, other — instead of leaving the article out.
What is genuinely hard
Russian has no articles at all. There is nothing to translate from, so the article is not a word they get wrong, it is a word they do not see. On top of that another and other look like one word to them and behave like two.
Typical error to expect
I went to cinema · She is best student in our class — the article simply dropped, because Russian has none [high priority, A2/B1]
the Moscow, the London — the put in front of a city or a country that does not take it [A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
Vocabulary comes first because the twelve places are the nouns every later determiner attaches to, and because a picture-match gives meaning in four minutes with no explanation at all. The city quiz that follows is not decoration: it recycles the words in somebody else's mouth and hands the class thirteen city names, which is exactly the material the grammar needs — Rome takes no article, the UK does. The grammar itself is discovered from two sentences about Liverpool on p.65: they say the rule, you do not. Pronunciation sits after the move break as a change of gear and it is the same grammar from the other side — /ðiː/ or /ðə/ is decided by the sound that follows, which forces them to notice the word they keep dropping. The practice block ends with error correction rather than production, because ex.7 on p.65 is a list of the six article mistakes Russian students actually make. The productive stage puts the article where it can be heard: a description of a real city with the name taken out.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today we learn the shortest word in English — and the one that costs the most. In Russian there is no a and no the, so your brain does not hear them missing. By the end of the lesson you can describe a city so well that we guess it — and you will put every article in.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, two rules of block 5 recalled and a first count of the city words they already have

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.

  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): open the new unit: Books open, page sixty-four. New unit — the city. And the first question of the lesson is the smallest one in English: a or the?

Vocabulary 1 · places in a city
pairs → class
Why: Twelve places on twelve photographs: meaning is instant, and the mingle that follows gives the repetitions. These are also the nouns every determiner in the grammar stage will attach itself to.

WHAT WE DO: twelve places on twelve photos: ex.1–2 p.64 (track 84) plus a Quiz-Quiz-Trade mingle

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 first, and it is a speaking task, not a word list (TB p.125): demonstrate with your own question — Where can you book a table? (a restaurant). Students write five questions individually, then test a partner. 4 minutes.
  2. ex.2 — elicit a couple of the words from a definition first (a very tall building → skyscraper), then match the twelve words to photos A–L in pairs. Key: A statue · B skyscraper · C shopping area · D old town · E art gallery · F mosque · G stadium · H fountain · I embassy · J temple · K palace · L cathedral
  3. Play track 84 — check and repeat chorally.
  4. Sort out the three religious buildings on the board once, with a bracket: cathedral, mosque, temple. Then never mention it again.
  5. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with place cards, 4 minutes: Quiz your partner. Coach if he's wrong. Trade cards. Find a new partner!
  6. ex.3 to finish, in pairs, two minutes: Which of these has our town got? Which has the capital got?
📺 On screen: track 84 in the whiteboard app with the twelve photos on the screen (Backup if the board is down: read the twelve places aloud twice yourself and use the photos in the paper book; the Quiz-Quiz-Trade cards are printed in advance and need no technology at all)
👦 Students do: write questions, match, repeat, then mingle with cards. Expect 6–7 of 12 unaided. Typical error: shopping area shortened to shopping — accept it in speech, correct it in writing

Grammar · discovery · determiners
board → solo
Why: Guided discovery from two sentences about Liverpool: they state the rule themselves — first time a, second time the — which is the only way an article ever gets learnt by a speaker of a language that has none.

WHAT WE DO: guided discovery of a/an and the from the Liverpool sentences, then the ten-gap text (ex.1–2 p.65)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Books closed. This is a city in England. The city has a famous football team and was the home of the best pop group in the world, The Beatles. Which city is it? (Liverpool.)
  2. Then the three questions that do the teaching (TB p.126): First I said a city and then the city. Why? (first you say a, then you say the when you say the word again) · Did I say best pop group or the best pop group? (the best) · Why? (we use the with superlatives).
  3. Books open. ex.1 — complete the rules. Key: We use a/an to introduce something for the first time. We use the to talk about something already mentioned.
  4. ex.2 — fill the first gap together and ask why (Why is it a great holiday? — Rosa is talking about the holiday for the first time). Then they work alone, 4 minutes, and explain their answers to a partner in pairs. Key: 1 a · 2 the · 3 a · 4 an · 5 the · 6 the · 7 the · 8 the · 9 a · 10 a
  5. Point at the board: Grammar reference SB p.157, answer key TB p.266 — for anyone who wants the rule written out at home.
📺 On screen: the two Liverpool sentences on the board plus the ten-gap text on screen (Backup: the text is printed in the book on p.65 — the screen only saves them looking down, and the board carries the sentences either way)
👦 Students do: answer the three questions, then complete the rules and the text. Expect the first/second-mention rule to be stated correctly by two students and understood by everybody. Typical error: gap 10 filled with the — a/an is also the normal article in a description

Pronunciation · the
class → pairs
Why: Two pronunciations of the commonest word in English, decided by one thing only — the sound that follows. It takes six minutes and it makes their English sound English.

WHAT WE DO: the two pronunciations of the: ex.3–4 p.65 (tracks 86–87)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB p.126: demonstrate with the embassy /ðiː/ and the Trevi Fountain /ðə/, then check they know what a vowel and a consonant are: «Are /e/ and /əʊ/ vowel or consonant sounds?» (vowel) · «And /p/ and /z/?» (consonant). Tell them there is often a linking /j/ between /ðiː/ and the vowel that follows: the oldest = /ðiːjəʊldɪst/.
  2. Play track 86 for ex.3 and let them state the rule. Key: /ðiː/ before a vowel sound · /ðə/ before a consonant sound
  3. ex.4 — put the ten items into the table, then play track 87 to check and repeat. Key: /ðiː/ the apple, the Arctic, the Earth, the Indian Ocean, the orange, the umbrella · /ðə/ the dog, the North Sea, the River Nile, the Statue of Liberty
  4. One extra thirty seconds that pays for itself: say the umbrella with /ðə/ and let them hear how wrong it sounds. Rules argued about are rules remembered.
📺 On screen: tracks 86–87 in the whiteboard app (Backup with no sound: say all ten items yourself — you are the recording, and for this task a live voice is actually better because you can exaggerate. The table is printed on p.65)
👦 Students do: listen, sort, repeat. Expect the vowel/consonant test to be the hard part, not the pronunciation. Typical error: they sort by the LETTER rather than the sound — the umbrella is /ðiː/, the university would not be

Grammar · practice · determiners
pairs → class
Why: Controlled practice, then correcting other people's sentences — faster and safer than producing them — and the six sentences of ex.7 are precisely the six mistakes Russian students actually make with articles.

WHAT WE DO: all, both, another, other: ex.5–7 p.65, ending on error correction

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.5 — underline the examples and complete the rules. Elicit the meaning of other (as well as the thing already mentioned). Then write the five model sentences on the board with the verbs underlined: All the cities are beautiful. Both cities are beautiful. Another famous city is Granada. The other famous city is Granada. Other famous cities are Granada and Seville. Key: We use both to talk about two things. We use all to talk about a total number of things. Another means «one more».
  2. ex.6 — books closed first: What is the biggest island in the world? (Australia) What is its capital city? (Canberra). Books open, complete the eight gaps individually, 5 minutes. Key: 1 All · 2 both · 3 Other · 4 both · 5 Another · 6 all · 7 another · 8 other (the text is about Australia: Sydney, Cairns and Melbourne)
  3. Kagan RallyCoach for the check: A says the answer and the reason, B coaches, then they swap.
  4. ex.7 — correct the six sentences. This is the heart of the stage: five of the six are the exact article mistakes Russian students make. Key: 1 It's the biggest museum in my town. · 2 It's not boring like other computer games. · 3 The T-shirts both cost £15. · 4 This phone has a very good screen and camera. · 5 I went to the park and I played football with my friends. · 6 We went on holiday to the Edinburgh.
  5. Kagan Showdown on the six corrections: everybody writes, everybody reveals at Boards up!, nobody is exposed alone.
📺 On screen: the eight-gap Australia text on screen and the five model sentences on the board (Backup: everything is printed on SB p.65 — put the five model sentences in chalk and run the stage from the paper book)
👦 Students do: complete, coach, correct. Expect ex.7 items 1 and 5 to be the ones nobody sees. Typical error: both given a singular verb — point at the underlined verb on the board rather than explaining

🎯 Speaking out · guess my city
pairs → fours
Why: The productive outcome: a description of a real city with no name in it, which cannot be written without articles and determiners. The guessing is what makes the class listen.

WHAT WE DO: ex.8 p.65: write a description of a city without naming it, and make the class guess

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. TB p.126 ex.8: students choose three cities and write a description of each without naming it — where it is, how many people live there, transport, culture, why it is famous — offering a choice of four city names to guess from. Pairs join into fours and read their descriptions out.
  2. Set the frame on the board so the writing has a shape: Where it is · how many people live there · one famous place · why people go there · four names to choose from. Six sentences, no more.
  3. 8 minutes to write in pairs. Circulate with a correction sheet and log article errors silently — you will not correct anything out loud during this stage.
  4. Pairs join into fours: read your description, the other pair chooses from your four names and says WHY. 6 minutes.
  5. Kagan Numbered Heads Together for the round-up: number off in the fours, call a number, that student reads their group's best sentence aloud.
  6. Close the loop with the errors you logged — but write them on the board without names, three sentences, and let the class repair them in thirty seconds.
📺 On screen: the writing frame on the board and the class's three biggest cities from 8–14′ still visible (Backup: the frame is dictated and copied into notebooks — this stage needs no technology at all)
👦 Students do: write six sentences, read them out, guess. Expect the descriptions to be twice as good as free speaking because the frame does the planning. Typical error under time pressure: the article disappears again — that is exactly what the correction sheet is for

WB Unit 11 (p.44–47)
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can name twelve places in a city and use a/an, the, all, both, another and other correctly in description.
  • Subsidiary. Listening to a radio quiz for specific information (SB p.64 ex.5–6); the two pronunciations of the (ex.3–4); correcting six typical article mistakes (ex.7 p.65).

  • choose between a/an and the on the «first time / already mentioned» test
  • use all, both, another and other with the right verb form

Structure
This is a city in England. The city has a famous football team. · All the cities are beautiful. · Both cities are beautiful. · Another famous city is Granada. · Other famous cities are Granada and Seville.
Form
a/an = first mention · the = already mentioned, and with superlatives · all + plural verb · both + plural verb · another + singular · other + plural. No article with a plain city name.
Pron. watch
the = /ðiː/ before a vowel sound (the apple, the Earth, the umbrella) and /ðə/ before a consonant sound (the dog, the River Nile). Watch the linking /j/: the oldest = /ðiːjəʊldɪst/.
Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I went to cinema yesterday» — what's missing? → the cinema. Russian has no articles, so this is the mark Russian students lose most often.
«This is a city in England. ___ city has a famous team.» Which word, and why? → The — because we have already said it once. First time a, second time the.
«We went on holiday to the Edinburgh» — correct it. → to Edinburgh. A city name takes no article.
«Another» or «other»? ___ famous cities are Granada and Seville. → Other — the noun is plural, so another (= one more) cannot fit.

Assumptions: SB p.64–65, syllabus lesson 34. They have met a and the every year since Give Me Five and still drop them; what is new here is the all / both / another / other set and the pronunciation rule. Of the twelve places they usually know five or six; embassy, old town, shopping area and the cathedral/temple/mosque distinction are the ones that need work.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the OGE — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 20–26 (put the given word into the right grammatical form inside a text) — articles and determiners are one of the lines of the national codifier — and speaking task 3, the monologue about your town.

The article is the cheapest mark in the paper and the one Russian students lose most often, because Russian has no articles at all. A determiner is not a word to memorise; it is a decision — first time or already mentioned — and that decision is exactly what this lesson trains.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the OGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
