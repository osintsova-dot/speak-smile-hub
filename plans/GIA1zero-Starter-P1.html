Into the lesson: welcome + “you already know English”
Opening · welcome + “what I already know”
door → circle

WHAT WE DO: a smiling Hello meeting at the door; a rewind of life: which English words do you ALREADY know; bridge: “you're not from zero”

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. At the door, a hand or fist bump for each one: Hello! I'm Oksana. Welcome! — their name back, I repeat it.
  2. Circle: English words you ALREADY know? — I collect them on the board (football, OK, pizza, wifi…). We get 15–20!
  3. Bridge: “You did NOT arrive from zero — here are your words. Today we add the main thing: saying hello and naming your own things. By May — you'll be chatting. That's true.”
  4. Rule #1 goes on the wall: Mistakes are how we learn. I demonstrate: I make a mistake on purpose, fix myself, smile.
📺 On screen: board: a cloud of “your” words · the rule #1 poster
👦 Students do: they name familiar words — a first success in 3 minutes; they see the safety rule. Expected: surprise — “I already know 20 words!”.

Name games: getting to know each other
Hook · name games
circle · standing

WHAT WE DO: the group's names through games: Kagan RoundRobin name+gesture round the circle → snowball → “catch the name”; teams of 3 and the scoreboard

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Game 1 by RoundRobin (the course's first Kagan structure: round the circle, in turn, nobody skipped — one by one, round the circle!): each one — I'm Dasha! + their own gesture; the class echoes in chorus: Hello, Dasha! + the gesture.
  2. Game 2, a light “Snowball” round the circle: I'm Miron. — He's Miron, I'm Vika! (I help, I don't demand a long chain).
  3. Game 3, “Catch the Name”: a ball; you throw it — you name the person you threw it to: Hello, Artem!
  4. Seated in teams of 3; the course's points scoreboard goes on the wall: “points are always team points”.
📺 On screen: a soft ball · the team scoreboard
👦 Students do: they play name games standing up — everyone moves and laughs; they sit down in trios. Expected: names are caught, the ice is broken. Typical error: a Russian “privet” — a smile: In English — hello!

WHAT WE DO: p.6 ex.1–2 step by step: the Max/Lara dialogue with a picture → we fit in 3 phrases → audio 1 → choral reading by role

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Picture on p.6: Where are they? (at school — the first day back after the holidays, class 2A). I fill in the context in Russian — meaning matters more.
  2. Candidate phrases in chorus ×2: Hi Max · I'm fine · I don't know.
  3. Ex.1 in pairs: fit in the phrases (key: I'm fine · I don't know · Hi Max). Audio 1 — check: Were you right? High five!
  4. Reading by role: the class in half (half Max, half Lara), then SWAP — choral safety before any individual work.
  5. Pairs read the dialogue, swap roles. I help as I walk round.

Try it: a ladder of greetings (Mix-Pair-Share)
Try it · greetings ladder + ex.3
pairs mill

WHAT WE DO: Kagan Mix-Pair-Share (the ritual's first launch!): a ladder of greetings with movement + ex.3 synonym pairs with support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I teach the Kagan Mix-Pair-Share ritual as a procedure, commands in English: MIX — walk around! … PAIR UP — face your partner! → a question from the board → SHARE! One dry run with no language — just the mechanics.
  2. A mill drill, 3 rounds: Hello! I'm… What's your name? · How are you? — I'm fine, thanks! And you? Every round — a new partner.
  3. Ex.3 at the desks, pairs: match the synonyms (key: Hello=Hi · fine=OK · don't know=not sure · Thank you=Thanks · Goodbye=Bye). Support: two smileys for “formal/friendly”.
  4. Mini-conclusion in chorus: to a friend — Hi/Thanks/Bye; to an adult — Hello/Thank you/Goodbye.
📺 On screen: SB p.6 ex.3 · register smileys on the board
👦 Students do: they walk around greeting three partners (the pairs ritual is launched!), match synonyms with support. Expected: they take to the mill format right away. Typical error: forgetting And you? — an arrow gesture back.

Discover: my/your — whose is it?
Discover 2 · my/your — whose is it?
desks · teams

WHAT WE DO: a first, SUPPORTED touch on pronouns/possessives: my/your/his/her from the dialogue; ex.4 table filled in TOGETHER with me

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. From the dialogue: I'm Callum. MY name's Callum. What's YOUR name? Anchor on the board: my=mine · your=yours · his=his · her=hers (with picture arrows).
  2. Ex.4, we fill in the table TOGETHER on the board (not solo!): I→my, you→your, he→his, she→her, it→its, we→our, they→their. We read it in chorus, word pairs.
  3. Pre-support: the possessive goes BEFORE the word: my bag, her book.
  4. Mini-drill with classroom objects: I hold up Vika's pen: Her pen! — the teams answer in chorus; 6 objects.
  5. Ex.5 — only #1–2, together and orally (the rest not today: no overload).
📺 On screen: SB p.6 ex.4 · the anchor table with pictures (stays up on the wall)
👦 Students do: they build the table together with the teacher, drill with classroom objects in chorus. Expected: his/her get mixed up — that's normal, the anchor stays on the wall for a long time. Typical error: he's instead of his — for now just a model, no theory.
📓 Interactive notebook — Get_Involved_A1_Starter_Grammar1_Who_Whose_Visual_Takeaway_A5
A5 grammar card — WHO? → WHOSE?: the two-column table of subject pronouns against possessive adjectives (I–my · you–your · he–his · she–her · it–its · we–our · they–their) with a model sentence for every line and the rule WHO tells us the person, WHOSE shows OWNERSHIP
  • Glue the card in; pupils highlight the WHO column in one colour and WHOSE in another.
  • Cover the right-hand column and rebuild it from memory in the margin.
  • Point out the trap pair: it's (= it is) against its (ownership).
  • The card stays open through the challenge as the self-check key.

WHAT WE DO: the first written product: a card about myself — Hello! I'm… + 3 things with a colour; from the frame; reading it to a partner by Kagan Timed Pair Share (the listener draws what they hear)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Frame on the board: Hello! I'm ___ . This is my ___ . It's ___ (colour). I've got a ___ and a ___ . — I simplify the last line to My ___ is ___. if it's too hard.

  5. The cards go on the “Our class” wall (the course's first exhibition!).

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can greet, introduce themselves and ask How are you? using the p.6 dialogue as a model, and name 5+ personal objects with colours (my bag, a red notebook).
  • Subsidiary. An emotional start to the course: the group's names are learned through games; the rituals are launched (greeting at the door, Mix-Pair-Share, quiet signals, teams of 3); pronouns/possessives and a/an — a first, SUPPORTED touch; the “I CAN” final #1.
  • Personal. The most important pair of the year: the child decides whether it's safe here. Nobody is called on without being ready, choral comes before individual, mistakes are fixed with warmth. Success today = “I want to come back on Thursday”.
By the end, students can…
  • say hello, give their name, ask How are you?
  • name classmates (name game passed!)
  • say 5+ objects with my/a/an + colour
  • show the class signals (quiet signal, pair signal)

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“How are you? — what do we answer?” → I'm fine / I'm OK (+ thanks — polite!).
“my book or my a book?” → my book: my already “holds” the word, a isn't needed.
“Made a mistake — what happens here?” → Nothing bad: mistakes are how we learn!

Assumptions: True zero/near zero: scattered words from life (hello, OK, banana) are there, a system isn't. International words and Cyrillic-Latin crossovers help. Russian is legal in the lesson for meaning, English is for language. Course rule #1 (said out loud EVERY Starter pair): mistakes are how we learn — here nobody laughs at mistakes, here we grow from them. The teacher makes mistakes on purpose and fixes them out loud.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Fear of the first lesson (“I won't understand anything”)The first 15 min — only success-guaranteed actions: name, hello, choral, gestures. Clear to everyone = safe for everyone.
They don't know the group's names and feel shyName games twice a pair (at the start and in the middle); I keep using everyone's names myself all the time.
Pairs don't know how to work togetherWe learn the Mix-Pair-Share ritual as a procedure: signal → found a partner → face each other → whisper volume.
Different starting points (some already know something)For the “know-it-alls” — the helper role: help your pair, but let your partner do the talking.
a/an scares them as a ruleWe give it as “music”: an apple — it's just easier for the mouth. An anchor rule with no exceptions, a light drill.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» / «Showdown!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
