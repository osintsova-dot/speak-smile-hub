Into the lesson + a P3 review (what I did yesterday)
Opening · check-in + rewind

WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin for the review: entry; a P3 rewind: a “what I did yesterday” chain with support from the wall; a bridge to the description

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Entry: Hi! One past verb each — look at the wall if you need!
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. P3 rewind round the circle: Yesterday I ___ . — one sentence per person (+1 to the team for a clean form).
  4. Bridge: You can write about YOUR past. Today — about someone else's. First a character from a film. Then a real hero.
📺 On screen: verb wall (open) · scoreboard
👦 Students do: they give one sentence each round the circle about yesterday. Expected: the forms are noticeably cleaner after the homework. Typical error: “I goed” — a neighbour points at the wall.

Breaking down the model: three paragraphs
Discover · how a description is built
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: the model p.92 step by step: ex.1 two questions → ex.2 seven gaps with a support → ex.3 three paragraphs = three tasks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. We look at the poster: These people are in a film. Do you know Jean Grey?
  2. Ex.1 (key TB p.105): who the character is and why the author likes her. We read the text ALOUD by paragraph, I go sentence by sentence, the class repeats the key phrase.
  3. Ex.2 in pairs, 4 min, the text open (key TB p.105: New Jersey · she was eight · the car accident · she was unhappy at home and had problems at school · she met Professor Charles Xavier · went to study at the Xavier Institute · X-Men).
  4. Ex.3 all at once (key TB p.105: 1 c · 2 a · 3 b).
  5. The formula on the board, large (stays up all lesson): §1 — who he/she is and what he/she can do · §2 — family and childhood (THE PAST!) · §3 — why I like them.
📺 On screen: SB p.92 · board: the three-paragraph formula (stays up)
👦 Students do: they read paragraph by paragraph, find information in pairs, uncover the structure. Expected: the formula takes away the fear of “an essay”. Typical error: in §2 they write the present — I tap the word THE PAST.

12–19′
Presenting: three positions of also
Discover 2 · three positions of also
board · teams

WHAT WE DO: ex.4 three rules step by step → yellow card “not at the end” → ex.5 rewrite and guess the character

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I find three sentences with also in the text together with the class, underline them on the slide.
  2. Ex.4 step by step (key TB p.105): 1 after be — She's ALSO a good person. · 2 before other verbs — She ALSO HAD problems. · 3 between can and the verb — She can ALSO move objects.
  3. A yellow card on the board: NOT at the end! — ‘She's my favourite also’ — no. This is the number-one mistake (TB p.105).
  4. Drill: I give a sentence — the teams show the position NUMBER on their fingers (1, 2 or 3), then say it in full. ×6.
  5. Ex.5 p.93 by teams, 4 min: rewrite and guess the character. Key TB p.106: Thor · Jack Sparrow · Sherlock Holmes · Katniss Everdeen · Lisa Simpson.
📺 On screen: SB pp.92–93 · board: three positions of also + a yellow card · a slide with underlined examples (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they show the position on their fingers, rewrite and guess the characters. Expected: the guessing keeps interest on the grammar. Typical error: also at the end — we fix it in chorus.

Writing: chunk 1 (paragraphs 1–2)
Build · writing chunk 1

WHAT WE DO: THINK: choosing a character + notes from the bank → §1 and §2 using a support, 9 minutes

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. THINK (ex.6): a character from ex.5 or your own favourite — from a film, book, or series. A list on the board to help.
  2. Notes on four points (SB p.93): general information · family · early life · why you like him/her. Notes — single words, not sentences. 3 min.
  3. Reinforcement (TB p.106): together we check the forms of the needed verbs against the wall and the list SB pp.126–127.
  4. A fish support on the board: §1 My favourite character is ___ . He/She's a ___ in ___ . He/She can ___ . §2 He/She was born in ___ . His/Her father was ___ . When he/she was ___ , ___ .
  5. WRITE chunk 1: §1 and §2, 6 minutes, a visible timer. I go round and fix only the past.
📺 On screen: SB pp.92–93 Skills boost · board: formula + fish support · verb wall · timer
👦 Students do: they choose a character, write notes, then two paragraphs using the support. Expected: 4–5 sentences from everyone. Typical error: they write notes as sentences — “one word, not a phrase”.
💪 Support: a fish printout with ready-made openings for all three paragraphs. ⭐ For fast finishers: +1 sentence about the character's appearance (Challenge TB p.106).		notebooks · timer
28–36′
Writing: chunk 2 + checking
Build 2 · chunk 2 + checking

WHAT WE DO: §3 in 5 minutes → CHECK by three questions → peer review ex.7 by two ticks

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. WRITE chunk 2: §3 “why I like this character”, 5 minutes. Support: I like ___ because he/she's ___ , and he/she's also ___ .
  2. At least ONE also — in the right place!
  3. CHECK (ex.6) three questions on the slide: Three paragraphs? Past simple correct? Also in the right position? — we check our own work with a coloured pen.
  4. Peer review (ex.7), only two ticks: 1 — is the past simple correct? 2 — is ‘also’ in the right place? We swap notebooks in pairs, 2 min.
  5. I read aloud the two best §3's (+2 to the author's team).
📺 On screen: CHECK slide · coloured pens · §3 support on the board (Backup: everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they finish writing, check themselves and their partner's work on two points. Expected: peer review catches also better than self-checking. Typical error: “everything's fine” — the rule: only two ticks, no marks.

Play · Quick review + Grammar round-up
desks → standing

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: a game review before the test: 4 rounds from Quick review p.93 + the round-up moved from P3, p.90 ex.8

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ⚡ After the writing — stand up. Rules: teams, answers on mini-boards STANDING, the key straight to the screen, mutual help within the team is allowed.
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face-down, on the call No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up at once — I see every answer, nobody can hide.
  3. R1 be: Laika ___ a dog from Russia. · Singers ___ (−) on social media a hundred years ago. · ___ Grace Hopper a celebrity? — Yes, she ___ .
  4. R2 regular: watch · study · stop · dance → past (+ the rule number).
  5. R3 irregular: go · have · see · take (the wall is OPEN — today it's a support, not cheating).
  6. R4 vocab (audio 52–54): 2 jobs by definition · 1 date in words · 1 feeling in context.
  7. R5 Grammar round-up p.90 ex.8 “A statue for Mary Anning” — the first 5 items as a team (key TB p.104: was · studied · wasn't · don't · wants).
  8. Wrap-up: Your weakest round is tonight's homework. The test is next lesson — and you're ready.
📺 On screen: quiz slide + keys · mini-boards · SB pp.90, 93 · audio 52–54 (Backup: no track — I read the script aloud at listening speed; everything on the slide goes onto the board before the lesson (or onto printed cards, one per crew).)
👦 Students do: they answer as teams with mutual help, see their weak spot. Expected: the irregular verbs are still the weakest — an honest signal. Typical error: they argue within the team — I give them 10 seconds to decide.

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 video → breaking down the model p.95 step by step: how many frames, whose voice, why the pictures

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 (key TB p.107): She changed the world for a lot of women.
  2. We open p.95. We count together: How many pictures? (seven) How many words in each? (not many!)
  3. Ex.3 (key TB p.107): the text is in “I” — it makes the story personal, like the hero is speaking to you.

  5. Ex.2 as a race: what's NOT in the comic (key TB p.107: her sister's name Emily and the date of death).
  6. The brief on the board: 5–7 frames · in the first person “I” · past tense · short captions.

Creating a comic by template
Create · a comic by template
desks · pairs

WHAT WE DO: first 5–6 captions in the first person from the language bank, then we draw; a 13-minute timer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Order rule: Captions FIRST. Pictures after. Language is the mark, not the art.
  2. A language bank on the board (caption starters): I was born in ___ in ___ . · When I was ___ , my family ___ . · I studied ___ . · I was the first woman to ___ . · Later, I opened ___ . · I ___ (went / had / met / saw).
  3. Roles in the pair: one writes the captions, the other marks out the frames; ⚡ at the 7th minute — stand up, swap SEATS and roles.
  4. An A3 storyboard template with six empty frames — I hand one to every pair.
  5. I go round and fix ONLY the past tense and “I”. A 13:00 timer, a warning 3 minutes before the end.
📺 On screen: A3 storyboard template (6 frames) per pair · board: caption language bank · verb wall · timer
👦 Students do: they write captions from the bank, then draw, swap roles. Expected: 5–6 frames with text from every pair. Typical error: they start drawing — I only hand out pencils at the 6th minute.

WHAT WE DO: swapping comics with a neighbouring pair + a short 30–40 sec presentation; peer review ex.11 by three questions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.10: pairs swap comics with a neighbouring pair and read them.
  2. Presentation (our soft bar): Thirty seconds. Say WHO your hero is, ONE thing she did, and WHY she's a hero. Use ‘I’ if you want — like in the comic.
  3. Format: first every pair presents to their partner pair (everyone speaks at the same time), then 3 pairs by choice — for the class.
  4. Peer review (ex.11), three questions, one sentence each: Is the person a good unknown hero? Is the comic clear? Does it tell important things about the life?
  5. Applause for every pair, points to the teams.
📺 On screen: comics · timer · SB p.95 ex.11
👦 Students do: they show the comic to their partner pair, then volunteers — to the class. Expected: pride, everyone gets to perform. Typical error: they read the captions in a monotone — “tell it, don't read it”.

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can write a short three-paragraph description of a fictional character with past simple and correct also, and make and present a comic about an unknown hero.
  • Subsidiary. The model is broken down step by step; also is presented through three positions with a support; writing in chunks of 4–6 sentences; Quick review p.93 through games with mutual help; a comic by template with a language bank; Grammar round-up p.90 ex.8 (moved from P3) as a team quiz.
  • Personal. A double product, but for 🐣 writing is the priority. If time is tight, we make the comic 5 frames, not 7: it matters more that everyone wrote their own text and heard praise.
By the end, students can…
  • organise a description into three paragraphs
  • put also in the right place in three sentence types
  • write 5–6 sentences about a character with support
  • present a comic about a hero in simple sentences

Structure
My favourite character is … · She's a superhero in the … films. · She was born in … · Her father was a teacher. · She's also a good person. · I was born in Bristol in 1821.
Form
also goes AFTER be · BEFORE other verbs · BETWEEN can and the verb (SB p.92 ex.4) · §1 and §3 — present tense, §2 — past; the comic is in the first person (I was born…).
Pron. watch
also /ˈɔːlsəʊ/ — two syllables, stress on the first · born /bɔːn/ — silent r.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“She's a good person also — is that right?” → No: She's ALSO a good person.
“She also had problems or She had also problems?” → She ALSO HAD — before the ordinary verb.
“Which paragraph has the past tense?” → The second one — family and childhood.

Assumptions: The past from P2–P3 is active, but still needs support (the verb wall stays open all lesson). They put also at the end, the way you'd put “too” in their native language — that's the main target. Project work in pairs is familiar from U1–U6.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
