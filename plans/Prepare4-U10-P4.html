🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Use the seven thinking collocations (make up your mind, have second thoughts, cross your mind, give someone a hint, have a thought, lose your concentration, use your imagination) and read the illusions article for detail, justifying every true/false from the text.
What is genuinely hard
Collocations cannot be assembled word by word — «make up your mind» is one brick, and Russian offers no matching brick («решиться» is one word). The set has to be learned as chunks with their exact prepositions and pronouns.
Typical error to expect
brochure · advert(isement) · imagination · concentration · believe — the block's misspelling traps; a misspelt word scores nothing, in a class test and later in the exam
An opinion with no reason attached — I think e-books are better full stop — scores as no opinion at all in the speaking [national exam report]
Why the lesson is built this way
The lesson opens on the five illusions printed in the book — thirty seconds of genuine astonishment is the cheapest engagement of the year, spend it. The collocations arrive from the conversations ABOUT those pictures (track 94), so every chunk is anchored to a moment of real surprise. The Stroop race IS the move break — the lesson's own content does the stirring. The reading recycles the chunks and quietly plants next lesson's grammar (was designed by, were painted): the text is the passive's quarry, exactly as Unit 19 did it. The productive stage makes students SELL an illusion to a partner with the collocations compulsory — chunk retrieval under pleasant pressure.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Look at picture E and say the colours — not the words, the COLOURS. You can't. Your brain reads before it obeys you. Today: why your eyes lie, who earns money from it — and seven English chunks for what happens in your head while they do.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts. The rewind pulls the language this lesson builds on back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, the best review first-lines get their fame, and the bridge into the illusions

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — review headlines. Read aloud the three best FIRST LINES from the collected book reviews (no names unless they volunteer). The class votes the one that sells hardest.
  3. One reported-speech callback: Anya wrote that the story WAS incredible — see what she did? The shift, in the wild.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): bridge: Books make you see things that aren't there. So do your own eyes. Page one hundred and twelve — and don't say anything for thirty seconds.
📺 On screen: three first lines read by voice from the collected notebooks — voice and paper are the media, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: listen, vote, take the compliment. Expect authors to glow — publication is the point of the genre

WHAT WE DO: name the aim and rail the seven bricks

  3. Set the bar: Half a brick is a wrong answer. The whole chunk or nothing.

👦 Students do: listen; one student repeats the brick rule. Typical problem — «can't we just say decide»: yes, and B1 says make up your mind; both live on the rail

WHAT WE DO: ex.3 halves (track 94 again) + ex.4 completions — the rail fills

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

Key: collocation halves: 1 lost my concentration · 2 give me a hint · 3 cross your mind · 4 had a thought · 5 having second thoughts · 6 make up my mind · 7 use your imagination (verify the matching letters against your printed TB).
  2. Fill the board rail brick by brick as pairs confirm them; choral drill each brick ONCE, whole: make-up-your-mind. having-second-thoughts.
  3. ex.4 solo, 3 minutes, pair check. Key: 0 had a thought · 1 make up your mind · 2 having second thoughts · 3 crossed my mind · 4 give us a hint · 5 lose my concentration · 6 using my imagination (verify against your printed TB).

📺 On screen: track 94 replayed in the whiteboard app (Backup: the halves are printed in the book — match on paper and confirm against the key above by voice)
👦 Students do: match, drill whole bricks, complete. Expect cross your mind and have a thought to blur — anchor them: crossing = it visited; a thought = it arrived and you're telling us. Typical error: make up my thoughts

A5 glue-in sheet — INSIDE YOUR MIND: seven chunks about thinking, each with a definition and a model — cross your mind (suddenly think of something) · give someone a hint (give a small clue) · have a thought (think of an idea) · have second thoughts (begin to doubt a decision) · lose your concentration (stop focusing) · make up your mind (decide) · use your imagination (create ideas in your mind) — labelled by what each one is about: idea · hint · thought · doubt · focus · decision · creativity

WHAT WE DO: 90 seconds: picture E is the move break

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

👦 Students do: race the Stroop, lose gloriously, use a brick to describe it. The row times make it a team sport for 60 seconds

WHAT WE DO: ex.1 gist + first careful pass through the five worlds

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Pre-teach on the board: trick (v) · appear (= seem) · camouflage · pedestrian crossing (recycled from Unit 6!).
  2. Gist race, 3 minutes, books open: One question: what is the article mostly about — how illusions work, how they're USED, or illusions in nature? Key: gist: A — the ways optical illusions are used (verify against your printed TB).
  3. Map the five worlds on the board as students find them: ads · fashion · roads · nature · plates. The content: Illusions trick our brains: the Ancient Greeks studied them 2,000 years ago and scientists still can't fully explain them. Five worlds of examples: advertising (Akiyoshi Kitaoka's rotating snakes, made for a New Year's card, later used in a car ad) · fashion (Alexander McQueen's striped dress that makes the wearer look slimmer) · roads (3D pedestrian crossings painted in China — drivers slow down) · nature (camouflage that hides hunters and prey) · plates (the same food looks MORE on a small plate — restaurants use the trick).
  4. Park two sentences VERBATIM in the board corner: «The dress was designed by McQueen» · «3D crossings were painted on roads» — remember these. Next lesson they explode.
📺 On screen: track 95 is the recording of the text — optional; the text is in every book (Backup: skip the audio entirely, the stage is built on silent reading)
👦 Students do: race for gist, map the worlds, notice the parked lines. Expect the plates paragraph to trigger instant self-recognition — ride it into the true/false. Typical problem: gist answered with a detail — re-ask «MOSTLY about»

WHAT WE DO: ex.2 justified from the text + ex.3 highlighted words

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Kagan RallyCoach on ex.2: A decides items 1–3 and reads the deciding line aloud, B coaches; swap for 4–6. Key: 1 T (scientists can't fully explain) · 2 F (Kitaoka made it for a New Year's card — the car ad came later) · 3 F (several celebrities bought one, so more than one was made) · 4 T (animals use illusion to hunt) · 5 T (drivers slow down) · 6 F (smaller plates make food look MORE — the healthy trick is a smaller plate) (verify against your printed TB) — and flag honestly: if your book disagrees with me on item 3, we check the Teacher's Book together.
  2. ex.3 fast in pairs: the five highlighted words to meanings. Key: 1 trick — e make someone believe something that is not true · 2 appears — b seems · 3 catch — a stop from escaping · 4 encourage — c make more likely to do · 5 pay attention — d watch, listen or think carefully.
  3. Collect with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Item five — number threes!
  4. If the pace allows, Talking points: What practical uses of optical illusions have you seen? · Which illusion from the unit is your favourite so far?
📺 On screen: the text is in every book — paper is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: decide with receipts, match words. Expect item 2 (Kitaoka) to catch keyword-matchers — the card came FIRST, the ad later; the quote settles it. Typical error: true/false from the wow, not the text

Why: 🎯 Speaking out · sell an illusion

WHAT WE DO: pitch one illusion use to a partner — bricks compulsory

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Setup: everyone picks ONE world from the article (ads, fashion, roads, nature, plates) or one picture from p.112. Two minutes solo: three notes — what it is, why it works, one brick you'll use.
  2. Kagan Timed Pair Share, 2 minutes each way: SELL your illusion — You won't believe it, but drivers slow down for paint. It never crossed their minds it was flat! Partner asks one sceptical question.
  3. Swap partners once and sell again, faster and better — second-hand pitches are allowed to steal the best lines they heard.
  4. Finale: two volunteers pitch to the class; the class votes with the brick-finger — a raised finger per chunk heard.
  5. You walk and log: half-bricks and missing becauses — they open lesson 5.
📺 On screen: the pitch stems in chalk (It works because… · It never crossed my mind that… · Use your imagination:…) — the board is the medium, so no backup is needed
👦 Students do: pitch, doubt, re-pitch. Expect round 2 to be audibly slicker — name it. Typical error: the pitch retells the text — push for «sell it to ME, don't summarise it»

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
«I'm having second thoughts» — did I decide before? → yes; am I sure now? → no: the decision is wobbling.
«It crossed my mind» — did I think about it for long? → no: the thought walked through and left.
«Give me a hint» — do I want the full answer? → no: a small push, not the solution.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Chunks assembled word by word («make up my thoughts»)Sell them as bricks: seven cards on the board rail, never split. Every use in class must be the WHOLE brick — half-bricks are gently bounced back.
The illusions eat the lesson — ten minutes of «wow» and no EnglishBudget the wonder: 30 silent seconds + 3 minutes of ex.1 talk, then the pictures only return as speaking fuel. The timer is visible.
True/false answered from memory of the wow, not from the textThe quote rule: an answer without its line read aloud does not count. RallyCoach enforces it pair-internally.
imagination/concentration misspelled in notebooksBoth words syllable-clapped once (i-ma-gi-NA-tion) and boarded; the WB anagram homework tonight drills exactly these.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
