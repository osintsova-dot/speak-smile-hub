Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, children will be better able to follow & enjoy the story «Giant Joe» (CD2 T21) and understand “It's time to (get dressed / have breakfast)!” — Grandpa keeps putting off the story because it's time for the next routine.
  • Subsidiary. Predict from the pictures; listen (CD2 T21) with the story cards; play It's time to…! & the dynamic Giant Joe's breakfast game; act tiny bits; answer story questions & circle Grandpa's story (PB p.31); Phonics Hero — the /aɪ/ spellings ie igh y i-e, blend & speed-read p.72–74.
  • Personal. Let the story breathe — big Grandpa voice, pauses, the running joke of “Not now… it's time to…”. Don't test every word; celebrate joining in. Give every child a routine card so all are involved.
By the end, pupils can…
  • follow the story & point to what happens in each picture;
  • understand “It's time to (get dressed)!”;
  • name the routines Giant Joe does in Grandpa's story.

Form
It's time to + routine (a giving-instructions chunk). Can we have a story, please? = polite request (recycled).
Pron. watch
patient, funny Grandpa Not now… it's time to…; giant, booming I'm Giant Joe!
Phonics
Phonics Hero 2.0 — the long /aɪ/ sound written ie · igh · y · i-e. Jolly /ie/ action → blend (p-ie → pie, n-igh-t → night, f-l-y → fly, b-i-ke → bike) → speed-read p.72–74.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Grandpa says it's time to have breakfast — do we eat or sleep?” → eat!
“Who is Giant Joe — a real giant, or a story?” → a story Grandpa tells!

Assumptions: All ten routines & What do you do every day? are active from L1–L2; reading continues in the Phonics Hero workbook. The story-time routine (listen · point · join in) is well established from earlier units. It's time to… is new but very supported by mime.

Likely problemWhat you do
Story audio a bit fastPlay once for fun, then again pausing on each picture; point & mime as you go.
Children lose the threadUse the six big story cards frame by frame; ask a yes/no picture question after each.
Everyone wants to run at onceGive one routine card each; only the child with the called routine runs.
Just says the routine, not the phrasePuppet models It's time to… HAVE BREAKFAST!; child echoes with the mime.
Wriggling during listeningGive a job: wave when you hear a routine! — active listening.

9–17′
Phonics Hero 🔤 ⚡
carpet, moving

① Recognise / review. Phonics Hero lesson 26 · long /aɪ/ — sounds ie · igh · y · i-e  (all say /aɪ/). 🦝 Speaky shows each with its Jolly action (gesture technique); children do the action and NAME the sound — they already know all 26. We don't drill bare sounds: teacher voices, children gesture.

② Blend / build. Build the word: p-ie → pie, n-igh-t → night, f-l-y → fly, b-i-ke → bike — arm-sweep + a meaning picture. ▶ Blend relay — Speaky shows an /aɪ/ word; teams blend it and race to touch its picture.

③ Speed reading. open Phonics Hero 2.0 (our workbook) p.72–74 — finger under each word, read the row FAST as a group (whole words, playful, celebrate speed — real reading from zero, GMF1-style).

Tricky words: read review Red tricky words together as whole words (we don't sound tricky words out).

17–26′
Curiosity input · Story «Giant Joe»
carpet

Predict. Show the six story cards — Mimi & Dylan stay with Grandma & Grandpa. What do they want Grandpa to do? Take 2–3 guesses (Russian OK).

Listen 1 — for fun. Play the story (CD2 T21) with the cards. Pause on each picture — enjoy the running joke: Can we have a story? — Not now! It's time to get dressed! …have breakfast! Children point & join the routines they can.

Listen 2 — spot the routine. Wave when you hear GET DRESSED! children wave when they hear it — active listening.

📱 Coursebook app (Macmillan digital): Navio App — play the daily-routines vocabulary game and watch the Giant Joe story video on screen; children tap the routine they hear.

26–36′
Game · It's time to…! + Giant Joe's breakfast (19) ⚡
carpet / floor space

▶ It's time to…! Say It's time to… ? and mime a routine; children name it. Fast, several rounds — everyone answers.

▶ Giant Joe's breakfast Genki Steal the Bacon, re-skinned: two teams line up; routine flashcards in the middle. 🦝 Speaky (Grandpa) calls It's time to… have breakfast! — one runner from each team races to grab it, mimes it and says the routine. Every routine called → everyone runs at least once.

💪 Support: run, grab & mime the routine. ⭐ Extension: say the whole line It's time to have breakfast!

 routine flashcards ×10 · Speaky (Grandpa)
36–42′
Reading corner 📖
carpet

Read-aloud. Read our own Speak & Smile story-book «What Time Is It, Mr Wolf?» aloud — Mr Wolf goes through his day by the clock — a gentle time-and-routine read, calm after the running game and perfect for our “it's time to…” story.

Hold up each spread — children look at the pictures, do the gestures and echo the key line It's time to get up! It's time for breakfast!. Pre-readers: they listen, watch & repeat, they don't read the words.

Point to the clock on each page — children guess It's time to…! and mime it; cheer at the end.
