Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to follow, read and act out the story «An underwater adventure» and use comparatives in context: “It's bigger than a fish. It's heavier than a shark. It's a whale!”
  • Subsidiary. Break Luke's code («SUBMARINE») — recycles the alphabet code; Values — protecting sea animals / rubbish in the sea (the “gold coins” are really cans!); story skills: predict → listen → read along → Read & correct → act out; consolidate the L2 comparatives inside a real story.
  • Personal. Let the story breathe — big voices, pauses, prediction. Notice comparatives as they sound (raise a “-er than” card), don't over-drill — it's story time. Give every child a role.
By the end, pupils can…
  • retell 2–3 things that happen on the underwater trip;
  • read their role's lines from the book;
  • correct false statements: “No, they don't. They go on a mini submarine.”

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“It's bigger than a fish AND heavier than a shark — what big animal is it?” → a whale!
“The shiny objects — real gold coins or rubbish?” → rubbish — old cans! Put them in the bin.

Assumptions: Comparatives (-er + than) and the 12 sea animals are active from L1–L2; the alphabet code was learnt earlier (recycled in pre-reading). Act-out routine is familiar from earlier units. Readers follow the text with a finger.

Listen 1 — gist (CD2 41, books closed). Listen: what do the children go on? What do they find at the bottom? Check ideas.

Listen 2 — read along (books open pp.52–53). Finger under the text. Pause per frame: It's bigger than a fish… and heavier than a shark… What is it? — A whale! Raise the “-er than” card each time it sounds.

Values (act. 4). The shiny objects are cans, not gold! What rubbish can we find in the sea? Why is it dangerous for sea animals? What can WE do? 3–4 hands, Russian OK, recast key words.

Read & correct · comprehension
carpet → desks

Read and correct (act. 3, Talk Partners). Statements: The children go on a boat… true or false? Pairs correct the false ones fully: No, they don't. They go on a mini submarine. 5 statements — every pair speaks.

Which frame? (Showdown). Mini-whiteboards: you read a line — “It's a whale. Amazing!” — which frame? — all write the number, Show me! 4 rounds.

✔ Assess: the boards are your check — who follows the story? The rest sit with you in the board game.

💪 Support: point to the right frame in the book. ⭐ Extension: add a second true fact about that frame.

28–40′
Act out
carpet, groups of 4

Model a frame. You + 3 pupils play the “big animal” frame with big drama: Look! It's bigger than a fish! And heavier than a shark! It's a WHALE!

Groups of 4 (Lily · Josh · Ravi · Grandad/Captain). Rehearse 2 frames with books as scripts. Rotate roles once.

Showtime. Each group performs ONE frame (30–40 sec — fast carousel, no long waits). Audience task: shout the sea animal / the message (“It's rubbish!”) at the end.

💪 Support: shortest lines / the “Hurray! We're rich!” chorus for the shyest child. ⭐ Extension: add an extra line from the frame.
