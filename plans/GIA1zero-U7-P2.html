WHAT WE DO: Timed RoundRobin for the review: entry; a P1 rewind woven in: a “job + date” chain about a celebrity; a bridge to the past

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Entry: Hi! One job word each — no repeats!
  2. This is Kagan Timed RoundRobin: strictly round the circle against the timer, one turn — one person, no skipping: Clockwise, one each — keep it moving, no skips!
  3. P1 rewind as a chain by teams: Neil Armstrong is an astronaut. His day is the twentieth of July. — one celebrity per team (+1 for the date in words).
  4. Bridge: Neil Armstrong IS an astronaut? He walked on the Moon in 1969. He isn't an astronaut now. So… he WAS an astronaut. Today: the past.

👦 Students do: they name a celebrity with a job and a date in a chain. Expected: dates are noticeably cleaner after the homework. Typical error: the date in numbers — I point at the years ruler.

Hook · was/were from the article

WHAT WE DO: discovering the rule from yesterday's text: is→was, are→were; we build a support table on the board

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. I write 4 sentences on the board (Warmer TB p.100): 1 Usain Bolt and Simone Biles ___ sporting heroes. 2 Doug the Pug ___ a celebrity animal. 3 Gladiators ___ sporting heroes. 4 Laika ___ the first animal astronaut.
  2. Which two are about NOW? (1 and 2 — are, is) Which two are about the PAST?
  3. We open p.87 and find the answer in the text: were, was. Chorus.
  4. I build a SUPPORT TABLE on the board: I / He / She / It → WAS ‖ You / We / They → WERE. Next to it — wasn't / weren't.
  5. Was is the past of is and am. Were is the past of are. That's all — the whole system.
📺 On screen: board: 4 Warmer sentences → SUPPORT TABLE was/were (stays up all lesson) · SB p.87
👦 Students do: they find the forms in the text themselves — a discovery, not a dictation. Expected: “oh, that's easy!”. Typical error: they try to add -ed — “no, be is special, it has its own word”.

WHAT WE DO: Showdown: ex.1 rules → a choral drill with gestures → ex.2 by (+)/(−) signs, step by step, with a support

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Ex.1 together (key TB p.100: we/you/they → were, weren't · I/he/she/it → was, wasn't). Full forms: was not · were not — and the contracted ones.
  2. I frame it as Kagan Showdown: EVERYONE writes at the same time, boards face-down, on the call No peeking… Boards up! they all hold them up at once — I see every answer, nobody can hide.
  3. Drill with gestures: I say the subject — the class says the form in chorus, pointing a hand at the right half of the table. She — ? (was) · They — ? (were) · The gladiators — ? (were) · Laika — ? (was) · My brother and I — ? (were) ×10, I speed up.
  4. Negative with the same gesture + crossed arms: She WASN'T. They WEREN'T.
  5. Ex.2 step by step: read the sentence → look at the (+) or (−) sign → point a hand at the half of the table → write. Key TB p.100: was · wasn't · were · was/wasn't · was/were.
  6. Check all at once on mini-boards.
📺 On screen: board: support table · mini-boards · SB p.88 ex.1–2
👦 Students do: they answer in chorus pointing at the table, fill in ex.2 step by step. Expected: pointing at the table solves half the errors. Typical error: “my brother and I was” — we count the people on our fingers: two → were.

WHAT WE DO: a moving drill: a living line and Quiz-Quiz-Trade — everybody speaks at the same time, not a single spectator

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ▶ The living line: the class stands in two rows facing each other. Question on the board: Where were you at eight o'clock yesterday? — an exchange of answers I was at home / at school / at my grandma's. On the whistle, one row shifts — a new partner. 4 shifts.
  2. I change the question: Where were you on Sunday morning? Who were you with?
  3. ▶ Quiz-Quiz-Trade: a card with a picture (place/time). Ask — answer — swap — new partner! 3 min.
  4. We come back together: three answers to the class, I write them on the board as a model.
📺 On screen: place/time cards (12–15 pcs) · board: question frame · whistle
👦 Students do: everybody speaks at the same time in two rows, then swaps cards — they move for 8 minutes. Expected: the form settles through repetition in motion. Typical error: “I was in home” — the frame “at home” on the board.

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:

WHAT WE DO: presenting 12 adjectives: ex.1 the article → ex.2 emoji A–F with miming → pronunciation → the frame I was / I felt

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Bridge: Being famous — is it always nice? How do celebrities FEEL?
  2. Ex.1 (article p.89): we read five sentences, everyone shows on their fingers 1–5 how much it's about them. Five bold words: interested · bored · pleased · worried · tired — chorus + miming.
  3. Ex.2 emoji A–F: we react with our FACE, then name it. Key TB p.102: A surprised · B hungry · C relaxed · D ill · E afraid · F thirsty. The extra word — well.
  4. Well means healthy — the opposite of ill.
  5. Pronunciation (TB p.102): relaxed — 2 syllables, ends /t/ · thirsty — silent r, a long /ɜː/ · bored — 1 syllable, silent r.
  6. Frame on the board: I'm ___ . / I was ___ yesterday. — never ‘I have hungry’.

WHAT WE DO: writing in a chunk: 4–5 sentences about yourself at six using a support → we read it to a partner, the partner asks one question

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ⚡ Before writing — a 30 sec shake-up: stand up, stretch, sit down.
  2. Support on the board (exactly ex.6 p.88, but about yourself): When I was six… My best friend was ___ . My favourite food was ___ . My school was ___ . My teacher's name was ___ . I was ___ (feeling word!).

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can use was/were and wasn't/weren't in affirmative, negative, Yes/No questions with short answers and Wh- questions — about themselves and about people from the past.
  • Subsidiary. A full step-by-step presentation with a table and supports; drills only through games; 12 adjectives to describe feelings via emoji and miming; listening (news report) in two passes with the subskill matching speakers; writing in chunks “Me when I was six”.
  • Personal. ⚠️ This is the FIRST big new grammar of the past. Give it room to breathe: one form at a time, each through a support, not a single dry drill. If the group gets tired — I cut the game, not the table.
By the end, students can…
  • say where they were yesterday with was/were
  • make negatives with wasn't/weren't
  • ask Was/Were…? and give a true short answer
  • match three speakers with what they say

Target language
Lexis
afraidboredhungryillinterestedpleasedrelaxedsurprisedthirstytiredwellworried
Structure
I/He/She/It was · You/We/They were · wasn't / weren't (was not · were not) · (Wh-) + was/were + subject: Was Laika an astronaut? · Were The Beatles from London? · Where were you yesterday? · Yes, she was. / No, they weren't. (short answer WITHOUT the full n't form: NOT ‘Yes, she wasn't’)
Form
was = the past of is/am · were = the past of are · there was / there were = the past of there is/are · in a question was/were COMES FIRST.
Pron. watch
was in a question — weak /wəz/, in a short answer — strong /wɒz/ (SB p.117; keys TB p.101) · bored 1 syllable, relaxed 2 syllables with a /t/ at the end, worried 2 syllables (TB pp.101–102).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“I am → past?” → I WAS. “They are → past?” → They WERE.
“Yes, she wasn't — is that allowed?” → No! Yes, she was. / No, she wasn't.
“Where you were yesterday?” → No: Where WERE you yesterday? — was/were comes first.

Assumptions: They know be in the present (is/am/are) — this is the ONLY support we build the past on. Some feelings are familiar (tired, hungry); pleased, worried, relaxed, interested are new. We do NOT touch regular and irregular verbs today.

Likely problemWhat you do

They forget it's the PASTA red timeline on the board: NOW on the right, YESTERDAY on the left. Any was/were — I point left with my hand.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
