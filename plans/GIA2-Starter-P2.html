🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Family and appearance is the most «babyish»-looking topic in the Starter, and it is exactly the one teenagers meet with a sigh. So it is delivered as field work: the agent's personal file and a photofit the class has to identify. Possessives and have got are working under load here (the name once, then his/her/their; the description strictly through has got), not inside an exercise. The apostrophe ' / 's is presented as «paperwork precision» — in a file one mark in the wrong place means a different person. The clinic of errors collected silently in P1 also belongs here.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Your passport is registered. Now the file. Every agent has a personal file: who is in the family, and what the agent LOOKS like. Two jobs today. One: the Records Office — full family file, correct apostrophes, because 'my cousins name' and 'my cousins' names' are two different people. Two: the Photofit Lab — you describe a classmate, and the class identifies them. If nobody guesses, your description failed. Files open.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: The passport is done — now the personal file: family and appearance. First the records office (family + possessives, the apostrophe decides who you are talking about), then the photofit lab: you describe a classmate and the class has to identify them.

🔥 Challenge · possessive duel
crews · mini-boards

WHAT WE DO: ex.6–7 they complete the possessives table themselves → ex.8 the apostrophe → an 8-second duel «how many owners?»

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.6 with no explanation: The example is there. The box is there. Close the table. Two minutes. The key chorally, and they formulate the rule.
  2. ex.7 in writing, then Kagan Showdown: everyone writes, and on the call Showdown! — boards up all at once.
  3. ex.8 the apostrophe: I give them a working test and repeat it all lesson — How many owners? Does the word already end in -s? One owner → ’s. Several and already ending in -s → just ’.
  4. The duel, 8 seconds per turn: I show a phrase in Russian («the house of my grandparents») — the crew writes the English version with the apostrophe. An apostrophe error = the turn burns.
📺 On screen: SB p.8 ex.6–7 + p.9 ex.8 (the side box «possessive ’s and s’») · mini-boards · 8-sec timer
👦 Students do: they build the rule themselves, then they fire. Expected: his/he's and its/it's are the main confusion, and the mini-boards make it visible to everyone. Mistake: «my parent's names» — the neighbour catches it, not me

Photofit lab

WHAT WE DO: p.9: ex.1 find the people in the picture → ex.2 build the description table → audio 5: five descriptions ↔ people

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.1 as a race: Four descriptions, eleven people. Find them. Ninety seconds. Key: 1 B · 2 J · 3 C · 4 E.
  2. ex.2: they build the description table out of ex.1 themselves (General description · Hair · Hair colour · Eyes · Other). Fast finishers add their own words — as the TB says.
  3. Audio 5 as a field recording: Five radio descriptions. Match them to the people. You get ONE listen at full speed, then one more. Key: 1 K · 2 H · 3 G · 4 A · 5 D.
  4. Going over the misses: Which word decided it for you? 'Grey beard'? 'No hair'? — that is exactly the training in precise description.
  5. Kagan Inside-Outside Circle, 3 minutes: the outer circle describes a person from the picture, the inner circle guesses the letter, then everyone shifts by one.

👦 Students do: they look for the people, build the table, listen. Expected: on the second listen almost everyone gets it; the arguable ones are G and A (beard/glasses). Mistake: «She has got blue hairs» — hair is uncountable, I fix it with a recast

WHAT WE DO: ex.4–6 the three have got tables are closed as a race → ex.7 questions → the production line: 8 seconds for a question + short answer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. ex.4–6: Three tables, four minutes, all from the examples. Close them. Keys chorally. I give the rule about any in the negative in one sentence (as the TB says).

PRODUCT · the personal file
pairs → stage

WHAT WE DO: the personal file: 6 lines about your partner (3 on family + 3 photofit) → the identification lab on stage

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. A 4-minute interview in pairs: Ask, take notes. You may invent an agent instead — your choice, nobody asks why.
  2. 6 lines of writing, the checker works down the checklist: the name once, then his/her/their · the apostrophe checked with the two questions · has got, not have got · the description complimentary.
  3. On stage, the identification lab: Read your photofit. No name. The class identifies the person. If nobody guesses — the description failed, no points.
  4. The judges hold the rubric; an identification on the first attempt — +2 to the crew of whoever wrote it.
  5. File APPROVED — the stamp for a file with no errors of form.
📺 On screen: the file checklist on the slide · timer (backup: I write the 4-point checklist on the board at the start of the block and do not rub it off)
👦 Students do: they interview, write, check, identify. Expected: identification turns accuracy of language into a sport — details like «has got glasses AND a grey beard» appear by themselves. Mistake: reading off the page in a monotone — the judges take off fluency

🏠
Homework

Core (the task from the Teacher's Book, p.16): write a description of your family — 1–2 sentences about each of them: who they are and what they look like (My uncle Sasha has got short grey hair and glasses.). At least 5 people. It may be about an invented agent — the rule from the lesson applies at home too.

🚀 Bonus (+3 on the leaderboard): a photofit of a celebrity — 4 lines with has got, no name. In P3 we read them out and the class guesses in 3 attempts.

🔁 Words: the Starter cards — the «family» (14) and «appearance» (17) sets, 5–7 minutes.

⚠️ There is NO Starter section in the Workbook — the homework comes from the Teacher's Book + our cards · we read the photofits at the start of P3
📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, agents can write a 6-line personal file about another person (family + appearance) using possessive adjectives, possessive ’s/s’ and have got accurately, and describe a classmate so precisely that the class identifies them.
  • Subsidiary. The 14 family words are sorted into Male/Female/Both (SB p.8 ex.1) and practised on the three readers' profiles (ex.3–5); the describing-people table is built from the picture (p.9 ex.1–2) and checked against audio 5; the have got tables are closed as a race (ex.4–6). Brain teaser p.9 ex.9 — as a bonus logic task.
  • Personal. This is where I run the first clinic: the 4 errors collected silently in P1 are gone over anonymously, 5 seconds each. I watch that the descriptions of appearance stay complimentary — I say the rule BEFORE the task, not after an incident.
By the end, students can…
  • sort 14 family words and explain a family relationship
  • use my/your/his/her/its/our/their without slips
  • put ’s and s’ in the right place
  • describe a person with have got so precisely that others identify them

Structure
I/You/We/They have got black hair. / haven't got blue eyes. · He/She/It has got short hair. / hasn't got glasses. · My nephew's name is Jia. (singular + 's) · Their sons' names are Caleb and Damien. (plural in -s + ')
Form
I → my · you → your · he → his · she → her · it → its · we → our · they → their · in the negative with have got, a plural noun takes any: They haven't got any children. · 3rd person: has got / hasn't got (not have) · the short answer never repeats got: Yes, he has (not «Yes, he has got»)

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
«his» or «he's»? → his = whose (his name), he's = he is (he's tall).
«its» or «it's»? → its = possessive, it's = it is.
«She have got a cat» — what's wrong? → she has got.
«Yes, I have got» — is that the short answer? → no, the short one is: Yes, I have.

Assumptions: SB pp.8–9, KTP pair 2 (lessons 3–4). Family and have got are familiar from A1+; what is genuinely new is nephew/niece, the plural apostrophe (s’) and the precision a description needs when it has to identify someone.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
«Family? That's for little kids»The topic is never named — what is named is «the personal file» and «the photofit». On top of that the gate shows immediately: hardly anyone writes nephew, niece, grandparents’ house correctly. Familiar ≠ under control.
Descriptions of appearance turn into digsThe rule comes before the task, not after: Compliments only. A rude description scores zero and the crew loses the round. The judges watch this as closely as the grammar.
They put the apostrophe in at randomA two-question test that I repeat all lesson: «how many owners?» and «is there already an -s?» One → ’s. Several and already ending in -s → just ’.
Someone does not want to talk about their own familyI always give a choice: your own file OR the file of an invented agent / character. I announce the choice up front, so that nobody has to refuse in public.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
