Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to form, pronounce and use adverbs of manner derived from adjectives (regular -ly, -y → -ily, irregular fast/hard/well/early) and to talk about themselves and their personal style in a semi-controlled exchange.
  • Subsidiary. Listening for gist and specific detail (a conversation about fashion & music of the past); recycle Unit 2 clothes adjectives from spread 1; integrate present simple + adverbs of frequency for talking about habits.
  • Personal. Keep TTT low; set an ICQ before every task; run guided discovery, not rules-first; use Timed Pair Share so both partners speak; log errors for delayed correction, not on-the-spot.
By the end, pupils can…
  • build adverbs from the 15 target adjectives and use the right spelling rule;
  • catch gist + 4–6 specific details in an audio conversation;
  • answer “What do you like wearing? / How important is fashion?” with a reason and an opinion.

Skills chunks
I usually wear… · Today I'm wearing… · I got it last weekend… · because it's comfortable · I really like… · I (don't) think… · How often do you…?
Form
adjective + -ly (quick→quickly) · consonant + -y → -ily (heavy→heavily) · -le → -ly (gentle→gently) · irregular: fast/hard/early stay the same, good → well. Adverb describes a verb/action, adjective describes a noun.
Pron. watch
unstressed -ly /li/; healthily = 3 syllables /ˈhelθ·ɪ·li/; stress in politely on syllable 2; contrast good (adj) vs well (adv).
Skills focus
Listening: gist → detail (Sara & her grandma, fashion & music of the past). Speaking: talking about yourself & your style, present simple + adverbs of frequency + reasons/opinions.
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“She is careful” — describing a person or an action? → a person (adjective).
“She drives carefully” — what does it describe? → the action / how she drives (adverb).
“Do we say He sings good?” → no → He sings well.

Assumptions: learners know Unit 2 clothes adjectives (comfortable, skinny, brand new, smart, trendy, well-/badly-dressed) and past simple from spread 1; they can read model texts and take notes. They meet adverbs of frequency from Prepare 3 (revision). Grouping: pairs for controlled & speaking work; teams of 3–4 for Quiz-Quiz-Trade / Showdown (12 = 3×4 · 10 = 3+3+4 · 9 = 3×3).

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Over-generalise: fastly, hardly, goodlyKeep an “Irregular” column on the board; highlight fast/hard/early/well as a fixed set to memorise.
healthily / seriously mispronounced or over-syllabledBack-chain by syllable, short choral drill; mark the stress.
Adjective used where adverb is needed (She sings good)Use the contrast frame + CCQ; drill the “person vs action” test.
In speaking, learners stay in one tensePoint to the tense track (usually = present simple · today = present continuous · last weekend = past simple).
Panic at unknown words in the audioSet the gist task first, play twice, and pre-teach only prom / going-away if needed.
One partner dominates the talkTimed Pair Share — equal timed turns, listener has a note-taking job.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes this way, 3–2–1” — countdown to full attention, pens down.
Volume 0–3 — name the level: 0 silent · 1 pair whisper · 2 pair talk · 3 presenting.
“Track the speaker” — turn to whoever is talking.
Timed turns — visible timer; on the beep, swap roles.
“Freeze — hands up” — mid-task reset without raising your voice.
Delayed correction — errors go on the board, fixed as a class at feedback.

Lead-in / recycle
whole class → pairs

Warm up & retrieve. Board a quick photo of a well-known style. Describe this person in three words. Elicit Unit 2 adjectives from spread 1 (smart, casual, trendy, comfortable, second-hand…).

▶ RallyRobin (pairs, 90 sec): Take turns — one clothes adjective each, don't repeat your partner. This is a spaced-retrieval starter of last lesson's lexis, feeding straight into today's topic.

Hook the topic. Today we talk about HOW we do things — and about your own style. Look at the two example sentences. Board: Hip hop became popular internationally. / He shouted at us angrily. — Which word tells us HOW?

Vocabulary — Adverbs (guided discovery)
board → SB → teams

Discovery (SB p.16, Ex 1). What is the adjective inside each adverb? internationally → ? · angrily → ? · hard → ? Draw out the rule from the learners, don't give it.

Build the table (SB p.16, Ex 2). Three columns on the board: + -ly · -y → -ily · Irregular. In teams, sort the 15 adjectives (amazing, bad, careful, early, fast, good, healthy, heavy, honest, lazy, lucky, polite, quick, rude, serious). Model amazing → amazingly.

CCQ & contrast. “careful” — person or action? “carefully” — person or action? Board the contrast: She is careful / She chooses carefully. Highlight the irregular set fast · hard · early · well: These four never take -ly.

Pron. drill. Chorus healthily · seriously · politely; mark stress; contrast good / well.

Controlled practice (SB p.16, Ex 3). Learners complete 8 sentences with the right adverb. ▶ RallyCoach in pairs — A solves & says it aloud, B coaches & ticks; swap each item. ICQ: Do you write the adjective or the adverb? (→ the adverb).

Listening — fashion & music of the past
whole class → pairs

Set the scene (SB p.17, Ex 1). Show the Beatles photo. What do you know about them? What are they wearing? Brief pair chat (30 sec).

Gist (Ex 2, track 07). Listen to Sara and her grandma. What does grandma talk about — a, b or c? Play once, check in pairs, whole-class check. ▶ Showdown: on the count, teams reveal their letter card.

Detail — first half (Ex 3, track 08). Read the four options first, then listen and choose. Play once (twice if needed). Pairs compare, then class check.

Detail — second half (Ex 4, track 09). Complete the sentences with words from the box (beards, blue, dresses, hair, jacket, jeans, loose-fitting, necklaces, tights). Listen and complete, then listen again to check.

Exam bridge (OGE, light). This is exactly the OGE skill — read the questions first, then listen for the detail.

Language focus — Prepare to speak
whole class → desks

Model & notice (SB p.17, Speaking Ex 3–5, track 10). Listen to Harry talk about himself. Which phrases from the Prepare to speak box does he use? Elicit: present simple + adverbs of frequency for habits (I usually…, I always…, I often…), reasons (because…), likes/dislikes (I really like…), opinion (I think… / I don't think…).

Question form (Ex 2). Learners choose the correct words to build the four questions (What clothes do you like wearing? How often do you buy…? Where do you usually buy…? How much do you spend…?). Quick pair check.

📓 Interactive notebook — “Talk about yourself + your style” (A5 glue-in). Glue in Prepare4_Unit2_Speaking_Personal_Style: the Mia–Leo model with the tense track in the margin (usually = present simple · today = present continuous · last weekend = past simple), a three-part PHRASE BANK (DESCRIBE / GIVE A REASON / KEEP IT GOING) and the six-step speaking route (usually wear → today → favourite outfit → where you got it → is fashion important). Tiered frames (EAL sentence-frame scaffold): support level — I usually wear ___ because ___; stretch level — learners drop the frame and link two tenses in one turn.

58–70′
Controlled → semi-controlled speaking
pairs

▶ Timed Pair Share (SB p.17, Speaking Ex 6). Partner A answers the four questions using the notebook phrase bank; Partner B listens and notes one adverb of frequency + one reason A uses. Beep — swap roles. Use at least one adverb and one reason in each answer.

Push for range. Second round, new partner (▶ Mix-Pair-Share): walk the six-step route from the notebook. This time, use three tenses in one answer — what you usually wear, what you're wearing today, and where you got it.

Monitor. Circulate, collect 4–6 errors + 2 good examples for delayed feedback. Note under-users of adverbs.

Freer production — “Style spotlight”
teams → whole class

Personalised task. In teams, each learner gives a 30–40 sec “style spotlight”: Describe your everyday style, one outfit you love, and say how important fashion is to you — with a reason. The others ask one follow-up question each (Really? Why? What about you?).

▶ Numbered Heads Together. Number off in teams; call a number — that learner reports one interesting thing a teammate said (accountability, everyone stays ready to speak).

Exam bridge (OGE speaking, light). Talking about yourself with a reason and an opinion is exactly what the speaking monologue rewards.
