🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): Two real things in one pair: phrases that work in an actual shop (they can be carried into a trip and into life), and the first big written product of the unit. For new students the dialogue is built as a staircase: video → choral shadowing → a ready dialogue with gaps → their own on the model. The photo description climbs the same staircase: questions → notes → text on a skeleton → checklist. because and so get a physical anchor as early as the warm-up.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today you learn the sentences that work in a real shop, in any country: How much is it? Can I try it on? Have you got this in a medium? First we watch Archie buy a sweater, then we say the phrases together, then you play the scene yourselves — with a script if you need one. And after that: your first photo description, step by step. Two words will hold it together: because and so.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: We learn the phrases that work in a real shop; we watch Archie buy something, repeat them chorally and act the scene out (a script is allowed). In the second half — describing your own photo step by step, with the linkers because and so.

WHAT WE DO: the dialogue from the ready text → swap in your own item and price → two run-throughs with a change of roles

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Run 1: we read the ex.5 dialogue in parts, with the cards on the desks. Read it twice — then swap roles.
  2. Kagan Inside-Outside Circle: the inner circle are the assistants, the outer one the customers; after each round the outer circle moves on by one. The dialogue text may stay in their hands.
  3. Run 2: we change only THREE things — the item, the price and the size. Everything else comes from the text. Small changes — big confidence.
  4. I walk round and prompt with gestures (a hand pointing for “over there”, a look at the label for “hold on”, handing an item over for “here you go”).

WHAT WE DO: the two descriptions A and B: why the writer likes them (ex.2), the where/what table (ex.3), because and so (ex.4–5)

  4. ex.5 in writing with a self-check. Key: 1 so · 2 because · 3 because · 4 so · 5 because.
  5. I point it out: the photo — present continuous; 'I always wear hats' — present simple. Both, in one text.

WHAT WE DO: 6 questions → notes → 4–6 sentences on the skeleton → checklist → swap with a partner

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Step 1: Choose ONE photo (or draw one). Answer the six questions — NOTES only: where · why there · what you're wearing · who with · what they're doing · why you like it.
  2. Step 2: the sentence openers on screen — In this photo, I'm … · I'm wearing … · … is wearing … · I like this photo because … We write 4–6 sentences. The word bank is open.
  3. Step 3: the CHECK checklist p.45 on your own work — present continuous for the photo? present simple for the habit? are because and so in place?
  4. Step 4: swap with a partner on ex.8 (three questions) — the partner underlines but does NOT rewrite.
  5. The descriptions go on the class wall next to the photos.
📺 On screen: the 6 questions on the slide · the opener skeleton · the checklist · word bank · timer (Backup: the word bank written on the board and uncovered before the lesson)
👦 Students do: they write up the staircase with support. Expect: 4–6 sentences from everyone; the partner check catches because/so

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can play a clothes-shopping dialogue using 8+ Key phrases and write a photo description following the model, with because and so.
  • Subsidiary. The Archie video (ex.2–5); sorting the phrases into assistant/customer; the model descriptions A and B; the ex.3 table; the because/so rule (ex.4–5); the CHECK checklist p.45.
  • Personal. The writing happens in class with my support; no “finish it at home” — at home they only copy it out neatly. The dialogue goes chorally with the video first, then at the desk, and only volunteers come to the front.
By the end, students can…
  • ask for an item, a price and a size in a shop
  • play a shopping dialogue with a partner
  • write 4–6 sentences describing a photo
  • use because for a reason and so for a result

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
Are “I'm just looking” and “I'm looking for” the same? → no: the first means “I'm only browsing”, the second means “I'm searching for”.
Is “It's a bit small” rude? → no, it is an ordinary polite shop phrase.
“I like this photo so it's funny” — what is wrong? → a reason is needed: because.
Which tense do we describe a photo in? → present continuous: I'm standing, I'm wearing.

Assumptions: SB pp.43–45, KTP pair 26. The THINK→PERFORM skeleton comes from the book; with new students the dialogue goes from a ready text to their own, not the other way round.

Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
