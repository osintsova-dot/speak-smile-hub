🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Retrieve and repair everything from Units 9 and 10 — the technology words, the four futures and the future continuous, enough / too / very, the wildlife words, the at-phrases and all three conditionals — and then produce it under test conditions in the two official Cambridge unit tests.
What is genuinely hard
The Unit 10 grammar sections mix zero, first and second conditionals deliberately, so the first decision on every item is which type it is — and a student who does not make that decision consciously will put will after if at least twice. The Unit 9 vocabulary section is spelled letter by letter: satellite and experiment have to be exactly right.
Typical error to expect
«If it will rain, we will stay at home» — the if-half takes the present simple. This is the single most reliable conditional error of Russian-speaking candidates and it is on the block test twice.
environment, endangered, population — three words the whole unit stands on and all three are misspelled by half a group. They are worth a written drill, not an oral one.
Why the lesson is built this way
The first half is retrieval, not re-teaching, and it uses the book's own Review 3 spread — which covers Units 9–12, so only the sections that belong to our two units are played and the class is told that in one honest sentence. The Workbook listening that was deliberately left blank last lesson is done here, with the recording, because it is the one exam skill homework cannot deliver. Then the official review game, filtered to Units 9–10. The second half is the real paper, briefed honestly — and today the briefing carries one extra sentence, because the next lesson is the half-year paper and the class needs to hear that now, not on the day.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Forty-five minutes to get it all back, then forty-five to show it. One warning: on the Unit ten grammar, three different conditionals are mixed on purpose, so the first question on every item is «which type is this?». And one piece of news — next lesson is the half-year paper, and you will see the scale before you start it.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, and the rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — today's input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, ninety seconds of retrieval with a spelling penalty, and the shape of the day

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One sentence — what are you doing this evening? Any answer counts as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — the block in twelve words. Kagan RallyCoach in pairs: one pen, one paper, ninety seconds — twelve words from Units 9 and 10, taking turns, spelling each one aloud as you write it.
  3. Swap papers with the pair behind and mark. One point per word, minus one for a spelling error — announced before they start, because that is exactly how the Unit 9 paper works.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): name the day honestly: First half: get everything back. Second half: show it. Nothing new today.
📺 On screen: the two word walls, still up from the whole block — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: write in turns, then mark someone else's list against the wall

Why: A specific briefing removes the fear that comes from not knowing the shape — and naming the half-year paper today prevents it from hijacking this lesson's test.

WHAT WE DO: what is on the two papers, how long they take, and one sentence about next lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Put the day's blocks and the clock on the board so the class sees the whole 45 minutes at once.
  2. Brief the tests from the specification table in this plan: Unit 9 — technology words spelled letter by letter, very/too/enough, and the future forms twice. Unit 10 — wildlife words, the at-phrases, and conditionals in two sections.
  3. The warning that saves marks, said now: On the conditional sections, all three types are mixed on purpose. Before you write anything, decide which type it is — look at the if-half first.
  4. Explain which parts of Review 3 are being used and why: The page covers Units nine to twelve. Eleven and twelve are January. We do Vocabulary one, two and five, Grammar two and four.
  5. Then thirty seconds about next lesson, deliberately: Next lesson is the half-year paper. Eighty marks, forty-five minutes, Units one to seven, and the scale goes on the board before you start. Nothing about it will be a surprise.
  6. Promise the keys: Papers back next lesson with the answers.
📺 On screen: the timetable, the test sections and the conditional warning on the board — chalk and paper only, nothing to fail
👦 Students do: listen and ask; two students repeat the timings and the conditional warning back to the room

WHAT WE DO: WB p.43 Listening, exercises 1–4 (WB track 05) — the page left blank last lesson

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Say why it was left blank in one sentence: Listening is the one thing you cannot do properly at home without me. So we do it here.

WHAT WE DO: SB p.75 Grammar exercises 2 and 4, plus a conditional-type sorting table

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Three tables, three tasks, three minutes each, groups rotate on your signal.
  2. Table A — the future continuous: Review 3 Grammar ex.2, five items from a box ('ll be enjoying, will be studying, will be happening, will be waiting, will be travelling). The sheet says one thing: in progress at a moment in the future.
  3. Table B — conditionals: Review 3 Grammar ex.4, five items across all three types (caught, has, gets, knew, suggest). Rule on the sheet: write the TYPE before the form.
  4. Table C — the four futures: eight sentences you write from Unit 9 exercise 4, to be labelled ARRANGEMENT, PLAN, PREDICTION or IN PROGRESS and then completed.
  5. Kagan Showdown at each table: everyone writes privately, all reveal together, disagreements settled before the rotation.
  6. Monitor for the block's two errors and repair on the spot: will after if, and will for an arrangement.
📺 On screen: the three table sheets, prepared in advance from the Review pages — nothing to fail
👦 Students do: everyone writes at every table and reveals at the same moment; nobody watches

Why: Review game · «What's the word?»

WHAT WE DO: the official Cambridge review game, filtered to Units 9–10

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Set it up in ninety seconds: «WHAT'S THE WORD?» — PREPARE_5_Review_game_Units_09-12.pdf. Two teams, A and B, one sheet each, and the sheets must not be visible to the other team. Team A reads out a sentence from their sheet, pausing at the gap (they do NOT say the word printed in capitals — that is the answer). Team B chooses the word from the box at the bottom of their own sheet. Three points if they get it first time, two at the second attempt, one at the third. One word in each box is never used.
  2. Announce the filter and why: the sheets cover Units 9–12, and Units 11–12 have not been taught yet. Play only the lines whose answer belongs to our two units: invention · fuel · pump · power · display · plug in · satellite · enough · too · crops · creature · population · rainforest · endangered · jungle · habitat · landscape · environment · at once · at risk · at least. Mark the Unit 11–12 lines on both sheets with a highlighter BEFORE the lesson and say aloud why they are skipped — they come back in five weeks. The follow-up printed in the rules is worth two minutes at the end: find FIVE words that could fill the same gap
  3. Play with the scoring exactly as printed — three, two, one — because the descending points are what keep the losing team thinking.
  4. Two minutes before the end, stop and run the follow-up printed in the rules on one sentence: find FIVE words that could fill the same gap.
📺 On screen: one A and one B sheet per pair of teams, cut along the dotted line and pre-marked. Backup: if you have only one copy, run it as a whole-class game with you reading team A's sheet
👦 Students do: teams listen to each other and choose from a word box; the team decides together before anyone answers

WHAT WE DO: Unit 9 Standard and Unit 10 Standard — 64 items, about 35 minutes with checking time

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silence, and you sit down. Do not walk the rows for the first ten minutes — it makes nervous students slower.
  2. Answer only procedural questions, never linguistic ones.
  3. Write the remaining time on the board at 15 minutes and at 5 minutes. Say nothing else.
  4. Stronger students who asked for the Plus versions sit those instead — the sections are identical, so the timing does not change.
  5. At minute 30, one sentence: Five minutes. Spend them on the spelling in the vocabulary sections and on the if-half of every conditional.

👦 Students do: work silently for 35 minutes, then check rather than sit idle

Two official Cambridge unit tests (Standard), 64 items in about 35–40 minutes — comfortably inside the 45-minute half, with checking time. Answer keys are in the same two folders. Likely problemWhat you do

Anxiety about the half-year paper arrives during the block testDeal with it in the briefing, before either paper: name the date, the length, the number of marks and the fact that the scale goes on the board before they start. Thirty seconds now buys a calm test today AND a calm test next lesson.

Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.

🇷🇺 Where this lands in the EGE (Russian state exam, grades 10–11) — teacher's note, never taken into class

The same skill is tested by: Tasks 19–31 (grammar and vocabulary — thirteen marks of the written paper).

The block test is built the way the exam block is: produce the form and spell it right. A conditional with will in both halves, or environment written with an extra n, scores nothing at all — which is why the briefing names both ghosts aloud before the papers go face down.

Who needs this: not every student in the group will sit the EGE. Those who will get the trainer link in the homework as an optional extra. The rest are told nothing — the lesson works as language teaching without it.
