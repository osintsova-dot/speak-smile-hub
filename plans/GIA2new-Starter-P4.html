🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Why it is built this way (for the teacher): The fourth pair closes Starter and launches the year. For the “new students” track there are two delicate spots here. The first is the diagnostic: Macmillan has no Starter test at all (the Teacher's Book points you to the Diagnostic test in the TRC), and that is very lucky, because giving new students a graded test in their fourth lesson is a sure way to lose half of them. So: no marks, nothing in the register, they check themselves against the key, and the paper stays with them. The second is the presentation: over three pairs the card has been built piece by piece, and today they speak about themselves in full for the first time. With the sheet in hand and the crew right there — so that it becomes a win, not an ordeal.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Today your card is finished. Three parts, one person — you. First we go round four stations and warm everything up. Then a diagnostic: listen carefully — no marks, nothing in the register, and I don't even collect the papers. You check your own work and you keep it. It shows YOU what to practise. And then: you present your card to the class. With your notes. And then Unit One opens.”
In simpler words, if the class needs support: Today the card is finished in full. First four review stations, then a diagnostic — no marks, nothing in the register, I don't even collect the papers, you check your own work. And at the end you introduce yourself to the class, with your sheet in hand.

WHAT WE DO: 4 stations of 4 minutes, each with cards and a support table on it: this is a warm-up, not a test

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Four stations, four minutes each. Cards and helper tables are ON the stations — use them. This is a warm-up, not a test.
  2. Station 1 · Countries and nationalities: cards (28 of them), Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade: show a card — the partner says the nationality — swap.
  3. Station 2 · Family and appearance: cards (31 of them) + the family tree, Kagan RallyRobin: taking turns, one word and one sentence with it.
  4. Station 3 · be and have got: support tables on the table, six gap-fill lines in writing, the key in an envelope — they check themselves.
  5. Station 4 · can: the list of verbs and qualifiers, four sentences in writing (aff. · neg. · question · short answer), the key in an envelope.
  6. The personal list starts here already: Write down what was hard for YOU. That's your list, nobody else's.
📺 On screen: station sheets and envelopes with the keys · Starter cards (75 of them) · support tables · timer (backup: four stations = four corners of the room, the tasks on A4 sheets)
👦 Students do: they go round four stations with support. Expect: station 3 (the forms) is the hardest, station 1 the quickest. Problem: they skip the envelope with the key and don't check themselves — I remind them at every rotation

WHAT WE DO: 26 minutes: 7 sections, 60 points — from easy to hard, with every section explained before the start

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Before handing them out I go through the sections out loud, showing the paper: Section one: countries. Section two: words. Section three: 'be'… Everything you did in four lessons. Nothing new.
  2. Twenty-six minutes. Easy things first. Stuck? Leave it, go on, come back. And out loud: If you reach section five — that's already good.
  3. I put the listening on in the 16th minute, twice: audio 5 (appearance descriptions, SB p.9 ex.3) — a format they know from P2.
  4. The last section is writing: Six lines about you: name · age · country · family · what you look like · one thing you can do. In this section the word bank STAYS on the screen — the aim is a connected text, not memory.
  5. For the fast ones, a quiet bonus task on the back: describe a classmate in five lines.
📺 On screen: the diagnostic papers (the specification is in the reference note at the foot of the plan) · audio 5 · word bank for the writing section · timer (backup with no sound: I read the descriptions myself, twice)
👦 Students do: they work in silence and without panic. Expect: almost everyone gets to section 6; the writing goes easily, because it is their own card. Problem: they give up on a hard section — the “skip it and come back” rule was stated before the start

Self-check + stickers
solo → board

WHAT WE DO: they mark themselves against the key, circle every slip, write out THREE things to practise + stickers

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Keys on the screen. Mark your own paper. Circle every slip — spelling and apostrophes too. The papers stay with them, I don't collect them.
  2. Now the important part: write THREE things to practise before Unit One. Three, not thirty.
  3. The overall picture with no names, four typical slips on the board: short answers (Yes, I am / Yes, he has) · the apostrophe in the plural (parents’) · has got in the 3rd person · can with to.
  4. Stickers in the margin: 🟢 got it · 🟡 so-so · 🔴 didn't get it — for each section.
  5. Meanwhile I go quickly along the rows and write down a map of the class FOR MYSELF: who needs which supports in Unit 1.
  6. 🟢🟡🔴 Self-check stickers: after every mini-test the student sticks a sticker in the margin themselves: 🟢 got it · 🟡 so-so · 🔴 didn't get it. This is NOT a mark — it is my map of who to go to during the next task. I say this out loud every single time.
📺 On screen: the keys on the slide + a printout for every desk · stickers (Backup: I read the keys out and write the tricky ones on the board)
👦 Students do: they check themselves and see their errors before any mark. Expect: everyone's three “holes” are different — and that is exactly right. Problem: “I was nearly right” — the rule is to circle every single slip

WHAT WE DO: the product: 6–8 lines about yourself (all three parts together) → crew circle → volunteers to the class, with the sheet

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 5 minutes to assemble it: Put your three parts together into ONE text. Six to eight lines: who I am · my family · what I look like · what I can do. You already wrote every line — just join them.
  2. Checklist on the desk: country with from? · has got with he/she? · apostrophe? · can with no to and with a qualifier?
  3. The first round is compulsory, inside their own crew: everyone reads their text to the crew. With the sheet.
  4. The second round is to the class, volunteers only: Who wants to introduce themselves to everyone? Notes in your hand, crew standing behind you.
  5. I praise specifically and only for something specific: Clear! Four systems in one text! Great detail about your family! I don't go over a single error at the board.
📺 On screen: the students' three card parts · the 4-point checklist · word bank · timer
👦 Students do: they join up what they wrote and read it out. Expect: almost everyone comes out, because they have already read it in the crew. This is the main win of Starter for new students. Problem: they read in a monotone — I don't correct it, a first public presentation matters more than intonation

How the year works: units, missions, points
How our year works
board → circle

WHAT WE DO: we close Starter and explain how the year works: a unit of 8 pairs, project, test, leaderboard

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Starter is finished. Now the real year. Here's how it works.
  2. A unit = 8 pairs. I draw eight boxes on the board and label them in plain words: vocabulary → reading → grammar → vocabulary and listening → grammar → speaking → project → test.
  3. The project. Every unit ends with something your crew MAKES and shows. In Unit One you design your ideal school timetable.
  4. The test. I say it honestly and in advance: Unit tests DO have marks — unlike today. But you'll always know exactly what's on them: everything we did in class, nothing else.
  5. The leaderboard. New unit, new crews, new board. Nobody carries a bad start.
  6. The structures. I name the things that have already worked, so that from now on one word is enough: Quiz-Quiz-Trade · RallyRobin · Timed Pair Share · Find Someone Who · Showdown.
  7. The hook: Unit One: School life. The question is 'What is an ideal school?' and at the end you design one.
📺 On screen: the 8-box diagram of a unit on the board · the leaderboard sheet (backup — the whole diagram is drawn in marker, no slides needed)
👦 Students do: they see the map of the year as a whole. Expect: the honest warning about marks on unit tests removes the future shock and raises their trust in today's “no marks”

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, learners can present themselves to the class in 6–8 lines, using all four Starter systems together (be · possessives · have got · can), and name three things to practise before Unit 1.
  • Subsidiary. A round-up of all five vocabulary sets in stations with support; an entry diagnostic (60 points, no mark) with immediate self-check against the key and stickers; an introduction to how the year works: a unit of 8 pairs, project, test, leaderboard.
  • Personal. I never say the word “test”. I don't collect the papers — I promised and I keep to it, otherwise the trust is gone for the rest of the year. I run the presentation in two rounds: first in the crew (safe), then to the class — and volunteers only, though by that point almost everyone volunteers.

Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“Is this a test? Will there be a mark?” → no. It doesn't go in the register and I don't collect the papers. It is there for you, to show what to practise before Unit 1.
“Can I look at the cards?” → at the stations you can and you should, in the diagnostic no. Otherwise you won't see what already holds by itself.
“I've got a lot of mistakes — is that bad?” → what is bad is not finding them now. In the fourth lesson a mistake takes five minutes to fix.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“Diagnostic” still sounds like a testThree times in the pair, in the same words: no marks, nothing in the register, I don't collect the papers. And I really don't collect them — otherwise the trust is gone for the rest of the year.
Someone doesn't finish in 26 minutesThe sections run from easy to hard, and I say it out loud: “if you got to section 5, that's already good”. Unfinished work is not a failure, and it goes into the gap list as “didn't get there”, not as “mistake”.
Fear of presenting in front of the whole classTwo rounds: first they read in their own crew (everyone, compulsory), then to the class — volunteers only. By that point almost everyone volunteers, because they have already read it aloud once.
Stations without supports fall flatEvery station has cards and a support table on it. The point of the stations is to warm them up, not to test them: the testing comes in 20 minutes.
Classroom management — signals
«Eyes up in 3-2-1» — count down calmly → all eyes on you at «1». No shouting over noise, ever.
Visible timer — timer on screen = we work; timer stops = pens/hands down. The timer is the boss, not you.
Volume dial — arm up/down = louder/quieter; «radio whisper» for rehearsals.
«Boards up!» — mini-whiteboard rounds: everyone commits, all boards up together — nobody hides, nobody shouts first.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, class answers with two and freezes — instant reset mid-game.
English-first — ideas in Russian are welcome, POINTS only for the English version — recast and repeat.
