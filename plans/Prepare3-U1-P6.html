Aims & objectives
  • Main aim. By the end, learners will be better able to read short texts, signs and messages for meaning and purpose and to answer A2 Key Reading Part 1 (3-option multiple choice) and Part 2 (matching) items using exam strategies — meaning-matching over word-spotting, timing, and always answering.
  • Subsidiary. Retrieve & recycle Block 1 language in an interleaved way — personality adjectives, personal details, geographical features, animals, Present Simple vs Present Continuous, stative verbs; develop peer-assessment and self-correction (translate sheet).
  • Personal. Keep teacher-talk lean; give an ICQ before every timed task; model the strategy with a think-aloud (I do → we do → you do) before releasing to independent practice; run low-stakes retrieval, not high-stakes testing.
By the end, learners can…
  • identify the text type & purpose of a sign / notice / message and pick the option that matches the meaning, not just the words;
  • answer a Part 2 matching set by reading the questions first, scanning for evidence and rejecting distractors;
  • work to time, guess-and-move, and leave no blanks;
  • recall & write Block 1 sentences (Units 1–2) from memory and self-correct.

Strategy talk
Who wrote it and why? · It's the same meaning, not the same words. · Where's your evidence? · Don't get stuck — guess and move on.
Exam format
Part 1: 6 short texts (signs, notices, messages) → choose A/B/C — the sentence that explains the meaning. Part 2: 7 questions → match statements to three short texts (A/B/C).
Concept checks (CCQs) · Assumptions
“In Part 1, do we pick the option with the same words or the same meaning?” → same meaning.
“In Part 2, do we read the texts first or the questions first?” → questions first, then scan.
“A word in the text matches a word in the question — is that always the answer?” → No (word-spotting trap / distractor).
“There's no negative marking. Should we ever leave a blank?” → Never — always answer.

Assumptions: learners met Units 1–2 language across Pairs 1–4 and sat no test yet on it (Pair 5 review + block test may run adjacent). They can read A2 short texts independently. Exam format Parts 1–2 is being formally introduced here for the first time this year. Mixed reading speed is likely → tiering and pair support built in.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
Word-spotting — choosing an option just because a word appears in bothThink-aloud the trap on the first item; teach “same meaning, not same words”; ask Where's your evidence? every time.
Running out of time / getting stuck on one itemSet a visible timer per part; drill Guess and move on. Come back at the end.
Over-translating every wordGist first: Read for the message, not every word. Underline key words only.
Leaving blanksNo negative marking — always answer. Best guess beats an empty box.
Weaker readers fall behind in the timed setTiered handout (fewer items / glossed words); pair a stronger + developing reader for the check phase (Rally Coach).

Exam orientation 🧘
board

Map the paper. Show the A2 Key Reading & Writing shape; today = Part 1 (6 short texts: signs, notices, messages → 3-option multiple choice) and Part 2 (7 questions: match statements to three short texts A/B/C).

Elicit strategy, don't lecture. What kind of text is a sign? Who writes it? Why? Build the two golden rules on the board: ① Match the meaning, not the words. ② Always answer — no blanks.

CCQ. Same words or same meaning? → meaning. Leave it blank? → never.

I do (think-aloud). Take the first Part 1 item on SB p.120. Model out loud: Who wrote this? Why? Option A repeats a word — trap. Option C explains the meaning — that's it.

We do. Items 2–3 together, hands up: Where's your evidence? Reject each distractor by naming why it's wrong.

You do. Learners finish Part 1 individually (SB p.120–121). ICQ: Alone or in pairs? → alone. Timer: 6 minutes.

▶ Rally Coach pair-check: partner A explains an answer + evidence, partner B coaches/challenges; swap. Not "what did you get?" but why?

Peer-mark & feedback ⚡
teams of 4

Peer-mark (formative). Swap papers; mark against the key. Rule: for every wrong answer, write why the right option wins (evidence) — not just the letter.

▶ Showdown / Numbered Heads: for the two trickiest items, teams agree an answer + evidence; you call a number — that learner answers for the team. Spotlight the distractor and why it traps.

Delayed error correction. Board the 3–4 language slips you noted (e.g. stative verb in Continuous); learners correct in pairs. Restate the two golden rules.
