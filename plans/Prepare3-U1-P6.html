🎯 Why this lesson looks like this — the design in one card
Language aim
Get the MEANING of a short text when the answer is said in different words, and be able to explain why the wrong option is wrong. That is the whole reading skill, in both exams.
What is genuinely hard
Teenagers read fast and answer from memory. The correct option is nearly always a paraphrase, while the wrong one repeats a word from the text word for word — and the hand reaches for exactly that one (TB p.252: must = have to).
Typical error to expect
They pick the option that repeats a word from the text. The correct option is almost always paraphrased; the wrong ones copy the wording [exam report]
Students mark False where the text simply says nothing: they confuse «contradicts» and «not mentioned». In the exam that third option is Not stated [high priority, A2/B1]
Why the lesson is built this way
Format is taught through one worked example, not through a list of rules: students hear the reasoning behind a right answer before they are asked to produce one, so the strategy arrives attached to a real question. Each part then gets a timed, silent run, because a strategy that only works with help is not a strategy. Distractor analysis has a stage of its own at 60–70′ rather than being folded into feedback: looking at how the trap was built teaches far more than ticking answers, and it needs students to already own their choices. The OGE version comes last on purpose — by then the skill is warm and the Russian format reads as new packaging rather than as a new subject, which is exactly the message of the lesson. Writing their own extra question at the end turns the class from answering traps to building one.
How to announce it (say this, 30–40 seconds):
“Open page 120. Six short messages, three answers each. And here is the trick: the wrong answer usually contains a word from the text, and the right one doesn't. So today we hunt meanings, not words. Every answer needs a line — underlined. No line, no point.”

Why: Every teenager says one English sentence before the lesson proper starts, so the first word out of their mouth is English. The rewind pulls last lesson's language back into working memory — new input lands on something.

WHAT WE DO: greeting, the test handed back with one shared error repaired, and the bridge into the Prepare for the exam section

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. 2 minutes — welcome check-in. Meet them at the door, one question each, one short answer: Hi! One word — how are you today? … Good. Sit down and open your book. Any answer is accepted as long as it is in English.
  2. 3 minutes — last lesson rewind. Hand the test papers back and give exactly 2 minutes of silent reading. Look at your five mistakes. Do not compare with your neighbour yet.
  3. One error the whole block shares goes on the board and the class repairs it chorally: I'm knowing this word. → I know this word.
  4. Bridge into the lesson (30 seconds): You showed what you know. Now look at what it is FOR — open page 120.
📺 On screen: the sentence to be corrected goes on the board — the board is the medium here and the papers are on paper, so nothing has to be displayed and no backup is needed
👦 Students do: look through their own papers and repair the shared sentence. Expect a calm reaction if the test was honestly «from the classroom». Typical problem: they start comparing marks at once — hold the rule «your own mistakes first»

WHAT WE DO: set the frame of the day: the skill is one, but it can be asked for in different ways

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Draw two columns on the board, without any drama. On the left, in the coursebook: three short texts, seven questions, question → text. On the right, in the OGE: six texts A–F, seven questions, text → question, one question left over.
  2. The key line of the lesson, and it gets repeated: Different papers, same skill: find the meaning, not the word.
  3. Say the exam bit calmly and briefly, with no pressure: Some of you will take an English exam in Year 9, some of you will not. Today is not about the exam — it is about reading properly. But you will see both versions, because they teach the same thing.
  4. Set the bar: By the end you can say WHY a wrong answer is wrong. That is the whole skill.
📺 On screen: a slide with the two columns «in the coursebook / in the OGE» (Backup: the same two columns in chalk on the board — they stay up all lesson and are needed again at 70–82′)
👦 Students do: compare the two columns. Expect them to spot straight away that «there are more questions than texts» — that is the way into the 70–82′ stage. Typical problem: they start worrying about the exam; take it off the table in one sentence, «two years away and not for everybody»

WHAT WE DO: the format of Part 1 and the first question worked through together (SB p.120)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the Exam information: Six short texts: messages, notes, notices, signs, postcards. Each has one question with three options A/B/C.
  2. Put the three Exam tips on the board: 1) look at the text and decide WHERE you would see it (a phone, a noticeboard, a shop door); 2) say the main idea of the text in your own words BEFORE you read the options; 3) read all three options to the end: the wrong one often repeats the words of the text.
  3. Work through question 1 together: Question 1 (SB p.120): a message from Dom to Jules — «I tried on my ski jacket and it's too small. Have you or your brother got one I could take on holiday?»
  4. Kagan Think-Pair-Share: What kind of message is it? Which option is right? Why are the other two wrong?
  5. Key and explanation (TB p.252): Answer A. Dom wants to borrow a jacket — his own is too small. B is wrong: he is talking about the size of HIS OWN jacket. C is wrong: he is asking whether Jules or his brother has one, not asking for his own back (TB p.252).
  6. Name the word that decides it: borrow — everything hangs on it.
📺 On screen: question 1 (Dom's message to Jules) on the screen, underlined live in front of everybody (Backup: copy the message and the three options onto the board before the lesson — the students have exactly the same thing open in the book on p.120)
👦 Students do: work the example through in pairs and name the deciding word. Expect half the class to go for B at first. Typical error: they cling to small, which appears both in the text and in the option

WHAT WE DO: questions 2–6 in exam conditions, then a paired check where the reason is the answer

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silent mode: Six minutes. Alone. Underline the key words. Go. Write the finish time on the board.
  2. After 6 minutes — Kagan RallyCoach: partners take turns explaining their choice. Not the letter — the reason. 4 minutes.
  3. Collect answers with Kagan Numbered Heads Together: Question four — number ones, boards up!
  4. Key (TB p.252): 1 A · 2 C · 3 B · 4 B · 5 A · 6 C (TB p.252)
  5. Do not explain it yourself — ask the students who got it right to show the line.
📺 On screen: the finish time written on the board — the board is the medium here, no backup needed; while they work nothing is displayed at all and the task is open in the book on p.120–121
👦 Students do: work in silence, then explain their choices in pairs. Expect 4–5 out of 5 from most of them. Typical error: question 5 (Bridgetown) — they take C because it carries familiar words about places

WHAT WE DO: the format of Part 2 and a synonym hunt across the seven questions (SB p.122)

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Read the Exam information: Three short texts and seven questions: which text does each question belong to (A/B/C). The texts: «Three unusual homes» (SB p.122): Sarah lives on a houseboat on a river; Andrea lives in a lighthouse; Trudi lives in a house her father built, with walls and a roof of earth and grass.
  2. Pre-teach lighthouse and houseboat from the pictures on the page.
  3. The key step (TB p.253): find a synonym for every question BEFORE anybody reads the texts. Kagan Quiz-Quiz-Trade with synonym pairs, 4 minutes.
  4. Check against: TB p.253 asks you to collect the synonyms in advance: 1 other people = friends · 2 safe = comfortable · 3 happy with = satisfied by · 4 bright = not dark · 5 spending the night = sleeping over · 6 needs a lift = has to ask for help getting somewhere · 7 feels less comfortable = isn't very happy
  5. Predict before reading: What will each person say about her home?
📺 On screen: the seven questions on the screen with the key words underlined on them live (Backup: the seven questions copied onto the board while students underline in their own book on p.122; the synonym cards are printed, so they need nothing at all)
👦 Students do: underline key words and find synonyms. Expect needs a lift to be new to almost everyone. Typical error: they underline everything — cap it at two words per question

Reading Part 2 · exam conditions
solo → pairs
Why: Second timed run — this time the rule is that every answer needs an underlined line of evidence.

WHAT WE DO: the «Three unusual homes» task: seven questions in exam conditions plus a check by line

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Silent mode: Ten minutes. Alone. Every answer needs an underlined line.
  2. After 10 minutes — check in pairs: Read out the line you underlined. If your partner has a different line, one of you is wrong. 5 minutes.
  3. Key (TB p.253): 1 A · 2 B · 3 A · 4 C · 5 A · 6 B · 7 C (TB p.253)
  4. Say nothing about the errors yet — the analysis stage is next.
📺 On screen: the finish time on the board — the board is the medium here and no backup is needed, since nothing is displayed while they work and the texts and questions are open in the book on p.122
👦 Students do: work in silence, then compare lines. Expect 5–6 out of 7. Typical error: questions 3 and 7 (both about comfort) — Sarah and Trudi get mixed up

Why the wrong answer is wrong
class
Why: Analysing distractors teaches more than checking answers: they see the trap being built, so they recognise it next time.

WHAT WE DO: analysis line by line: why this option is right and why the others are not

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go through every Part 2 question on the formula «line → why»: 1 A — «Lots of my friends live on houseboats too» · 2 B — «But the building is very strong, and I never feel afraid.» · 3 A — «We haven't got much space, but we don't mind – it's enough for us.» · 4 C — «Lots of light comes in» · 5 A — «They always sleep really well and love being on the water.» · 6 B — «my parents have to drive me everywhere» · 7 C — «I often get too hot and want to open a window.»
  2. Come back to the two disputed ones (3 and 7) and separate the two women on the board: Sarah — small space, but happy. Trudi — lots of light, but too hot in other people's homes.
  3. Pick up the three most disputed Part 1 questions from the TB: 2 (School café): C — «This is how students can give their opinion of the menu». A is wrong: the box doesn't give information. B is wrong: new dishes aren't mentioned. || 3 (Abby's message): B — in the right option contact = the email in the text. A is wrong: the club hasn't started yet. C is wrong: Abby thinks several people want to join. || 4 (Year 9 art exhibition): B — «how long they've got to finish their paintings»: the notice gives a deadline (by Friday, 12th). A and C aren't in the text at all.
  4. Name the conclusion of the lesson: Where marks go: students pick the option that contains a word from the text. At A2 the correct option is almost always a SYNONYM, not the same word (TB p.252: must = have to).
📺 On screen: highlight the evidence lines straight in the text on the screen (Backup: read every line out loud while students underline in their own books, and keep a «question → whose line» table on the board)
👦 Students do: find the lines and explain them. Expect the «read the paraphrase, not the word» move to click once the disputed questions have been unpacked. Typical error: they keep hunting for the exact word — send them back to the synonym table

WHAT WE DO: the same skill in the Russian wrapper: task 12 in the school trainer, plus writing an extra trap question of their own

👩‍🏫 Teacher does — step by step:
  1. Go back to the two columns on the board and open the right-hand one: Six short texts labelled A–F and seven heading-questions. Each text takes one question. Seven questions, six texts — one question is always extra. Today's text is about Antarctica — the block topic (planet and nature), so the Unit 2 words work directly.
  2. What is the same: Same skill as Reading Part 2 in the coursebook: find the phrase that carries the same meaning, not the same word. What is different: Three differences: six texts instead of three; you match a question to a text, not a text to a question; exactly one question is left over, and students must name it out loud — that is the self-check that everything lines up.
  3. Open the trainer link (the same one goes home as optional homework): https://speakandsmile.online/oge.html#/hw?reading:mt_49E1EB — it opens straight away, no password.
  4. Teams of 3–4, one phone or laptop per team, 6 minutes. Six texts, seven questions. One question is extra — and you must name it.
  5. Kagan Showdown: on your command every team reveals the number of the extra question at once. Check against: A–1 · B–7 · C–3 · D–4 · E–6 · F–5 (the extra question is 2). The app shows the key itself the moment students submit.
  6. Trap workshop (4 minutes): each team writes its OWN extra question for the same text — one that looks as if it fits. Your extra question must use a word from the text. The winning trap is the one that fools the most people.
📺 On screen: task 12 open on the screen or on the teams' phones (Backup with no internet: print the same task in advance through «Print worksheet» in the teacher mode of the app, one sheet per team — then nothing has to be displayed at all)
👦 Students do: do the OGE version and invent an extra question. Expect them to get the mechanics inside a minute, because Part 2 has just been done. Typical error: they forget to name the extra question — and that is exactly the step that shows whether the rest lines up

📋 Preparation reference — read BEFORE the lesson, not in class
  • Main aim. By the end, students can complete Reading Parts 1 and 2 in exam conditions and explain why each wrong option is wrong.
  • Subsidiary. The «where would I see this?» strategy for short texts; underlining the key words of a question before reading; collecting synonyms for the wording of the questions.
  • Personal. Do not give the key straight after the timed run: pairs explain their choices to each other first. The key goes up only once the arguments have been heard.
By the end, students can…
  • say where a short text would be seen and what its main message is
  • underline the key words in a question before reading
  • match a question to the right text and quote the proving line
  • explain why a wrong option is wrong

Concept checks (CCQs) & ICQs · Assumptions
An option contains a word from the text — is that good news or suspicious? → suspicious: the correct option is usually paraphrased, the wrong one copies the text word for word.
You answered but underlined nothing — does it count? → no: the rule of this lesson is «no line, no point».
You didn't understand two words in the text — is the task lost? → no, the task tests meaning, not vocabulary size (Exam tip, SB p.122).
Six texts but seven questions — is that a misprint? → no, in the OGE one question is always extra, and naming it out loud is part of the check.

Anticipated problems & solutions
Likely problemWhat you do
“This is obvious” — they answer in forty seconds and get it wrongMake one step compulsory: underline the key words in the question first, read the options second. An answer with nothing underlined is not accepted.
They argue “I'm right” with nothing to stand onOnly a line from the text settles an argument: finger on the line, read it out loud.
Part 2: Sarah and Trudi get mixed up (both talk about light and about size)Separate them in advance: Sarah has little space but is happy with it; Trudi has a lot of light and quiet. Keep two contrasting key words for them on the board.
“Why are we doing two exams at once?” — at the OGE stageAnswer honestly and briefly: there is one exam, it is two years away, and not everybody here will sit it. Today is not about the exam but about the fact that one and the same skill can be asked in different ways — and both times the student who reads for meaning wins.
Classroom management — signals
“Eyes up in 3-2-1” — a calm countdown → on “1” every pair of eyes is on you. Never talk over the class.
Visible timer — timer running = we work; timer stops = pens down. The timer is the boss, not you.
“Boards up!” — a mini-whiteboard round: everyone answers at the same moment — nobody hides, nobody shouts out first.
Silent mode — palm down = we work alone and in silence: listening or writing. Questions afterwards, not during.
One clap — two claps — you clap once, the class claps twice and freezes — an instant reset in the middle of a game.
English-first — an idea may be started in Russian, but the POINT is only given for the English version: reformulate and repeat it.
